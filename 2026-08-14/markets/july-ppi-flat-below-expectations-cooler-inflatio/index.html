July producer prices flat in cooling inflation sign; traders trim September Fed hike bets

Financial & Markets desk · anchored on Reuters

Wholesale prices were flat in July, the Bureau of Labor Statistics reported Thursday, a smaller increase than expected and the latest sign that inflation pressures are easing after an earlier ramp-up. The producer price index was unchanged for the month, below the 0.2% Dow Jones consensus estimate and after a revised 0.1% decline in June, previously reported as a 0.3% drop. Excluding food and energy, core PPI rose 0.2% versus the 0.3% forecast, while core PPI excluding trade services increased 0.4%. On an annual basis, headline PPI rose 4.7% and core PPI 4.2%.

The report followed several indicators showing that after inflation accelerated earlier this year, fueled by the Iran war and President Donald Trump's tariffs, price increases are beginning to ease. Wednesday's consumer price index rose just 0.1% in July, with headline annual inflation at 3.4%; core CPI rose 0.2% monthly and 2.5% annually, back to its pre-war level. "Net, net, pipeline pressures at the lower stages of production are not adding to the inflation risks the consumer faces," said Chris Rupkey, chief economist at Fwdbonds. "It counts as good news that for a second consecutive month, PPI final demand prices have not gone up adding to the cost of living crisis faced by Americans."

Within the July PPI, services prices rose 0.2%, boosted by a 6.5% surge in portfolio management, a category that can show outsized first-month-of-quarter gains due to reporting requirements. Goods prices fell 0.7% as energy dropped 3.1%, including a 5.7% slide in gasoline; food prices fell 0.9%, while core goods prices rose 0.1%.

Market reaction was positive but muted: stock futures ticked up, Treasury yields moved lower, and traders further reduced odds of a September rate hike from the Federal Reserve. After heavy odds on a move at the Sept. 15-16 FOMC meeting, traders have switched to pricing in a hike in October or December. Federal Reserve officials have been weighing the inflation data as several key officials have pushed for hikes to return inflation to the 2% target. Reuters reported the cooler data may force Warsh's divided Fed to hold the line on rates.

In other economic news, initial jobless claims rose to a seasonally adjusted 209,000 for the week ended Aug. 8, up 9,000 from the prior period and above the 204,000 estimate.

Separate Fed commentary from TD Securities' Gennadiy Goldberg and Molly Brooks, published by FXStreet, said the Fed's decision to halt Reserve Management Purchases after tapering them from $40bn to $10bn per month reflects soft money market rates and an ample reserve buffer, not imminent Quantitative Tightening. They expect RMP to resume at a reduced $5-10bn/month pace in November 2026 before any balance sheet changes in 2027. "Markets may worry that this is the first step on the road back to Quantitative Tightening (QT), but we believe the halt will be temporary and purchases will resume in November to help smooth over money market functioning ahead of year-end." They also noted the Fed's implementation instructions still direct the New York Fed to "increase the System Open Market Account holdings of securities through purchases of Treasury bills."
