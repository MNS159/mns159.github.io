International & World Ukraine War

Ukraine war: civilian deaths rise; drone innovations; Black Sea truce offer

International & World desk · anchored on Reuters

Russian jet-powered drones struck a home in Sumy region, northern Ukraine, early Friday, killing a 29-year-old woman and her 9-year-old son, officials said. The strike came a day after the United Nations reported that July was the deadliest month for Ukrainian civilians since March 2022, with at least 437 civilians killed and 2,610 wounded. "Every month this year the number of killed and injured civilians has increased. That trend accelerated sharply in July," said Danielle Bell, head of the U.N. human rights monitoring mission in Kyiv. Overall, since Russia's invasion on Feb. 24, 2022, the U.N. has documented at least 16,874 civilian deaths, including 820 children, and 51,273 wounded.

Ukraine has proposed a deal with Russia for both sides to halt attacks on civilian ships in the Black Sea, in an apparent attempt to reopen a crucial grain corridor, Reuters reported. Kyiv made the offer through a third party; Moscow had yet to respond. Since July, Ukrainian attacks have effectively closed the route for commercial Russian shipping, hitting nearly 220 vessels. Russia on Thursday struck Ukraine's port of Izmail in the Odesa region. Separately, Ukrainian drones struck the major Gazprom Neftekhim Salavat refinery in Bashkortostan, deep inside Russia, and the port of Ust-Luga in the Leningrad region, a key oil and gas export terminal. Russia's Defense Ministry said its air defenses overnight intercepted 553 Ukrainian drones over 18 regions, Crimea, and the Azov and Black seas.

Financial & Markets Central Banks

July producer prices flat in cooling inflation sign; traders trim September Fed hike bets

Financial & Markets desk · anchored on Reuters

Wholesale prices were flat in July, the Bureau of Labor Statistics reported Thursday, a smaller increase than expected and the latest sign that inflation pressures are easing after an earlier ramp-up. The producer price index was unchanged for the month, below the 0.2% Dow Jones consensus estimate and after a revised 0.1% decline in June, previously reported as a 0.3% drop. Excluding food and energy, core PPI rose 0.2% versus the 0.3% forecast, while core PPI excluding trade services increased 0.4%. On an annual basis, headline PPI rose 4.7% and core PPI 4.2%.

International & World Health

Ebola outbreak in DR Congo spreads to sixth province, deadliest ever warning

International & World desk

The Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of the Congo has spread to a sixth province, officials said Thursday, a day after the head of the World Health Organization warned the outbreak was on track to surpass the deadliest in history. The current outbreak has killed more than 2,100 people out of more than 4,500 cases, according to government figures released Tuesday. At the same point in the 2014-16 West Africa outbreak, which killed at least 11,000 people, only 755 cases had been recorded, according to WHO data.

Israeli settlers besiege West Bank Palestinian homes; US envoy calls it terrorism

International & World desk

About a dozen Palestinian households in the occupied West Bank village of Qusra were told to leave their homes during an Israeli military operation to remove Israeli settlers who had besieged three homes since Sunday, cutting off power and water supplies. One of the targeted houses belongs to a Palestinian-American businessman, drawing an unusually sharp rebuke from U.S. Ambassador to Israel Mike Huckabee, who called the settlers' actions "a horrific act of terror" and "disgusting." The Israeli military said it dismantled two illegal outposts near the homes and detained one Israeli, while the government spokesman described the settlers' behavior as "deplorable" and promised arrests.

USS George Washington to replace USS Abraham Lincoln amid reports of dire conditions

The United States is sending the USS George Washington aircraft carrier to the Middle East to relieve the USS Abraham Lincoln, a US official told CNN, as reports of poor living conditions and mental health strain aboard the Lincoln have prompted calls for a congressional investigation. The Lincoln, which left San Diego in November for what was scheduled as a Pacific deployment, was rerouted to the Persian Gulf in January to support US operations in the war with Iran and has now spent more than 250 consecutive days at sea, including a record-breaking number of days without a port stop, according to military outlets Navy Times and Stars and Stripes.

Google DeepMind Faces Restructuring, Potential Layoffs as Hassabis Pushes for Independent AI Safety Body

Google DeepMind (GDM) is undergoing a major restructuring that will shift its focus away from developing cutting-edge, flagship AI models toward smaller, more cost-efficient Flash models, according to exclusive reporting from the Chinese outlet ifanr, republished by 36Kr. The reorganization is expected to result in large-scale layoffs, potentially affecting one-third or more of the team's 7,000 to 8,000 employees. People familiar with the matter said the cuts are intended to streamline redundant personnel, including algorithm hires who do not perform algorithm work. Employees may transfer internally to avoid layoffs, and some teams have already been merged into other Google business units.
