Databricks raises $5B at $190B valuation, up from initial $1B target

Tech / AI desk

Databricks announced Thursday that it has raised $5 billion in a new funding round at a $190 billion valuation, far exceeding the $1 billion the company initially intended to raise. CEO and co-founder Ali Ghodsi told TechCrunch that the fundraising snowballed after The Information published an article about a potential raise during a company conference in June, triggering a flood of investor interest totaling $15 billion. To avoid alienating long-term backers, Databricks issued more stock and closed the round at a valuation that rose from the $188 billion disclosed in July.

The round was led by Coatue and included Blackstone, MGX, various T. Rowe Price accounts, and new investor Sixth Street Growth, the firm founded by former Goldman Sachs CIO Alan Waxman. Approximately two dozen venture capital firms participated. Ghodsi said Databricks has reached a $7 billion annualized run-rate revenue, growing at 80%, and is cash-flow positive. Its core cloud data warehouse product accounts for $1.5 billion of that run rate and is growing 100% year-over-year. The company’s database for agents, Lakebase, launched in June 2025, has already hit a $100 million revenue run rate, and its AI chatbot tool Genie is “insanely popular,” according to Ghodsi.

Despite having raised $20 billion over the past 20 months, Databricks sought additional capital because of the high cost of AI. Ghodsi noted the company has multibillion-dollar cloud commitments with all three major hyperscalers and an AI research team of 100 people. The company is also active in M&A, having recently acquired Electric, the maker of the lightweight Postgres database PGlite, as well as AI cybersecurity company Panther in June and two other startups in March. Terms of the Electric deal were not disclosed.

Databricks’ repeated private fundraising rounds have become a topic of discussion in Silicon Valley, with some joking that the company is running out of alphabet letters for round names. Ghodsi told CNBC that he still intends to take the company public eventually, but for now he prefers to invest in AI out of the public eye. Given the overwhelming investor demand — $15 billion in interest for a deal he initially wanted to cap at $1 billion — Ghodsi sees no urgency to rush an IPO.
