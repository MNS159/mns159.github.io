Google DeepMind Faces Restructuring, Potential Layoffs as Hassabis Pushes for Independent AI Safety Body

Tech / AI desk

Google DeepMind (GDM) is undergoing a major restructuring that will shift its focus away from developing cutting-edge, flagship AI models toward smaller, more cost-efficient Flash models, according to exclusive reporting from the Chinese outlet ifanr, republished by 36Kr. The reorganization is expected to result in large-scale layoffs, potentially affecting one-third or more of the team's 7,000 to 8,000 employees. People familiar with the matter said the cuts are intended to streamline redundant personnel, including algorithm hires who do not perform algorithm work. Employees may transfer internally to avoid layoffs, and some teams have already been merged into other Google business units.

The shift marks a strategic retreat from competing directly with OpenAI, Anthropic, and other frontier model developers. Google has accepted that it cannot sustain unlimited investment in ultra-large models, and is instead prioritizing Flash models that offer better cost performance for powering its core products—Search, Gmail, Android, YouTube, and Maps—which serve billions of users daily. The GDM team's overall OKR score for the most recent performance review year was 0.5 out of 1.0, reflecting poor performance that has limited its ability to secure resources for training larger models. Google has no plans to update its Pro model line in the short term, and the Gemini 3.5 Pro model, which was never publicly released, has already fallen behind competitors on mainstream benchmarks.

The restructuring has also reshaped leadership. Demis Hassabis, the co-founder of DeepMind who had been the de facto head of GDM, has stepped down from day-to-day leadership to become Alphabet's Chief Scientist and Chairman of DeepMind. His successor, Koray Kavukcuoglu, has significantly less real power. The top-level reporting line for Google's AI-related businesses now leads to Jen Fitzpatrick, Senior Vice President of Core Systems and Experiences, who has gained more authority. Some former GDM teams have been merged into other business units, eroding their autonomy. Meanwhile, Google CEO Sundar Pichai announced that the Gemini App has surpassed 1 billion monthly active users, making it the company's 14th product to reach that milestone and its fastest-growing one.

Separately, The Wall Street Journal reported that Hassabis has been pushing for the creation of an independent organization to establish safety standards for the development of increasingly powerful AI systems. In the weeks before leaving his CEO role, he held discussions with senior U.S. government officials and executives at other leading AI companies. His proposal centers on an industry-supported body that could set common safety standards and best practices as companies race toward artificial general intelligence (AGI). Hassabis has compared aspects of the proposed framework to international efforts used to promote cooperation and establish safeguards in other transformative fields. In a separate proposal published this summer, he suggested creating an industry-funded standards organization.

The broader context reflects a shift in mindset among Silicon Valley giants as the AI industry matures. After years of indulging in grand narratives about AGI and scaling laws, companies are moving away from vanity-driven pursuit of cutting-edge achievements. Google's adjustments signal that even a powerhouse like the company must prioritize cost-effective AI that serves existing products over unlimited bets on flagship models. The changes also come amid reports that Google co-founder Sergey Brin had been motivating the GDM team in recent months, though ifanr described that information as outdated. As of press time, Google had not responded to ifanr's requests for comment on the restructuring and layoff details.
