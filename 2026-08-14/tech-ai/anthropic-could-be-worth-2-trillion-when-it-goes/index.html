Anthropic could be worth $2 trillion when it goes public

Tech / AI desk

Anthropic investors expect the AI startup to float at a valuation of $2 trillion or more in October, a figure that would eclipse SpaceX and make the lab’s debut the largest-ever initial public offering, according to a report from Ars Technica.

Half a dozen of the company’s backers told the Financial Times that Anthropic’s rapidly rising revenue would enable it to more than double its current valuation in a planned autumn float. Investors expect the Claude maker’s annualized revenue to be between $100 billion and $120 billion by the end of 2026—up more than tenfold over the course of that year, using the startup’s preferred measure inferring full-year sales from recent performance.

Anthropic lacks a publicly listed US peer that would provide a benchmark for its valuation. But companies seen as AI beneficiaries, such as Palantir and Nebius, have traded this year at roughly 55 times revenue. “If Anthropic is growing 800 percent a year, you’d think at the incredibly low end they would trade at 30 times [revenue],” said one investor, adding that would make the company worth $3 trillion.

Several investors said senior Anthropic executives had yet to fix the valuation target for the IPO, even in private conversations, but backers have built their own financial models. A listing at that level could unlock billions of dollars in gains for early investors but would also test public markets growing more nervous about the AI boom.

The bullish projections come despite mounting challenges, including rising competition from Chinese rivals, pressure for AI regulation, and a simmering feud with the US government. Those concerns—particularly the Commerce Department’s temporary ban on Anthropic’s best models—contributed to overall revenue growth slowing in June, according to two investors. However, they said the company rebounded and continued to grow at an extraordinary rate even by Silicon Valley standards.

Sources: Ars Technica
