USS George Washington to replace USS Abraham Lincoln amid reports of dire conditions

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

The United States is sending the USS George Washington aircraft carrier to the Middle East to relieve the USS Abraham Lincoln, a US official told CNN, as reports of poor living conditions and mental health strain aboard the Lincoln have prompted calls for a congressional investigation. The Lincoln, which left San Diego in November for what was scheduled as a Pacific deployment, was rerouted to the Persian Gulf in January to support US operations in the war with Iran and has now spent more than 250 consecutive days at sea, including a record-breaking number of days without a port stop, according to military outlets Navy Times and Stars and Stripes.

Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth defended conditions aboard the Lincoln, calling the reports "completely misrepresented." During a visit to Panama on Thursday, Hegseth said, "We make sure every ship, every crew, every captain has everything we can provide them at every single moment," adding, "Some deployments are longer than others, and I have more respect and gratitude for those sailors than anybody." Hegseth also said the US Navy could maintain its blockade of Iran's ports "indefinitely" by rotating ships in and out.

The Navy Times and Stars and Stripes reported that the deployment has included 40 days of continuous combat operations in the US Fifth Fleet area and 250 consecutive days at sea. Sailors and family members described food rationing, water shortages, plumbing issues, a stretch of more than a week without laundry service, and deteriorating mental health. Stars and Stripes quoted one sailor as saying, "We already had an instance where watchstanders had to stop someone from jumping." Another sailor said, "All we want is a date home." The Lincoln made a brief port call in Oman in early July, allowing it to stock up on fresh provisions, but sailors were only permitted to disembark into a secure compound.

Senator Ruben Gallego (D-AZ) called for an official oversight visit by a bipartisan Senate delegation to "investigate the alarming conditions," saying, "The way he's treating our service members as he carries out this illegal war is not just disgusting; it's dangerous." Senator Richard Blumenthal also called on the Navy to account for conditions aboard the Lincoln, citing "widespread reports of shortages of basic supplies, water contamination, plumbing issues, deteriorating mental health, deck safety concerns" and other problems. The reports followed a direct appeal by family members to Acting Secretary of the Navy Hung Cao and other senior officials in town hall meetings late last week.

The USS George Washington departed Vietnam earlier this week, according to Reuters and The Wall Street Journal. The swap was described by a US official as planned. Meanwhile, President Donald Trump claimed on Wednesday that the US has "total control" over the Strait of Hormuz and speculated he might try to permanently police it, writing online, "Our Naval Blockade is being called, by everyone, 'A WALL OF STEEL,' and there is nothing Iran can do about it." Hossein Taeb, head of Iran's Basij paramilitary unit, disputed that assessment, saying the Strait is "under Iran's control and management."

Traffic through the Strait of Hormuz remains severely limited. According to maritime intelligence firm Kpler, only 13 vessels crossed the waterway on Thursday, compared with an estimated 120 daily before the war. The Abu Dhabi National Oil Company (ADNOC) said two of its ships were attacked in the strait on Thursday night. The UK Maritime Trade Operations (UKMTO) reported that a vessel sustained minor damage from a drone strike, with no injuries or environmental damage. Since the war began, UKMTO has received more than 50 reports of incidents involving damage to vessels in the Strait of Hormuz, the Persian Gulf, or the Gulf of Oman.
