Ukraine war: civilian deaths rise; drone innovations; Black Sea truce offer

International & World desk · anchored on Reuters
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

Russian jet-powered drones struck a home in Sumy region, northern Ukraine, early Friday, killing a 29-year-old woman and her 9-year-old son, officials said. The strike came a day after the United Nations reported that July was the deadliest month for Ukrainian civilians since March 2022, with at least 437 civilians killed and 2,610 wounded. "Every month this year the number of killed and injured civilians has increased. That trend accelerated sharply in July," said Danielle Bell, head of the U.N. human rights monitoring mission in Kyiv. Overall, since Russia's invasion on Feb. 24, 2022, the U.N. has documented at least 16,874 civilian deaths, including 820 children, and 51,273 wounded.

Ukraine has proposed a deal with Russia for both sides to halt attacks on civilian ships in the Black Sea, in an apparent attempt to reopen a crucial grain corridor, Reuters reported. Kyiv made the offer through a third party; Moscow had yet to respond. Since July, Ukrainian attacks have effectively closed the route for commercial Russian shipping, hitting nearly 220 vessels. Russia on Thursday struck Ukraine's port of Izmail in the Odesa region. Separately, Ukrainian drones struck the major Gazprom Neftekhim Salavat refinery in Bashkortostan, deep inside Russia, and the port of Ust-Luga in the Leningrad region, a key oil and gas export terminal. Russia's Defense Ministry said its air defenses overnight intercepted 553 Ukrainian drones over 18 regions, Crimea, and the Azov and Black seas.

In a development highlighting the technological arms race, former Ukrainian Defence Minister Mykhailo Fedorov announced that Ukraine's first jet-powered interceptor drone, designed to counter Russian jet-powered Shahed drones, is undergoing combat testing. The interceptor can reach speeds exceeding 600 km/h. "We are now seeing a trend: the Russians are gradually replacing regular Shahed drones with jet-powered ones," Fedorov said. Meanwhile, a Russian drone attack on a passenger train in Odesa region killed the engineer and assistant engineer; President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said 340 people were on board. Glide bombs also hit a 10-story apartment block in Donetsk, killing one and wounding 16, with Zelenskyy stating Russia launches about 220 glide bombs daily near the front line.

In Crimea, a bomb attack killed Robert Shageyev, a former Ukrainian naval officer who defected to Russia in 2014. The regional governor said a Russian woman was arrested on suspicion of carrying out the assassination for Ukrainian special services. Separately, a Russian plan to kill a Ukrainian American citizen in Warsaw was thwarted, Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk said. The suspect, allegedly recruited by Russian intelligence, was detained on Aug. 7. Tusk called the target "inconvenient to the Putin regime." The U.S. State Department said it was aware of the reports. NATO fighter jets also shot down a drone that entered Latvian airspace, with Latvian authorities saying it entered as a result of Russian electromagnetic warfare.

Russia and Ukraine exchanged bodies of killed soldiers on Thursday. Ukraine's prisoners of war agency said 261 bodies were returned to Ukraine, while Russia received 24. Ukraine condemned Russian parliamentary elections scheduled for next month in occupied areas, calling any vote on its sovereign territory illegal. The head of the U.N. human rights mission warned of a sharp increase in civilian casualties, and Ukraine's air defense capabilities remain strained. A Guardian analysis noted that Patriot missile supplies have run out, with global production of about 840 interceptors per year insufficient to counter Russia's heavy ballistic missile use, leaving Kyiv facing a winter with potentially severely disrupted power infrastructure.
