Israeli settlers besiege West Bank Palestinian homes; US envoy calls it terrorism

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts slightly center-left

About a dozen Palestinian households in the occupied West Bank village of Qusra were told to leave their homes during an Israeli military operation to remove Israeli settlers who had besieged three homes since Sunday, cutting off power and water supplies. One of the targeted houses belongs to a Palestinian-American businessman, drawing an unusually sharp rebuke from U.S. Ambassador to Israel Mike Huckabee, who called the settlers' actions "a horrific act of terror" and "disgusting." The Israeli military said it dismantled two illegal outposts near the homes and detained one Israeli, while the government spokesman described the settlers' behavior as "deplorable" and promised arrests.

Qusra Mayor Abdel Azim Wadi told the BBC that the village had been declared a closed military zone and that Israeli forces were using some of the vacated Palestinian houses as barracks. The Israel Defense Forces (IDF) said the operation aimed "to protect the residents" and that soldiers would not operate inside the Palestinian home that had been besieged. However, residents reported that soldiers entered homes, forced families out, and confiscated property. Qaher Odeh, one of those told to leave, said more than 20 soldiers broke down his door on Thursday morning, shouting "open up." After about 10 hours, some families were allowed to return but found their homes ransacked, with food thrown from refrigerators and cash stolen.

The crisis began on Sunday when settlers blocked the road to the Palestinian families' gates and cut water and power. The first Israeli soldiers who arrived prayed with the settlers, an act for which the military later said they would be disciplined. On Wednesday, an attempt by security forces to dislodge the settlers led to confrontations with a larger group; security cameras showed soldiers using tear gas and stun grenades. Qusay Abu Ridi, a family member trapped inside the Palestinian-American-owned home, said settlers remained in the area hiding from the army. His brother, Loui Ridi, who resides in Ohio, complained to the U.S. embassy.

The United Nations Human Rights Office called on Israeli authorities to "immediately" act against the settlers, saying the criminal actions were clearly aimed at forcing Palestinians to leave their homes and land. EU foreign policy spokesperson Anitta Hipper urged Israel to take "concrete action to prevent settler violence." According to UN figures, 76 Palestinians, including 18 children, have been killed in the West Bank this year by Israeli forces or settlers; three Israelis have been killed in confrontations with Palestinians. Since January 2023, 127 Palestinian communities in the West Bank have experienced full or partial displacement, the UN reports.

All Israeli settlements in the occupied West Bank are illegal under international law, but the current Israeli government has promoted rapid expansion, emboldening settlers and leading to a dramatic rise in settler violence aimed at driving Palestinians off their land. The village of Qusra, located in one of the most violent pockets of the northern West Bank and surrounded by settlements, has faced repeated attacks. Last month, a newly completed mosque was set on fire with Hebrew graffiti sprayed nearby. In July, settlers surrounded a Palestinian home in the neighboring village of Jalud for more than two weeks, after which the owners fled and settlers took over the property, where they remain.

Speaking at a UN meeting, Israel's Ambassador Danny Danon dismissed the idea that settlements are an obstacle to peace, asserting that they will remain and continue to grow because Israel has biblical, historic and legal rights to the land. Israeli government spokesman David Mencer said the government would investigate the Qusra incident, arrest those responsible, and prosecute them. Meanwhile, the IDF said it is in ongoing contact with Qusra's local leadership and working to avoid disruption to the daily lives of residents in the area.
