Ebola outbreak in DR Congo spreads to sixth province, deadliest ever warning

International & World desk

The Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of the Congo has spread to a sixth province, officials said Thursday, a day after the head of the World Health Organization warned the outbreak was on track to surpass the deadliest in history. The current outbreak has killed more than 2,100 people out of more than 4,500 cases, according to government figures released Tuesday. At the same point in the 2014-16 West Africa outbreak, which killed at least 11,000 people, only 755 cases had been recorded, according to WHO data.

Jean Kaseya, director general of the Africa CDC, said a death had been recorded in the previously unaffected Bas-Uele province. The man had traveled from Isiro, in Haut-Uele province, to Buta, the capital of Bas-Uele, where he died. Six of DRC’s 26 provinces have now been affected, mainly along the country’s north-eastern border. More than 3,400 cases have been recorded in Ituri province, where the outbreak was first reported. Uganda has reported 20 cases, all in the capital, Kampala; the last case there was reported on June 21 and no community transmission has been recorded.

The outbreak is caused by the rare Bundibugyo strain of the virus, for which there are no approved vaccines or treatments. Clinical trials of two possible treatments began last month in Ituri, and WHO Director General Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus said Wednesday that two vaccines developed specifically for this strain were now being tested on people for the first time. Researchers said in a paper published this week in the journal Nature Medicine that this strain is genetically different from previous outbreaks in 2007 and 2012, making it likely the outbreak began when the virus jumped from an infected animal to a human. Last week, Kaseya said officials were worried the virus could be mutating and that the Africa CDC was planning further studies with the WHO.

The outbreak was declared on May 15, but the WHO says sequencing now shows it began months earlier in February. Health authorities have said between 60% and 70% of new cases are recorded outside contacts being monitored, and that the disease is spreading at an alarming rate. Dr. Mohamed Yakub Janabi, the WHO regional director for Africa, said: “We are chasing the virus; the virus is ahead of us.” His colleague Abdirahman Mahamud, head of the WHO’s emergency response division, said that if the pandemic response was implemented “at the same time across the five transmission zones, we are expecting a turnaround in three months.” Even with that, Mahamud said the peak of the outbreak would not be reached for six months under a “moderate” scenario and that the outbreak could last nine to 12 months.

The outbreak is unfolding amid strikes by some unpaid health workers, global development aid cuts, threats by rebel groups, anger from long-traumatized communities, and misinformation claiming that Ebola is not real. Health workers at the Nizi treatment centre in Ituri province went on strike Thursday, leading to the temporary closure of the centre, saying they had not been paid for three months. The true scope of the outbreak remains unknown. Ebola is rare but highly contagious and can be contracted from body fluids such as vomit, blood or semen, including from dead bodies, and from contaminated surfaces and materials such as bedding and clothing. The disease it causes is severe and often fatal.
