CMS final rule barring federal funds for youth gender-affirming care takes effect in October, legal challenges expected

Health & Science desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts slightly center-left

The Trump administration’s Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services finalized a rule Tuesday barring the use of federal Medicaid and Children’s Health Insurance Program dollars for gender-affirming medications and surgery for minors, set to take effect Oct. 13. The rule prohibits federal coverage for gender-affirming medication and surgery for anyone under 18 through Medicaid, and for anyone under 19 through CHIP. Mental health care may still be covered with federal funds, and states are not prohibited from using their own Medicaid dollars for this care.

The agency received nearly 35,000 public comments before finalizing the regulation, and it responded to those concerns in the final document, which legal experts said may strengthen the rule against expected legal challenges. “I think they’ve learned more about vulnerabilities that have thwarted their past efforts in this and other areas,” said Travis Jackson, a health care law partner. Carmel Shachar, director of the Health Law and Policy Clinic at Harvard Law School, said the rule “feels less out there than some of the other proposals.” Massachusetts Attorney General Andrea Campbell’s office confirmed she plans to challenge the rule in court.

CMS asserts it has “independent legal authority” to decide which types of care the federal government funds, noting that the ban on federal abortion coverage was dictated by Congress via the Hyde Amendment. The rule also cites a 1976 regulation that barred federal funding for sterilizations of people under 21. Critics argue the agency’s unilateral move without a directive from Congress is unusual. “If they had a bunch of examples, they would cite them,” said Katie Keith, director of Georgetown’s Center for Health Policy and the Law.

The final rule includes a six-month tapering period allowing federal funds to continue covering hormones until April 2027. Clinicians have said there is no safe way to wean transgender people off their medications. The rule relies on a 2024 HHS report questioning the standards of care for transgender youth, while omitting a recent report from the Netherlands that contradicted it. Travis Jackson noted, “There are legitimate questions as to whether or not the record in front of HHS truly supports the actions that HHS is taking in the rule.”

Doctors and advocates said the change would deny potentially lifesaving care to vulnerable youth. KFF estimated about 130,000 young transgender people covered by Medicaid or CHIP live in states where such care is legal. Jennifer Levi of GLAD Law said the rule is part of a broader attack on transgender people and predicted legal challenges. The same week, HHS released a report and documentary called “Wolves in White Coats” and referred dozens of health care groups to the Justice Department for investigation of billing practices related to gender-affirming care.
