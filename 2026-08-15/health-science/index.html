Luigi Mangione pleads guilty to federal stalking charges in killing of UnitedHealthcare CEO Brian Thompson

Health & Science desk

Luigi Mangione pleaded guilty on Friday to federal stalking charges in the killing of UnitedHealthcare CEO Brian Thompson, admitting in Manhattan federal court that he trailed the executive to an investor conference and shot him on a New York City street in December 2024. Mangione, 28, told the court, "On the morning of Dec. 4, 2024, I shot Mr. Thompson in Manhattan, and he died." He said he made a gun with a 3D printer, traveled to New York, and used a ruse—posing as an investor at a multibillion-dollar firm—to contact the company for details on the event. Prosecutors plan to seek a life prison term at his sentencing, set for Dec. 18.

FDA approves Bristol Myers' Zenbexus, first CELMoD for multiple myeloma

Health & Science desk

The U.S. Food and Drug Administration on Thursday granted accelerated approval to an oral treatment for advanced multiple myeloma made by Bristol Myers Squibb, marking the debut of a new class of medicine for the blood cancer and the first drug cleared by U.S. regulators using a more sensitive measure of remission. The drug, iberdomide, will be sold under the brand name Zenbexus and approved in combination with Johnson & Johnson's Darzalex (daratumumab and hyaluronidase-fihj) and dexamethasone for adults with multiple myeloma who have received at least one prior line of therapy, including a proteasome inhibitor and an immunomodulatory agent.

FDA Approvals

FDA approves first mRNA flu vaccine for older adults; Moderna's mFlusiva targets ages 50+

Health & Science desk

The US Food and Drug Administration has approved the nation's first mRNA-based flu vaccine, Moderna's mFlusiva, for adults aged 50 and older, the agency announced last week. The vaccine received standard approval for those 50 to 64 and accelerated approval for adults 65 and older, a two-pronged pathway negotiated after the FDA initially rejected Moderna's application in February. The accelerated approval requires Moderna to conduct a post-marketing study this coming flu season to confirm that the vaccine's antibody advantage translates to clinical benefit compared with enhanced vaccines already recommended for older adults.

Antitrust

Epic Systems under federal, state scrutiny over anticompetitive practices

Health & Science desk

The Federal Trade Commission is examining Epic Systems Corp., the nation’s largest vendor of electronic health records, for potential violations of antitrust law as part of a broad inquiry into the company’s business practices, according to four people who were recently contacted by investigators. The probe is in its early stages and may never lead to charges against Epic, whose dominant market position and control of Americans’ health data has rapidly accelerated in recent years. Attorneys general in states across the country have also joined the inquiry, reaching out to interview subjects or their private lawyers for information, according to three people who spoke with STAT. The people contacted said the intensity of interest and the number of entities involved has been ramping up in recent months.

Health Policy

CMS final rule barring federal funds for youth gender-affirming care takes effect in October, legal challenges expected

Health & Science desk

The Trump administration’s Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services finalized a rule Tuesday barring the use of federal Medicaid and Children’s Health Insurance Program dollars for gender-affirming medications and surgery for minors, set to take effect Oct. 13. The rule prohibits federal coverage for gender-affirming medication and surgery for anyone under 18 through Medicaid, and for anyone under 19 through CHIP. Mental health care may still be covered with federal funds, and states are not prohibited from using their own Medicaid dollars for this care.
