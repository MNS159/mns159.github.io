FDA approves Bristol Myers' Zenbexus, first CELMoD for multiple myeloma

Health & Science desk

The U.S. Food and Drug Administration on Thursday granted accelerated approval to an oral treatment for advanced multiple myeloma made by Bristol Myers Squibb, marking the debut of a new class of medicine for the blood cancer and the first drug cleared by U.S. regulators using a more sensitive measure of remission. The drug, iberdomide, will be sold under the brand name Zenbexus and approved in combination with Johnson & Johnson's Darzalex (daratumumab and hyaluronidase-fihj) and dexamethasone for adults with multiple myeloma who have received at least one prior line of therapy, including a proteasome inhibitor and an immunomodulatory agent.

Zenbexus is part of a Bristol research effort to develop a new and more potent class of blood cancer drugs called CELMoDs (cereblon-modulating protein degraders) that work by redirecting a cell's natural machinery to eliminate cancer-causing proteins. The FDA's decision represents a milestone for targeted protein degradation, validating CELMoDs as a viable modality. The clearance was supported by data from the Phase 3 Excaliber-RRMM trial, in which the Zenbexus combination showed a minimal residual disease (MRD)-negative complete response rate of 41% compared with 21% for a regimen of Darzalex, Takeda's Velcade (bortezomib) and dexamethasone at any time during a median follow-up of 16 months. MRD-negativity is a stringent measure of response, defined in the trial as the ability to detect one myeloma cell among 100,000 normal cells in patients who achieved a complete response or better.

Zenbexus is the first new drug to win an accelerated approval based directly on MRD as a surrogate endpoint rather than traditional longer-term metrics such as progression-free survival (PFS). The FDA had issued a draft guidance in January on the potential use of MRD and complete response to support accelerated approval in myeloma, following unanimous endorsement of the endpoint from an external advisory committee in 2024. The Excaliber-RRMM trial remains ongoing to assess PFS as its other primary endpoint, with results expected this year; Bristol Myers is required to provide confirmatory evidence, such as PFS, to convert the accelerated approval to a full nod.

In an interview ahead of the approval, Cristian Massacesi, Bristol's chief medical officer, said: 'With the Zenbexus approval, Bristol is the first company to bring a novel class of drugs to patients with multiple myeloma, opening venues for many more potential combinations.' In an Aug. 13 statement, Massacesi added: 'As the first approved CELMoD, Zenbexus marks the arrival of a new treatment class and is an important milestone in our efforts to expand what is possible for patients with multiple myeloma. And we believe this is only the beginning.'

The label for Zenbexus includes a boxed warning for embryo-fetal toxicity and serious venous and arterial thromboembolism. With only 4% of patients in Excaliber-RRMM having received a prior anti-CD38 antibody, the FDA spells out that there is 'limited data' for the combination in CD38-pretreated patients. Bristol Myers is running another Phase 3 trial, Excaliber-Maintenance, testing Zenbexus as first-line maintenance therapy following stem cell transplant in newly diagnosed multiple myeloma. The company's second CELMoD agent, mezigdomide, is also under FDA review in second-line-plus multiple myeloma, with a target decision date of May 13, 2027.

Analysts noted that Zenbexus brings an additional growth catalyst as Bristol's older blood cancer blockbuster Revlimid continues to see revenue decline after losing U.S. market exclusivity in 2022. William Blair analysts projected that Zenbexus' U.S. sales could surpass $1 billion in early 2031, citing the drug's monthly price tag of $28,000. Citi analysts said of Bristol in an Aug. 13 note: '[E]ach incremental contribution from the CELMoD franchise helps strengthen the transition from a company dependent on legacy products to one supported by a more diversified set of growth assets.' RBC Capital Markets analyst Trung Huynh wrote that the portfolio of CELMoDs, inherited through Bristol's acquisition of Celgene, is 'underappreciated by investors.' Huynh speculated that the Zenbexus combination might start 'displacing' other second-line, Darzalex-based regimens.
