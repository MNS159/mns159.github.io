OpenAI and Anthropic slash prices as Chinese AI rivals gain ground

Tech / AI desk

Leading US AI labs OpenAI and Anthropic have introduced steep price cuts on their newest models as cost-conscious customers increasingly turn to cheaper alternatives from Chinese competitors. OpenAI said it is cutting prices for GPT-5.6 Luna, its fastest and most affordable model, by 80 percent, while Anthropic launched Claude Opus 5 at half the price of its most capable model, Fable 5. According to Silicon Data’s token price index, the overall cost that customers pay for models from top US labs has fallen nearly a quarter since mid-July.

The price war has been driven by rising bills that are pushing companies to curb usage and seek cheaper models. Both OpenAI and Anthropic are shifting some enterprise customers from flat-rate subscriptions to usage-based billing, where charges depend on computational resources consumed. Tokens—the units of data language models process—are the basis for many customer bills, and the index tracks their cost decline.

Chinese AI developers, including Moonshot and DeepSeek, have made inroads with users from Silicon Valley to Europe by offering cut-price alternatives. Open-source Chinese models that can be freely downloaded and modified by developers have also contributed to downward pressure on prices, even as US labs have historically competed chiefly on performance. The performance gap between leading US and Chinese models has narrowed with a flurry of recent releases from Chinese labs.

Some prominent companies are already switching. DoorDash and Airbnb have said they have begun using Chinese-made models to reduce their AI bills. In response to cost pressures, businesses have imposed caps on AI usage or tested cheaper alternatives, further eroding demand for the premium-priced models from US labs.

The pricing moves come as OpenAI and Anthropic plan initial public offerings at valuations in the trillions of dollars, with investors seeking evidence that massive spending on AI can generate returns. The shift toward cheaper Chinese models has raised concerns in the US tech industry that American developers could lose customers even as they spend heavily to maintain their technological edge.
