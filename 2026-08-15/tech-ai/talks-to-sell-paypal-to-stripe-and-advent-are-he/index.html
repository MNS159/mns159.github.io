PayPal Sale Talks with Stripe and Advent Intensify, WSJ Reports

Tech / AI desk

Negotiations to sell PayPal to Stripe and private equity firm Advent International have intensified and could result in a deal within weeks, according to a new report by the Wall Street Journal citing unnamed sources. The prospect first emerged in July when Stripe and Advent offered to buy PayPal for $60.50 per share, valuing the fintech company at approximately $53 billion. PayPal initially balked at the offer, but talks reportedly continued.

The discussions come as PayPal CEO Enrique Lores pursues a turnaround plan for the company, which has struggled in recent years after surging during the pandemic-era e-commerce boom. Lores, who joined PayPal in March after years at HP, has already taken steps to revive the business, including an executive shuffle and a reorganization into three operating units: checkout solutions and PayPal; consumer financial services and Venmo; and payment services and crypto. In May, Lores told investors that PayPal would recommit to fundamentals, including 'becoming a technology company again.'

PayPal’s turnaround also includes cost-cutting measures expected to reduce its workforce by 20% over the next two to three years. The company was founded in 1998 by a group of Silicon Valley luminaries, including Peter Thiel, Elon Musk, Max Levchin, and Luke Nosek.

PayPal declined to comment on the report. A Stripe spokesperson said the company does not 'comment on rumors or speculation.'
