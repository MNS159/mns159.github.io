Ebola outbreak in DR Congo spreads to sixth province, kills 2,000

International & World desk

The Ebola virus is spreading at a record pace in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, with the outbreak reaching a sixth province and killing 2,000 people out of more than 4,300 recorded cases since it was officially declared on May 15, according to health authorities and the World Health Organization. The WHO has warned that the current outbreak, driven by the rare Bundibugyo strain for which no approved vaccine or treatment exists, could surpass the 2014–2016 West African epidemic that killed 11,000 people. The WHO expects the outbreak to peak in six months.

The epicenter of the outbreak is the northeastern province of Ituri, which has been plagued by insecurity and hosts a large number of internally displaced persons. Many cases have also been reported in North and South Kivu provinces, where authorities have lost control over entire areas in a years-long conflict with M23 rebels and affiliated groups. The rapid spread has been attributed to large-scale population movements and ongoing violence in affected areas. The virus has now spread to a sixth province, and there are growing concerns it could reach the capital, Kinshasa, a city of 19 million people.

In Kinshasa, hospitals are scrambling to prepare for a potential outbreak. The Ministry of Health has designated Cinquantenaire Hospital to admit and fully isolate patients if necessary, with 20 beds currently available that can be expanded to 200 or 250, according to Christian Ngandu, the hospital's coordinator. However, the WHO has said several thousand beds would be needed in a worst-case scenario. Carlos Mupili, president of the Supreme Council of Civil Society, called the current capacity insufficient, saying, 'Twenty beds are insignificant in this city, and a single hospital is not enough.'

Contact tracing has been slow, with only about 80% of contacts tracked, far below the 95% needed to break transmission chains. WHO Director-General Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus expressed concern that many people dying from the virus had not been registered on official contact lists before burial, and noted that many infections originate from funerals. A recent scare occurred when a boat was stopped at the port of Maluku, about 65 kilometers upstream from Kinshasa, after a passenger died with Ebola symptoms; more than 200 passengers were isolated but tested negative.

Scientists are conducting clinical trials of two antiviral treatments, and work on vaccines is underway, with promising results so far. WHO chief scientist Sylvie Briand said no mutations of the virus have been observed in the current outbreak, which has been a small source of hope. Meanwhile, in Ituri province, 27 demobilized former rebel fighters have been deployed as community mobilizers in the Tchomia locality to promote prevention measures, such as handwashing and symptom recognition, and to encourage reporting of suspect cases. The UN peacekeeping mission MONUSCO supported the project, which serves both public health and stabilization goals.
