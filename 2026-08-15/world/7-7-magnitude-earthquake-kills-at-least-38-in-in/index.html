7.7-magnitude earthquake kills at least 38 in Indonesia

International & World desk

A powerful 7.7-magnitude earthquake struck off the coast of Indonesia's Flores region early Saturday, killing at least 38 people, collapsing buildings and triggering landslides, the country's disaster mitigation agency said. The shallow quake, with a depth of 10 to 15 kilometers, hit at about 5 a.m. local time (22:00 GMT Friday) about 68 kilometers north-northwest of the city of Ende in East Nusa Tenggara province, according to Indonesian and U.S. geological agencies. Dozens of aftershocks followed, including one of magnitude 6.1. A tsunami warning was issued but lifted about three hours later when no significant sea-level rise was detected, though tsunami waves of less than one meter were recorded in several areas.

The death toll has been rising as search and rescue operations continue. The head of the National Disaster Management Agency, Suharyanto, said rescuers recovered at least 38 bodies, mostly in the devastated regencies of Sikka, Manggarai and East Manggarai. Sixteen bodies were pulled from a landslide in the village of Reok in Manggarai. At least 13 people were rushed to hospitals, including two with serious injuries. In the port town of Maumere, the main town in Sikka regency, rescuers found 20 dead, six severely injured and two trapped under rubble, said Fathur Rahman, head of the city's rescue agency. Eight of those deaths were caused by a landslide. Video from the port showed large chunks of concrete breaking off the terminal building and the gangway to a ship collapsing, throwing some passengers into the sea.

The earthquake caused widespread damage and panic across Flores island. Emanuel Melkiades Laka Lena, governor of East Nusa Tenggara province, said many victims were killed in their sleep by collapsing rubble. In Nagekeo regency, the area closest to the epicenter, communication signals were affected and rescue teams were blocked by landslides; one team attempted to reach the area by ferry. About 2,000 residents in Nagekeo evacuated, and damage to houses, warehouses and government facilities was reported. Power outages in cities and villages hampered information flow and complicated rescue efforts, said East Nusa Tenggara Police Chief Rudi Darmoko. Landslides in Ende regency cut off the Trans-Flores highway, a roughly 700-kilometer mountain road spanning the island. Preliminary reports indicated 54 houses were severely damaged, along with five healthcare facilities, three places of worship, six government buildings, an educational institution and a warehouse.

Residents described the terror of the quake. Arnold Welianto, who lives in the coastal village of Talibura in Sikka, said he was awoken by "very strong shaking" and immediately went to his child's room. He told the BBC that many people fled into the hills and that the sea had receded, causing panic. Yulian Juita Ekalia, a university lecturer from Ruteng, about 60 miles from the epicenter, told AFP: "I have never felt an earthquake this big. It felt like we were on a trampoline — it was really scary." In Nagekeo, resident Yohanes Babo said people were panicking and running to the hills. At St. Peter Major Seminary in Sikka, students and nuns fled in panic when the roof of an assembly hall collapsed; the rector, Rev. Guidelbertus Tanga, said at least one priest suffered a broken leg after jumping from a second-floor building. Local television showed patients at several hospitals being evacuated, with staff setting up temporary treatment areas outdoors.

Indonesia, a vast archipelago of more than 17,000 islands, is prone to earthquakes and volcanic activity because of its location on the "Ring of Fire," an arc of volcanoes and fault lines in the Pacific Basin. A powerful earthquake caused a tsunami in 1992 that killed about 2,500 people on Flores. In 2018, a magnitude 7.5 quake spawned a large localized tsunami and liquefaction that killed more than 4,400 people in the city of Palu. In 2004, a massive magnitude 9.1 earthquake off Sumatra triggered a tsunami that killed 230,000 people in a dozen countries. The disaster agency advised people to remain calm, stay away from coastal areas and structures showing signs of damage. Three helicopters and a rescue vessel were deployed to assist with logistics and emergency response, given the region's island geography and transportation challenges.
