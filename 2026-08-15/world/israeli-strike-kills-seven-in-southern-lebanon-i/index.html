Israeli strike kills seven in southern Lebanon in deadliest attack since June

International & World desk · anchored on AP

An Israeli air strike on a family home in the village of Ansar in southern Lebanon early Saturday killed seven people, including two women and three children, and wounded three others, according to Lebanese state media. The strike, which destroyed the home, was the deadliest reported Israeli attack in Lebanon since agreements reached in June led to a significant reduction in hostilities between Israel and Hezbollah.

Lebanon’s National News Agency (NNA) said the strike hit a house on the outskirts of Ansar, in the Nabatieh district. Emergency teams from the Islamic Message Scout Association, the Islamic Health Authority and Lebanon’s Civil Defence worked to remove rubble and transfer the dead and wounded to hospitals in Nabatieh. The Israeli military said it struck Hezbollah infrastructure in southern Lebanon in response to actions taken by the group against Israeli soldiers operating in a security zone.

The NNA reported that Israeli attacks intensified early Saturday, with a series of air strikes on the Ali al-Taher Ridge near Nabatieh al-Fawqa, and two other strikes near a teachers’ training centre and the Husseini club in the same area. The valley between Ansar and Zarariyeh was also hit in what the agency described as the first strike to reach that depth since the ceasefire two months ago. Closer to the border, Israeli forces destroyed several homes in Bint Jbeil and opened heavy machinegun fire toward houses along the Kounine-Saf al-Hawa road. An Israeli air attack also hit the al-Mashaa neighbourhood in al-Mansouri shortly before midnight.

Violence has decreased since a US-Iran memorandum of understanding in June and a subsequent US-sponsored framework agreement between Lebanon and Israel, which calls for Hezbollah’s disarmament, the gradual withdrawal of Israeli forces and the deployment of the Lebanese army in southern Lebanon. However, Israeli attacks and demolitions have continued in the south. Lebanese Prime Minister Nawaf Salam said the seven killed were not military targets, stating, "The seven martyrs of the Israeli strike on the village of Ansar are not 'military infrastructure' and the women and children who were killed are not military targets." He added that "Israel must halt this escalation," which he called "extremely dangerous and undermines efforts to consolidate stability in the south."

On Friday, Hezbollah leader Naim Qassem criticized Lebanese authorities over the implementation of the framework, accusing them of pressuring the army while Israeli attacks continued. "How can you accept this ongoing humiliation of the Lebanese army, exposing it to bombardment and Israeli pressures, instead of acting as its protectors?" he said. The strike comes amid broader regional tensions, including attacks on vessels in the Strait of Hormuz and Houthi strikes on a Red Sea port in Yemen.
