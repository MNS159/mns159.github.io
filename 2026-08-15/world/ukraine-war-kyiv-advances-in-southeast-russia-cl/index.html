Ukraine war: Kyiv advances in southeast, Russia claims settlements; Patriot missile shortage looms

International & World desk · anchored on Reuters

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky announced on Saturday that Kyiv had struck a Russian rocket and space centre in Samara using Flamingo missiles, as well as the Savasleyka military airfield, a base for Russian fighter jets. Zelensky described the strikes as 'new significant results' from deep operations, noting the Samara target was approximately 900 kilometres from Ukraine's border. 'Our plan of long-range sanctions against Russia for this war is being implemented, and it is important that Russia's war potential be reduced,' he said. Local authorities in Samara confirmed that industrial infrastructure had sustained 'localised damage' after what they described as a 'massive attack' that was repelled by air defences.

Zelensky signalled further long-range strikes targeting Russian oil refineries and other facilities, as well as operations in the Black Sea and the Sea of Azov. 'The long-range sanctions plan is being implemented, and there are more and more of our mid-range strikes as well,' he said in his nightly address. 'In fact, we have succeeded in blocking off Russia's south, and we are expanding this strategic operation, quite rightly, in response to Russian strikes on our ports and our cities.' The Ukrainian president also signed a sanctions package targeting individuals and companies involved in what he described as the illegal export of Ukrainian grain from Russian-occupied territories.

Meanwhile, a critical shortage of Patriot air defence interceptors is looming for Ukraine. According to an analysis by Jack Watling, a senior research fellow for applied military sciences, supplies of Patriot missiles have run out this year, causing intercept rates to drop from more than 70% to none. Russia produces approximately 750 Iskander ballistic missiles per year, in addition to air-launched Kinzhal missiles and repurposed air-defence missiles. To have a high probability of intercepting a single ballistic missile, two to three interceptors are typically required. Global production of Patriot interceptors stands at about 840 per year, with 240 being older PAC-2 models and 600 being PAC-3s. The US attack on Iran, which prompted Tehran to fire ballistic missiles across the Middle East, has created an acute global shortage, as Iran has launched upwards of 170 ballistic missiles in a single day during recent conflicts. Watling warned that Ukraine is unlikely to receive enough Patriots before winter to protect its critical national infrastructure, and that Kyiv must plan for a winter with severely disrupted power.

Kremlin officials have reiterated Russia's unwillingness to engage in peace talks that freeze the current frontline, emphasising a commitment to a 'long-term, reliable, and sustainable' settlement on Moscow's terms. Russian security council deputy chairperson Dmitry Medvedev said Russia will suspend or end the war 'exclusively' on its own terms. In a separate development, North Korean state media KCNA reported that Moscow and Pyongyang are coordinating on matters concerning national security and stability. North Korean leader Kim Jong Un said the legacy of 'blood ties and combat friendship' between the two countries has become a cornerstone of their relations.

The United Nations Human Rights Monitoring Mission in Ukraine (HRMMU) reported that July 2026 was the deadliest month for civilians since Russia's invasion began, with at least 437 civilians killed and more than 2,600 injured. This represents a 30% increase from June, which was previously the worst month on record. Among the 437 killed were 17 children, making July the month with the highest child casualties since April 2022. 'Every month this year the number of killed and injured civilians has increased. That trend accelerated sharply in July,' said HRMMU Head Danielle Bell.

In a technological development, Ukrainian forces recently destroyed a Russian first-person view drone that was radio-controlled using frequency-hopping and spread-spectrum (FHSS) technology, which enables Russian drones to evade Ukrainian electronic warfare jamming by rapidly switching frequency bands. Drone warfare expert Dmytro Putiata warned that this could expand the kill zone, currently within 15 to 25 km from the frontline. Separately, NATO jets were scrambled on Friday after a drone entered Latvia's airspace during Ukrainian attacks on Russian ports, with Italian Eurofighter jets taking off from Lithuania and Turkish F-16s from Estonia in response, according to NATO spokesperson Allison Hart.
