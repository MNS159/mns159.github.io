International & World Natural Disasters

7.7-magnitude earthquake kills at least 38 in Indonesia

International & World desk

A powerful 7.7-magnitude earthquake struck off the coast of Indonesia's Flores region early Saturday, killing at least 38 people, collapsing buildings and triggering landslides, the country's disaster mitigation agency said. The shallow quake, with a depth of 10 to 15 kilometers, hit at about 5 a.m. local time (22:00 GMT Friday) about 68 kilometers north-northwest of the city of Ende in East Nusa Tenggara province, according to Indonesian and U.S. geological agencies. Dozens of aftershocks followed, including one of magnitude 6.1. A tsunami warning was issued but lifted about three hours later when no significant sea-level rise was detected, though tsunami waves of less than one meter were recorded in several areas.

The death toll has been rising as search and rescue operations continue. The head of the National Disaster Management Agency, Suharyanto, said rescuers recovered at least 38 bodies, mostly in the devastated regencies of Sikka, Manggarai and East Manggarai. Sixteen bodies were pulled from a landslide in the village of Reok in Manggarai. At least 13 people were rushed to hospitals, including two with serious injuries. In the port town of Maumere, the main town in Sikka regency, rescuers found 20 dead, six severely injured and two trapped under rubble, said Fathur Rahman, head of the city's rescue agency. Eight of those deaths were caused by a landslide. Video from the port showed large chunks of concrete breaking off the terminal building and the gangway to a ship collapsing, throwing some passengers into the sea.

Ukraine war: Kyiv advances in southeast, Russia claims settlements; Patriot missile shortage looms

International & World desk · anchored on Reuters

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky announced on Saturday that Kyiv had struck a Russian rocket and space centre in Samara using Flamingo missiles, as well as the Savasleyka military airfield, a base for Russian fighter jets. Health & Science FDA Approvals

FDA approves first mRNA flu vaccine for older adults; Moderna's mFlusiva targets ages 50+

The US Food and Drug Administration has approved the nation's first mRNA-based flu vaccine, Moderna's mFlusiva, for adults aged 50 and older, the agency announced last week. The vaccine received standard approval for those 50 to 64 and accelerated approval for adults 65 and older, a two-pronged pathway negotiated after the FDA initially rejected Moderna's application in February. The accelerated approval requires Moderna to conduct a post-marketing study this coming flu season to confirm that the vaccine's antibody advantage translates to clinical benefit compared with enhanced vaccines already recommended for older adults.
