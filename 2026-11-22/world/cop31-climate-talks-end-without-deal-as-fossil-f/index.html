COP31 climate talks collapse as EU, others reject deal lacking fossil fuel mention

International & World desk

United Nations climate talks in Antalya, Turkey, collapsed on Sunday after nearly 200 countries failed to reach an agreement, with the European Union and several other nations rejecting a final draft text that omitted any explicit reference to fossil fuels. The breakdown marked the first time the annual UN climate summit has ended without a deal since Copenhagen in 2009.

The proposed agreement, known as the Antalya Pact, would have acknowledged for the first time that global warming is on course to exceed the 1.5 degrees Celsius (2.7 degrees Fahrenheit) threshold set by the 2015 Paris Agreement. However, key oil- and gas-producing countries opposed mentions of 'fossil fuels' in the text, and the revised draft also dropped a $120 billion target for funds to help poorer countries adapt to climate change. The final version referred to earlier decisions, including the 2023 Dubai agreement, but did not explicitly mention fossil fuels and retained provisions on carbon capture and storage, a technology promoted by some oil-producing nations but criticized by environmental groups.

The European Union formally rejected the deal, with EU climate chief Wopke Hoekstra telling reporters after walking out of the talks, 'That is not acceptable.' Ireland's Climate Minister Darragh O'Brien, speaking on behalf of the EU, said the bloc would not endorse a decision 'that does not even mention fossil fuels, which is the key driver of climate change, and we will not accept negotiating backward in time, and we will not accept a COP that greenwashes fossil fuel.' After the EU walked out, other countries including Panama, the Marshall Islands, Norway, and Vanuatu also objected. The Marshall Islands delegation said, 'We have been let down on all sides. We were presented with the worst possible response to overshoot, a response to enhance oil and gas.'

UN Secretary-General Antonio Guterres expressed 'a very profound frustration,' saying, 'I am deeply disappointed that COP31 has ended without consensus on an overarching outcome.' He criticized powerful fossil fuel interests for obstructing climate action, accusing them of distorting scientific evidence, delaying policies, and prioritizing profits. The Least Developed Countries group, representing 44 nations most vulnerable to climate change, said, 'We struggled to find meaningful outcomes that respond to the priorities of the most vulnerable countries like us. We cannot afford to see backsliding.'

COP31 President Murat Kurum, Turkiye's environment minister, said, 'We did everything we could. You should not hurt people in the world who really expect something out of this by just being hung up on one word or phrase.' He added that no new text would be proposed. Chris Bowen, Australia's climate minister and COP31 president of negotiations, called the collapse 'very disappointing,' noting that 'an obvious issue is the transition away from fossil fuels, where some parties insisted that must be mentioned. Other parties insisted that if it was mentioned, they would veto.' Russia's delegate argued that shrinking oil and gas supplies would lead to 'dirtier types of fuel and energy poverty,' while Saudi Arabia said it believed 'climate change requires all solutions on the table.' The United Kingdom's lead climate negotiator, Kate Hughes, said the draft 'moves us backwards on the science,' and Energy Secretary Miatta Fahnbulleh said the summit 'sought to confront hard truths' but failed to build consensus.
