Bahrain says Iran strikes industrial facility as tanker struck in strait

International & World desk · anchored on Reuters

Bahrain’s air defence systems intercepted several Iranian aerial attacks on Saturday, but a civilian industrial facility on Sitra Island was struck, causing a fire and damage, the interior ministry said. No casualties were reported initially, though the ministry later said a Bahraini citizen with disabilities and a Pakistani resident were injured, the latter critically. The attacks ended a weeks-long lull in the conflict that began with US-Israeli strikes on Iran in late February.

Two tankers were also hit in the Strait of Hormuz, according to the UK Maritime Trade Operations (UKMTO). One was struck by an unknown projectile and another sustained damage in its engine room. Iran claimed that a vessel caught fire after hitting a mine in a restricted zone and separately said its Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) had shot down a US drone over Qeshm Island, the 20th such drone it had downed. The US Central Command (CENTCOM) rejected Iranian claims that an F-16 fighter jet was attacked, stating all American military aircraft in the region were safe.

The IRGC claimed responsibility for attacking two US bases in Bahrain, Sheikh Isa and Juffair, in response to what it called US illegal actions. Iran also unveiled a new missile launcher capable of firing two liquid-propellant missiles simultaneously. Meanwhile, Saudi Arabia’s air defences intercepted a Houthi drone launched toward Riyadh, and the Saudi Foreign Ministry issued a strong condemnation of the Iranian attack on Bahrain, affirming full solidarity. The UAE and Jordan also condemned the strike as a violation of Bahrain’s sovereignty and a dangerous escalation.

Iran is reportedly preparing to send 14 oil tankers through the US naval blockade, raising the prospect of a direct confrontation with American warships and threatening energy security across the Arabian Gulf. Shipping through the Strait of Hormuz has already fallen 65% below pre-war levels. The US, which has been escorting ships through the strait, faces the central question of whether Washington will forcibly stop the tankers. The American Embassy in Bahrain warned citizens of increased threats from Iranian-backed Houthis and Iranian attacks targeting US assets in the region.

Australia’s embassy in Saudi Arabia advised its citizens not to travel to the kingdom, citing volatile security, drone and missile attacks on airports and oil facilities, and significant air travel disruptions. In Lebanon, the army arrested three people for smuggling 264 kilograms of explosives from Syria. Israel’s defence circles are examining the scenario of a preemptive Iranian strike, believing Tehran could attack first if it concludes a joint US-Israeli assault is imminent. The IRGC warned that Iranian airspace remains a “red line” and that any violation would be met with a “harsh, decisive and powerful response.”
