Stock market correction deepens; analysts debate bounce vs. further losses

Financial & Markets desk

The stock market sell-off has deepened, with the Nasdaq Composite firmly in correction territory and the Dow Jones Industrial Average and S&P 500 approaching similar thresholds, according to multiple financial outlets. As of the Nov. 19 market close, the Nasdaq was down 13.5% from its all-time high of 27,722.75 set on Oct. 6, while the Dow had fallen 9.7% from its high and the S&P 500 was down 8.2%. The speed of the decline has been notable: both the Nasdaq and S&P 500 were at all-time highs in early October, and the Nasdaq has dropped 10.1% in just three weeks. A further fall to 22,178.20 on the Nasdaq would mark a 20% decline from its peak, meeting the common definition of a bear market. Some analysts argue that given the rapid descent from a high just six weeks ago, such a drop could also be classified as a crash.

The sell-off has been particularly severe in certain sectors and stocks. Nvidia, which was the world's most valuable company for most of this year, has lost roughly a third of its value in three weeks, accelerated by the sudden resignation of its founder and CEO of over 33 years, Jensen Huang. The Motley Fool's Daniel Foelber noted that while Nvidia's decline may have gone too far, the business is 'definitely weaker without Huang,' and he shifted his stance on the stock from buy to hold pending clarity on national security risks, restructuring, and leadership. Other pockets of the market have seen crash-like declines: Taiwan was hit hardest among country ETFs, down 23.2% in dollars last week, followed by South Korea down 13.7%, according to Yardeni QuickTakes. Gold fell to $3,555 per ounce, its lowest since September 2025, and copper dropped 9.9% to $5.48 a pound. Even Bitcoin has undergone rapid declines.

Despite the sell-off, analysts are divided on the outlook. Yardeni QuickTakes described the sell-off as a buying opportunity, noting that global profits are still booming and forward earnings remain at record highs both in the U.S. and overseas. The U.S. MSCI's forward price-to-earnings ratio has fallen to 17.7, while the emerging markets MSCI trades at just 7.9, its lowest since the Great Financial Crisis. Yardeni wrote, 'We said valuations were at or near a bottom. They went lower. We still think the panic selling will abate soon.' MarketWatch highlighted a timing model from Norm Fosback's 'Seasonality Timing System,' which has a strong long-term track record and suggests the market has an upside bias for the next two weeks. However, the Motley Fool cautioned that volatility could be swifter and more severe due to widespread access to information, high-frequency trading, and zero-fee brokerages, and that index concentration—Nvidia alone made up around 8% of the S&P 500 at its peak—amplifies moves.

Bonds rallied worldwide as investors fled stocks, sending yields lower. The U.S. 10-year Treasury yield fell 24 basis points to 4.92% from 5.16% a week earlier, according to Yardeni. The dollar has been a safe haven, with the DXY rising to 103.8 from an intraday low of 101.1 on Nov. 4. In Asia, South Korea's stock market has crashed even as its forward earnings are up 325.1% year-to-date, cutting its forward P/E to a record low of 3.2. Yardeni noted that investors are betting analysts' estimates are too high due to concerns that a slowdown in the AI buildout will depress memory chip prices, but South Korean exports soared 93.9% year-over-year in October to a record, led by shipments to China and the U.S. In Japan, headline CPI rose to 2.4% year-over-year in October, above the Bank of Japan's 2.0% target, raising the odds of a rate hike.

For investors, the Motley Fool recommended buying high-quality stocks and ETFs during periods of intense volatility, but cautioned against assuming every beaten-down stock is cheap. Foelber pointed to ASML, a leader in semiconductor equipment, as a clearer buying opportunity than Nvidia, and suggested beaten-down growth stock ETFs like the Vanguard Growth ETF and Vanguard Mega Cap Growth ETF for broad exposure. He advised investors to 'only buy stocks you are OK with holding even if volatility continues to intensify.' The stakes are high as the crucial holiday shopping period approaches, with some stocks already in bear market territory.
