Hakeem Jeffries' past Jerusalem comments resurface as he prepares to become House speaker

Politics desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum
✓ Facts
  • House Democrats nominated Hakeem Jeffries for speaker on a Wednesday vote, positioning him to become the first African American speaker of the House when the new Congress convenes in January.
  • Jeffries won the secret-ballot vote to remain House Democratic Caucus leader by a 251-11 margin.
  • Jeffries grew up in Brooklyn, worked as a corporate litigator at Paul, Weiss and later in-house at CBS before entering politics; his mother taught Sunday school at Cornerstone Baptist Church in Bedford-Stuyvesant.
  • In a 2020 interview with AIPAC that recently resurfaced, Jeffries said New Yorkers consider Jerusalem the city's '6th Borough' and described Israel as living in a 'tough neighborhood.'
  • In a July letter to colleagues, Jeffries wrote that 'a meaningful change in direction is needed' on U.S.-Israel security arrangements, while also condemning Rep. Thomas Massie's (R-Ky.) effort to cut $3.3 billion in U.S. military aid to Israel.
  • At least eight incoming members with ties to the Democratic Socialists of America, plus Reps.-elect Brad Lander and Scott Wiener, have called for ending or embargoing U.S. aid to Israel.
  • Jeffries has not used the term 'genocide' to describe the Israel-Hamas war and has rejected a full cutoff of U.S. aid to Israel.
○ Analysis

Hakeem Jeffries is on track to become the first Black speaker of the House, capping a career that moved from corporate law to Brooklyn politics. According to New Yorker writer Jason Zengerle, Jeffries is a cautious, consensus-seeking leader who has generally avoided ideological labels — except on Israel, where he has long been identified as a strong supporter. That identification is now colliding with a broader Democratic shift: as more members, including a growing DSA-aligned bloc and some established lawmakers, push to cut or condition aid to Israel, old comments like Jeffries' 2020 'sixth borough' remark are resurfacing and drawing scrutiny. Jeffries himself has shifted rhetorically since the Gaza war began in 2023, calling for changes to U.S.-Israel security arrangements, but he has stopped short of embracing the harsher positions some in his party now hold.

◐ Debate

Democratic

  • Jeffries (D) wrote to colleagues that 'a meaningful change in direction is needed' in U.S.-Israel security arrangements, proposing aid be restructured like agreements with other Western allies, while reaffirming that U.S. support for Israel's existence 'must remain ironclad.'
  • DSA-aligned incoming Democrats, along with Reps.-elect Brad Lander and Scott Wiener, have called for ending or fully embargoing U.S. military and economic aid to Israel.

Republican

  • Rep. Thomas Massie (R-Ky.) pushed an amendment to cut $3.3 billion in U.S. military aid to Israel from a State Department appropriations bill, a move Jeffries publicly opposed.
Steelman

The resurfaced comments highlight that Jeffries occupies a shrinking middle ground on Israel — more supportive than his party's rising progressive wing but more critical than his earlier career suggested — making the issue a genuine test of his ability to hold together an increasingly divided caucus.

Implications

As speaker, Jeffries will likely face recurring pressure from progressive Democrats to harden his stance on Israel aid, even as he seeks to preserve caucus unity and mainstream support ahead of future votes on security assistance and messaging legislation.
