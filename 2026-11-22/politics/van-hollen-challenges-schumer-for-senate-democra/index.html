Van Hollen challenges Schumer for Senate Democratic leader; Trump says it will be 'hard' to work with Schumer

Politics desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum
✓ Facts
  • Sen. Chuck Schumer (D-N.Y.) has led Senate Democrats since 2017, as both minority and majority leader.
  • Sen. Chris Van Hollen (D-Md.), 67, launched a challenge to Schumer for Senate Democratic leader after Democrats' midterm wins in November.
  • Van Hollen said on NBC's 'Meet the Press' his support is 'north of 15' senators, short of the 28 votes needed to win; Fox News reported Schumer has 15 senators in his camp.
  • Van Hollen's publicly identified backers include Sen. Bernie Sanders (I-Vt.), Sen. Jeff Merkley (D-Ore.), Sen. Elissa Slotkin (D-Mich.), and Sens.-elect Abdul El-Sayed and Peggy Flanagan.
  • Senate Democrats and senators-elect will hold a leadership vote in December to choose the next majority leader.
  • President Trump wrote on Truth Social Sunday it would be 'very hard' to work with Schumer as majority leader, calling him 'one of the worst' leaders and predicting he will 'fail again.'
  • Rahm Emanuel said Schumer should commit to stepping down as leader after the next Congress, according to ABC News.
  • Van Hollen confirmed on 'Meet the Press' that he will not run for president in 2028, regardless of the leadership race's outcome.
○ Analysis

This is an internal Democratic power struggle triggered by the party's strong midterm showing, which puts Senate Democrats on track to hold the majority in January and makes the question of who leads them — Schumer or a challenger — consequential for control of the chamber's agenda. The leadership vote is decided within the Democratic caucus, with 28 votes needed among senators and senators-elect to win. Van Hollen represents the more progressive wing of the party and has drawn support from figures like Bernie Sanders, while Schumer's camp argues his decade of leadership helped deliver the recent wins now fueling Democratic confidence.

◐ Debate

Democratic

  • Sen. Chris Van Hollen argues the caucus wants change, that Democrats are 'on probation' with voters, and that 'you don't earn people's trust by going back to business as usual.'
  • Sen. Chuck Schumer has said he welcomes 'discussion about the future of our caucus' and maintains he has 'the votes to continue serving as Democratic leader.'
  • Rahm Emanuel says Schumer should commit to stepping down as leader after the next Congress.

Republican

  • President Trump predicts it will be 'very hard' to work with Schumer as majority leader, calling him 'one of the worst' leaders in history and saying he will 'fail again.'
Steelman

Both sides claim they have sufficient support heading into December: Van Hollen says his backing is 'north of 15' and growing, while Schumer maintains he already has 'the votes to continue serving as Democratic leader,' leaving the actual outcome genuinely contested until the caucus votes.

Implications

The December caucus vote will determine who leads Senate Democrats into the new Congress, settling the contest between Schumer and Van Hollen for control of the party's agenda in the chamber.
