Ravens offered Colts a fourth-round pick for Daniel Jones before Indianapolis released him

Sports desk

The Baltimore Ravens offered the Indianapolis Colts a fourth-round draft pick and a conditional fifth-round selection for quarterback Daniel Jones before the NFL trade deadline on Nov. 10, according to multiple reports. The proposed deal would have sent a 2027 fourth-round pick to Indianapolis, along with a fifth-rounder that would have been returned to the Colts if Baltimore advanced to the playoffs. The Minnesota Vikings also expressed interest in Jones, but the Colts held onto him, then released him on Friday after a 26-3 loss to the Houston Texans.

Jones, 29, struggled in 11 games for the Colts in 2026, throwing for 1,974 yards, seven touchdowns and 13 interceptions. In the loss to Houston, he completed 12 of 27 passes for 144 yards and an interception. Indianapolis subsequently turned to Anthony Richardson as its starting quarterback. The Colts had told Jones before the deadline that he would remain with the team, and Jones was surprised when he was informed of his release on Friday, a source told OutKick.

The Colts privately said after the trade deadline that they declined the Ravens' offer because they did not believe they were receiving good value in return, according to a league source cited by Fox News. However, the offer was not necessarily final, as there were still days remaining before the deadline. The Ravens eventually traded for Arizona Cardinals quarterback Jacoby Brissett, giving up a 2027 fourth-round pick and a 2028 seventh-rounder in return.

The decision to release Jones without receiving any draft compensation has drawn criticism. The Colts had already traded offensive guard Quenton Nelson, defensive tackle DeForest Buckner, and wide receivers Keenan Allen and Josh Downs at the deadline, while holding onto Jones and star running back Jonathan Taylor despite reported offers. The Colts are also responsible for the remainder of Jones' guaranteed salary if he goes unclaimed on waivers, which is expected.

Jones is expected to clear waivers on Monday and is unlikely to sign immediately with another team, according to ESPN and NFL Network. Instead, he and his representatives plan to study the landscape for the right opportunity, particularly if a playoff contender suffers an injury at quarterback. A team signing Jones after he clears waivers could potentially add him for the NFL minimum, as the Colts remain liable for his guaranteed salary.
