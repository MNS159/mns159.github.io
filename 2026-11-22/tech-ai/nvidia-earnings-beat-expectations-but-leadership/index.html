Nvidia earnings beat expectations but leadership crisis deepens

Tech / AI desk

Nvidia reported soaring revenue for the third quarter of fiscal 2027, beating expectations and raising its outlook, even as a public campaign led by Elon Musk and other tech executives pushes for the return of former CEO Jensen Huang. The company’s interim CEO, Colette Kress, told investors on the earnings call, “Hey, we’ve got this,” while Huang himself has said he is prepared to come back, according to a petition circulated by Musk and others. The conflicting signals have created what TechCrunch’s Kirsten Korosec described as “the tale of two Nvidias this week.”

Nvidia’s revenue rose 103% year over year to $115.8 billion in the quarter ended Oct. 25, driven by a 110% jump in data center revenue. The company forecast $130 billion in revenue for the current quarter, an improvement of 91% over the year-ago period, and raised its fiscal 2028 revenue guidance to 75% growth, up from a prior expectation of 70%. The Vera Rubin chip platform generated nearly $20 billion in revenue last quarter, which Nvidia called “the fastest product ramp in Nvidia’s history.” Management said demand for its chips continues to outpace supply.

Despite the strong financial results, Nvidia faces a deepening leadership crisis. Huang resigned as CEO, and the company is searching for a permanent replacement. Kress, serving as interim CEO, has publicly stated that Huang’s return is not happening, but TechCrunch noted that Nvidia’s stance shifted rapidly: “A week and a half before that [the company said], ‘We stand behind Jensen. We have every confidence in his leadership.’” The board appears determined to avoid being forced into a decision by outside pressure, particularly from Musk, and is trying to “present control,” according to Korosec. An investigation into how much and who knew about chips allegedly being sold secretly to China remains ongoing, further complicating any potential return.

On the China front, Nvidia announced it will stop selling its H200 chips in the country, coinciding with the Trump administration tightening export restrictions and closing a loophole that allowed Chinese companies to remotely access chips in data centers set up in Malaysia and elsewhere. Critics estimate that 20% of Nvidia’s business ultimately comes from China through indirect sales, meaning the crackdown could significantly affect the company’s bottom line, though Kress said the company’s “long pipeline” remains strong.

Looking ahead, Nvidia’s improved guidance signals sustained demand for advanced chips, which also benefits ASML Holding, the near-monopoly maker of extreme ultraviolet lithography machines used to manufacture Nvidia’s 3-nanometer and 4-nanometer processors. However, the leadership drama is far from resolved. As TechCrunch’s Anthony Ha put it, “I don’t know what it would take for that to happen. I don’t necessarily think it’s that likely, but I also don’t think that just because they’re saying it’s not happening now doesn’t mean it will never happen.” The board’s steadfastness in quickly shooting down the petitions, Tim De Chant added, “suggests there’s maybe a little bit more going on behind the scenes than we know on the outside.”
