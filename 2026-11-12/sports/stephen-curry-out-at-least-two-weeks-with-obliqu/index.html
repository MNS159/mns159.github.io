Stephen Curry out at least two weeks with oblique abdominal tear

Sports desk

Golden State Warriors star Stephen Curry has been diagnosed with an oblique abdominal tear and will be reevaluated in two weeks, the team announced Thursday. The injury occurred during the first quarter of Wednesday night's loss to the Houston Rockets, when Curry became tangled with Rockets forward Alperen Sengun. Video showed Sengun grabbing and twisting Curry's arm, after which Curry grabbed his side. He hit his lone 3-pointer of the game moments later, again grabbing at his side, and exited at the next dead ball, not returning. Warriors coach Steve Kerr said after the game that concern about the injury was "high" and that Curry told him he had "never done it before."

An MRI on Thursday confirmed the oblique abdominal tear. While two weeks is the best-case scenario for reevaluation, the timeline does not include any ramp-up process Curry would need before returning to the floor, meaning his absence could extend beyond that period. The severity of the tear is unclear; oblique strains—essentially minor tears—have sidelined NBA players for varying lengths in recent seasons, from Jose Alvarado's 10 days to Tari Eason's 37 days. More severe tears, such as those suffered by Orlando Magic's Paolo Banchero and Franz Wagner two years ago, resulted in absences of 10 weeks and more than a month, respectively.

Curry had been off to a scorching start to the season, averaging 30.1 points per game on 50.3/45.7/98.7 shooting splits as the Warriors won eight straight games for the first time since their 2022 championship season. The team entered Thursday at 8-4 with a top-three offense, but that margin is thin: Golden State is already without Jimmy Butler, Kristaps Porziņģis, Brandon Williams, and Gary Payton II, and wing Will Richard is playing through a ligament tear in his thumb. The Warriors now face their most difficult stretch of the season, beginning with a visit to the San Antonio Spurs on Friday followed by a back-to-back against the Dallas Mavericks and New Orleans Pelicans.

Curry is the latest NBA star to face an extended injury absence early in the 2026-27 season, joining a list that includes Jalen Williams, Chet Holmgren, Anthony Edwards, Tyrese Haliburton, Kawhi Leonard, Paolo Banchero, Jalen Duren, and Kon Knueppel. Any extended absence for Curry would be a brutal blow for the Warriors, who have little margin for additional injuries on an aging roster and are not equipped to cover for his absence.
