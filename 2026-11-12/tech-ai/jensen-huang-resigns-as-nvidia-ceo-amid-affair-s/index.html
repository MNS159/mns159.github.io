Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang resigns amid affair scandal and FBI probe into alleged Chinese spy ties

Tech / AI desk

Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang has resigned, the company announced Thursday, following revelations that he had two extramarital affairs — one with a woman U.S. authorities believe was a Chinese spy and another with a senior Nvidia executive. The 63-year-old co-founder, who led the chipmaker to become the linchpin of the global AI boom, will be replaced by chief financial officer Colette Kress on an interim basis. Nvidia shares fell more than 10 percent in pre-market trading after the announcement, a decline that would wipe off more than $400 billion in market capitalization.

The scandal erupted after reports that Huang had a relationship with Alice Huang (no relation), an executive at Megaspeed, a Southeast Asian buyer of Nvidia chips that U.S. authorities suspect of diverting advanced semiconductors to China in violation of export controls. The Financial Times reported that Alice Huang has surfaced in Beijing and is barred from Singapore for giving police inaccurate information. A second affair, with Nvidia vice president of healthcare Kimberly Powell, was reported Wednesday by The New York Times; CNBC said rumors of that relationship had circulated for years. The board met to consider Huang's ouster, and his resignation followed swiftly.

Megaspeed, Nvidia's largest Southeast Asian customer, has drawn intense scrutiny. The Straits Times visited its Singapore office and found no signs of activity; another tenant said it was always dark. The company's sole director, Tan Yong Pong, appears to have vanished. Short seller Culper Research alleged that over 20 percent of Nvidia's fiscal 2026 compute revenue was driven by China through illegal GPU diversion and Southeast Asian intermediaries. Nvidia reportedly intervened in a 2025 Commerce Department investigation of Megaspeed, halting the inquiry. Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick has become a fall guy, with Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent confronting him and more than half a dozen Republicans demanding answers.

The scandal extends beyond Megaspeed. Super Micro, a partner run by Huang's friend Charles Liang, is entangled in a separate case: three executives allegedly defrauded the U.S. by using a front company to buy $2.5 billion of Nvidia chips for Alibaba. Another firm, New Matrix, is an approved Nvidia buyer with ties to blacklisted Chinese military contractors. Huang had insisted Nvidia saw no evidence of chip diversion, but analysts say the board's decision to cut him loose suggests more allegations may emerge. Huang had no succession plan and was the only CEO Nvidia had ever known, with 60 direct reports.

The political stakes are high. President Donald Trump was informed about Huang's alleged spy girlfriend in 2025 and apparently took no action; Nvidia has reportedly donated $25 million to Trump's planned ballroom. Senator Elizabeth Warren, set to chair the Senate Banking Committee in January, has signaled interest in the matter. Democrats will control both chambers of Congress. If Huang knew chips were being diverted and facilitated sales, he could face criminal charges including bribery, espionage, and smuggling. Elon Musk posted on X that hiring Huang would be an honor, but the broader question remains how high the scheme reached and what else was an open secret at Nvidia.
