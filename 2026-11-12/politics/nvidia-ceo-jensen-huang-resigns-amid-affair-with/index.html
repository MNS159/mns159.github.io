Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang resigns amid affair with alleged Chinese spy; agrees to brief Congress

Politics desk
✓ Facts
  • Nvidia co-founder and CEO Jensen Huang resigned on Thursday following disclosures of two extramarital affairs.
  • The first affair, reported by the New York Times on Oct. 29, involved businesswoman Alice Huang, who is linked to Singapore-based firm Megaspeed and whom U.S. officials allege is a Chinese intelligence asset.
  • A second affair, reported Wednesday, involved Kimberly Powell, Nvidia's vice president of healthcare and an 18-year company employee.
  • Three House Republicans, including Foreign Affairs Committee Chair Brian Mast and Permanent Select Committee on China Chair John Moolenaar, asked Huang in a Nov. 4 letter to testify before their committees by Nov. 18; as of Wednesday the lawmakers said they had not yet heard back from Huang and were considering using subpoena power if he did not agree to appear.
  • Sen. Elizabeth Warren (D-Mass.) said Huang's resignation 'does not absolve' him, other executives, or Nvidia of responsibility.
  • Nvidia chief financial officer Colette Kress has been named interim CEO.
  • Nvidia shares fell more than 13% at the New York market open following the resignation announcement, erasing more than $500 billion in market value; the company remains valued at nearly $4 trillion.
  • President Trump called Huang 'honourable' and said he had done 'a fantastic job,' and floated Apple's Tim Cook as a possible successor.
○ Analysis

Jensen Huang built Nvidia into the dominant supplier of the computer chips powering the global artificial-intelligence boom, making him a central figure in U.S.-China technology competition and a frequent presence alongside heads of state, including both Trump and Xi Jinping. His resignation came after back-to-back reports of extramarital affairs, one of which involved a woman U.S. officials allege is tied to Chinese intelligence, raising national-security questions that go beyond personal conduct and into corporate governance at a company considered strategically critical to U.S. tech leadership. Lawmakers' push for Huang to testify, and their readiness to consider subpoenas, along with the market's sharp reaction, underscore that both Congress and investors are treating the matter as more than a personnel issue, even as Nvidia's board faces pressure over how it handles the leadership transition.

Steelman

Across political and market commentary, the shared concern is less about Huang's personal conduct itself and more about what it reveals regarding security vetting and governance at a company whose chips are considered a linchpin of U.S. technological and national-security interests.

Implications

Attention now turns to whether Huang agrees to testify before the House committees that requested his appearance by Nov. 18, or whether lawmakers move to use their subpoena power. Nvidia's board also faces pressure to name a permanent successor—whether an internal figure like Colette Kress or an outside name such as Tim Cook—capable of maintaining the company's technological edge and its relationships across Washington, Beijing, and the broader AI supply chain.
