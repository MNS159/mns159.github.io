Senator opens investigation into whether RFK Jr. lied to Congress about Samoa trip

Politics desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts left
✓ Facts
  • Sens. Ron Wyden (D-OR), incoming chair of the Senate Finance Committee, and Angela Alsobrooks (D-MD) sent a letter dated Wednesday to Children's Health Defense (CHD) launching an investigation into whether HHS Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. lied to Congress.
  • The letter requests documents on Kennedy's 2019 Samoa trip, his 2024 presidential campaign, his Senate confirmation process, his September 2025 speech to CHD's convention, and all CHD communications with federal officials since Jan. 20, 2025.
  • CHD has been given a compliance deadline of Dec. 4.
  • During January 2025 confirmation hearings, Kennedy testified that his 2019 Samoa trip had 'nothing to do with vaccines.'
  • A January 2019 letter obtained by The Guardian and the Associated Press—via a Reporters Committee for Freedom of the Press open-records lawsuit—shows Kennedy asked the Samoan prime minister to study MMR vaccine safety, using the words 'vaccine' or 'vaccination' eight times.
  • Samoa's 2019 measles outbreak, which began months after Kennedy's trip, caused 5,700 cases and 83 deaths, mostly children under five.
  • HHS has called allegations that Kennedy lied to Congress 'false' and 'outright defamatory,' saying the trip was 'unrelated to vaccines.'
  • Knowingly giving false testimony to Congress is a crime, though prosecutions are rare.
○ Analysis

The investigation targets the credibility of testimony Kennedy gave during his 2025 confirmation hearings, when he denied any vaccine-related purpose for a 2019 trip to Samoa that preceded one of the deadliest modern measles outbreaks. Documents obtained through litigation by press organizations appear to contradict that testimony, showing Kennedy directly raised MMR vaccine safety with Samoan officials before the trip. The probe, launched by the incoming Senate Finance Committee chair and a Health, Education, Labor and Pensions Committee member, uses a formal but non-subpoena letter to CHD, the anti-vaccine group Kennedy led until December 2024 and whose former staff now hold influential roles at HHS under him. This action follows the Democratic Party regaining Senate control in the midterms, giving Wyden and allies subpoena power if CHD does not cooperate by the Dec. 4 deadline. The episode sits within a broader pattern of Democratic lawmakers signaling intent to scrutinize or seek accountability from Kennedy, while legal experts note that any Justice Department prosecution for false congressional testimony is unlikely under the current administration, making congressional hearings, subpoenas, or impeachment proceedings the more probable near-term mechanisms for accountability.

Steelman

The dispute hinges on a factual contradiction—Kennedy's sworn claim that his Samoa trip had 'nothing to do with vaccines' versus a documented 2019 letter in which he raised MMR vaccine safety with Samoan officials—leaving the core question of intent and legal exposure unresolved pending CHD's document production and HHS's fuller response.

Implications

If CHD misses the Dec. 4 deadline or provides incomplete records, Wyden has signaled he could escalate to formal subpoenas, intensifying the clash between the Democratic-controlled Senate and the Trump administration over Kennedy's tenure. The investigation could also feed into broader impeachment efforts already floated by some Democrats, even as experts caution that criminal prosecution for false testimony remains unlikely, making congressional oversight the primary avenue for accountability in the near term.
