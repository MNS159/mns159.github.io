Measles cases and costs surge as U.S. nears loss of elimination status

Health & Science desk

The United States is on the verge of losing its measles elimination status this week, as a committee of the Pan American Health Organization (PAHO), a regional World Health Organization body, meets for four days starting today to decide the matter. Cases have soared in recent years, with more than 5,000 reported nationally as of November 7 and at least eight deaths this year. In January, then-CDC official Ralph Abraham described the potential loss as 'the cost of doing business,' according to STAT.

Indiana has been particularly hard hit, with 145 confirmed cases in 2026. Knox County, Indiana, declared a public health emergency even though no cases have been confirmed there, in an effort to raise awareness and prevent outbreaks. One case has been reported in neighboring Daviess County. Knox County Health Officer Alan Stewart, M.D., said the best protection is vaccination, noting that 97 percent of Indiana cases have been in unvaccinated individuals. The emergency declaration allows the county to lower the vaccination age for children to 9 months, or as early as 6 months with a doctor's note.

In Ohio, Geauga Public Health confirmed a measles case on Wednesday, with exposure at seven locations in Geauga and Lake counties, including Berkshire Middle School, Kent State University's Geauga Campus, a restaurant, a choir concert, a sporting goods store, a steakhouse, and an urgent care center. The infected person was unvaccinated, according to Heather DiCioccio, director of nursing for the Lake County General Health District and Geauga Public Health. Officials are working to identify contacts, but the number of potentially exposed people is not yet known. Symptoms typically appear 7 to 21 days after exposure, meaning they could emerge through November 28.

Measles is extremely contagious, spreading through coughing and sneezing, and nine out of 10 unprotected people exposed will become ill. Health officials urge anyone who may have been exposed and is not immunized to contact their health care provider and monitor for symptoms, calling ahead before visiting a medical office. The Knox County Health Department emphasized that the measles vaccine is a safe, live attenuated virus. The U.S. elimination status decision from the PAHO committee is expected by the end of this week's meeting.
