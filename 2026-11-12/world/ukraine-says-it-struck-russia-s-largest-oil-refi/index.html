Ukraine Strikes Russia's Largest Oil Refinery in Siberia, Completing Campaign Against Top Five Refineries

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

Ukraine confirmed on November 11 that its Special Operations Forces struck Russia's largest oil refinery, the Omsk Oil Refinery in Siberia, approximately 2,500 kilometers from the Ukrainian border. The attack, which involved deep-strike drones traveling up to 3,000 kilometers, set a new record for the farthest Ukrainian drone strike on a Russian oil refinery. The Omsk facility, owned by state energy giant Gazprom, processes over 21 million metric tons of crude oil annually and is responsible for one out of every six liters of Russian gasoline and diesel fuel.

According to Ukraine's General Staff, the facility has a refining depth of about 99%, one of the highest in Russia, and produces gasoline, diesel, aviation fuel, and petrochemical products that supply the Russian military. The attack caused a fire near the site, according to the independent Russian Telegram channel Astra, though the precise extent of damage was not immediately clear. Omsk Governor Vitaly Khotsenko reported that Ukrainian drones attacked the region for over eight hours, prompting air raid sirens, the closure of airspace, restrictions on mobile internet, and reports of blackouts in some districts. Khotsenko said emergency workers were dispatched to sites impacted by "falling drone debris" but did not specify which facilities were struck.

The Omsk strike was the latest in a broader Ukrainian campaign that, in less than two weeks, has hit all five of Russia's largest oil refineries. Since October 30, Ukrainian Defense Forces have also targeted the Kirishi, Ryazan, Nizhny Novgorod, and Yaroslavl refineries, as well as the smaller Novokuibyshevsk and Saratov refineries, according to the General Staff. The Kirishi refinery (KINEF) in the Leningrad region, with an annual capacity of about 20 million metric tons, was struck on November 9, damaging an atmospheric crude oil distillation unit, a primary oil-processing unit, and two cooling installations. The Saratov refinery, hit on November 7, suffered damage to its only primary crude oil distillation unit, two fuel hydrotreatment units, pipelines, and a 10,000-cubic-meter storage tank, forcing it to suspend operations.

Ukraine's General Staff stated that the strikes on primary oil refining units are intended to reduce fuel production for the Russian military and cut the revenues Moscow uses to finance its war. According to Reuters, citing two industry sources on November 10, Ukrainian drone strikes since late October have affected facilities representing nearly 30% of Russia's total refining capacity. President Volodymyr Zelensky has identified "long-range sanctions" as one of Ukraine's strongest forms of leverage against Russia, and the success of Ukraine's domestic long-range drone development has made targets deep in the Russian rear, such as the Omsk refinery, newly vulnerable to attack.
