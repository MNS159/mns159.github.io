Russia-Ukraine War

US envoys meet Putin in Moscow as Ukraine reports new Starshield disruptions

International & World desk · anchored on Reuters
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts slightly center-left

US envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff met Russian President Vladimir Putin in Moscow on Thursday, a day after two days of US-mediated talks between Russian and Ukrainian delegations in Abu Dhabi ended without a major breakthrough. The talks produced an agreement on a large prisoner swap, with the exact number still to be announced. Kushner described the meeting with Putin as "substantive and detailed," while Witkoff said a new round of trilateral negotiations had been set and suggested European representatives could be included for the first time.

Putin repeated his maximalist demands during the meeting, insisting that any foreign troops in Ukraine would be "absolutely unacceptable" and would be treated as legitimate targets, effectively resuming the war. He also proposed a five-day "humanitarian mission" to the contested frontline city of Kostyantynivka in eastern Ukraine, similar to a recent US-brokered evacuation in the Russian-occupied city of Oleshky. Ukrainian presidential adviser Dmitry Lytvyn expressed skepticism, saying Russia used the Oleshky operation to bring in equipment and rotate troops under the guise of a humanitarian effort.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky said Thursday that Ukraine's military is facing new "systematic" restrictions to Starshield, the military-grade version of SpaceX's Starlink satellite internet service. Zelensky said units have recorded significant disruptions and speed restrictions for several days, limiting Ukrainian drones to 161 kph (100 mph), and that a military investigation determined the restrictions were not random errors. "Unfortunately, this is helping the Russians kill more Ukrainians and carry out more accurate strikes against Ukraine," he said. A similar disruption occurred in late October and lasted about a week before being resolved.

Zelensky spoke at a European Political Community summit in Dublin, where more than 40 European leaders gathered to discuss continental security and Ukraine. He said political changes, including Democrats taking over the US Congress in January and upcoming French elections, could create a "window of opportunity" for a peace deal, but insisted Ukraine will not surrender Russian-occupied territory. NATO Secretary-General Mark Rutte said the US, Europe and Ukraine are "very much aligned" on security guarantees but need a ceasefire or peace deal first.

Fighting continues along the 1,200-kilometer front line. Russia launched 123 drones, 73 of them jet-powered, and missiles at Ukraine overnight, with hits recorded at 17 locations. Ukraine's military said it has struck seven Russian oil refineries in the last two weeks, including five of Russia's largest. A Russian serviceman was killed in a car explosion in St. Petersburg early Thursday; Russian media identified the victim as a colonel. The US envoys are expected to travel to Dublin to meet Zelensky after their Moscow talks.
