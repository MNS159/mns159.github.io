Israeli airstrike on Gaza's Nasser Hospital kills six, including surgeon

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts slightly center-left

An Israeli airstrike on Nasser Hospital in Khan Younis, southern Gaza, killed six people and wounded at least 28 others early Thursday, according to Gaza's Health Ministry. Among the dead was Dr. Rizq Abu Shaira, a general surgeon. The strike hit the hospital's surgical wing, causing extensive damage across multiple floors, with debris scattered over beds and equipment.

The hospital's director, Dr. Ahmed al-Fara, said two missiles struck the al-Yasin surgery building but only one exploded. He described the attack as a deliberate targeting of the health sector, noting that medical staff, patients, and companions were killed. Dr. Mohammed Qdeih, a surgeon wounded in the head, said he was trapped under rubble and recalled Dr. Abu Shaira as 'the kindest person in this department' and 'one of the best' surgeons.

The Israeli military said it carried out a 'precise strike' targeting Mohammed Abu Ismail, a commander of Hamas's Khan Younis Brigade, and other fighters who it alleged were using the hospital as a headquarters to plan attacks. Israel accused Hamas of storing weapons and operating command rooms inside the facility, a charge Hamas denied, calling the strike a 'war crime.' The hospital's director insisted no warning was given before the attack.

This was the seventh time Nasser Hospital has been struck during the war. Under international law, hospitals are protected, but can lose that status if used for military purposes. The World Health Organization said it has documented 1,056 attacks on healthcare in Gaza over the past three years, resulting in 1,010 deaths. WHO Director-General Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus called for protection of health facilities, stating they 'must never be militarized or attacked.'

The airstrike comes despite a US-brokered ceasefire between Israel and Hamas that took effect in October 2025. Since then, more than 1,620 people have been killed in Gaza, according to the territory's health ministry. The broader war began with the Hamas-led attack on southern Israel on October 7, 2023, which killed about 1,200 people and saw 251 taken hostage. Since then, over 74,650 people have been killed in Gaza, the health ministry says. The humanitarian situation remains dire, with heavy rains recently flooding tens of thousands of tents housing displaced families.
