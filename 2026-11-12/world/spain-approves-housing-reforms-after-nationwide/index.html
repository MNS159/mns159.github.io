Spain approves housing reforms after nationwide strike over costs

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

The Spanish parliament passed housing measures on Thursday limiting evictions and real estate speculation, a day after a nationwide general strike over the country's housing crisis drew hundreds of thousands of protesters and sparked clashes in Barcelona. The interim lower house ratified the decrees in a 35–34 vote, but their duration depends on the outcome of a snap election scheduled for 29 November, in which the leading opposition Popular Party has said it will repeal the measures if it takes power.

The strike, Spain's first general strike in 14 years, was called by the country's main unions after parliament blocked earlier housing decrees. Some 120,000 people joined a demonstration in Madrid and more than 50,000 turned out in Barcelona, where masked men set fire to rubbish bins and smashed shop windows as police fired tear gas. Power usage in Spain fell by 7.5% during the early hours of Wednesday, according to data from operator Red Eléctrica, compared with a drop of more than 15% during the last general strike in 2012.

The housing crisis was triggered by widespread protests over affordable housing and rising living costs after the eviction of 87-year-old Maricarmen Abascal from a flat in Madrid where she had lived for seven decades. She died in hospital weeks later. Rental prices in the capital have risen sharply; the monthly cost of a 50 sq m apartment in Madrid averaged €1,170 in October, while Spain's average gross monthly salary is about €2,460, meaning rent consumes nearly half of gross pay, well above the 30% affordability threshold recommended by experts.

The first decree passed includes a measure extending protections against evictions for tenants deemed by social services to be in a precarious financial or social position, as long as regional authorities cannot offer them an alternative residence. Another measure aimed at reducing real estate speculation by stopping any company involved in the real estate market from buying properties for less than 70% of their value was rejected 34–35 after the centre-right Basque party voted with the right-wing bloc, arguing the measure could lead homeowners to withdraw properties from the market and drive prices higher.

Socialist Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez called snap elections last month after parliament failed to approve his initial housing reform plan. He has encouraged workers to strike, writing on social media: 'Your eight-hour day. Your pension. Your public healthcare… These were collective conquests.' The conservative People's Party, which leads in polls, accused the government of weaponising the strike for electoral gain. PP leader Alberto Núñez Feijóo called the general strike 'nonsense' and the unions 'mafia' and 'trash'. Party spokesman Miguel Tellado accused the government of exploiting the strike to warn voters against allowing a right-wing government to be formed. Sánchez told Spanish TV: 'What is nonsense is to question the rights of workers to strike.'
