Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang resigns after second affair, spying allegations

International & World desk

Jensen Huang, the 63-year-old co-founder and CEO of Nvidia, resigned on Thursday after revelations of a second extramarital affair, including one with a woman whom U.S. intelligence believes was spying for China. The announcement came after a board meeting late Wednesday, with Nvidia stating that Huang was stepping down immediately as president, CEO and board member. Chief financial officer Colette Kress will serve as interim CEO while the board searches for a permanent successor, ending Huang’s 33-year leadership of the AI chip giant.

The New York Times first reported that Huang had an affair with a senior Nvidia executive who reported directly to him. Both are married; Huang married his wife Lori in 1985. The second affair emerged just three days after Huang admitted a relationship with Alice Huang, a former executive at Megaspeed, a Singapore-based cloud computing firm that runs AI data centers across Southeast Asia and is a major Nvidia client. U.S. intelligence agencies concluded Alice Huang was a Chinese intelligence asset passing information to Beijing; the intelligence behind that assessment remains classified. The U.S. government is also investigating whether Megaspeed secretly sent Nvidia chips to China, violating export curbs on high-end semiconductors. Megaspeed denies the allegations and has pledged to cooperate with investigators. Alice Huang has been barred from entering Singapore and Taiwan.

Nvidia’s market capitalization has fallen by well over $600 billion since the first affair emerged on Sunday, dropping to $4.2 trillion. Shares were down more than 9% in premarket trading Thursday, after falling 10% earlier in the week. The stock had hit all-time highs just a month ago as U.S. technology giants continued to announce billions in spending on AI infrastructure. Despite the turmoil, many analysts expect little impact on near-term sales due to high demand for Nvidia’s advanced chips. “While Jensen is the heart and lungs of the Nvidia story…the company and its leading-edge GPU technology will go on,” said Dan Ives, a tech analyst at Yorkville Ives.

Huang, known for his leather jackets and hands-on involvement in product decisions, had no formal succession plan, according to The Wall Street Journal. Bloomberg reported that Nvidia’s board is working with advisers to identify permanent successors. Potential candidates include Kress, other senior executives, and Huang’s children: daughter Madison, a senior director of product marketing, and son Spencer, who works in the firm’s robotics division. Longer-term possibilities floated by analysts include former Apple CEO Tim Cook and ex-Google CEO Eric Schmidt. Huang’s sudden exit is seen as disruptive to Nvidia’s relationships with AI hyperscalers, Taiwanese chip producer TSMC, and foreign governments, especially China, where he served as the company’s main lobbyist on chip export rules and persuaded President Donald Trump to ease some curbs last year.

The scandal has also intensified the U.S.-China AI rivalry. Beijing accused the U.S. on Thursday of using the spying claims as a pretext to impose a technological blockade on China. Chinese AI firms are reportedly trying to rent Nvidia-powered computing capacity at data centers outside China amid fears of further U.S. restrictions. The issue is expected to dominate talks next week between Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping at the APEC gathering in Shenzhen. Analysts see the biggest long-term risk in strategy and execution, as Huang was the leader who drove Nvidia’s bets on key technologies. However, some argue that Nvidia’s central role in the AI hardware buildout and its deep expertise in chip design and software make it less dependent on his day-to-day input.
