Saudi Arabia vows serious response after deadly Houthi airport attacks

International & World desk

Saudi Energy Minister Prince Abdul Aziz bin Salman vowed a 'serious' response to recent attacks on the kingdom's airports and oil and gas facilities by Yemen's Iran-backed Houthi movement. 'The events of recent weeks are serious. Our response must be equally serious,' he told the WPC Energy Congress in Riyadh on Monday. The attacks have killed at least 16 people since last Thursday, according to Saudi authorities: three Saudis in strikes on an airport facility and an aircraft on Thursday, 13 people in a missile strike on Terminal 3 of Riyadh's King Khalid International Airport on Saturday, and a Pakistani resident in a projectile strike on Terminal 4 on Sunday. The Houthi-run health ministry said a child was killed and six others injured in a Saudi airstrike on a home in the Yemeni capital Sanaa on Sunday.

The Saturday attack on Terminal 3 killed four Saudis, two Bangladeshis, and one each of American, Egyptian, Jordanian, Syrian, Palestinian, Sudanese, and Indian nationals. King Fahd International Airport in Dammam was also attacked Sunday evening with no casualties reported. The Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted a drone in Eastern Province and a missile toward Khamis Mushait. Houthi military spokesman Yahya Sarea said the group launched missiles and drones at Riyadh and Dammam airports and two oil and gas facilities in retaliation for 124 Saudi airstrikes in Yemen over the previous 24 hours. Sarea warned airlines and passengers against using Saudi airports and airspace except for travel to Mecca and Medina, vowing to 'continue their escalating operations deep inside Saudi, imposing the equation of a blockade for a blockade and an escalation for an escalation.'

The attacks come amid a broader unraveling of a four-year informal ceasefire. The Houthis ousted Yemen's government from Sanaa in 2014, and a Saudi-led coalition intervened in 2015 to restore it. In July, the Houthis announced a 'maritime embargo' against Saudi Arabia and began missile and drone attacks on Saudi airports, oil facilities, and tankers in the Red Sea, saying they were retaliating for a Saudi blockade of ports and airports in their territory. Last month, the Houthis launched a surprise offensive along Yemen's southwestern Red Sea coast, seizing areas overlooking the Bab al-Mandab Strait, a critical chokepoint for oil shipments. The seizure contributed to a surge in global oil prices after the Strait of Hormuz was effectively closed due to the US and Israel's war with Iran.

The attacks drew widespread international condemnation. UN Secretary General António Guterres said he was 'deeply shocked' and warned that attacks on civilians and civilian infrastructure violate international law. US President Donald Trump said he would 'make a decision' about joining Saudi strikes on the Houthis; the US military already provides intelligence and targeting support to the coalition. Iran, which denies providing weapons to the Houthis, expressed 'deep concern' and called for 'dialogue and engagement.' In his address, Prince Abdul Aziz acknowledged that 'energy security cannot be taken for granted' and that recent developments had 'tested our infrastructure, our institutions, our people, and our resilience,' but promised the kingdom would overcome the challenges.

Amid the real attacks, numerous viral videos claiming to show the strikes on Riyadh airport have been debunked as AI-generated fakes, according to a DW Fact check analysis. Videos shared millions of times on X, TikTok, and other platforms contained visual glitches, implausible movements, and AI watermarks. One video that appeared to show an explosion at the airport was found to have been online since March 2026, months before the attack. Another video was created using AI based on a 2016 stock photograph of the airport. The fact check found that the short length and grainy quality of many clips made them difficult to verify, and they were often shared by accounts that regularly post AI-generated content.
