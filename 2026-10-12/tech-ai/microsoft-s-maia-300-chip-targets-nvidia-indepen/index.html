Microsoft Unveils Maia 300 AI Chip, Targeting Reduced Reliance on Nvidia

Tech / AI desk

Microsoft announced its third-generation in-house AI chip, the Maia 300, positioning it as the company's future primary AI inference system. The chip is designed to reduce Microsoft's reliance on GPUs from Nvidia and AMD by improving efficiency and handling the latest reasoning and agentic AI workloads. According to Scott Guthrie, executive vice president of Microsoft's cloud group, the company expects the Maia 300 to deliver 40 percent more tokens per megawatt compared to leading GPU-based systems.

The Maia 300 is a successor to the Maia 200, which was announced in January and is already running production AI workloads across Microsoft's services, including Microsoft Copilot, GitHub Copilot, and Microsoft Foundry. The new chip will begin rolling out to production AI workloads in 2027. Microsoft plans to scale the Azure Maia family to multi-gigawatt deployments starting that year, making Maia the primary AI inference system for Microsoft's products and services, Guthrie said.

The efficiency gains in the Maia 300 come from a new chiplet architecture built on TSMC's N2P process node. The design separates compute, memory controller, and I/O functions, increasing the silicon area dedicated to compute performance and allowing each function to use the most suitable technology. In a full 96-accelerator configuration, the Maia 300 delivers up to 7x the rack-scale token throughput of the Maia 200, according to Microsoft.

Microsoft described the Maia 300 as a complete AI system designed for emerging AI workloads, including long-context reasoning, latency-sensitive conversational experiences, and sustained, multi-step agentic tasks. "Maia 200 is the best performing AI infrastructure in our fleet, measured in real world metrics for AI services," Guthrie said. "Maia 300 is a complete AI system designed for the new types of AI workloads emerging today."

The chip is also central to Microsoft's broader AI strategy, including its work on developing its own AI models to compete with Google DeepMind, OpenAI, and Anthropic. Mustafa Suleyman, CEO of Microsoft AI, stressed the importance of owning the full stack down to the silicon, saying, "We have to work across the full stack, all the way down to the silicon. At that scale we need a platform that we own, and that we co-design with our models. That's why running our Microsoft AI models on top of the Maia chip is a critical focus for our team."
