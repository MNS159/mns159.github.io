Meta launches Muse Wonder, its first frontier AI models

Tech / AI desk

Meta's Superintelligence Lab on Monday released its first frontier AI models, Muse Wonder and Muse Wonder Full. The models are available starting today to Muse Code subscribers, and Wonder can also be accessed through the Meta Model API at $4 per million input tokens and $20 per million output tokens.

Meta says the new models can rival the industry's best, pointing to internal testing on several benchmarks in which Wonder and Wonder Full achieved scores comparable to Anthropic's Claude Fable 5.1 and OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra. The company also positioned them against GPT-6 Astra, Claude Opus 5.5, and Claude Fable 5.1. It remains unclear how the models stack up against Google's Gemini 4 Argon. Relative to the lightweight, low-latency Muse Spark, the Wonder models are larger and more capable, with higher benchmark scores and better results on complex tasks like coding.

Meta placed particular emphasis on 'active memory,' the models' ability to retain and synthesize information from users over long periods. According to the company's testing, Muse Wonder scores higher on MemoryBench, a benchmark that challenges continual learning and memory construction, than Fable, Opus, or Astra. 'We put a tremendous amount of effort into making sure your model can remember what's important to you over days, weeks, months, and beyond,' said research scientist Kavya Kopparapu in a video accompanying the launch.

Wonder Full will be available only to Muse Code subscribers and will not be served through the API. 'Together, they mark the next step on our scaling ladder and a major leap toward personal superintelligence,' Meta said in its blog post. Both models were trained at Meta's Prometheus data center in central Ohio, which came online earlier this year.

The release is Meta's first frontier-model launch since Scale AI founder Alexandr Wang restructured the company's AI efforts in August of last year. In that time Meta has shipped the lightweight Muse Spark model and its Muse AI agent but avoided the kind of state-of-the-art releases associated with OpenAI and Anthropic. The launch is part of Meta's aggressive push to reestablish itself as a major AI force, built on billions of dollars in talent and infrastructure, the image model Muse Image, and a focus on bringing AI agents to consumers through Muse.

Meta says it eventually plans to power its Muse agent with Wonder, starting with subscribers, and to develop specialized models it 'can bring to everyone using Muse,' though it has not shared specifics or a timeline. With the Wonder release, Meta is signaling it is making up for lost time in the race for frontier AI.
