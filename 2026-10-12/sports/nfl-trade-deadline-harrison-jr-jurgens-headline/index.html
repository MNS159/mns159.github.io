NFL trade deadline: Harrison Jr., Jurgens, Porter headline multi-player moves

Sports desk

With the NFL trade deadline approaching on Nov. 10, teams have been active in recent weeks, completing several significant trades involving high-profile players. The most notable moves include the Arizona Cardinals trading wide receiver Marvin Harrison Jr. to the Houston Texans, the Philadelphia Eagles sending offensive lineman Cam Jurgens to the Baltimore Ravens, the Pittsburgh Steelers dealing cornerback Joey Porter Jr. to the Dallas Cowboys, and the Minnesota Vikings trading quarterback J.J. McCarthy to the New York Giants.

The Cardinals traded Harrison Jr., the No. 4 overall pick in the 2024 draft, to the Texans on Oct. 12. Arizona received a 2027 second-round pick and a 2027 sixth-round pick, while Houston also received a 2027 seventh-round pick. Harrison Jr. had five receptions for 85 yards and one touchdown in four games this season, averaging 0.6 yards per route run, the third-lowest among players with at least 100 routes, per Next Gen Stats. He will reunite with former Ohio State teammate C.J. Stroud in Houston. NFL.com graded the trade a B+ for the Cardinals and a C for the Texans, noting the premium price for a player who has not performed at expectations.

The Eagles traded Jurgens to the Ravens on Oct. 7. Baltimore received Jurgens and a 2027 seventh-round pick, while Philadelphia received a 2027 fifth-round pick and a 2028 second-round pick. Jurgens, a two-time Pro Bowler, had struggled after moving to guard due to an injury to Landon Dickerson, and the Eagles felt comfortable moving him due to confidence in younger linemen such as Willie Lampkin. The Ravens needed a center after losing Tyler Linderbaum in free agency and suffering multiple injuries at the position, including Danny Pinter, Jovaughn Gwyn, and Ethan Pocic all landing on injured reserve. NFL.com gave the Eagles an A- grade and the Ravens a B+. Jurgens entered the concussion protocol after the trade, according to NFL Network.

The Steelers traded Porter Jr. to the Cowboys on Oct. 1 after failed contract negotiations. Pittsburgh received a 2027 sixth-round pick and a 2028 second-round pick, while Dallas received Porter. According to Sports Illustrated's Albert Breer, Porter sought $30 million per year, but the Steelers were unwilling to go past the high $20s in new money. Porter had missed the first three games with a back injury and then pulled himself from practice after reporting back pain. The Cowboys, who ranked last in EPA per pass allowed (0.36), needed cornerback help. NFL.com graded the deal an A- for Dallas and a C+ for Pittsburgh.

The Giants acquired McCarthy from the Vikings on Sept. 28 for a 2027 fifth-round pick. McCarthy, the No. 10 pick in the 2024 draft, had fallen to third on the Vikings' quarterback depth chart behind Kyler Murray and Carson Wentz. The Giants needed a backup quarterback after Jaxson Dart sustained a season-ending injury in Week 2. The Vikings chose to move on from McCarthy, ending his brief stint in Minnesota.

These trades carry significant implications for the playoff races. The Texans, after an 0-4 start, hope Harrison can revive their offense alongside Stroud. The Ravens aim to stabilize their offensive line with Jurgens, while the Cowboys address a glaring secondary weakness. The Eagles stockpiled future draft capital, now holding two first-round and two second-round picks in 2028. The Steelers moved on from a disgruntled player, and the Giants secured a young quarterback with starting potential as depth.
