MLB sues players' union, asks court to declare lockout legal

Sports desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts slightly center-right

Major League Baseball filed a lawsuit Wednesday against the MLB Players Association in federal court in the Southern District of New York, asking a judge to declare that a lockout would be legal and to prevent the union from dissolving its status as the players' bargaining representative. The legal action comes days before the current collective bargaining agreement is set to expire Monday night, after which the league and its 30 team owners are expected to lock players out of team facilities and cut off contact with team officials.

The league described the suit as a defensive measure. MLB spokesperson Glen Caplin said, 'Today’s filing is a defensive measure simply intended to keep the parties at the bargaining table to work towards a new collective bargaining agreement and a full 2027 season.' Caplin added that the league believes the union is preparing to 'disclaim' its status as the players’ representative in order to pursue an antitrust lawsuit, calling that a 'nuclear option' that would threaten MLB's antitrust exemption. 'We have asked a federal court in the Southern District of New York to proactively disarm the Union’s nuclear option of disclaiming,' Caplin said.

The players' union responded sharply, calling the lawsuit 'completely frivolous.' In a statement, the MLBPA said, 'We will respond appropriately, but MLB's decision to run to court in the middle of negotiations rather than bargain in good faith is incredibly disappointing.' The union has not threatened to dissolve or disclaimed its status, and the two sides remain in negotiations, though fundamental disagreements persist over the financial structure of the sport.

At the center of the dispute is a proposal from ownership to implement a salary cap and floor system, which the union has refused to consider. The league contends the union is stonewalling and threatening antitrust litigation to force MLB to abandon the cap proposal. The players have proposed increasing revenue sharing from higher-income teams to lower-income teams as an alternative, arguing that the cap proposal is designed to increase owners' profits and franchise valuations rather than improve competitive balance. The league has largely ignored that suggestion, according to the union.

The lockout, if it proceeds, would jeopardize the start of the 2027 season. The league has said it cannot sit idly by while the union's tactics threaten a full season, while the union blames ownership for demanding a fundamental restructuring of the game. With the CBA expiration imminent and negotiations showing little sign of resolution, the lawsuit adds a new layer of tension to the already contentious labor situation.
