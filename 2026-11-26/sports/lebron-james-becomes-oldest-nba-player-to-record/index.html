LeBron James becomes oldest NBA player to record a triple-double as 76ers top Heat

Sports desk

LeBron James became the oldest player in NBA history to record a triple-double, posting 25 points, 12 assists and 10 rebounds to lead the Philadelphia 76ers to a 111-104 victory over the Miami Heat in an NBA Cup group-stage game on Wednesday night in Philadelphia. James, who turns 42 on Dec. 30, broke his own age mark for a triple-double at 41 years and 330 days old. The win improved the 76ers to an Eastern Conference-best 15-4 and snapped Miami's seven-game winning streak, dropping the Heat to 10-7.

James scored a season-high 25 points, including a stretch of 14 consecutive points in the second quarter that turned a three-point deficit into a 62-53 halftime lead. He shot 4 of 6 from three-point range, highlighted by a 35-foot pull-up three. Tyrese Maxey led all scorers with 30 points for Philadelphia, and rookie VJ Edgecombe added 18 points. Giannis Antetokounmpo led Miami with 32 points and 14 rebounds. The 76ers played without Joel Embiid (illness) and lost Jaylen Brown to a hamstring injury late in the first half.

James now has 126 career triple-doubles, a total that exceeds the combined career triple-doubles of Larry Bird, Michael Jordan, Kobe Bryant and Scottie Pippen, according to the Associated Press. He also connected on a halfcourt lob to Edgecombe for a layup early in the fourth quarter and later set up forward Dominick Barlow for an alley-oop dunk. "That's my favourite aspect of my game besides my IQ," James said of setting up teammates. "To be able to go out there and just play like a quarterback role and watch these guys run the right routes, and it's my job to put it on time and on target, it was fun."

Heat coach Erik Spoelstra, who coached James during Miami's back-to-back championships in 2012 and 2013, said before the game: "Father Time always wins. But right now, he's giving Father Time hell." James addressed his ability to step up when the team needs him most: "If they need me to dust off the cape from time to time, I can still accomplish that."

Both teams are now 2-1 in East Group B of the NBA Cup. Miami would have clinched the group with a win. The idle Dallas Mavericks became the first team to reach the Cup knockout round, clinching West Group A when the Phoenix Suns lost 116-104 at Houston. In that game, Kevin Durant became the fifth player in NBA history to score 33,000 points, joining James, Kareem Abdul-Jabbar, Karl Malone and Kobe Bryant. Houston's Alperen Şengün recorded his 15th career triple-double with 23 points, 11 rebounds and 11 assists, passing Hakeem Olajuwon for the second-most triple-doubles in franchise history.

In other NBA Cup action, the Minnesota Timberwolves remained unbeaten in Cup competition at 3-0 with a 113-106 win over the Oklahoma City Thunder. Jaden McDaniels had 32 points, 10 rebounds and 5 assists, becoming the third player in Timberwolves history to log at least 30 points, 10 rebounds and 5 assists in consecutive games, joining Karl-Anthony Towns and Kevin Garnett. The defending NBA and NBA Cup champion New York Knicks beat the Cleveland Cavaliers 121-102 for their sixth straight win. At Portland, the Trail Blazers defeated the San Antonio Spurs 116-111, leaving West Group C with four teams tied at 2-1. Spurs star Victor Wembanyama scored 44 points and grabbed 16 rebounds in the loss.
