Steelers owner Art Rooney II backs Mike McCarthy, calls Aaron Rodgers insubordinate after release

Sports desk

Pittsburgh Steelers president Art Rooney II publicly backed head coach Mike McCarthy on Thursday, calling quarterback Aaron Rodgers insubordinate and saying his recent actions could not be tolerated. Rooney's comments came a day after Rodgers was released by the team following a 35-minute interview on ESPN's "The Pat McAfee Show" in which he berated McCarthy and accused him of sleeping through team meetings and skipping walk-throughs. Rodgers had been benched earlier in the week after giving McCarthy an ultimatum: relinquish play-calling duties or release him.

"I just want to say that I have 100% confidence in Mike McCarthy and I expect he'll be our coach this season, next season and beyond," Rooney told the Pittsburgh Post-Gazette. "The big thing, what happened this week, is insubordination can't be tolerated in any organization. When you start getting ultimatums, you can't live with that." Rooney also said Rodgers was "not a good teammate" and criticized the timing of his public comments, noting the Steelers are days away from a game against the Denver Broncos. "There's maybe a time and place after the season if he wanted to kind of unload," Rooney said. "He talks about wanting to be a good teammate, but you don't do that to your teammates a couple days before the game, that's for sure."

Rooney revealed that McCarthy had taken blame for some of the team's offensive struggles to protect Rodgers. "The sad thing is Mike took some of the bullets for Aaron in terms of some of the plays that were called, particularly in the red-zone troubles that we had," Rooney said. "Mike took some of the bullets for things that were going wrong down there. He tried to protect him, and it just wasn't working." One possible example cited was late in the first half of Pittsburgh's Week 8 win over the Browns, when the Steelers called three straight passing plays inside Cleveland's 10-yard-line despite running effectively. McCarthy accepted blame for those calls afterward.

Rooney expressed optimism that backup Mason Rudolph can lead the offense. Rudolph, a nine-year veteran with 20 career starts, delivered for the Steelers in a similar spot in 2023. Asked if he regretted bringing Rodgers back, Rooney said he was disappointed with how things turned out. "We certainly had hopes that he would be successful here, and I thought at times we certainly got on a roll," he said. "We had high expectations. It's easy to say, knowing what I know today, what would I do? But we had a good experience last year, and so I kind of wasn't expecting it to go this way this year."

The Steelers are scheduled to host the Denver Broncos on Friday at 3:00 p.m. ET. Despite the team's last two ugly losses and some sources corroborating Rodgers' claims about McCarthy, Rooney said he expects McCarthy to remain the coach "this season, next season and beyond."
