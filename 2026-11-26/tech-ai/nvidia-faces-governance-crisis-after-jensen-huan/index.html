Nvidia faces governance crisis after Jensen Huang reinstated as CEO amid code-of-conduct violations

Tech / AI desk

S&P Global Ratings is considering downgrading Nvidia’s credit rating, citing governance concerns and “key man risk” following the ouster and reinstatement of CEO Jensen Huang, who admitted to violating the company’s code of conduct. The agency expects to complete its review within 90 days and could cut Nvidia’s rating from AA to AA-, the fourth-highest on its scale. Although Nvidia would retain its investment-grade status, a downgrade could raise its borrowing costs and ripple through the AI infrastructure loans it guarantees.

Huang addressed the code-of-conduct violation during his first all-hands meeting after returning as CEO, emphasizing that no one is exempt from the rules but that violations do not automatically lead to termination. “Facts, circumstances, a lot of assessments” still matter, he said, according to Wired. The admission has created an awkward cultural risk: employees may feel embarrassed or angry about working for a company where the CEO broke the rules without facing consequences, ethics experts warn.

Matt Plass, chief market strategy officer at LRN, a compliance training firm, said that for many workers, ethics are paramount. “They’re really embarrassed and angry when senior leadership lets them down,” he told TechCrunch. Plass noted that companies typically remove an executive after an investigation and then launch a “hearts-and-minds initiative” to rebuild trust. But that playbook is harder at Nvidia, where Huang is seen as nearly inseparable from the company’s success and no clear succession plan exists.

The governance concerns extend beyond culture. S&P cited “weak executive oversight and lack of succession planning” as reasons for the potential downgrade. In August, Nvidia announced plans to arrange $500 billion in financing for AI infrastructure with six financial institutions, including Goldman Sachs, Apollo, and Blackstone, with Nvidia guaranteeing 25% of the loans. Even before Huang’s ouster, lenders had been demanding larger guarantees, Bloomberg reported. A downgrade would likely intensify those demands, raising costs for neoclouds and other infrastructure companies that rely on Nvidia’s backing.

Despite the turmoil, an employee campaign for Huang’s reinstatement suggests morale has not yet suffered. However, as pressure mounts in the coming months, sidestepping the code of conduct could cost Nvidia something harder to measure: its employees’ pride in the company. Plass acknowledged that for firms where the CEO is a “godhead figure,” dealing with such infractions is “much, much harder.”
