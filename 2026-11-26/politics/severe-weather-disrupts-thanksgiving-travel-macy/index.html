Severe weather disrupts Thanksgiving travel; Macy's parade postponed

Politics desk
✓ Facts
  • Macy's postponed its 100th annual Thanksgiving Day Parade in New York City from Thursday to Saturday due to a powerful nor'easter, the first weather-related postponement in the parade's history.
  • New York Gov. Kathy Hochul declared a state of emergency for Long Island, New York City and Westchester County; New Jersey Gov. Mikie Sherrill declared a state of emergency across all 21 of the state's counties.
  • The National Weather Service issued a high wind warning for the New York area from 10:00 a.m. Thursday to 1:00 a.m. Friday, with gusts up to 60 mph, along with forecasts of heavy rain, coastal flooding and inland snow.
  • Record 'king tides' hit California during Thanksgiving week, with tides reaching 8 feet 11 inches in San Diego and 8.19 feet in Los Angeles, breaking records set in late October; a portion of a pier in northern California collapsed into the ocean.
  • AAA projected 81.5 million Americans would travel at least 50 miles from home for Thanksgiving between Nov. 24 and Nov. 30.
  • A flood warning was issued for Vancouver, British Columbia's coastline due to king tides, and additional storms were forecast for the Pacific Northwest.
  • NYC Mayor Zohran Mamdani said the postponement was necessary due to extreme weather, confirmed an expanded police presence for Saturday's rescheduled parade, and said he would not attend due to a scheduled surgery.
  • President Trump called for a probe into GDP data, according to The Hill, though further details of the demand were not specified in available reporting.
○ Analysis

The disruptions stem from two distinct weather systems converging over the Thanksgiving holiday: a nor'easter moving up the East Coast bringing high winds, heavy rain and coastal flooding, and a rare set of 'king tides' — the year's highest predicted tides — hitting California and the Pacific Northwest, intensified by a strong El Niño pattern. Together these systems affected one of the busiest travel weeks of the year, with tens of millions of Americans attempting to travel, and forced the cancellation of a century-old cultural tradition, the Macy's parade, for the first time due to weather rather than wartime material shortages. State officials in New York and New Jersey invoked emergency declarations to mobilize resources and urge caution, while California and Canadian officials issued flood warnings as tide records were broken for the second time in a month.

Implications

The nor'easter and king tides are expected to continue moving through the affected regions into Friday, meaning further travel delays, coastal flooding and infrastructure risk are possible before conditions stabilize. The rescheduled Macy's parade on Saturday will be watched for weather conditions and whether performers, balloons and attendees can proceed safely, while coastal communities in California and the Pacific Northwest will continue monitoring tide levels for additional flooding risk.
