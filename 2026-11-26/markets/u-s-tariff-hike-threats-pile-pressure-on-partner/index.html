U.S. tariff-hike threats pile pressure on partners, raising inflation and rate-hike risks

Financial & Markets desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

The Trump administration on Nov. 25 proposed new tariffs targeting 14 major trading partners, including China, the European Union, Vietnam, India, South Korea, Taiwan, Switzerland, and Singapore, as part of a strategy to curb excess industrial capacity and pressure countries to conclude trade deals with Washington. The proposed rates start at 15% for the EU, South Korea, Switzerland, and Taiwan and rise to as much as 35% for Vietnam, with China facing a capped rate of 20% on top of an existing 25% tariff. The U.S. Trade Representative's Office said it intends to cap the new duties at lower levels for countries that have completed trade deals or are still negotiating, giving some nations a strong incentive to speed up talks.

China faces a potential doubling of tariffs to 40% if the trade truce reached in South Korea last October is not extended beyond January, according to the USTR. Beijing has rejected Washington's claims of excess capacity, with Commerce Ministry spokesperson He Yadong calling on the U.S. to "rectify its wrongdoing and return to the right track of resolving issues through dialogue." Wendy Cutler of the Asia Society Policy Institute said Beijing "seems prepared to live with the new rates provided that they don't exceed 20%," and noted that Washington is using the tariffs as leverage to secure shipments of critical minerals and agricultural goods. Analysts said the proposal appears aimed squarely at China, with the word appearing hundreds of times in USTR reports on other countries.

India and Vietnam face proposed tariffs of 25% and 35%, respectively, but those rates could fall to 17% and 20% if ongoing talks produce reciprocal trade agreements. Vietnam's trade ministry said Thursday it was working to complete a deal as soon as possible. Singapore faces a maximum potential rate of 20%, which could fall to 10% with an agreement; its Ministry of Energy, Trade and Industry said it was "very disappointed" and argued the proposal failed to account for the structure of its economy. Deputy Prime Minister Gan Kim Yong said about 33% of direct exports would be affected, and that the effective tariff rate would rise to 10.3% from 7.8% under the highest levy, adding, "I'm also very surprised that this has happened because this is not something that you would expect from an important major trading partner."

Exemptions for semiconductors and other electronics should cushion the impact on South Korea and Taiwan, analysts said. Taiwan's effective tariff rate would rise to about 9% from 6%. Switzerland, which negotiated a deal in November 2025 that slashed U.S. duties to 15%, now faces an additional 15% tariff from a new Section 301 investigation; its Economy Ministry said it rejected the allegations and assumed the latest recommendation was consistent with the earlier deal. The European Union rejected the finding that Europe was a source of industrial overcapacity, with Commission spokesperson Olof Gill saying the EU expects any measures to respect the existing 15% ceiling on EU goods. EU trade ministers are due to speak with USTR Jamieson Greer on Monday.

The proposed tariffs threaten to complicate the Federal Reserve's fight against inflation, which could hurt the stock market. Research from the Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis indicates that tariffs contributed 0.5 percentage points to headline inflation as of June 2026. The S&P 500 and Nasdaq Composite have already fallen approximately 10% and 17% from their October highs, with investors worried about persistent inflation and the expectation of higher interest rates. Citi analysts said European retailers such as Adidas, H&M, Puma, and Primark could see downgrades on full-year 2027 earnings if the measures stay as announced. Inga Fechner of ING noted that U.S. protectionism is becoming increasingly difficult to unwind, and that even a Democrat-controlled Congress would face challenges reversing tariffs while President Trump retains veto power.
