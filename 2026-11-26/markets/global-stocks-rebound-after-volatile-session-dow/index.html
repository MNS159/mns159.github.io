Dow erases 1,100-point loss in volatile session; S&P 500 and Nasdaq end lower but well off lows

Financial & Markets desk · anchored on Reuters

The Dow Jones Industrial Average erased an intraday loss of more than 1,100 points on Wednesday, closing 164.25 points higher, or 0.33%, at 50,182.95, in a dramatic reversal that traders attributed to light holiday trading and a bullish blog post by investor Michael Burry. The S&P 500 and Nasdaq Composite also staged sharp comebacks but finished in negative territory: the S&P 500 fell 0.25% to 7,182.78, and the Nasdaq dropped 0.62% to 23,585.80. At their session lows, the Dow had lost 2.2%, the S&P 500 3.2%, and the Nasdaq 4.6%.

The rebound was fueled in part by the circulation of a blog post in which Burry said his portfolio is "temporarily almost entirely long," and by reduced trading volumes ahead of the Thanksgiving holiday. The Dow was lifted by gains in International Business Machines, Salesforce, and Sherwin-Williams, while S&P 500 sectors including materials, energy, and real estate all rose. However, AI-related stocks continued to decline: Nvidia dropped nearly 4%, Micron Technology fell 4.5%, Oracle lost more than 5%, and Alphabet shed over 2%. The State Street Technology Select Sector ETF (XLK) ended nearly 1% lower. Semiconductor maker Ambarella plunged more than 19% after reporting a light current-quarter forecast and missing third-quarter earnings and revenue estimates.

David Wagner, head of equities at Aptus Capital Advisors, described the market as "very bifurcated" between AI and non-AI stocks. "The market is pulling back obviously due to a pullback in tech, pullback in semis, pullback in the AI narrative, but right now, I don't see that being a growth scare. I see it being a sentiment-based scare" tied to factors such as the composition of Congress in the new year. He added that there remains a "big worry" that AI concerns could shift from sentiment to a growth scare "if the market starts pricing in too much Fed activity of hiking rates."

Investor sentiment was weighed by several macroeconomic headwinds. October's personal consumption expenditures (PCE) price index showed headline inflation rising 0.3% month over month, putting the annual rate at 3.8%, up from 3.7% in September. Core PCE, excluding food and energy, increased 3.3% year over year, slightly above the Dow Jones forecast. The data reinforced expectations for interest rate hikes from the Federal Reserve in December and January, according to reports. The White House also unveiled new tariffs on more than a dozen major trading partners, including China and the European Union, adding to the risk-off mood.

Wednesday's volatile session followed a losing day for the S&P 500 and Nasdaq on Tuesday, when tech stocks gave back gains as Nvidia sank and Anthropic fell below its IPO price. The choppy trading is the latest in a month marked by sharp swings, including the ouster and reinstatement of Nvidia's Jensen Huang and the Democrats' sweep in the midterm elections, as investors debate the future of the AI build-out against a worsening macroeconomic outlook. U.S. stock markets will be closed Thursday for Thanksgiving and will close at 1 p.m. ET on Friday. Dow Jones futures rose modestly Thursday afternoon, along with S&P 500 and Nasdaq futures, as global markets showed signs of stabilization.
