Israeli judges recommend dropping most serious charges against Netanyahu

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

Judges in Benjamin Netanyahu's long-running corruption trial recommended on Wednesday that prosecutors drop the most serious case against him, saying the prospects of securing a conviction were low. The recommendation, delivered in a highly unusual hearing at the Jerusalem District Court, could give the caretaker prime minister a boost as he fights for political survival following an inconclusive general election last month.

The judges addressed three cases against Netanyahu, who has been charged since 2019 with bribery, fraud and breach of trust. In Case 4000, which alleges he granted favours to telecommunications company Bezeq in exchange for positive coverage on a news site owned by the company's chair, Judge Rivka Friedman-Feldman told Attorney General Gali Baharav-Miara that 'the chances of conviction are low – to an extent that warrants reconsidering the matter,' according to Israeli media. In Case 2000, concerning an alleged deal with Israeli daily Yedioth Ahronoth for favourable coverage, the judges questioned whether the conduct constituted a criminal offence and called for the matter to be reconsidered. In Case 1000, involving accusations that Netanyahu and his wife received gifts and payments from an Australian billionaire in return for political favours, the judges suggested prosecutors and the defence seek an agreement to conclude the proceedings, though a verdict remained an option.

The judges do not have the power to drop charges themselves; their recommendation is directed at Attorney General Baharav-Miara, a longstanding political opponent of Netanyahu who has challenged several government policies. Responding to the court, she said, 'Every statement and proposal raised just now by the court' will be considered, adding, 'We are open to examining everything. Including things that were argued in the past.' She also noted that her office had previously been ready to accept a proposal from Netanyahu's lawyers to conclude proceedings through a deal, saying, 'Our extended hand remains.'

Netanyahu, who has consistently denied the allegations and called the cases a political witch-hunt, said in a televised statement that the 'fabricated cases' against him were falling apart. 'Ten years of terrible suffering for me, my family, my son,' he said. 'Ten years of incitement to hatred that wounds the soul.' He called for the trial to be ended 'immediately' and urged political unity, saying, 'I know what enormous and immediate dangers the state of Israel faces. There is only one way to deal with them. To unite in order to deal with them.' None of Israel's main opposition politicians have yet commented on the judges' advice.

The development comes as Netanyahu's rival Gadi Eisenkot attempts to form a coalition government after last month's election, in which Netanyahu's parliamentary bloc won more seats but both sides fell short of an outright majority. Legal scholar Barak Medina of The Hebrew University of Jerusalem called the judges' intervention 'unprecedented in its nature,' noting that they 'expressed their views before the prosecution had an opportunity to present its arguments.' Netanyahu remains subject to an arrest warrant from the International Criminal Court for alleged war crimes and crimes against humanity in Gaza, which he also denies.
