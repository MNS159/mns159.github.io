Two Italians killed in Kuwait drone strike; Iran denies involvement

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

Two Italian nationals were killed and two other workers wounded on Thursday when falling shrapnel from intercepted Iranian drones struck a vital site in Kuwait, according to Kuwait's Ministry of Defence. The victims were technicians for the German arms manufacturer Rheinmetall Italia, working at an air-defence surveillance station performing maintenance on systems used by the Kuwaiti Armed Forces, Italy's foreign ministry confirmed. The wounded include a Kuwaiti and a Pakistani national.

Kuwait's army said the attack was the result of 'heinous Iranian aggression' and that its forces had detected and intercepted a number of drones from Iran. The two Italians died from their injuries after being hit by falling debris as authorities attempted to shoot down the incoming drones, the defence ministry stated. Italian media identified the deceased as a 36-year-old and a 65-year-old who was nearing retirement.

Iran has strongly denied responsibility. Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi, in a phone call with his Kuwaiti counterpart Jarrah Jaber Al-Ahmad Al-Sabah, 'firmly rejected allegations regarding an Iranian drone attack on Kuwait, stating that the Islamic Republic of Iran had no involvement whatsoever,' according to state TV. Araghchi offered to conduct a 'joint investigation with the Kuwaiti side' into the incident. Armed forces spokesman Sardar Shekarchi said there had been 'no offensive or military operations in the region' and suggested the accusations were a cover for 'increasing insecurity in the region.'

The attack occurred while the Kuwaiti National Guard and military were meeting with an Italian delegation that included the commander general of the Italian Carabinieri, Lieutenant-General Salvatore Lungo, and the Italian ambassador to Kuwait, according to Al Jazeera's Zein Basravi. Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni expressed 'deepest sorrow' and condolences to the victims' families. European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen called for increased pressure on Iran, stating that 'security in the Gulf is a security issue for us all.'

The incident comes just over a week after Kuwait said it had repelled overnight Iranian drone attacks on an airbase hosting US forces, the first such strikes in more than two months. The broader conflict began with US-Israeli strikes on Iran in late February, followed by Iranian missile and drone attacks on Israel and Gulf Arab states hosting US military assets. Gulf states have been caught in the crossfire, with US military facilities and energy installations on their soil targeted. Jasem Mohamed Albudaiwi, secretary-general of the Gulf Cooperation Council, condemned the 'brutal' attack, stressing that Iran's continued strikes on Kuwait constitute a dangerous escalation and threaten regional security.
