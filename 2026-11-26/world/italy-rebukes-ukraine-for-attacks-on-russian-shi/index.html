Italy rebukes Ukraine for attacks on Russian ships in Mediterranean, citing Libya sovereignty violation

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

Italy has rebuked Ukraine for carrying out attacks against Russian vessels in Libyan waters, saying Kyiv was wrong to violate Libya's sovereignty. The strike, which occurred Sunday night, targeted a Russian warship and a cargo vessel transporting military equipment off Libya's coast, according to Kyiv. Ukraine's Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha described the vessels as legitimate targets, arguing Kyiv had the legal right to defend itself against Russian aggression, and stressed that Ukraine respects Libya's territorial integrity.

The attack on the warship at Benghazi port in eastern Libya killed two naval officers of the self-styled Libyan National Army (LNA), according to Libya's eastern-based forces. Musa al-Koni, vice president of Libya's Tripoli-based Presidential Council, called the attack 'a declaration of war against the Libyan state.' The LNA, which controls eastern and southern parts of Libya and maintains close relations with Russia, promised to retaliate 'at the appropriate time and place, and by the appropriate means.'

Italy's Foreign Minister Antonio Tajani said it was a mistake for Ukraine to violate Libya's sovereignty and that Rome, which has a small military presence in Libya, had received no prior notice of the operation. 'Italy was absolutely unaware of Kyiv's intentions to strike in Libyan territorial waters, even inside a port,' Tajani told Italy's Corriere della Sera newspaper. Italy's far-right League party, a member of the coalition government, also criticized the attack, saying it risked sowing instability inside Libya.

The rebuke marks a rare rift between Italy and Ukraine. Italy has largely supported Ukraine since Russia's full-scale invasion in 2022, providing more than 3 billion euros ($3.29bn) in military aid, according to data from the Kiel Institute, and backing sweeping sanctions against Russia. However, there has been division within Italy's government over further aid, with Deputy Prime Minister Matteo Salvini, who heads the League party, opposing more weapons shipments or funding. Matteo Villa, a senior research fellow at the Italian Institute for International Political Studies (ISPI), said the attack off Libya is likely to deepen these domestic tensions, giving Salvini an opportunity to question whether Western military assistance risks enabling Ukraine to pursue objectives that run counter to Italy's own national interests.

Italy's concerns are fueled by its geographical proximity to Libya—its southern islands lie 300km across the Mediterranean from the Libyan coast—as well as its economic and strategic interests there. Last year, Libya accounted for roughly a quarter of Italy's crude oil imports, making it Italy's largest supplier, and the country is the main departure point for migrants reaching Italy by sea. Alessia Melcangi, a nonresident senior fellow at the Atlantic Council, said Italy's condemnation partly serves as 'damage control' to help Rome balance its competing geopolitical interests. 'Rome needs to demonstrate that its relationships with Kyiv, Tripoli and Benghazi give it meaningful influence over developments affecting its security,' she said.

The attacks are part of a broader Ukrainian campaign to strike Russian vessels and infrastructure far from Ukraine. In recent weeks, Ukraine has targeted a Russian cargo vessel off the Finnish coast, a Russian corvette in the Mediterranean, and a dry cargo vessel allegedly carrying weapons off Libya. Security expert Elisabeth Braw of the Atlantic Council said Ukraine is showing it can hit Russian ships 'wherever and whatever waters they are in.' Joachim Weber, a security expert at the University of Bonn, warned that the risk of an accidental exchange of fire is rising as Russia provides more military escorts for its shadow fleet, and that war could become 'omnipresent' if attacks on civilian vessels increase.
