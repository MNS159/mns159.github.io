England survive scare to beat Pakistan by eight wickets in third Test, winning series 3-0

Sports desk

England sealed a 3-0 series victory over Pakistan with an eight-wicket win in the third Test at Edgbaston, getting over a dramatic start on the fourth day that briefly threatened one of Test cricket's greatest turnarounds. Chasing 130 for victory, the home side were shaken when Mohammad Abbas dismissed both openers — Ben Duckett for a golden duck and Emilio Gay leg before wicket — in the first over, leaving England at 2-2.

Captain Joe Root then survived a major let-off on 23, his stumps struck by Mohammad Ali only for the delivery to be overturned as a no-ball after the bowler was found to have overstepped. Root went on to make 62 not out, sharing an unbroken 128-run stand for the third wicket with Jordan Cox, who scored a composed 59. Victory was sealed just before lunch, giving England a clean sweep after a turbulent summer.

Pakistan had rebounded at a disadvantage in the series after being bowled out for 133 on the first day, England responding with 453. The tourists fought back in their second of two innings with 449 — helped by Shan Masood (95), Razaullah (81), Babar Azam (76) and Awais (59) — setting England a target near 130. Debutant Razaullah, one of seven players called into the squad after Pakistan fell to heavy losses in the first two Tests, had earlier taken 4-148 with the ball and then dazzled with the bat on Friday afternoon, hitting 81 from 63 balls to give his side a fighting chance.

Pakistan's series has been marred by crisis: they threatened to abandon the second Test over an interview broadcast by Sky featuring the sons of Imran Khan, replaced head coach Sarfaraz Ahmed with Mike Hesson, and dropped seven players from the squad. Razaullah is seen as a silver lining, but the visitor’s top order failed to cope consistently until the final days.

For England — playing without head coach Brendon McCullum (who returns for a white ball series from Tuesday onwards) and with Kevin Pietersen on the coaching staff — the wintering, Stephen Fleming arrives once series completed, while the healthy increase from Cox adds to the selection puzzle. Root, back as captain after Ben Stokes retired in June, ends the summer on a high note: three Test wins in a row after a previous run of two wins in 10.

England start a white-ball series against Sri Lanka in Southampton on Tuesday, with the transfers. Meanwhile, the pros and cons of an erratic but hard-fought series show England must improve before touring winter games in South Africa, Bangladesh, and Australia.
