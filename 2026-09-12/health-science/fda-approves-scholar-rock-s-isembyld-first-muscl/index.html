FDA approves Scholar Rock's Isembyld, first muscle-targeted therapy for spinal muscular atrophy

Health & Science desk · anchored on Reuters

The U.S. Food and Drug Administration on Friday approved Isembyld (apitegromab-mstn), the first-ever therapy designed to target muscle loss in spinal muscular atrophy (SMA), offering a new treatment option for patients ages 2 and older who are already receiving an SMN2-targeted therapy. The approval, granted to Scholar Rock, marks a shift from slowing disease progression to actively improving muscle function, according to the Muscular Dystrophy Association (MDA).

Isembyld is a monthly infusion that works by inhibiting myostatin, a protein that limits muscle growth. The approval was based on the pivotal Phase 3 SAPPHIRE study, which showed that patients receiving Isembyld in combination with an SMN2-targeting drug experienced statistically significant improvements in motor skills after one year, while those in the placebo group declined. Additional data from the earlier TOPAZ and ONYX studies supported the findings. Scholar Rock CEO David Hallal called the approval a "defining moment for the SMA community," adding that the company had delivered "a therapeutic breakthrough" after decades of failed attempts to harness myostatin inhibition.

SMA is a rare genetic neuromuscular disease and a leading genetic cause of death in infants. Patients often require lifelong support to maintain mobility, breathing, and independence. The approval is specifically for individuals who are already on SMN-enhancing therapies, which target the survival motor neuron 2 (SMN2) gene to protect the neurons that control movement. Isembyld addresses the ongoing need to counteract motor decline even when SMN-targeted treatments are in use.

The Muscular Dystrophy Association, which has invested more than $50 million in SMA research and over $1.2 billion in neuromuscular disease research overall, highlighted its role in supporting the foundational science behind the therapy. Se-Jin Lee, MD, PhD, of the University of Connecticut School of Medicine and The Jackson Laboratory, whose early work on myostatin was funded by MDA, said the organization's investment "enabled me to explore the biology of myostatin and demonstrate that blocking it could profoundly increase muscle growth." MDA Chief Research Officer Angela Lek, PhD, said the approval "is what progress looks like" and reflects the translation of scientific discoveries into disease-modifying drugs.

Families and clinicians responded with optimism. Amanda Stanton, whose son Dash lives with SMA and serves as an MDA Ambassador, said the news "feels like a big step forward, not just for Dash, but for everyone living with SMA." Pediatric neurologist Randal Richardson, MD, MDA Care Center Director at Gillette Children's Hospital in Saint Paul, Minnesota, said the era of merely slowing SMA progression "has evolved to a new one focused on improving function." Scholar Rock plans to hold industry update webinars for families on October 6, 2026, and for clinicians on October 7, 2026.
