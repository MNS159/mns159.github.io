Novartis and Novo Nordisk heart drug failures may chill cardiovascular field

Health & Science desk

Back-to-back failures of cardiovascular drugs from Novartis and Novo Nordisk earlier this month could have a chilling effect across the pharmaceutical industry, according to reports. Just before Labor Day, Novartis announced that its cardiac therapy, pelacarsen, failed in a widely watched, seven-year clinical trial. The drug was designed to reduce rates of heart attack or stroke by lowering lipoprotein a, or Lp(a), a type of lipid that, at elevated concentrations, increases the likelihood of severe heart problems. An estimated 20% of people have abnormally high levels of Lp(a).

Novartis' pelacarsen was the first attempt at a new type of cardiovascular treatment targeting Lp(a). Multiple other drug companies are also pursuing their own Lp(a) medications, investing billions of dollars in the process.
