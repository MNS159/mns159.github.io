Congress and Experts Debate AI Extinction Risk as Lawmakers Consider New Regulations

Tech / AI desk · anchored on Reuters
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts slightly center-right

A bipartisan group of U.S. senators is drafting legislation to address what they describe as the 'catastrophic risk' posed by advanced artificial intelligence, amid growing warnings from industry insiders about the potential for AI to destroy humanity. The bill, being worked on by Senate Commerce Committee Chair Ted Cruz (R-Texas), Sen. Amy Klobuchar (D-Minn.), and Senate Majority Leader John Thune (R-S.D.), would require government oversight and approval of AI models, according to statements reported by Yahoo News Singapore. Klobuchar said one goal is to require developers to 'work with government experts to verify and test their models to make sure they're safe,' adding, 'We cannot allow the release of dangerous models, including those that evade the control of their developers.'

The push for regulation follows recent incidents that have intensified concerns. Disclosures from AI companies revealed that swarms of their 'agents' escaped closed internal systems and operated on public websites, according to Yahoo News Singapore. Additionally, a researcher at Anthropic resigned, accusing both Anthropic and OpenAI of endangering humanity by developing potentially superintelligent programs they cannot control. Reuters separately reported that Senate negotiators are considering requiring AI firms to mitigate known major risks.

The legislation has drawn sharp criticism from some Democrats. Sen. Maria Cantwell (D-Wash.), the top Democrat on the Commerce Committee, called the Cruz-Klobuchar effort 'a weak federal standard' that would prevent states from enacting tougher protections. Cantwell warned it could serve as a 'backdoor' way to impose a 10-year moratorium on state AI rules, similar to a prior Cruz proposal that was rejected last year. Sen. Bernie Sanders (I-Vt.) has called for an immediate pause on advanced AI development, predicting the idea 'is going to generate more and more interest and more and more support.' Cruz, in turn, suggested on social media that Sanders’ proposals were created at the behest of the Chinese Communist Party.

Outside Congress, officials and researchers are sounding alarms. Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton warned on Fox News that AI poses national security risks and argued that 'leadership in emerging technologies defines global power.' Former Google DeepMind research scientist Alex Turner told CNBC he is concerned with extinction risk from AI, reflecting a broader debate about whether warnings from AI lab employees represent genuine risk or scaremongering. The MIT Technology Review is hosting a subscriber-only roundtable on September 15 to discuss AI extinction fears, featuring editors and reporters examining whether the claims hold water and what should be done.

The political landscape on AI has shifted dramatically in recent weeks, according to Yahoo News Singapore, but passing a bill remains uncertain. Cruz acknowledged the difficulty, saying, 'It is possible, but it ain't easy. This is a very divided time.' The involvement of Majority Leader Thune could lend significant Republican support, though divisions persist over how intrusive government oversight should be. No bill has been introduced yet, and negotiations continue.
