AI Safety

Anthropic reports users bypassed safeguards for bioweapons research; BBC confirms attempt blocked

Tech / AI desk

Anthropic, the artificial intelligence company behind the Claude model, said it has identified and disrupted multiple attempts this year by scientists to use its technology for research that could help develop biological weapons. In a threat intelligence report published Thursday, the company detailed five case studies in which actors “circumvented controls” and made efforts to “obfuscate” the purpose of their research to dodge safeguards. The cases involved some users in nations that Anthropic prohibits from accessing its models, including Russia, China, and Iran.

One case involved a researcher from an “unsupported region” who “spent weeks planning” experiments involving avian influenza with Claude. Anthropic said its safety filters restricted the work to its weakest models. The company emphasized it could not be sure the scientists intended to cause harm, noting that the same information needed to create biological weapons could also be used to develop a vaccine. Anthropic said it had banned the accounts mentioned in the report but did not disclose the names of the research institutions or the nations where the incidents took place.

The report also detailed other misuse cases detected between December 2025 and August 2026. Claude was used by actors linked to a Russia-based cyber espionage campaign and by an Iranian propaganda institution. The company listed cases ranging from fake dating apps and hotel Wi-Fi scams to surveillance built to identify dissidents. Anthropic also accused Chinese AI firms of trying to replicate Claude’s capabilities. The report noted six cases where Claude was used to develop software for conventional weapons, including firearms, missiles, armed drones, bombs, and other munitions, as well as targeting and control systems.

Anthropic said none of the misuse cases involved its Claude Fable or powerful Mythos-class models, with the exception of one instance of distillation — the process of training smaller AI models using larger, more expensive models. The company said it had incorporated its findings into its processes “to better prevent, detect, and disrupt these activities in the future” and had shared intelligence with authorities and industry partners where appropriate. Jacob Klein, Anthropic’s head of threat intelligence, told the New York Times the situation was “an incredibly nuanced situation,” adding, “You are not seeing someone in a comic book kind of way say, ‘Hey, I want to build a biological weapon to kill everybody.’”

The warnings have prompted calls for action. In the US, Democratic Senator Bernie Sanders has introduced legislation to ban AI superintelligence and temporarily pause advanced AI development. “When scientists tell you there is a chance, a chance that it could have a cataclysmic impact on humanity, you’ve got be a moron not to say, slow it down,” he said Thursday on BBC’s Newsnight. President Donald Trump has rejected such fears, saying Thursday he was concerned “if we don’t win AI, we’re going to be put in a very bad position.” The report comes after a top safety researcher at Anthropic warned AI is advancing so quickly he believes there is a greater than 10% chance it “could kill all humans” within the next decade.
