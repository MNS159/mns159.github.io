Russian strikes kill at least four in Ukraine as UN warns of toughest winter

International & World desk

Ukraine faces its "toughest winter" since Russia's full-scale invasion began in 2022, a top United Nations official warned, as Russian drone and missile strikes killed at least four people and wounded dozens more across the country over recent days. Alexander De Croo, head of the UN Development Programme and the third most senior UN official, told the BBC that Russia is "very systematically" targeting civilian and energy infrastructure, creating a risk of "humanitarian catastrophe" if power and water supplies fail during winter months when temperatures can drop to -20°C.

In the latest wave of attacks, Russian forces struck two fuel stations in Kyiv on Friday, killing two people and injuring others, according to Mayor Vitali Klitschko. The strikes came a day after similar attacks on fuel stations in the capital, suggesting a new campaign focused on civilian areas. In the Dnipropetrovsk region, five people were killed and 67 wounded in a daytime drone strike on a shopping mall in Pavlohrad on Thursday, Ukrainian officials said. Earlier in the week, strikes on shopping malls in Zaporizhzhia and Sumy killed two people and injured 28. Two more people died when Russian drones hit a border crossing between Ukraine and Moldova.

Russia's Defence Ministry said its forces had struck industrial facilities including the Zaporizhstal steel plant in Zaporizhzhia and an Interpipe Steel complex in Dnipro. The ministry also reported hitting two cargo vessels in the Black Sea port of Chornomorsk. At least two people were killed in strikes that hit the ArcelorMittal plant in Kryvyi Rih, the company announced, adding that production facilities had also been damaged. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy warned that Ukraine would retaliate if Russia targets the national power grid this winter, saying, "Russia knows that if they strike—and they are preparing to strike our energy system in the winter—Russia will receive the same."

De Croo described the task of repairing Ukraine's power network before winter as "almost an impossible task," noting that workers are reconstructing sites under constant air alarms and know they are targets. He said the UN Development Programme is helping rebuild damaged parts of the energy system and establish more decentralized plants less vulnerable to attack. Zelenskyy, speaking at the Yalta European Strategy event, called for international partners to pursue a step-by-step approach toward agreements, including an energy truce to safeguard power systems of both nations and open navigation in the Black Sea.

The UN official's warnings come as both sides continue to deny deliberately targeting civilians. Russia has increasingly used faster, jet-powered drones in around-the-clock attacks, while Ukraine has launched its own deep-drone strikes on Russian oil, gas, and industrial facilities. Zelenskyy warned that the Black Sea blockade severely restricts agricultural exports, comparing the situation to shipping disruptions in the Strait of Hormuz, and stated, "Agriculture and energy are two challenges for the whole world today."
