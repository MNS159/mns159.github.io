Tim Cook Steps Down as Apple CEO, Phil Schiller Exits Top Roles

Tech / AI desk

Apple CEO Tim Cook stepped down from the role on September 1, handing leadership to John Ternus, the company’s senior vice president of hardware engineering. In a farewell memo to staff, Cook said he is “not leaving Apple” but will serve as executive chairman, managing political relationships including those with President Trump and the Chinese government. The transition comes as longtime Apple executive Phil Schiller also departs his posts as head of the App Store and Apple events, a move reported by Bloomberg that sees him retain the title of Apple Fellow while working on unspecified initiatives.

Schiller, who joined Apple in 1987 and became head of marketing in 1997, helped launch the App Store alongside Steve Jobs in 2008 and took over its leadership in 2015. In recent years, he stepped away from his role as senior vice president of marketing, handing it to Greg Joswiak in 2020. As head of the App Store—which facilitated more than $1.4 trillion in transactions last year—Schiller navigated legal battles and government regulations that forced Apple to alter its app distribution and payment policies. Following his departure, the App Store division will move under services head Eddy Cue; Carson Oliver will continue to lead the store with the help of Ann Thai, who oversees app distribution tools and third-party marketplaces.

Cook’s departure is part of a broader executive exodus at Apple that presents a major challenge for new CEO John Ternus. Former chief financial officer Luca Maestri stepped down in January 2025, followed by departures of COO Jeff Williams, design chief Alan Dye, and AI head John Giannandrea. Environmental and policy lead Lisa Jackson retired last year, and general counsel Kate Adams will be replaced by former Meta chief legal officer Jennifer Newstead. Ternus, who led hardware engineering across Apple’s full device lineup, oversaw recent product launches including the ultra-thin iPhone Air, the lower-cost MacBook Neo, and AirPods with hearing health features. His elevation signals a renewed focus on hardware and product execution, with hardware playing a key role in the AI era—Apple this year offered earlier releases of the Mac mini and Mac Studio driven by AI demand.

In his final memo, Cook wrote: “I love this company and the team behind it, and I couldn’t let this day pass without sending a note to you, to tell you how grateful I am … for the privilege of a lifetime serving as your leader.” He emphasized the company’s culture: “We share a belief that what we build matters, and that we have both the opportunity and the responsibility to leave the world better than we found it.” Cook also referenced Steve Jobs’s vision of leaving a “dent in the universe,” saying Apple makes it possible because of “what we value and how we see the world.” On X, Cook wrote: “My title changes tomorrow, but the love I have for the Apple community never will. Thank you for being a constant source of inspiration.” Apple did not immediately respond to a request for comment.
