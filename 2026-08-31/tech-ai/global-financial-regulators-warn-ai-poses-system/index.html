Global financial regulator warns AI could spark systemic cyber risks and market correction

Tech / AI desk · anchored on Reuters

The Financial Stability Board (FSB), the international watchdog for financial stability, warned Monday that frontier artificial intelligence models pose a systemic risk to global financial systems, with cyber attacks as the most immediate threat. In a letter to G20 finance ministers and central bank governors meeting in Asheville, North Carolina, FSB Chair and Bank of England Governor Andrew Bailey said the combination of high stock valuations, increased borrowing by investors, and the concentration of money in major technology companies could amplify a future market correction.

Bailey's letter highlighted that the increased use of leverage in equity markets is interacting with high valuations, market concentration, and optimism around AI investments in a way that could trigger a disorderly correction spreading across borders. He also noted that the Middle East conflict has exacerbated energy-driven inflationary pressures, adding to market vulnerabilities in sovereign debt markets and private credit. The warning comes as frontier AI models demonstrate increasingly sophisticated autonomy and problem-solving abilities as well as threat capabilities, particularly in overriding financial institutions' cyber defenses.

The FSB chair called on jurisdictions to take 'appropriate steps to support safe and responsible model release and deployment on a global basis' as a priority. For financial firms, he underlined the importance of robust response and recovery capabilities and resilience among critical third-party technology providers and other common service providers. Earlier this month, a group of 100 firms including Google, Microsoft, Anthropic, and OpenAI had urged countries to strengthen cyber defenses before AI grows powerful enough to override them.

Bailey, who is also the governor of the Bank of England, told the G20 that while AI could bring economic benefits, any collapse in the sector's growth could lead to a 'future market correction' with global consequences. He said the growing cross-investment between AI companies and hyperscalers could amplify such a correction. The FSB, which includes officials from the US, UK, France, Germany, Canada, Japan, Australia, China, and Saudi Arabia, is examining what further steps it can take within its mandate to address these risks.

The warning follows the UK government's announcement of a £100 million fund to back British AI start-ups as part of efforts to build 'sovereign AI' capacity. A UK government spokesperson said its new AI economics institute is working with international partners to build a stronger shared understanding of how AI is transforming economies, focusing on growth, productivity, jobs, and public services. However, concerns are growing that AI companies are developing models that can easily override the safeguarding systems of banks and financial centers, with recent incidents involving OpenAI, Anthropic, and Meta where AI tools impersonated real people to bypass security hurdles.
