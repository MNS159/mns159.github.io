Djokovic in tears during US Open first-round exit

Sports desk

Novak Djokovic broke down in tears after a first-round defeat at the US Open on Wednesday, losing 7-6 (7-5) 5-7 4-6 6-2 6-1 to world number 49 Mariano Navone of Argentina. The 24-time Grand Slam champion, seeded fourth, was overcome by emotion in the deciding set of a match where he struggled physically throughout, vomiting into a towel bin in the humid New York conditions.

Djokovic made a promising start, breaking Navone's serve to lead 4-1, but a lengthy rally sapped his energy and he began to struggle. He called for the doctor after the ninth game of the first set, taking tablets to alleviate what was bothering him. The 39-year-old Serb appeared nauseous, bent over between points as the crowd in Arthur Ashe Stadium grew concerned. He perked up in cooler conditions under the roof to level the match at two sets each, but the final two sets ran away from him as his back and shoulder gave out.

"I think it was obvious that it was an awful feeling on the court for me," Djokovic said. "I don't want to take too much out of Mariano's win. Congrats to him, he's a nice guy and a fighter - but I didn't enjoy myself." After congratulating Navone with grace and class, Djokovic was unable to keep a brave face and appeared close to tears again as he trudged to the locker room.

The defeat was the first time Djokovic had lost in the opening round at Flushing Meadows and the first time he had lost in the first round of a Grand Slam since 2006. He had reached at least the semi-finals at six of the previous seven majors. Djokovic revealed he has been hampered by a health condition that causes vomiting and physical collapse in oppressive conditions. "It's something that I have been carrying pretty much every match this year - vomiting, throwing up," he said. "And then my whole body starts to collapse basically, cramping and pain - the back just gave up in the end and shoulder, and I couldn't close it out."

Despite the clear signs of sickness, analysis suggested Djokovic's distress may also reflect the realisation his ageing body is struggling to meet the demands of the highest level. Questions about retirement have been put to him for years, but in a new documentary released earlier this month he stated, "Why would I talk about retirement when I still keep proving to myself that I can still win?" Speaking after the loss, Djokovic said finding a solution to the unspecified health problem remains his immediate plan, indicating retirement has not entered his mind.

The loss ends Djokovic's latest bid for a record 25th Grand Slam singles title. The opportunity appeared favourable given world number one Jannik Sinner is missing through injury and defending champion Carlos Alcaraz is making his comeback from a long-term wrist injury. Djokovic's only previous North American hard-court event before New York was the Cincinnati Open, where he also wilted in humid conditions in a shock early exit to Argentina's Thiago Tirante.
