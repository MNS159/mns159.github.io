Lionel Messi announces retirement from Argentina national team after 20-year career

Sports desk

Lionel Messi officially announced his retirement from the Argentina national team on Monday, closing a two-decade international career that made him the country's all-time leader in appearances (207) and goals (125). The decision, posted on his social media accounts, comes roughly six weeks after Argentina's 1-0 extra-time loss to Spain in the 2026 World Cup final — his final match in a World Cup — and just over three weeks after his father and longtime agent, Jorge Messi, died on Aug. 8 at age 68.

In his statement, Messi wrote that he made the decision on July 21, two days after the World Cup final, and that his father's death left him "even more convinced of this decision than before." He said, "It was a decision that hurt and still hurts deep inside, but I understand that this is the right moment." Messi added, "I swear I always gave everything, not only in these last few years when we won everything, but before that too. I always fought and gave everything for this shirt, to bring you happiness and make you feel proud to be Argentine through football." He also cited the emergence of "great young players" as a reason for stepping away.

Messi's international career was defined by early frustration and late-career glory. After making his senior debut in 2005 at age 18, he lost three Copa America finals (2007, 2015, 2016) and the 2014 World Cup final to Germany. The 2016 Copa America defeat on penalties to Chile prompted a brief retirement that he reversed weeks later after an outpouring of support from fans. The breakthrough came in 2021 when he captained Argentina to the Copa America title with a 1-0 win over Brazil at the Maracanã, followed by the 2022 World Cup title in Qatar — Argentina's first since 1986 — where Messi scored 21 World Cup goals in his career, second all-time. He added a second consecutive Copa America in 2024 and returned to the World Cup final in 2026, extending his legacy as the national team's most decorated player and arguably the greatest of all time.

Messi's retirement leaves the Argentina national team without its iconic figure, but he will continue playing for Inter Miami in MLS, where he is under contract through 2028. It is unclear whether he will play a farewell match for Argentina, though his statement included, "I leave with peace and pride, knowing that together with my teammates we gave you moments we once dreamed of." The decision ends one of the most storied chapters in international soccer, transforming a career once criticized for lacking national-team silverware into a narrative of perseverance and ultimate triumph.
