FDA Approves Daraxonrasib as First RAS Inhibitor for Pancreatic Cancer; Stock Flat Despite Historic Approval

Health & Science desk

The U.S. Food and Drug Administration has approved Revolution Medicines' oral therapy Rasonque (daraxonrasib) for adults with metastatic pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) who have received at least one prior systemic therapy or who are not candidates for multiagent systemic therapy. The approval is historic as daraxonrasib is the first RAS inhibitor authorized as a targeted treatment for the most common form of pancreatic cancer. Despite the milestone, Revolution's shares remained relatively flat in the days following the announcement, closing the week at $207.88 after a 6% drop on Friday, as investors weighed the drug's pricing and market potential.

The FDA granted approval 6.5 months ahead of its March 11, 2027, target decision date under Priority Review, following Breakthrough Therapy and Orphan Drug designations. The decision was based on data from the Phase III RASolute 302 trial, in which daraxonrasib showed a median overall survival of 13.2 months, nearly double the 6.7 months seen with standard chemotherapy. "This drug showed unprecedented results in an area of high unmet need," said Angelo de Claro, MD, director of the FDA's Oncology Center of Excellence. Revolution has set a wholesale acquisition cost of $39,800 per month, which translates to $477,600 per year before discounts—more than double the list price of a year's supply of Keytruda. Prior to approval, the company provided the drug at no cost to more than 2,000 patients through an expanded access program.

Pancreatic cancer is among the most difficult to treat, with an overall five-year survival rate of 13%, according to the American Cancer Society. Revolution estimates that about 55,000 patients are diagnosed annually with de novo or recurrent PDAC, of whom roughly 19,000 proceed to second-line treatment. The company has additional Phase III trials ongoing in earlier-line PDAC, non-small cell lung cancer, and colorectal cancer, with data expected in the coming years.

Analysts offered a range of sales projections and price target adjustments. Truist Securities projected third-quarter sales of $12.7 million, rising to $101.7 million by year-end, and $2.3 billion by 2028. RBC Capital Markets forecast $28 million in third-quarter sales and peak sales of approximately $11.5 billion. Jefferies projected $281 million for 2026. Several analysts raised their price targets on Revolution shares, including Evercore ISI to $320, H.C. Wainwright to $280, and Oppenheimer to $260, all maintaining positive ratings. The label's breadth allows flexibility beyond second-line use, as noted by Jefferies analyst Maury Raycroft, because physicians can prescribe Rasonque to untreated patients deemed ineligible for multiagent therapy.

In other biotechnology news, Biohaven shares jumped 18% after granting SK Biopharmaceuticals an exclusive worldwide license to its Kv7 ion channel platform, led by opakalim, for up to $795 million. BioXcel Therapeutics shares plunged 75% after the company and two subsidiaries filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection, with a planned sale of substantially all assets to a subsidiary of Teva Pharmaceutical Industries.
