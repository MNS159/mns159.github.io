FDA expands Mounjaro approval to reduce heart attack, stroke risk in high-risk diabetes patients

Health & Science desk

The U.S. Food and Drug Administration has approved a new use for the diabetes drug Mounjaro, allowing its prescription to help prevent heart attacks and strokes in high-risk adults with type 2 diabetes, according to a report from CNN. The decision, announced Aug. 30, 2026, expands the indication for the medication, which is manufactured by Eli Lilly.

Eli Lilly said Mounjaro reduced major cardiovascular events by an additional 8% compared to its older diabetes drug, Trulicity. The precise mechanism behind the benefit was not detailed in the announcement, but the new approval targets a population already at elevated risk for cardiovascular complications due to their diabetes.

Mounjaro, known generically as tirzepatide, is also prescribed for weight loss under the brand name Zepbound. This dual use reflects the drug's ability to target multiple metabolic pathways, though the FDA's latest clearance is specifically for cardiovascular risk reduction in patients with type 2 diabetes.

Eli Lilly researchers are now testing newer drugs that target even more hormones, according to the company. No timeline for those trials was provided. The expanded approval marks a significant step in using diabetes medications to address heart disease, a leading cause of death among people with type 2 diabetes.
