US and Iran resume strikes; Trump issues new threats

Politics desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum
✓ Facts
  • The US struck two rocket launchers on Larak Island in the Strait of Hormuz on Sunday; US Central Command said the launchers were being prepared to deploy mines and rockets into the strait, and this was the first known US strike on Iran since Trump paused a renewed military campaign in late July.
  • Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) said the Larak Island strike killed two people and injured two others, and stated Iran retaliated with ballistic missile strikes on US bases at King Hussein and al-Azraq in Jordan; Jordan's army said it intercepted eight missiles.
  • Iranian state TV said Iran's military targeted an air base in the UAE with drones; the UAE's Ministry of Defence called the claim 'unfounded' but confirmed it shot down one drone.
  • President Trump posted apparent AI-generated videos on Truth Social depicting Iranian oil infrastructure exploding, including one captioned 'Kharg Island being blown to smithereens!!!,' and told Fox News the US would 'hit them hard' in response to Iran's retaliation.
  • Centcom Commander Adm. Brad Cooper said US forces — using Navy divers, Navy SEALs and air power — spent months clearing Iranian sea mines from the Strait of Hormuz's main international shipping lanes; officials told Axios that of more than 200 mine-like objects found, 11 were confirmed mines.
  • Oil flow through the Strait of Hormuz stood at about 5 million barrels per day as of Monday, down from more than 20 million barrels daily before the conflict began on Feb. 28, according to Reuters/Vortexa tracking data cited by Fox News.
  • Separately, Amnesty International USA said the US military's air strike campaign against boats in the Caribbean and Eastern Pacific, which began Sept. 2, 2025, has resulted in 227 deaths announced by administration officials as of its statement, including four deaths reported six days earlier.
○ Analysis

The renewed exchange of strikes marks an escalation in a conflict that began Feb. 28, when the US and Israel launched strikes on Iran and Iran retaliated against Israel, US bases and Gulf allies, effectively closing the Strait of Hormuz — a waterway through which roughly a fifth of the world's oil and gas once passed. Trump paused the US military campaign in late July, but Sunday's strike on Larak Island, followed by Iranian retaliation against US bases in Jordan and a disputed drone incident near the UAE, shows the pause has not held. The episode is unfolding alongside a broader US pressure campaign — new sanctions announced Aug. 24, a naval blockade, and a months-long, only recently disclosed operation to clear Iranian sea mines from shipping lanes — even as maritime authorities keep the strait at a 'severe' threat level and oil traffic remains far below prewar levels. The Amnesty International item, while concerning a geographically and legally separate US military operation against alleged drug-smuggling boats, is included here because it reflects a parallel debate playing out in Washington about congressional oversight, evidence, and the legality of US uses of lethal force abroad during the same period.

◐ Debate

Democratic

  • Amnesty International USA's Amanda Klasing argues the year-long US strikes on Caribbean and Pacific boats constitute illegal extrajudicial killings, noting authorities have not named victims or produced evidence, and calls for Congress to investigate and hold Trump, Rubio and Hegseth accountable — a framework of concern about executive overreach and lack of transparency that could extend, in the eyes of critics, to unilateral escalation with Iran announced via a Truth Social video rather than through Congress.
  • Critics in this camp would likely question the evidentiary basis and provocative nature of Trump posting an AI-generated video threatening to destroy Kharg Island, arguing that conducting strategic signaling this way — as Vance described it — bypasses normal deliberation and risks further escalation without public accountability.

Republican

  • Vice President Vance framed Trump's AI-generated Kharg Island video as a deliberate message to Iran, distinguishing it from an actual military announcement, and defending the administration's approach of signaling resolve without disclosing operational plans.
  • CENTCOM's Adm. Brad Cooper and administration officials frame the Larak Island strike and the broader mine-clearance and blockade operations as necessary, successful actions to protect international shipping and deter Iranian aggression, citing the assistance of nearly 1,500 commercial vessels and interdiction of 82 vessels attempting to breach the blockade as evidence of effective policy.
  • Trump's pledge to 'hit them hard' in response to Iran's strikes on US bases in Jordan reflects a position that decisive retaliation is warranted given Iran's direct attack on American military assets.
Steelman

Across both the Iran conflict and the separate Caribbean strike campaign, the central cross-cutting tension is the same: the US public is being asked to accept significant, lethal military actions — strikes on Iranian territory, mine-clearance operations, and boat strikes killing over 225 people — largely on the basis of government assertions (Centcom statements, Truth Social videos, unnamed casualty counts) that are difficult for outside observers to independently verify, regardless of whether one supports the underlying policies.

Implications

Continued tit-for-tat strikes raise the risk of further escalation between the US and Iran, with direct consequences for global oil markets and shipping through the Strait of Hormuz, where traffic remains far below prewar levels despite the US mine-clearance effort. Watch for whether Iran follows through on threatened further retaliation, whether Congress responds to Amnesty International's call for investigation into the separate Caribbean strike campaign, and whether either military operation prompts new congressional action on war powers or oversight.
