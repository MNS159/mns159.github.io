US and Iran exchange strikes, breaking monthlong pause in hostilities

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts slightly center-left

The United States and Iran traded direct military strikes on Sunday, ending a nearly monthlong pause in hostilities. The U.S. military said it targeted two Iranian rocket launchers on Larak Island in the Strait of Hormuz that were preparing to fire sea mines into the strategic waterway, according to a statement sent to NPR. Iran retaliated by launching ballistic missiles at U.S. forces at Al-Minhad Air Base in the United Arab Emirates and at bases in Jordan, though no missiles reached U.S. positions. Jordan’s armed forces said it shot down eight missiles that entered its airspace. The UAE reported intercepting drones launched from Iran over its territorial waters.

The U.S. strikes killed military personnel and civilians on Larak Island, according to Iranian armed forces. The island overlooks shipping lanes near the port of Bandar Abbas. Iran’s retaliation targeted the Jordanian bases and aircraft that Tehran said had supported the U.S. attack. Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian said Iran would respond decisively to any aggression but was not seeking war. “We will never remain silent in the face of any aggression and will respond decisively to the aggressors,” state media quoted him as saying.

The flare-up followed weeks of heightened tensions and new U.S. economic sanctions against Iran. On Sunday night, President Donald Trump posted an AI-generated video on his Truth Social account showing missiles striking Iran’s Kharg Island, where about 90 percent of Iran’s oil exports are processed. Vice President JD Vance said the video was meant as a “message” to Iran, warning of consequences if it continues threatening commercial shipping in the Strait of Hormuz. “You guys are shooting at commercial shipping. You guys are behaving like a terrorist country. You’ve got to stop or there are going to be some consequences,” Vance told reporters at an Air Force base in Maryland.

On Monday, Trump followed up with a social media post declaring Iran “a Failed Nation” and “Dead,” and said the only things preventing the regime’s collapse are “FAKE NEWS” and a willingness to kill protesters, whom he accused of committing “war crimes against humanity.” He alleged Iran had killed over 100,000 protesters, a figure not verified; Iranian authorities put the death toll at 3,117, including security forces. Trump also told Fox News, “We’re going to hit them hard. There will be a response” to the Iranian attack on U.S. forces in Jordan. However, a U.S. official said the military had not targeted Kharg Island in the overnight strikes.

The exchanges are the first direct fighting between the two countries since July 29, when Trump ordered a halt to attacks after talks with Gulf leaders. The U.S. and Israel launched a broader war on Iran on February 28, aiming to topple the Iranian regime, though Iran’s government has remained intact. The U.S. has since shifted focus to reopening the Strait of Hormuz, a narrow chokepoint for oil traffic that has caused global energy prices to spike. U.S. Central Command commander Adm. Brad Cooper said last week that U.S. forces had completed clearing naval mines from the strait’s international routes. Iran, however, reported that a supertanker had struck two mines over the weekend, and the Revolutionary Guard warned it would target other vessels violating Iran’s navigation restrictions.

The continued risk of confrontation underscores the absence of a permanent ceasefire. Meanwhile, an Iranian oil official ridiculed Trump’s video threat against Kharg Island, saying conditions there were calm and workers were repairing damage from earlier attacks. The last direct U.S. strike on Iran before Sunday occurred on July 29. Gulf states including the UAE, Kuwait, Bahrain, and Saudi Arabia, which host U.S. military personnel and installations, have been repeatedly targeted by Iran since the conflict began.
