Nepal-Tibet flood death toll nears 1,000 as rescuers race to reach trapped workers

International & World desk

The death toll from catastrophic floods along the Nepal-Tibet border has reached 939 in Nepal, with 16 additional deaths confirmed in Tibet, bringing the known regional total to 955, according to disaster authorities. Nepal’s disaster management agency reported 3,925 people missing nationwide, including 592 foreign nationals, as rescue operations entered their sixth day. In Tibet, Chinese state media listed 546 missing. The disaster has devastated hydropower projects along the Trishuli River, with at least 11 completed or under-construction plants damaged. Rescuers believe more than 600 workers remain trapped in mud-filled tunnels at the Trishuli-3A and Upper Trishuli-1 sites, while the number of unaccounted-for hydropower staff was revised down to 639 on Monday from an earlier count of 933.

Rescue teams, comprising Nepalese and Chinese military personnel along with experts from India, Korea, and Australia, have been using explosives to blast into the hillside and pumping air into tunnels in an effort to reach survivors. A team entered one tunnel on Monday but withdrew after finding no one. Australian tunnel expert Arnold Dix, advising remotely, described the terrain as resembling ‘a lunar landscape’ with ‘boulders bigger than houses’. ‘The actual signature of them has gone – everything is just missing,’ he said, though he expressed hope that the subterranean environment may have protected those inside from the deluge. Nepalese Prime Minister Balendra Shah said 279 people had been rescued from hydropower sites so far, and 11,379 people overall have been rescued across affected areas.

Family members of the trapped workers are growing desperate. ‘I wish we could find him. I wish he would come home,’ said Anushila Bhandari, wife of missing engineer Aurus Bhandari. Sita Pandey, whose husband was working at a plant, told the Nepalese daily Kantipur: ‘I still have a 50% hope that he is alive because there is an oxygen system inside.’ Jiban Khadka, whose relative is trapped, described the operation as ‘frustratingly slow’ and said ‘every second is meaningful to us’. Priya Kharel, whose brother Milan was an engineer at Upper Trishuli-1, said witnesses reported people inside the tunnel, adding: ‘There is hope that if the army reaches them quickly, they can be saved.’

The floods struck just days before the anniversary of the Gen Z-led protests that swept Shah, a 36-year-old former rapper, to power a year ago, and his young government now faces its first major national crisis. Information Minister Bikram Timilsina acknowledged the administration was ‘a bit confused’ on the first day, but said efforts had since intensified. Former foreign secretary Madhu Raman Acharya praised security agencies for their response, but former National Reconstruction Authority chief Govinda Pokharel criticized the government for not adequately involving local authorities and for hesitating to accept international tunnel rescue assistance from China and India, costing three crucial days. In the worst-hit districts, Nuwakot has 1,158 missing and Rasuwa 865 missing. The missing also include 45 army soldiers, 38 police officers, and 18 customs and immigration officials.

Far downstream in Chitwan district, authorities conducted makeshift burials after nearly 300 bodies washed onto riverbanks. ‘We don’t have enough freezers here… We had to choose this last alternative of temporary burial system. We are really overwhelmed,’ said district chief Ganesh Aryaal, adding that relatives could later retrieve remains after DNA testing. In India’s Uttar Pradesh state, authorities recovered 17 bodies from rivers flowing from Nepal, believed to be flood victims, though they are not yet in the official toll. Meanwhile, Nepalese and Chinese authorities have begun discussing a data-sharing mechanism to identify potential flood risks, Nepal’s Foreign Minister Shishir Khanal told Al Jazeera. Chinese authorities have imposed ‘administrative penalties’ on people speculating online about higher casualty numbers, and Nepal has also ordered the removal of content deemed misinformation.
