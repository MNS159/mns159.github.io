Over 6,000 Ebola cases confirmed in DRC as death toll nears 3,000

International & World desk

The Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of Congo has surpassed previous surges to become the worst the country has experienced, with new data confirming over 6,000 confirmed cases and nearly 3,000 deaths. The DRC Ministry of Communications reported on social media that 6,041 cases have been confirmed, including 1,366 recoveries and 2,911 deaths, with 619 patients currently in isolation or hospitalized.

United Nations Secretary-General Antonio Guterres described the current outbreak as “the fastest-spreading Ebola epidemic ever recorded,” as infections are outpacing the ability of frontline workers to track and respond. According to the Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention, many new infections are occurring among people not monitored through contact tracing. No approved vaccine or treatment exists for the Bundibugyo strain of the virus that is spreading rapidly.

Cases are concentrated in the eastern provinces, where densely populated areas face insecurity from armed conflict, displacement, and pre-existing humanitarian challenges. Medical charity Doctors Without Borders noted that existing health needs, along with airport closures and flight restrictions, are disrupting the flow of staff, diagnostics, referrals, and supply chains. In early August, frontline healthcare workers went on strike to protest unpaid wages and dire working conditions.

Neighbouring Uganda was declared free of the Ebola virus on August 28 after reporting no new cases for 42 days. The ongoing outbreak in the DRC continues to strain resources and response efforts amid the complex emergency.
