Russia fires nearly 1,500 drones at Ukraine in four days, threatens 'massive' energy strikes

International & World desk

Russia has launched an intensified aerial campaign against Ukraine, firing nearly 1,500 drones in four days—more than half of them jet-powered—and threatening 'massive strikes' on Ukraine's energy infrastructure, according to Ukrainian and Russian officials. The attacks have pummeled Kyiv and surrounding regions for a fifth consecutive day, damaging warehouses, a retail outlet and residential buildings, and disrupting food logistics. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said between Thursday and Sunday, Russia deployed roughly 1,500 drones, with around 800 being jet-propelled, adding that Moscow's tactic is 'to wear down Ukraine and Ukrainians.'

The drone barrages have rattled daily life in the capital. Almost constant air raid alerts have disrupted commutes and metro service, sending residents into shelters even during daylight hours. Repeated strikes on food distribution centers and retail hubs have left some supermarket shelves bare, with TSN reporting shortages of bread, potatoes, apples and oatmeal at some NOVUS locations, and supermarket chain ATB imposing purchase limits. Ukrainian Agriculture Minister Taras Vysotskyi said roughly 90% of the food logistics infrastructure operated by retail chains had been destroyed, but stressed that retailers would shift logistics and use alternative methods, and that Ukrainians 'will not be left without food.' Zelenskyy adviser Serhii Beskrestnov urged the public not to give in to rumors or panic, adding that European countries could fill any shortfall.

Ukraine is struggling to counter the fast-flying jet-powered drones, which are harder to intercept. Zelenskyy said four Ukrainian companies have developed interceptor drones, with one company having 'the most successful technology.' The Russian Defense Ministry said Monday it shot down 239 Ukrainian drones overnight over 14 regions, occupied Crimea and the Black Sea. At the same time, Russia's Defense Ministry announced it is preparing 'massive strikes' on Ukraine's energy infrastructure, framing the planned attacks as retaliation for continued Ukrainian strikes against Russian energy sites. The warning amplifies fears of another prolonged assault on Ukraine's power grid during the approaching winter, following a devastating strike on a warehouse near Kyiv on Friday that killed at least 38 people—the deadliest single Russian attack this year. Zelenskyy said the strike hit an ammunition storage facility in the village of Myla, triggering fires and explosions, and that an investigation had been launched.

Ukraine, meanwhile, has expanded its own campaign against Russian economic infrastructure, targeting oil facilities and warehouses linked to major online retailers such as Wildberries and Ozon. A Ukrainian missile attack Sunday evening on the Russian border city of Belgorod killed three people, according to acting regional governor Alexander Shuvayev. Retired Air Force Lt. Gen. Richard Newton told Fox News Digital that 'the momentum has swung in Ukraine's favor over the last year or so' due to its expanding use of long-range drones, but cautioned that Ukraine faces a critical vulnerability: a shortage of U.S.-made Patriot air defense interceptors, which are also in high demand elsewhere.

Behind the escalation, new reporting suggests Russian President Vladimir Putin fears how the war might end. The Economist reported that Putin has told people around him that 'they will hang me' if the war ends without Russia achieving control of all of Donbas, its stated minimum objective. Dissatisfaction with the war is growing among ordinary Russians, and among parts of the elite the mood is even more pessimistic, with one source arguing that nearly five years consumed by the conflict represents a failure regardless of its outcome. Newton argued that the intensifying confrontation makes NATO cohesion critical: 'A weakened NATO emboldens Putin and Russia not only against Ukraine but the entire alliance.' As schools in Ukraine are due to resume Tuesday, it remains unclear whether parents will send children to classrooms, though schools have air raid shelters and can hold classes online.
