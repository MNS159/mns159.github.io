Modi urges Putin to end Ukraine war as SCO summit highlights Russia's diplomatic strains

International & World desk · anchored on Reuters

Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi told Russian President Vladimir Putin that the war in Ukraine must end for humanity's sake, according to a Reuters report, as the two leaders met on the sidelines of the Shanghai Cooperation Organisation (SCO) summit in Kyrgyzstan's capital, Bishkek. The meeting comes against the backdrop of the fallout from the US-Israel war on Iran, which has disrupted global energy markets, and Russia's war in Ukraine, now in its fifth year.

The SCO is a bloc often seen as a counterweight to US influence, but India has resisted efforts to turn it into an anti-Western bloc, maintaining relationships with competing powers. India's position is under greater pressure as Russia remains under Western sanctions over the war in Ukraine, while India has faced US pressure over its purchases of Russian oil, alongside wider trade tensions. Former Indian ambassador to the US Meera Shankar told DW that India's relationship with Russia 'is not directed against any country but intended to help India meet its national goals.'

Energy has become the most visible change in the India-Russia relationship. Before Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022, Russia supplied only a small share of India's crude imports; discounted Russian oil rapidly made Moscow one of India's biggest suppliers. The disruption to traditional energy supplies has become more complicated this year due to the US-Israel war on Iran and disruption of traffic through the Strait of Hormuz, which have affected India's purchases from the Gulf and pushed up prices. Shankar said India is responding by diversifying its sources of supply, including both Russia and the US.

On defense, India's dependence on Russian equipment has been declining. The Ukraine war has raised questions over Russia's production capacity, delivery schedules and ability to supply spare parts. Aravind Yelery, an associate professor at Jawaharlal Nehru University, told DW that India 'is increasingly reluctant to commit to Russia's fifth-generation fighter program and has little appetite for new Russian hardware unless it can be incorporated into India's indigenous production plans.' While India has diversified its purchases, the terms are changing, with India looking for a relationship that contributes to its own defense production rather than simply buying finished systems.

Russia's growing dependence on China has also become an unavoidable part of the equation. Since the full-scale invasion of Ukraine began, China has become indispensable to Russia as a market, supplier and economic partner, reducing Moscow's ability to serve as a counterweight to Beijing. Strategic affairs expert C Raja Mohan told DW that 'Russia is no longer in a position to help India balance China,' and that India's emphasis is increasingly on specific interests—energy security, defense supplies, trade and access to technology—while its larger strategic weight rests on relationships with the US and its allies. Mohan described this as an 'America plus' strategy.

Rajan Kumar, a professor of Russian studies at Jawaharlal Nehru University, told DW that Putin wants reassurance that India will remain engaged despite pressure from Washington. Kumar said the meeting is likely about 'continuation of existing ties amid US pressure.' Energy trade and payment mechanisms, ways to increase Indian exports to Russia, defense cooperation and assessments of the conflicts in Ukraine and Iran are expected to remain important. The bigger test will come in Delhi in September, when Putin is expected to attend the BRICS summit. Separately, Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov claimed that Putin and Chinese leader Xi Jinping barely discussed Russia's war against Ukraine at the summit, saying it came up 'as a refrain, in passing,' according to Ukraine's Ukrainska Pravda.
