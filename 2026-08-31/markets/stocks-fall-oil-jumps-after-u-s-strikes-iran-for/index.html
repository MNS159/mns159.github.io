Stocks fall, oil jumps after U.S. strikes Iran for first time in weeks

Financial & Markets desk

U.S. stocks fell across the board Monday after the U.S. attacked Iranian rocket launchers near the Strait of Hormuz for the first time in weeks, pushing oil prices above $88 a barrel and reinforcing bets that the Federal Reserve will raise interest rates at its September meeting. The Dow Jones Industrial Average slipped 0.5%, the S&P 500 lost 0.4%, and the Nasdaq Composite fell 0.3% in early trading, though all three major indexes remained on track for monthly gains.

The renewed hostilities lifted Brent crude futures above $88 per barrel, while U.S. Treasury yields spiked: the 10-year yield rose 4 basis points to 4.76%, its highest intraday level since January 2025, and the 30-year yield climbed 5 basis points to 5.26%. The moves reflected growing concern that higher energy costs will feed into above-target inflation, particularly after Fed Chairman Kevin Warsh said in a Jackson Hole speech Friday that inflation is "running too hot" and the central bank has "work to do."

Over the weekend, traders increased bets on a 25-basis-point rate hike at the Fed's September meeting to 62%, up from roughly 40% a week ago. Investors are now looking ahead to the Labor Department's monthly jobs report on Friday and the consumer price index data next week for further clues on the economy's trajectory.

In corporate news, Nvidia said it would invest $3.5 billion in Taiwanese chipmaker MediaTek, deepening their collaboration on AI computing platforms. GameStop shares jumped about 3% after the video game retailer said it expects second-quarter net income of $290 million to $310 million, partly due to a $238 million gain from converting its eBay derivative position into 43.4 million shares of eBay common stock worth more than $4.9 billion.

Apple CEO Tim Cook stepped down Monday, turning over the role to senior vice president of hardware engineering John Ternus. Cook will stay on as executive chair. During his tenure, Apple's market value increased by roughly $4 trillion, and the company launched products including the Apple Watch, AirPods, and MacBook Neo, while growing its Services segment to over $100 billion in annual revenue.

The CBOE Volatility Index fell to 14.13 on Friday, its lowest level of 2026, but historically it tends to rise through September and early October. Separately, Bank of England Governor Andrew Bailey warned G20 officials that frontier AI models, such as those from OpenAI and Anthropic, pose a growing cyber threat to the global financial system, arguing that the risks "will not respect national borders" and could "undermine market confidence system-wide."
