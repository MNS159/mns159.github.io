Tim Cook Steps Down as Apple CEO After 15-Year Tenure; Stock Soared 2,279%

Financial & Markets desk

Tim Cook’s 15-year tenure as Apple CEO ended Monday, capping one of the most profitable runs in corporate history. Cook, 65, handed the role to hardware chief John Ternus and will become executive chair, a transition Apple announced in April. Under Cook, Apple’s market capitalization surged from $347 billion to more than $4.6 trillion, and a $1,000 investment in Apple stock on the day he became CEO would be worth about $23,800 today—roughly 3.6 times the return of the S&P 500 over the same period.

When Steve Jobs named Cook as his successor in August 2011, investors were initially skeptical. Cook, known for his supply-chain expertise, took over on Aug. 24, 2011, when Apple shares traded at a split-adjusted $13.44. Shares closed Friday at $319.70, a 2,279% gain versus the S&P 500’s 555% return. The stock hit a record close of $339.79 on July 28, 2026, and reclaimed the No. 2 spot by market value behind Nvidia. In 2026, Apple shares are up 15.3%, outpacing the S&P 500’s 12.1% gain.

Cook built on Jobs’ legacy by adding the Apple Watch, AirPods, Apple Pay, the Vision Pro, and a services business—including the App Store, iCloud, Apple Music, Apple TV+, and advertising—that generated $109 billion in fiscal 2025. Revenue quadrupled from $108 billion in 2011 to $416 billion in 2025, with iPhone sales rising from $47 billion to $210 billion. The iPhone still accounts for just over half of Apple’s sales, up from 43% when Cook took over.

Among the Magnificent 7 tech stocks, only Microsoft (up about 2,000%) and Meta (up about 1,400%) have posted smaller gains than Apple during Cook’s tenure. Warren Buffett’s Berkshire Hathaway, which first bought Apple shares in 2016, built a stake valued at more than $170 billion by 2023 and still holds Apple as its single largest holding, though it has gradually sold shares in recent years. Apple makes up more than 6% of the S&P 500, meaning most American investors own it through index funds and 401(k)s.

New CEO John Ternus inherits a cash machine, but investors are watching whether Apple can deliver exciting new products and address concerns that it trails Big Tech peers on artificial intelligence. Separately, Nvidia announced Monday it will invest $3.5 billion in Taiwanese chipmaker MediaTek to deepen their collaboration on AI computing platforms, including Nvidia’s NVLink Fusion technology. Nvidia shares rose about 1% on the news.
