AI capex surge continues: Nvidia stellar Q2 and Goldman warns of 'shocker'

Financial & Markets desk

The artificial intelligence capital expenditure boom shows no signs of abating, with Goldman Sachs warning investors to expect eye-popping spending from Big Tech for years to come, even as Nvidia’s blowout second-quarter earnings underscored the persistent supply-demand imbalance in AI chips. Goldman Sachs co-head of TMT research Eric Sheridan said on Yahoo Finance’s Opening Bid that supply and demand for AI are unlikely to find balance until the first half of 2028, meaning more capex, more revenue growth, and continued constraints on the supply chain. “Memory pricing is going up when the food chain is as constrained as it is. Pricing on chips are higher today than they were six and 12 months ago,” Sheridan said, adding that input costs are rising and companies are racing to acquire land and build data center shells years in advance.

The second-quarter earnings season provided a taste of the scale. Alphabet reported capital expenditures of $44.9 billion, slightly above Wall Street’s $44.7 billion forecast, and raised its full-year capex guidance to $195 billion–$205 billion from $180 billion–$190 billion, with executives flagging a “significant” increase for 2027. Tesla said it will commit $25 billion in capital expenditures for 2026, roughly three times its historical spending, with a further significant increase expected in 2027 as Elon Musk ramps up Optimus and robotaxi production. SpaceX’s second-quarter capex came in at $18.4 billion, well above analyst estimates of around $6 billion, and commentary from executives implied third- and fourth-quarter spending could remain at similar levels, pushing full-year capex to about $65 billion versus Wall Street’s $50 billion forecast. JPMorgan has warned that SpaceX could allocate $200 billion to AI spending in each of the next two years.

Nvidia’s second-quarter results reinforced the demand narrative. Revenue more than doubled, clearing analyst forecasts by the widest margin in two years, and the company projected 70% revenue growth for its fiscal 2028 year. CEO Jensen Huang said demand for the company’s products exceeds its ability to supply them. LPL Financial chief technical strategist Adam Turnquist noted that Nvidia’s forward sales guidance extends to 2028, something the company has not done in quite a while. “There’s massive amount of demand, not enough supply,” Turnquist said, adding that the AI story is continuing and there is no evidence the supply-demand imbalance is coming into balance. Nvidia stock climbed roughly 7% on Thursday following the report, lifting chip stocks broadly and pushing major indexes higher, before pulling back on Friday.

The spending spree has raised questions about whether it is fully priced into Big Tech stocks. Several companies, including Tesla, saw their stock prices hammered this past earnings season on spending concerns. Turnquist acknowledged the possibility of a temporary cooling-off period but cautioned against concluding that the earnings story is stalling. He also pointed to a notable trend change in the software sector, where AI disruption fears may be overdone, citing Salesforce’s strong earnings and low customer attrition as evidence of upside potential. “If you stack up software versus semiconductors, there’s been a notable trend change off very depressed levels,” he said, suggesting plenty of runway for software outperformance within the tech sector.

Cathie Wood’s ARK Invest bought $53 million worth of Nvidia stock after the earnings-driven pullback, continuing a pattern of adding to Nvidia holdings while trimming its position in Advanced Micro Devices. ARK sold 156,286 AMD shares on Friday as well. Nvidia stock was up 0.5% at $218.70 in premarket trading Monday. On a forward earnings basis, Nvidia is priced at 16.7 times expected profits, a discount to the S&P 500’s 19.7 times multiple, according to Barron’s, citing FactSet data. The purchase fits a pattern over recent months of Wood adding to Nvidia holdings while paring back AMD exposure.
