Politics National Security

US and Iran resume strikes; Trump issues new threats

Politics desk

The US struck two rocket launchers on Larak Island in the Strait of Hormuz on Sunday; US Central Command said the launchers were being prepared to deploy mines and rockets into the strait, and this was the first known US strike on Iran since Trump paused a renewed military campaign in late July.

The renewed exchange of strikes marks an escalation in a conflict that began Feb. 28, when the US and Israel launched strikes on Iran and Iran retaliated against Israel, US bases and Gulf allies, effectively closing the Strait of Hormuz — a waterway through which roughly a fifth of the world's oil and gas once passed. Trump paused the US military campaign in late July, but Sunday's strike on Larak Island, followed by Iranian retaliation against US bases in Jordan and a disputed drone incident near the UAE, shows the pause has not held. The episode is unfolding alongside a broader US pressure campaign — new sanctions announced Aug. 24, a naval blockade, and a months-long, only recently disclosed operation to clear Iranian sea mines from shipping lanes — even as maritime authorities keep the strait at a 'severe' threat level and oil traffic remains far below prewar levels. The Amnesty International item, while concerning a geographically and legally separate US military operation against alleged drug-smuggling boats, is included here because it reflects a parallel debate playing out in Washington about congressional oversight, evidence, and the legality of US uses of lethal force abroad during the same period.

International & World Middle East

US and Iran exchange strikes, breaking monthlong pause in hostilities

International & World desk

The United States and Iran traded direct military strikes on Sunday, ending a nearly monthlong pause in hostilities. The U.S. military said it targeted two Iranian rocket launchers on Larak Island in the Strait of Hormuz that were preparing to fire sea mines into the strategic waterway, according to a statement sent to NPR. Iran retaliated by launching ballistic missiles at U.S. forces at Al-Minhad Air Base in the United Arab Emirates and at bases in Jordan, though no missiles reached U.S. positions. Jordan’s armed forces said it shot down eight missiles that entered its airspace. The UAE reported intercepting drones launched from Iran over its territorial waters.

Health & Science Public health

NPR investigation finds U.S. heat deaths are five times higher than official counts

Health & Science desk

A two-year investigation by NPR and Boston University has found that the official U.S. tally of heat-related deaths dramatically undercounts the true toll, with an estimated 9,000 deaths per year from 2018 to 2025 attributable to heat — roughly five times the average of about 1,700 reported annually by the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention. In some years, the number may exceed 10,000, according to the analysis, which examined weekly mortality and temperature data for 530 U.S. counties representing about 73% of the population. The findings are scheduled for publication in a scientific journal.

International & World Natural Disaster

Nepal-Tibet flood death toll nears 1,000 as rescuers race to reach trapped workers

International & World desk

The death toll from catastrophic floods along the Nepal-Tibet border has reached 939 in Nepal, with 16 additional deaths confirmed in Tibet, bringing the known regional total to 955, according to disaster authorities. Nepal’s disaster management agency reported 3,925 people missing nationwide, including 592 foreign nationals, as rescue operations entered their sixth day. In Tibet, Chinese state media listed 546 missing. The disaster has devastated hydropower projects along the Trishuli River, with at least 11 completed or under-construction plants damaged. Rescuers believe more than 600 workers remain trapped in mud-filled tunnels at the Trishuli-3A and Upper Trishuli-1 sites, while the number of unaccounted-for hydropower staff was revised down to 639 on Monday from an earlier count of 933.
