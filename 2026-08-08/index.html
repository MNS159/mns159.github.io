International & World Middle East Defense

Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Pakistan sign mutual defence pact amid Middle East turmoil

International & World desk · anchored on Reuters

Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Pakistan signed a joint defence agreement in the Saudi city of Mecca on Friday, pledging that an armed attack on any one of them would be regarded as an attack on all three. The "Mecca Joint Defence Agreement" was announced after a meeting between Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman, Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdogan and Pakistani Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif. It comes after the U.S. and Israel attacked Iran on Feb. 28, prompting Tehran and its allies to strike targets in Saudi Arabia and other Gulf states and disrupt shipping through the Strait of Hormuz and the Red Sea. The pact follows nearly a year of negotiations, Reuters reported in January.

In a joint statement, the three governments said the agreement aims "to strengthen collective deterrence against any act of aggression" and provides for "the enhancement of all aspects of defence co-operation among the three States." It does not specify what obligations each party would shoulder in the event of an attack. A Turkish official said the pact was "purely defensive in nature," was not directed against any specific actor, was open to other regional countries and did not replace any existing bilateral or multilateral arrangements. Saudi Arabia and Pakistan had already announced a joint defence pact last year.

NIH plans to block visa-holding international researchers from key grant program

Health & Science desk

The National Institutes of Health intends to restrict eligibility for its "Pathways to Independence" award to U.S. citizens, permanent residents, and noncitizen nationals, according to an internal presentation reviewed by STAT. The change would effectively close one of the few funding avenues available to international students on visas pursuing biomedical research careers in the United States.

International & World Ukraine War

Russian strikes near Kyiv kill four, including child; Ukraine appeals for air defense

International & World desk

Russian ballistic missile and drone strikes overnight killed at least four people, including a child, in and around the Ukrainian capital Kyiv, local officials said Saturday. In the Brovary District east of Kyiv, a double-tap drone strike killed a grandfather, grandmother and their 3-year-old grandson in the village of Pukhivka, President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said. The child’s parents, his 15-year-old brother and a neighbor who had come to help were wounded. In Kyiv itself, six ballistic missiles caused fires at a building, garages and a business, killing one additional person, Mayor Vitali Klitschko wrote on Telegram. The Ukrainian Air Force reported that Russia launched six missiles and 151 attack drones overnight, striking 12 locations; Ukrainian forces downed 135 drones.

International & World Sudan Conflict

UN warns Sudan's war threatens 'entire generation' as drone strikes batter el-Obeid

The United Nations warned Friday that Sudan's three-year civil war is depriving an entire generation of its future, with more than 8 million children out of school, as the besieged city of el-Obeid faces relentless drone strikes and a growing risk of mass atrocities. Deputy Secretary-General Amina Mohammed told an informal Security Council session that nearly three-quarters of schools in Darfur have been damaged, destroyed or rendered unusable since fighting erupted in April 2023. 'Sudan has already lost a generation to its earlier wars, and it is losing another one now,' she said, noting that five in every six children are out of school and that parents keep them home because 'it is the only place left where they can keep them safe.'
