Ukraine War

Russian strikes near Kyiv kill four, including child; Ukraine appeals for air defense

International & World desk

Russian ballistic missile and drone strikes overnight killed at least four people, including a child, in and around the Ukrainian capital Kyiv, local officials said Saturday. In the Brovary District east of Kyiv, a double-tap drone strike killed a grandfather, grandmother and their 3-year-old grandson in the village of Pukhivka, President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said. The child’s parents, his 15-year-old brother and a neighbor who had come to help were wounded. In Kyiv itself, six ballistic missiles caused fires at a building, garages and a business, killing one additional person, Mayor Vitali Klitschko wrote on Telegram. The Ukrainian Air Force reported that Russia launched six missiles and 151 attack drones overnight, striking 12 locations; Ukrainian forces downed 135 drones.

The attacks come as Ukraine’s depleted air defenses have struggled to intercept Russian ballistic missiles, which travel at several times the speed of sound and dive steeply, leaving defenders only seconds to respond. Patriot systems supplied by the United States remain the only weapons in Ukraine’s arsenal able to reliably stop them, but officials say they lack enough batteries or interceptors to shield cities properly. Zelenskyy said this week that Ukraine received only a third as many air-defense missiles in the first half of this year as in the same period of 2025, attributing the shortfall partly to global stockpiles being drawn down by the U.S.-Israel conflict with Iran. He added that delays in receiving anti-ballistic systems from partners lead to “horrific casualties and destruction” and that stronger interceptor capability “could have saved lives” in recent deadly assaults.

Sven Coppens, director of Plan International’s Ukraine Response, said the pattern of multiple missiles launched at the same area within a short period leaves civilians less time to reach safety and places greater pressure on already stretched air defenses. He noted that drones are increasingly used against civilians in frontline communities, shrinking the space where people can feel safe.

In a separate development, Zelenskyy made his first official visit to Serbia on Saturday, meeting President Aleksandar Vučić to discuss economic ties, security and relations with the European Union. Serbia, historically close to Russia and dependent on cheap Russian gas, has refused to apply sanctions on Moscow, a stance that has slowed its EU accession. Vučić has provided non-military aid to Ukraine and promised help with post-war reconstruction.

Ukraine also struck targets inside Russia overnight, hitting the Ilsky and Syzran oil refineries and causing fires at both, the Ukrainian general staff said. Zelenskyy said such strikes aim to limit oil revenues Russia uses to finance its war. Ukraine also launched long-range drones at a warehouse of the online retailer Wildberries in Yekaterinburg, part of a campaign against logistics sites that support Russia’s military.

On Friday, the U.S. Senate passed a sweeping sanctions bill targeting Russian energy exports, including 100 percent tariffs on countries still buying Russian oil and gas. The bill, named after the late Senator Lindsey Graham, passed 86-11 and now requires House approval and President Donald Trump’s signature to become law. Zelenskyy welcomed the vote, and European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said it would help “drain Russia’s means to continue a war it cannot win.”
