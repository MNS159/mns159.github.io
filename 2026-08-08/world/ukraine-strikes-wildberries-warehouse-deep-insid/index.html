Ukrainian drones strike major Wildberries warehouse deep inside Russia

International & World desk · anchored on Reuters

Ukrainian long-range drones hit a warehouse belonging to Russia's largest online retailer, Wildberries, in the Ural Mountains city of Yekaterinburg, more than 2,000 km (1,200 miles) from the Ukrainian border, local officials and the company said on Friday. The regional governor, Denis Pasler, said three drones struck the warehouse's roof. The company said most merchandise was not damaged, though more than a dozen other Wildberries depots hit by Ukraine in recent weeks have burned down.

The Kremlin's representative in the region, Artyom Zhoga, said 800 people were evacuated from the warehouse and no one was hurt. Russian President Vladimir Putin chaired a meeting of the country's security council on Friday, stating in televised remarks that it would focus on additional measures to "strengthen … protection of infrastructure facilities", but gave no further details. The attack on the e-commerce hub follows a pattern of Ukrainian strikes deep inside Russian territory, targeting logistics and industrial infrastructure.

In other developments, Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy thanked the U.S. Senate on Friday for passing a bill backing sweeping energy sanctions on Russia, saying it helped increase pressure on Moscow. European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen also welcomed the vote, stating it complemented the EU's 21st sanctions package and showed that "Europe and the United States can once again show what historic partners can achieve when we act together." Zelenskyy also began his first official visit to Serbia on Friday, meeting with President Aleksandar Vučić to discuss economic ties and security, though Vučić has insisted his decision not to sanction Russia will not change.

On the ground, Russian strikes killed three people, including a child, in the Brovary district northeast of Kyiv, regional military officials said on Saturday. Three others were wounded, according to Kyiv's regional military administration chief, Tymur Tkachenko. In Kyiv, four people were wounded from overnight attacks, with Mayor Vitali Klitschko reporting fires in two districts. On Friday, Russian strikes on Ukraine's southeastern Dnipropetrovsk region killed two people and injured six, according to Governor Oleksandr Hanzha, amid nearly 50 attacks. Two people were killed near the town of Nikopol on the western bank of the Dnipro River, opposite the Russian-held Zaporizhzhia nuclear power plant. Russia's defense ministry said its forces struck two cargo vessels in the Black Sea being used in the Ukrainian military's interests.

In Russian-occupied Crimea, two people were killed after a Ukrainian drone struck an apartment building in Kerch on Friday, said the peninsula's Russia-installed governor, Sergei Aksyonov. A fire broke out at the Ilsky oil refinery and five people were wounded in Russia's Krasnodar region after a Ukrainian drone attack, local authorities said on Saturday. Separately, drones targeted an industrial facility in Russia's Samara region, the regional governor said, without identifying the enterprise.

In Germany, Chancellor Friedrich Merz convened the national security council on Friday to discuss an explosive-laden drone found near a Ukrainian cargo plane and the midair collision of another aircraft with an unidentified object this week. German Interior Minister Alexander Dobrindt called the discovery 'a hybrid attack' that spelled 'a new level of danger.' The Russian embassy in Berlin claimed the discovery was a 'fabricated provocation' set up to blame Moscow. Russia's supreme court also said on Friday it would consider a lawsuit to bar Yabloko, the only officially registered party opposing the war in Ukraine, from contesting parliamentary elections in September. The suit was filed by the pro-Kremlin nationalist party Rodina, whose leader denounced Yabloko as a Western-backed movement.
