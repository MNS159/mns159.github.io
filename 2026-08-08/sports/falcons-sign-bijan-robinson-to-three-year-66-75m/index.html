FIFA President Infantino denies allegations of affair and six-figure payout from Uefa

Sports desk

FIFA President Gianni Infantino has strongly denied allegations that he had an affair with a female employee at Uefa, who was given a six-figure severance payment by European football’s governing body, according to a Daily Telegraph investigation.

A spokesman for Infantino told the Telegraph the allegations were 'categorically untrue' and 'defamatory'. The claims centre on a payment made to the woman while Infantino served as Uefa's general secretary from 2009 to 2016, a role he left to become FIFA president.

Uefa confirmed that a departure payment was made to the individual in question, covering fees for her to complete an MBA at a local business school. It said the payment was in line with regulations at the time, which have since been tightened.

The allegations come as Infantino faces mounting pressure over his abortive plan to sell stakes in the World Cup to private investors, a proposal that has triggered calls for his resignation. Norway’s football federation has called for him to step down, while the Football Association (FA) has withdrawn its support.

However, Infantino retains the backing of senior executives, including the South American confederation (Conmebol), the Confederation of African Football (Caf), and the football associations of Mexico and Argentina. European teams had threatened a boycott of FIFA events despite the plan being abandoned, but no decision has been reached.

Infantino, 56, joined Uefa in 2000 and became its general secretary in 2009 before being elected FIFA president in 2016. The BBC has contacted FIFA and Infantino for further comment.
