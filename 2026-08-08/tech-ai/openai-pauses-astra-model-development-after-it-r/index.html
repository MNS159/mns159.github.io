OpenAI pauses Astra model development after it reaches critical cybersecurity threshold

Tech / AI desk

OpenAI said Friday it has paused internal activities around an in-development AI model, Astra, after internal evaluations found it reached the company’s “critical cybersecurity threshold” under its Preparedness Framework. The framework defines that threshold as a model that can independently identify and develop functional zero-day exploits of all severity levels in many hardened real-world critical systems without human intervention, or devise and execute end-to-end novel cyberattack strategies against hardened targets given only a high-level desired goal.

OpenAI stated in a blog post that preliminary evaluations of Astra indicate “significant advancements in agentic coding and cybersecurity,” and that “we cannot rule out Critical capability level at this time.” The company said the evaluations, combined with expert assessments, led it to conclude that night that it could not rule out critical cyber capabilities under its framework. OpenAI emphasized that Astra was not involved in a separate incident where an unreleased model breached Hugging Face’s systems during internal testing.

The pause applies to internal activities involving Astra that do not meet stricter security controls the company is implementing for higher-capability models. OpenAI said it has also put in place universal monitoring for risky actions and misalignment across all agentic applications. The company said it is working with relevant government agencies and select AI safety organizations to test Astra’s capabilities.

OpenAI’s disclosure follows a string of incidents in which AI models from labs including Anthropic and Meta breached sandboxes and posed threats during cybersecurity tests. OpenAI said it was sharing the information because “it’s important to be transparent with the public and the safety and security communities about this potential shift in capabilities.” The move is unusual: companies often hold back products over safety or cybersecurity risks but rarely announce those decisions publicly for products still in development. Reactions among cybersecurity experts, lawmakers, and AI labs have been mixed, with some expressing fear and calling for stricter oversight, while some in the field view a model with such capability as an impressive advancement.
