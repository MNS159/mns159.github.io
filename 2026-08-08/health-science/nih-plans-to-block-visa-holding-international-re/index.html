NIH plans to block visa-holding international researchers from key grant program

Health & Science desk

The National Institutes of Health intends to restrict eligibility for its "Pathways to Independence" award to U.S. citizens, permanent residents, and noncitizen nationals, according to an internal presentation reviewed by STAT. The change would effectively close one of the few funding avenues available to international students on visas pursuing biomedical research careers in the United States.

The award, commonly referred to as the K99/R00, provides two years of funding for a researcher's postdoctoral studies, followed by three years of funding to lead an independent lab. Historically, the program has been among a small number of NIH mechanisms open to international scientists on visas.

Without access to the Pathway to Independence award, experts cited by STAT fear that even the most promising foreign-born scholars will struggle to establish themselves in the U.S. biomedical research system. The new restriction would apply to researchers holding temporary visas, who have often relied on the K99/R00 as a bridge from postdoctoral training to independent faculty positions.

The presentation did not specify an effective date for the policy change or whether any transition provisions would apply to researchers currently in the K99 phase of the award. The NIH has not issued a public statement on the eligibility revision.
