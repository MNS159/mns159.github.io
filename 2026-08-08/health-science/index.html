NIH plans to block visa-holding international researchers from key grant program

Health & Science desk

The National Institutes of Health intends to restrict eligibility for its "Pathways to Independence" award to U.S. citizens, permanent residents, and noncitizen nationals, according to an internal presentation reviewed by STAT. The change would effectively close one of the few funding avenues available to international students on visas pursuing biomedical research careers in the United States.

Substance Abuse Policy

Federal grants banned for drug test strips, raising overdose fears

Health & Science desk

The Substance Abuse and Mental Health Services Administration (SAMHSA) has banned the use of federal grants to distribute drug test strips to the public, a move that harm reduction groups warn could lead to more overdoses. The policy, announced in late April, also prohibits federal funding for other harm reduction practices, including programs that supply clean needles, drug paraphernalia such as pipes, and specialized hotlines that people can call while using drugs so someone can intervene in case of an overdose.

Vaccines

New vaccine 89% effective against deadly shigellosis in early trial

Health & Science desk

A new vaccine candidate, WRSs2, has shown 89% efficacy in preventing shigellosis, a leading cause of diarrheal deaths worldwide, according to results published this summer in The Lancet Infectious Diseases. The trial, conducted jointly at Cincinnati Children's Hospital Medical Center and the Emory Vaccine Center's Hope Clinic in Atlanta, recruited 108 adults. Half received two oral doses of the weakened Shigella bacteria suspended in liquid, taken a month apart; the other half received a placebo of salt water. Among the placebo group, 81% became ill after deliberate exposure, experiencing diarrhea and fever, while the vaccine group saw a nearly 90% reduction in illness.
