Senate votes to confirm Todd Blanche as attorney general

Politics desk
✓ Facts
  • The Senate confirmed Todd Blanche as U.S. attorney general by a vote of 50-49 in the early hours of Saturday morning.
  • All Senate Democrats voted against confirmation, joined by Republican Sens. Susan Collins and Lisa Murkowski; Sen. Mitch McConnell was absent.
  • Blanche had been serving as acting attorney general and as the Justice Department's No. 2 official (deputy attorney general) after President Trump removed Pam Bondi.
  • Republican Sen. Bill Cassidy of Louisiana, who lost his party's renomination to a Trump-backed challenger, provided the decisive vote needed to confirm Blanche.
  • On Aug. 2, Blanche issued a written statement rescinding the order that created the Justice Department's $1.8 billion Anti-Weaponization Fund, which had been established as part of a settlement between Trump and the IRS over his leaked tax returns.
  • A separate DOJ statement issued the same day limited tax-audit protections from that settlement to Trump, two of his sons, and the Trump Organization, and specified the protections apply only retroactively; none of the statements were signed by all settlement parties, including Trump.
  • Before becoming deputy attorney general, Blanche served as Trump's personal defense lawyer starting in 2023 on cases involving hush-money payments, classified documents, and 2020 election-related charges.
  • Under Blanche's tenure, the DOJ charged former FBI Director James Comey and the Southern Poverty Law Center, and opened an investigation tied to E. Jean Carroll's lawsuits against Trump; Blanche also met with Jeffrey Epstein survivors for the first time last month after pressure from senators including Thom Tillis.
○ Analysis

Blanche's confirmation elevates a former Trump personal attorney to lead the 100,000-plus-employee Justice Department at a moment when the department's independence and adherence to normal legal procedure have become central points of contention. His nomination was complicated by the $1.8 billion Anti-Weaponization Fund — created via a Trump-IRS settlement — and by parallel provisions shielding Trump and his family from tax audits, both of which drew bipartisan scrutiny from senators worried about self-dealing and legal durability. Cassidy's decisive vote, cast despite his own political vulnerability after losing renomination, illustrates the pressure Republican senators faced to install a permanent department head rather than leave it under continued acting leadership, even amid unresolved doubts about Blanche's judgment.

Steelman

The dispute ultimately turns less on Blanche personally than on whether informal written assurances — unsigned by Trump and untested in court — can meaningfully constrain a fund and audit-immunity scheme that the president himself has publicly defended, meaning the underlying conflict may resurface regardless of who holds the AG title.

Implications

As attorney general, Blanche will oversee ongoing matters including the Comey and Southern Poverty Law Center prosecutions, the Carroll-related investigation, and any future attempts to revive the Anti-Weaponization Fund or audit protections, all of which are likely to face continued legal and congressional scrutiny given questions about the enforceability of his written assurances and the department's recent record of contested representations in court.
