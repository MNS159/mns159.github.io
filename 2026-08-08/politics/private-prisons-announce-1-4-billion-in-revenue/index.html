Private prisons announce $1.4 billion in revenue as immigration detentions climb

Politics desk
✓ Facts
  • CoreCivic and GEO Group reported combined revenue of $1.4 billion for the April-June 2025 quarter, announced Thursday.
  • CoreCivic reported $684.9 million in quarterly revenue, a 27% increase over the same quarter last year, according to CEO Patrick Swindle.
  • GEO Group reported $732.1 million in quarterly revenue, up nearly $96 million (15%) over the same period last year, according to CEO George Zoley.
  • CoreCivic separately made $1.6 billion in net proceeds from selling four facilities to the Department of Homeland Security, averaging $307,000 per bed per Swindle; this sale will be reflected in next quarter's earnings, not the figures announced Thursday.
  • The U.S. held nearly 66,000 people in immigration detention as of the reporting period, approaching record levels last seen in January, according to the Transactional Records Access Clearinghouse (TRAC).
  • Former GEO Group executive David Venturella is acting director of ICE; he has told lawmakers he divested his GEO Group stock.
  • Tom Homan worked as a paid consultant for GEO Group before becoming the administration's border czar, and Pam Bondi lobbied for GEO Group before becoming Attorney General.
  • A CREW analysis of Trump's financial disclosures found his brokers made 29 trades of GEO Group and CoreCivic stock since his second term began, starting on his 10th day in office, totaling hundreds of thousands of dollars.
  • GEO Group reported rising revenue from ankle monitors, smartwatches, and its SmartLink app under its ICE contract, and expects further increases following the end of Temporary Protected Status for Haitian immigrants.
○ Analysis

Private prison companies CoreCivic and GEO Group operate a significant share of U.S. immigration detention capacity under federal contracts, and their financial performance is closely tied to detention levels and enforcement policy. The Trump administration has stated a goal of maintaining 100,000 people in detention at once, and the two companies are actively expanding capacity — through new facility construction, continued operation of newly government-owned centers, and expanded electronic monitoring programs — to meet anticipated demand. The end of Temporary Protected Status for Haitian immigrants is cited by GEO Group as a specific driver of expected growth in its ankle-monitoring business, illustrating how policy changes translate directly into contract revenue for these companies.

Both parties' arguments hinge on the same underlying fact — private companies' revenue is structurally tied to detention and enforcement volume — meaning any policy shift that increases or decreases immigration enforcement will directly and predictably move these companies' bottom lines, which is the central tension observers across the spectrum are reacting to.

Implications

With CoreCivic's facility sale proceeds not yet reflected in reported earnings and both companies signaling further expansion — including new facility construction and growth in electronic monitoring tied to TPS terminations — quarterly revenues for these companies are likely to keep climbing if detention levels rise toward the administration's stated 100,000-person target, making future earnings calls and contract announcements a continuing barometer of immigration enforcement scale.
