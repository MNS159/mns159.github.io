Yemen War

Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia escalate as Yemen fighting intensifies

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

Forces aligned with Yemen’s government on Thursday said they had intercepted a drone headed toward Medina and a missile fired toward Riyadh, the latest in a surge of Houthi attacks on Saudi infrastructure. The attacks come amid an escalation in fighting along Yemen’s western Red Sea coast, where Saudi-backed government forces are battling the Iran-aligned Houthi group.

The Houthis, also known as Ansar Allah, have in recent days struck key Saudi sites. On Wednesday, a Houthi spokesman said the group had conducted “a high-quality military operation targeting Aramco in Dhahran,” as well as strikes on Riyadh’s King Khalid International Airport and a refinery in the capital. A Saudi Ministry of Energy official confirmed a building belonging to Aramco in Dhahran was hit, causing a “limited fire” but no casualties. Houthi attacks on Riyadh airport over the weekend killed 14 people and wounded more than 300; authorities said the airport would remain shut at least until Saturday as airlines cancelled flights. Attacks on Abha International Airport in southern Saudi Arabia killed two people.

Fighting on the ground in Yemen has also intensified. Yemeni government forces launched a counteroffensive called “Dawn of Yemen” to retake coastal territory seized by the Houthis near the Bab al-Mandeb strait, a vital maritime chokepoint. A government spokesperson said forces had carried out 2,309 “targeting operations” using combat aircraft, drones, artillery and rocket launchers, destroying 460 Houthi military vehicles. Government forces said they had retaken areas in Taiz governorate and repelled a large Houthi attack west of Taiz city, which remains under siege. The Houthi siege has caused severe shortages of fuel, gas and supplies; the UN World Food Programme reported that wheat flour stocks were nearly exhausted. The Red Cross said front-line hospitals in Marib, Taiz and along the Red Sea coast were registering unprecedented numbers of weapon-wounded patients, with some facilities admitting more than 700 casualties in a single week.

The Saudi-led coalition launched strikes on the Houthi-held capital, Sanaa, which the group seized in 2014. A 2022 ceasefire has broken down in recent months. Al Jazeera’s correspondent in Sanaa described the war as a tit-for-tat exchange of blows, with the Houthis seeking “air strike for air strike, capital for capital, airport for airport, and energy facility for energy facility.” Saudi Arabia “has much more to lose” from disruptions to air traffic, as Sanaa airport has been closed for years. The Houthis also signaled that the kingdom will face mounting economic costs: earlier this year they began striking Saudi-linked shipping near the Bab al-Mandeb strait, a route on which Saudi Arabia increasingly relies for oil exports since Iran closed the Strait of Hormuz in March.

The escalation has already disrupted major events. The Six Kings Slam tennis tournament in Riyadh, originally scheduled for next week, was pushed to December. Australia’s football team said it was monitoring the situation, and the Korea Professional Footballers Association expressed “serious concern regarding the safety of all players participating in the Asian Cup.” The Asian Football Confederation president said the 2027 Asian Cup, scheduled to begin January 7 in Jeddah, Riyadh and al-Khobar, could be postponed by a year if needed, but organizers are in “no hurry” to decide. A decision is possible by the end of November, with the AFC president noting that “things can change very quickly.”
