Forty percent of high school students report binge eating in new CDC survey

Health & Science desk

About 40% of high school students reported binge eating in the past month, according to a large new government survey by the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention. The biennial Youth Risk Behavior Survey found that 17% of nearly 7,000 students said they had engaged in binge eating within the last three days. This is the first time the survey has included a question on binge eating, providing the first data on disordered eating collected by the survey in a decade.

Binge eating was more prevalent among female students (19%) than male students (16%). It was also more common among Black (19%), Hispanic (20%), and multiracial (20%) students compared to white (15%) and Middle Eastern or North African students (10%). Researcher Ariel Beccia of Boston Children's Hospital said the finding counters longstanding stereotypes that eating disorders primarily affect white girls and young women. “Eating disorders are so underfunded and under-studied, in part because we did not have these kinds of epidemiologic data,” Beccia said.

The survey question defined binge eating as eating an unusually large amount of food in a short period with a loss of control. Questions on disordered eating were removed from the survey in 2015 to make room for queries about vaping, which was then on the rise. A coalition of eating disorder advocacy and research groups pushed for the binge-eating question because prior research showed it was the most pervasive form of disordered eating among youth, and because young people experiencing food insecurity are more likely to engage in binge eating.

The survey also found links between binge eating and frequent social media use (checking platforms at least hourly), poor mental health, and suicidality. Researchers noted the potential role of the ultra-processed food environment, with nutrition epidemiologist Lindsey Smith-Taillie pointing out that many such foods are engineered for overconsumption. Beccia emphasized that disordered eating is fundamentally a mental health issue, and she hopes the data will convince funders that eating disorders are a true public health issue requiring directed resources.
