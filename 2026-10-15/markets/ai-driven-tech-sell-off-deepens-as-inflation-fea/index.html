AI-Driven Tech Sell-Off Deepens Globally as Inflation and Debt Fears Rattle Markets

Financial & Markets desk · anchored on Reuters, AP

Technology stocks tumbled for a second consecutive day on Thursday, as hotter-than-expected inflation data and mounting concerns about debt-heavy artificial intelligence companies drove a global sell-off. The Nasdaq Composite fell 1.2% in early trading after posting a 1.88% decline on Wednesday, its worst daily drop since July. The S&P 500 slipped 0.4%, while the Dow Jones Industrial Average edged higher, supported by gains in healthcare stocks such as UnitedHealth and Amgen.

Investors reacted to September inflation figures that came in above expectations. The consumer price index rose 0.6% month over month and 3.7% year over year, compared with the 3.6% annual gain economists had anticipated. Core CPI, excluding food and energy, increased 0.3% monthly and 2.6% annually, also slightly above forecasts. A separate report showed wholesale inflation surged to its highest level in more than four years. “This puts the Federal Reserve very much in play for December,” said Philip Blancato, chief market strategist at Osaic. U.S. oil prices rose, with West Texas Intermediate settling at $95.09 per barrel.

The sell-off was led by semiconductor and AI-related names. Nvidia shares fell 2%, bringing their decline from a record high earlier this month to 10%. Advanced Micro Devices dropped 4%, Sandisk tumbled 6%, and Micron Technology sank 4%. TSMC, the world's largest contract chipmaker, fell 2% despite reporting record profit, after its CEO cautioned about rising component prices and macroeconomic uncertainty. CoreWeave, a data-center provider backed by Nvidia, lost as much as 7% on Thursday, extending a 15.5% plunge the prior day, as analysts scrutinized its tens of billions of dollars in debt amid rising interest rates.

Concerns extended beyond individual stocks to the broader AI ecosystem. Reports indicated that Anthropic, the AI startup planning an IPO, told investors its sales grew 45% to $17 billion last quarter—a sharp deceleration from more than 100% growth in the second quarter. The slowdown, combined with competitive pressure from Chinese chipmaker CXMT’s progress on advanced memory chips, fueled doubts about the sustainability of the AI buildout. The sell-off cascaded across global markets: South Korea’s Kospi plunged 7.6%, triggering a circuit breaker, with SK Hynix down 11% and Samsung Electronics down 9.5%. Japan’s Nikkei fell 4.9%, Hong Kong’s Hang Seng dropped 1%, and European technology stocks lost 3%, led by a 5% decline in Dutch semiconductor maker ASML.

Investor unease has been amplified by the heavy debt loads carried by so-called neocloud providers, which borrow aggressively to build data centers. Research firm Gartner forecast Wednesday that over 80% of such providers will cease operations by 2030, citing high costs and lack of unique business models. “Worries about the AI buildout, bubbly valuations, lower-than-expected earnings growth at leading US frontier labs, Chinese competition, inflation and rates sent investors running for the covers,” said Neil Wilson, a strategist at Saxo. Treasury bonds rallied, pushing yields lower, as investors sought safety.

The selling pressure comes ahead of the US midterm elections, adding another layer of uncertainty for AI stocks that have seen volatile valuations all year. Jefferies chief European economist Mohit Kumar noted that the US is spending more on AI as a percentage of GDP than it did on building railways in the 1800s, but cautioned that a potential Democratic win in Congress could be negative for the sector. “We expect the earnings season to justify that earnings and demand remain robust,” Kumar said, but recommended taking some profits before the midterms. The Federal Reserve’s next policy meeting in December now looms large, with inflation data keeping the possibility of further rate hikes on the table.
