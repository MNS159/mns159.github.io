Fed Governor Lisa Cook’s lawyers call White House panel ‘biased’ as Trump pushes to fire her

Financial & Markets desk · anchored on Reuters
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts slightly center-left

Federal Reserve Governor Lisa Cook’s lawyers have told the White House that a newly formed committee of inquiry set up to investigate an accusation against her is “demonstrably biased,” but did not rule out her eventual appearance before the panel. Lawyer Abbe Lowell said in a letter Tuesday that Cook cannot attend a hearing scheduled for Nov. 5 because she will be in Europe fulfilling official duties at financial and monetary policy meetings. The earliest she could appear would be after the Fed’s policy meeting Dec. 8-9, the letter stated.

The committee was announced last Friday by the White House as part of President Donald Trump’s extended effort to fire Cook from her seat on the seven-member board of governors. Trump has accused Cook of committing mortgage fraud in 2021, before she joined the board — a claim she has denied. No criminal charges have been filed. The White House memo said the committee will ultimately recommend whether there is “cause” to remove Cook. Under federal law, a president may fire a Fed governor only for cause, though no president has ever done so, reflecting the central bank’s traditional independence from politics.

Lowell’s letter, sent to White House counsel William Scharf, also raised concerns about the committee’s composition and process. All three members are Trump appointees: top economic adviser Kevin Hassett, Labor Secretary Keith Sonderling, and Equal Employment Opportunity Commission Chair Andrea Rich. The letter noted that Hassett has already stated publicly that the president has cause to fire Cook. “A hearing with demonstrably biased members, unspecified additional allegations, and an unclear schedule for the disclosure of any additional evidence … is not a process that respects the appearance or history of independence,” Lowell wrote.

Trump first attempted to fire Cook in August 2025, but the Supreme Court ruled earlier this year that she could remain in her position while litigation continued. In a footnote to that ruling, the court suggested Trump could try again if he provided adequate notice and an opportunity to respond — conditions the new committee of inquiry is designed to meet. The Supreme Court did not address whether Cook’s alleged actions meet the legal standard of cause.

Cook was appointed by former President Joe Biden and is the first Black woman to serve on the Fed’s board. The push to remove her is the latest in a series of moves by Trump that economists say have threatened the Fed’s political independence. The Justice Department earlier this year investigated then-Fed Chair Jerome Powell over testimony about a building renovation, though a judge threw out subpoenas in that case and it was later dropped. Trump has repeatedly demanded that the central bank lower its key interest rate.
