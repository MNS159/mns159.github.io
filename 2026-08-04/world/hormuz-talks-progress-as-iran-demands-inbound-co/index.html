Oil Prices Fall as US Hints Hormuz Deal Near; Iran Focuses on Oman Talks

International & World desk · anchored on Reuters
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

Oil prices fell to a three-week low on Tuesday after senior U.S. officials expressed optimism that a deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz could be reached within days. Brent crude, the global benchmark, dropped nearly 5% to under $80 a barrel, while U.S. West Texas Intermediate fell more than 5% to $76 a barrel, according to the BBC. The declines come as the market weighs the possibility of supply disruptions easing through the strategic waterway, which handles roughly one-fifth of the world's daily oil and liquefied natural gas supplies.

U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio told reporters that "there's been progress made in those talks, but not finality yet," adding that he hoped a resolution would come "very shortly." Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent told CNBC that "there is a chance we may have a deal today or tomorrow to open the strait" and move toward "a more normalized position in this conflict." Bessent said such an agreement would mean "freedom of movement" when asked whether Iran would be allowed to charge ships for passage. The Strait has been a central point in U.S.-Iran negotiations since the conflict began in late February, though no details of a potential deal have been released.

Despite the optimistic signals from Washington, Iran has not confirmed any direct talks with the United States. Tehran has instead focused on negotiations with Oman to split shipping routes in the Strait of Hormuz, according to Al Jazeera. Qatar, a key mediator, said it is continuing indirect efforts to avoid further regional escalation and revive diplomacy, though a Qatari foreign ministry spokesman acknowledged that no direct talks are currently planned. Over the weekend, President Donald Trump posted on social media that the U.S. had canceled planned strikes on Iran and that "the perimeters of a deal" had been reached, but Iran has not confirmed participation in the latest round of negotiations.

The conflict has driven up fuel costs globally. In the UK, the average price of petrol has reached £1.60 a litre, according to the RAC. In the US, gasoline prices average above $4 a gallon, with diesel near $5.40 a gallon, according to AAA. Stock markets rose Tuesday on the prospect of lower oil prices and positive corporate results, with the FTSE 100 closing up 0.2% and the FTSE 250 up 1.0%. BP shares fell 4.9% and Shell shares dropped 2.5% on the day, even as BP reported better-than-expected second-quarter profits of $10.31 billion, more than double the prior year's figure. The path to resolving the standoff remains uncertain, with Trump having described the situation as Iran's "last chance" to strike a deal while Tehran continues to insist it is not negotiating directly with the United States.
