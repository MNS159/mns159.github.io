Trans federal employees sue over revoked health coverage

Health & Science desk

A class-action lawsuit was filed yesterday by federal employees and their families against the Trump administration, challenging a mandate that prohibits insurance coverage of gender-affirming care. The lawsuit argues that the ban, implemented this year, creates significant barriers to potentially lifesaving treatment and has forced some workers to leave government employment. The affected health plans — the Federal Employees Health Benefits and the Postal Service Health Benefits — cover millions of federal workers, including an estimated 39,400 transgender people.

The plaintiffs filed administrative claims with the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission before pursuing the lawsuit, which accounts for the midyear timing. According to the complaint, the mandate presents even greater obstacles to accessing care than previous restrictions. Gender-affirming medical and surgical care had been covered under these plans since 2015, when the Obama administration lifted a prior ban. Over the past decade, both public and private insurance expanded coverage for such services.

Since returning to office, the Trump administration has taken steps to roll back access to gender-affirming care. As early as this month, a federal rule could be finalized that would prohibit Medicare and Medicaid from covering transgender-related treatment. The lawsuit is expected to be the first of several similar legal challenges to the administration's policies.

The case highlights the stakes for transgender federal workers, who now face the loss of insurance coverage for care they have relied on for years. The lawsuit seeks to overturn the mandate and restore coverage under the federal employee health programs.
