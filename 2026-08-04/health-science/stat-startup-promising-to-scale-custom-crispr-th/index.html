Aurora Therapeutics scraps lead program, cuts staff seven months after launch; larger rival cited

Health & Science desk

Aurora Therapeutics, a startup that aimed to develop personalized gene-editing drugs, has abruptly abandoned its lead program and reduced its workforce just seven months after its launch, STAT has learned.

A company spokesperson declined to disclose the number of employees laid off or whether any of the startup’s three-person leadership team would remain. Aurora’s chief scientific officer left the company earlier this year.

The move appears to have been prompted by competition from Beam Therapeutics, a larger, more established biotech. One month after Aurora launched, Beam announced it was already advancing its own set of custom gene editors for the exact same disease.

The rapid collapse highlights the intense pressure startups face in the personalized CRISPR field, where larger competitors with deeper resources can quickly overtake smaller entrants. No further details about Aurora’s remaining operations or plans were provided.
