UK regulator weighs in on whether AI scribes are medical devices

Health & Science desk

The U.K. medicines regulator has weighed in on a question that has divided the health-tech industry: whether artificial intelligence scribes that automatically draft clinical notes qualify as medical devices. The decision, reported in STAT’s Health Tech newsletter, carries implications for how these tools are developed, marketed, and used in the National Health Service.

The regulator’s position was not detailed in the available source material, which consists of a newsletter preview and a paywall notice. The article is exclusive to STAT+ subscribers, and the full text of the regulator’s reasoning, the specific AI scribe products under consideration, and any formal guidance documents were not included in the provided excerpt.

AI scribes use natural language processing to listen to patient-clinician conversations and generate draft notes for electronic health records. Proponents argue the tools reduce physician burnout and improve documentation accuracy, while critics and some regulators have questioned whether the software’s output influences clinical decisions enough to classify it as a medical device under U.K. law.

The newsletter also notes additional content on Dexcom’s FDA pilot program and a look at how scribes are being integrated into medical education, but no further details on those topics were provided in the source material.

The regulator’s stance is likely to shape how companies design and market AI scribes in the U.K. and may influence other jurisdictions considering similar questions. The full STAT+ article contains the complete analysis and is available to subscribers.
