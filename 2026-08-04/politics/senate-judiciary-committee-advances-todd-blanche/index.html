Senate Judiciary Committee advances Todd Blanche's attorney general nomination

Politics desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum
✓ Facts
  • The Senate Judiciary Committee voted 12-10 along party lines on Tuesday to advance Todd Blanche's nomination for attorney general to the full Senate.
  • Blanche currently serves as acting Attorney General and previously worked as President Trump's personal lawyer.
  • The committee vote follows a prior session that was canceled a week earlier after GOP Sens. John Cornyn (Texas) and Thom Tillis (N.C.) said they could not support Blanche.
  • Late Sunday night, Blanche posted two documents on X detailing a deal with Cornyn and Tillis: a signed memo formally rescinding the roughly $1.8-2 billion 'anti-weaponization' fund, and an unsigned statement narrowing the scope of audit immunity for Trump and his family tied to a prior IRS settlement.
  • CNN reported the White House — including chief of staff Susie Wiles — was directly involved in shaping Blanche's rescission order, and that Trump was aware of the deal but told reporters, 'I didn't look at it.'
  • Senate Majority Leader John Thune said he wants to schedule a floor vote quickly but could not guarantee confirmation before the August recess.
  • Blanche can afford to lose only two Republican votes on the floor because Sen. Mitch McConnell remains absent while recovering from a fall; Sens. Lisa Murkowski, Susan Collins, John Curtis and Bill Cassidy were described as undecided.
○ Analysis

The dispute centers on two Trump-era Justice Department actions Blanche oversaw: a nearly $2 billion fund meant to compensate people allegedly targeted by 'weaponized' government investigations, and a settlement giving Trump and his family limited immunity from IRS audits. Cornyn and Tillis withheld their committee votes until Blanche agreed to formally rescind the fund and narrow the audit-immunity language, a deal finalized over the weekend before Tuesday's vote. The committee's party-line approval sends Blanche's nomination to the full Senate, where the math is tighter than usual — Blanche can lose only two GOP votes given McConnell's absence — meaning several undecided Republicans could determine whether he is confirmed before the chamber's scheduled break.

◐ Debate

Democratic

  • Sen. Richard Blumenthal (D-Conn.) argued Cornyn and Tillis 'got nothing meaningful,' saying the tax immunity deal remains unlimited as to past liability and that the fund is 'still alive and well' as far as Trump is concerned, since 'he's the one who counts.'
  • Sen. Alex Padilla (D-Calif.) was interviewed by NPR ahead of the vote, reflecting Democratic scrutiny of the nomination and the process by which the deal was reached.

Republican

  • Sen. John Cornyn (R-Texas) defended the agreement, saying the fund's rescission 'is legally binding, so it's gone,' and that on the tax provision, getting Blanche's sworn testimony and DOJ's on-record interpretation was the most achievable safeguard.
  • Sen. Thom Tillis (R-N.C.) said he trusts Blanche because 'I believe he's an honorable man,' while acknowledging Trump could create a new fund and would then 'answer to the American people for not negotiating in good faith.'
  • Sen. John Kennedy (R-La.) said he isn't certain Trump won't renege on the agreement, but expects Blanche to ultimately be confirmed despite some Republican grumbling.
Steelman

Across both parties, the central uncertainty is the same: the rescission memo Blanche signed lacks the signature of Trump's personal lawyers who negotiated the original IRS settlement, leaving open whether the deal that secured his committee approval is actually binding on the president.

Implications

Blanche's nomination now moves to the full Senate floor, where his confirmation margin is unusually thin due to McConnell's absence and several undecided Republicans. Whether the Cornyn-Tillis deal holds — particularly given questions about its legal enforceability and Trump's own stated fondness for the rescinded fund — will likely remain a point of scrutiny both during floor debate and after any confirmation, especially if the fund or audit provisions resurface in another form.
