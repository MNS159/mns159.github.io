MLB Trades

Red Sox acquire Adley Rutschman from Orioles in deadline shocker

Sports desk

The Boston Red Sox pulled off the biggest surprise of Monday's MLB trade deadline, acquiring All-Star catcher Adley Rutschman from the division-rival Baltimore Orioles, according to multiple reports. The deal was part of a flurry of activity across the league as front offices made a late push before the deadline. The Red Sox also added left-handed reliever Erik Miller, while sending infielder Marcelo Mayer — the No. 4 overall pick in the 2021 draft — to Baltimore.

FIFA Governance

Wenger says scrapping FIFA private investment plan was 'absolutely necessary'; Jordan FA president accuses Infantino of 'blackmail'

Sports desk

FIFA's chief of global football development Arsene Wenger has said scrapping the plan to have private investment in FIFA competitions was "absolutely necessary and beyond question," as the fallout from the abandoned proposal continues. FIFA President Gianni Infantino was forced to withdraw the plan last week after days of criticism, including a threat from UEFA's 55 member nations to boycott all FIFA tournaments. Infantino had proposed creating a commercial subsidiary to run FIFA's main events, including the World Cup, with external investors able to buy stakes, and had offered FIFA's 211 member associations $40 million (£30 million) each if they backed the proposal.

NFL Contracts

Falcons sign Bijan Robinson to three-year, $66.75M extension, making him NFL's highest-paid running back

Sports desk

The Atlanta Falcons and running back Bijan Robinson agreed to a three-year, $66.75 million extension on Tuesday, according to Adam Schefter, ending the 24-year-old's brief training camp hold-in and resetting the running back market. The deal has a maximum value of $75 million, with $51 million guaranteed and $37 million due in the first year, making Robinson the NFL's highest-paid running back by average annual value ($22.25 million). The extension also paves the way for Detroit's Jahmyr Gibbs and Indianapolis's Jonathan Taylor, who had been waiting to see how the market would develop.
