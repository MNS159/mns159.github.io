Falcons sign Bijan Robinson to three-year, $66.75M extension, making him NFL's highest-paid running back

Sports desk

The Atlanta Falcons and running back Bijan Robinson agreed to a three-year, $66.75 million extension on Tuesday, according to Adam Schefter, ending the 24-year-old's brief training camp hold-in and resetting the running back market. The deal has a maximum value of $75 million, with $51 million guaranteed and $37 million due in the first year, making Robinson the NFL's highest-paid running back by average annual value ($22.25 million). The extension also paves the way for Detroit's Jahmyr Gibbs and Indianapolis's Jonathan Taylor, who had been waiting to see how the market would develop.

Robinson, a first-team All-Pro selection in 2025, led the NFL with 2,298 yards from scrimmage last season, rushing 287 times for 1,478 yards (5.1 yards per carry) and catching 79 passes for 820 yards with 11 total touchdowns. His 5,648 career yards from scrimmage are the most ever by a player age 23 or younger. The extension comes after Atlanta exercised his fifth-year option for 2027, worth $11.323 million fully guaranteed, and replaces his rookie deal that ran through 2026.

The Robinson extension is the latest in an expensive offseason for the Falcons under their new front office, led by franchise legend Matt Ryan (president of football operations), general manager Ian Cunningham, and coach Kevin Stefanski. Atlanta has also extended wide receiver Drake London (three-year, $35.2 million average, third at his position), tight end Kyle Pitts ($17.6 million average, third at his position), and guard Matthew Bergeron ($24 million average, tied for first at his position). The moves lock in what the team views as franchise cornerstones, but CBS Sports described them as 'the easy stuff'—the hard part remains finding a quarterback.

The Falcons have been mired in mediocrity, winning seven or eight games for five straight seasons, the longest such streak since the 1970 AFL-NFL merger. The team's search for a quarterback post-Matt Ryan has been plagued by failure. The previous regime signed Kirk Cousins to a four-year, $180 million deal in 2024, then drafted Michael Penix Jr. eighth overall a month later—a move then-GM Terry Fontenot justified by saying, 'If you believe in a quarterback, you have to take him... if he sits for four or five years, that's a great problem to have.' Cousins never regained his pre-Achilles form and is now with the Raiders; Penix has been either injured or underwhelming. He is currently in a quarterback battle with Tua Tagovailoa, though he is reportedly 'weeks away' from 11-on-11 work while rehabbing another major knee surgery. Tagovailoa, who came cheap because the Dolphins are still paying his salary, has his own injury and inconsistency issues.

Stefanski, who is the Falcons' third head coach since Dan Quinn's ouster, has a history of developing quarterbacks, having coached Brett Favre, Joe Flacco, and others. He said at his introductory press conference, 'We want to be a very intentional group when it comes to developing players. Obviously, the quarterback position is at the forefront of that.' Despite the strong offensive nucleus—Robinson, London, Pitts, Bergeron, and annual All-Pro Chris Lindstrom—the Falcons ranked 12th in offensive infrastructure, with a line that graded 16th in pass blocking and 10th in run blocking. The defense ranked 12th in DVOA. The team is considered a quarterback away from contention, but so much talent makes it difficult to bottom out for a high draft pick; Atlanta selected 15th last year and would have picked 13th this year if they had their first-round pick.

Looking ahead, the Falcons may need to find their quarterback in the 2027 draft class, though they likely won't have the top pick, or they could pursue a bridge veteran like Kyler Murray or Baker Mayfield, following the recent trend of reclamation projects that succeeded with Sam Darnold in Minnesota and Seattle. Ryan, Cunningham, and Stefanski face the NFL's toughest challenge: identifying and acquiring a franchise quarterback. As CBS Sports noted, 'The easy calls are done. Good work. Now comes the toughest challenge in sports, exactly why Ryan, Stefanski and Cunningham were hired in the first place.'
