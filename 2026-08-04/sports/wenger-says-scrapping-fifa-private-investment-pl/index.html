Wenger says scrapping FIFA private investment plan was 'absolutely necessary'; Jordan FA president accuses Infantino of 'blackmail'

Sports desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

FIFA's chief of global football development Arsene Wenger has said scrapping the plan to have private investment in FIFA competitions was "absolutely necessary and beyond question," as the fallout from the abandoned proposal continues. FIFA President Gianni Infantino was forced to withdraw the plan last week after days of criticism, including a threat from UEFA's 55 member nations to boycott all FIFA tournaments. Infantino had proposed creating a commercial subsidiary to run FIFA's main events, including the World Cup, with external investors able to buy stakes, and had offered FIFA's 211 member associations $40 million (£30 million) each if they backed the proposal.

Wenger, who has been in his role since 2019 and previously supported Infantino's proposal to play the men's World Cup every two years, issued a statement through FIFA a week after the investment plan was first released. In it, he demanded "transparency and integrity" from world football's governing body. "The recent events at FIFA deserve some clarity from my side," Wenger's statement read. "I was not involved in this strategic plan and first became aware of the project through media reports. The decision to withdraw the project was absolutely necessary and beyond question, because I firmly believe in an independent FIFA that serves our game with commitment, transparency, and integrity." Wenger is one of 18 FIFA officials named in a document preservation letter UEFA sent to FIFA threatening legal action.

FIFA Secretary General Mattias Grafstrom, seen as a close ally of Infantino, also joined the criticism in an internal memo to staff. "A sad and reproachable series of events - which were thankfully concluded with the project permanently abandoned - for as much as we feel dismayed by them, should not overshadow this reality," Grafstrom wrote. "We have all been thrown into the middle of a turmoil, which is difficult to comprehend and accept." He urged staff to remain focused on serving football and member associations, and assured them they would be "defended and safeguarded from the political context." Carlos Cordeiro, Infantino's senior adviser on global strategy and governance, is the only FIFA official to resign over the scandal, calling the proposal "a bad deal for football" that would "mortgage football's future."

Jordan FA President Prince Ali bin Hussein has accused Infantino of "blackmail" following the foiled plan. Prince Ali, who lost to Infantino in the 2016 presidential election, posted on X: "We pride ourselves in Jordan in upholding ethical values. We did not endorse him before and certainly will not now. But the whole situation amounts to blackmail and we refuse to give in to that." He detailed several grievances, including difficulties getting Jordanian fans visas for the World Cup in the USA, taxes imposed by the US government on Jordan's participation, and unpaid reward money for players from the Arab Cup in Qatar, a FIFA event. Prince Ali said that for months, FIFA had been refusing to help on these matters "until it was verbally communicated to me during the World Cup that if I endorsed Infantino it would go a long way to helping our FA out." He added that neither he nor the Jordanian FA endorse Infantino for re-election next year.

Despite calls for Infantino to stand down, the FIFA president has not spoken since the withdrawal of his proposals last Saturday. The Asian Football Confederation and Concacaf, which represents 41 nations from North and Central America and the Caribbean, backed UEFA in their stance against the plan. FIFA has been approached for comment.
