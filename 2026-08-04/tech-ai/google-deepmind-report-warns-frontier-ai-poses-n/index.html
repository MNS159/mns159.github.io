Google DeepMind report warns frontier AI poses new cybersecurity and biosecurity risks

Tech / AI desk

Google DeepMind has published a report warning that frontier artificial intelligence systems pose emerging threats beyond traditional cybersecurity, including nuclear and biological risks, as the technology advances toward artificial general intelligence (AGI). The report, titled “A Framework for Frontier AI and the Dawning of a New Age,” was released alongside a blog post by DeepMind CEO Demis Hassabis, who stated that AGI is “probably only a few short years away” and described its potential impact as being ten times that of the Industrial Revolution at ten times the speed.

The report identifies specific areas where advanced AI could cause significant harm, naming nuclear and biological risks as new threat vectors that require immediate attention. It notes that the current trajectory of AI development is marked by unprecedented acceleration, with progress outpacing understanding of the technology itself. Hassabis said that “nobody in the world knows for sure what is going to happen from here,” urging a cautious yet optimistic approach to public policy that balances innovation with responsible security practices.

To address these challenges, DeepMind proposes the establishment of a U.S.-based Standards Body, modeled on a public-private partnership, to assess and regulate frontier-class AI models. The body would define benchmarks for capability and encourage frontier labs to adopt best practices, including publishing detailed model cards and prioritizing safety research. Initially, the framework envisions a voluntary pre-release review process allowing assessment up to 30 days before a model’s public launch, with a potential transition to mandatory assessment requiring models to pass protocols before deployment in the U.S. market.

The proposed evaluation schedule calls for quarterly updates to benchmarks, with outdated or ineffective tests being deprecated and replaced to prevent overfitting. Testing would extend beyond standard cybersecurity concerns to include biological threats and other high-risk domains, focusing on agentic AI behaviors such as attempts to circumvent safety protocols or deceptive practices. Techniques would include digitally watermarking generated images and ensuring models produce interpretable reasoning. Funding would likely come mostly from industry to attract world-class technical talent and provide necessary compute resources for large-scale testing.

DeepMind suggests that the Standards Body could also promote a network of third-party auditors to support assessments and benchmark development. The framework could be escalated to include coordinating a slowdown in development if necessary. Hassabis concluded that this U.S.-initiated effort could serve as a foundation for international standards, recognizing that the implications of AGI are global in scope, and that “the future is not yet written,” requiring collective action to ensure the technology benefits all of humanity.
