SpaceX Reports First Quarterly Earnings Since IPO; Shares Down 24%

Financial & Markets desk · anchored on Reuters

SpaceX (SPCX) reported its first-ever quarterly earnings as a public company after the closing bell Tuesday, giving investors their first detailed look at Elon Musk’s rocket, satellite and AI business since the record IPO in June. Analysts surveyed by LSEG expected second-quarter revenue of $6.93 billion and a loss of 26 cents per share, according to CNBC. Bloomberg consensus pegged revenue at $6.81 billion and an adjusted loss of 24 cents per share, with adjusted EBITDA of $2.0 billion, Yahoo Finance UK reported. The company’s reported figures may not be directly comparable to consensus estimates because initial reports often contain unpredictable adjustments, CNBC noted.

SpaceX shares, which opened at $150 on June 12, have fallen 24% — wiping out close to $500 billion in market capitalization — and are roughly 50% below their intraday peak of $225.64 reached on June 16, according to CNBC and Yahoo Finance UK. The stock closed at $114 on Monday, up more than 5% on the day but down 15% from the IPO target price of $135 a share, CNN reported. Wall Street analysts have an average price target of $228 on SpaceX, according to FactSet; Oppenheimer has a $250 target.

The earnings report arrives two days before a lockup on up to 911.5 million shares expires Thursday, freeing some employees and pre-IPO insiders to sell shares equivalent to roughly triple the current tradable float, according to Yahoo Finance UK and CNN. Musk’s shares are locked up for at least one year after the IPO; other lockup periods expire before then. Epistrophy Capital’s Cory Johnson wrote that the lockup frees 'hundreds of millions of insider shares — roughly triple the current tradable float.' More shares on the market could increase volatility and raise SpaceX’s weight in indexes like the Nasdaq 100, which base weighting partly on publicly available shares, CNN reported.

SpaceX lost $4.9 billion in 2025 and disclosed a $4.3 billion loss in the first quarter of this year, the outlets reported. Revenue and the only source of profit came from the connectivity segment, which includes Starlink satellite internet, sold to consumers, governments and military agencies. For the second quarter, StreetAccount estimated segment revenue of $3.83 billion for Connectivity, $835 million for Space and $2.18 billion for AI, CNBC reported. Analysts at Oppenheimer expected a 'modest slowdown' in Starlink’s revenue growth due to rocket launch delays, CNN said.

Capital expenditure is a key focus. SpaceX spent $10.1 billion in the first quarter alone, including $7.7 billion on AI, after merging with xAI in February. S&P Global Visible Alpha analyst Melissa Otto noted that capex is projected to rise from $48.7 billion this year to $118.4 billion in fiscal 2028, while total debt could increase more than fivefold from $41.7 billion to $218.0 billion, Yahoo Finance UK reported. The company has said the AI market it aims to unlock is worth more than $26 trillion, according to its IPO prospectus, CNN reported. Also on investors’ minds is SpaceX’s announced acquisition of Cursor.

The conference call with management is scheduled for 4:30 p.m. ET. Oppenheimer equity analyst Timothy Horan said in a note, 'In our view, results are secondary to reading management’s body language and outlook.' Jay Ritter, professor emeritus at the University of Florida, told CNN: 'With this company, the value is so much based upon uncertain future.' Investors will also listen for updates on Starship launches, a potential tower catch of the Starship upper stage on the upcoming Flight 14, and Starlink subscription levels, CNBC reported.
