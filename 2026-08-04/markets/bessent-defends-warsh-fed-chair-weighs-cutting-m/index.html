Bessent defends Warsh; Fed chair weighs cutting meetings as markets demand reset

Financial & Markets desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts slightly center-left

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent defended Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh on Friday, saying financial markets are undergoing a "detox" from years of heavy Fed guidance, even as long-dated bond yields surged to multiyear highs and economists questioned the central bank's inflation-fighting credibility. Bessent questioned the need for an interest-rate hike, according to MarketWatch, as markets reacted to Warsh's first policy meeting as chair.

Warsh told reporters after the Fed's decision to hold rates steady that the central bank will "deliver 2% inflation and not a whisper more," according to Yahoo Finance. But markets responded with skepticism: the 30-year Treasury yield hit its highest level since 2007, reaching 5.2%, while the 10-year yield climbed roughly 48 basis points this year to 4.64%. Bank of America economists wrote that Warsh's tone came across as dovish, invoking the Texas expression "all hat, no cattle" to argue that hard rhetoric was not matched by action. "We don't think the Fed can get markets to do its work indefinitely by just talking tough," BofA economists Aditya Bhave and his team wrote. "It has to eventually walk the talk, or risk losing credibility." BofA expects the Fed to hike by 25 basis points in September, followed by two similar increases in 2026.

Warsh is also reportedly considering the most significant change to the Fed's meeting schedule in decades, potentially cutting the current eight sessions down to six rate-setting meetings a year, according to the New York Times, citing four people familiar with the matter. Bloomberg later reported Warsh floated a schedule of six rate-setting meetings plus two additional meetings focused on broader economic issues. Any decision could come before the Fed's September meeting, and Warsh would not need congressional approval because the Federal Reserve Act requires only at least four meetings a year. The current eight-meeting schedule has been standard since the 1980s, and next year's tentative dates have already been posted on the Fed's website.

Fewer meetings would reduce the staff workload tied to preparing briefing books and public communications, but would also give policymakers fewer routine chances to adjust rates. Unscheduled emergency meetings would become the fallback if inflation or labour market data shift sharply between scheduled sessions. The proposal fits a broader pattern of a more restrained communications approach under Warsh, which could increase the market's reliance on economic data releases rather than Fed commentary between meetings, according to investingLive.

Warsh has also broken with the Fed's long-standing practice of providing forward guidance about future policy moves. "No really, what is Kevin Warsh thinking?" asked Kai Ryssdal and Sean McHenry at Marketplace, noting that the absence of guidance leaves the Fed's plans for controlling inflation "still a little unclear." Claudia Sahm of New Century Advisors said the new strategy potentially "obscures the Fed's framework for understanding and reacting to economic conditions." Warsh told reporters the markets are "learning to play the ball, not the referee," according to Inc. But some analysts warned that a "less transparent Fed" creates "uncertainty in an already messy and unpredictable world," as Nicholas Spiro wrote in the South China Morning Post.

For the stock market, rising long-dated Treasury yields mean financing costs are increasing across the board, from mortgage rates to corporate financing for AI. "He [Warsh] is a little bit between a rock and a hard place," Barbara Doran, CEO of BD8 Capital Partners, told Yahoo Finance. "The implication for higher yields is not a good one for anyone holding debt." Despite the bond market pressure, stocks rebounded Thursday and Friday after blowout earnings from Microsoft and Amazon reinforced confidence in a resilient economy and strong AI investments, with markets continuing to rise ahead of SpaceX earnings on Tuesday.
