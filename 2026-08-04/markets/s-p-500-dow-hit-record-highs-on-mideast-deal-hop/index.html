S&P 500, Dow hit record highs on Mideast deal hopes and AI earnings

Financial & Markets desk · anchored on AP, Reuters

U.S. stocks surged Tuesday, pushing the S&P 500 and Dow Jones Industrial Average to record highs, as optimism over a potential deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz and a wave of strong corporate earnings, particularly in artificial intelligence-related tech stocks, lifted markets. The S&P 500 rose 1.5% to close at 7,600.50, just shy of its all-time closing high of 7,609.78, while the Dow climbed 693 points, or 1.3%, to a record 53,178.41. The tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite jumped 2.1% to 25,913.90, still about 4.3% below its own record set June 2.

Crude oil prices fell sharply, with Brent crude dropping 4.7% to $83.77 a barrel, after Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent told CNBC there is "a chance we may have a deal today or tomorrow" to open the Strait of Hormuz. President Donald Trump said Monday that talks with Iran were under way and that he wanted to give Tehran "every last chance" to sign a deal before facing renewed strikes, a day after he announced he had called off plans for a large-scale attack. Iran's Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei denied talks were happening, saying Tehran was engaged with Oman on shipping transit and that U.S.-Iran issues would be addressed in "next stages." The conflicting accounts did not dampen investor enthusiasm, as lower oil prices eased inflation fears and reduced fuel costs for airlines and other transport companies.

Earnings reports fueled the rally. Caterpillar, the Dow's second-largest component, gained 6% after reporting quarterly sales and revenue above $20 billion for the first time. Palantir Technologies soared 26% after CEO Alex Karp called its quarter "otherworldly," with U.S. government commercial revenue surging 90% year over year. The PHLX Semiconductor Index jumped 6%, lifting shares of Nvidia, Intel, and Micron. Boeing rose 8% after U.S. regulators certified its 737 MAX-7 planes for commercial service. United Airlines gained 5.8%, American Airlines 5%, and Norwegian Cruise Line Holdings 6.6%, all benefiting from lower oil prices. Tyson Foods rose 2.8% after reporting stronger-than-expected spring profit.

The rally followed a volatile July in which the S&P 500 swung as oil prices fluctuated between $72 and $102 a barrel amid war concerns, ultimately ending the month near where it started. Companies in the S&P 500 are on track to report spring earnings per share 47% higher than a year ago, according to FactSet, which would be the strongest growth since spring 2021. A report Monday showed U.S. manufacturing growth accelerated to its strongest level since 2022. The yield on the 10-year Treasury fell to 4.68% from 4.75% Friday, though it remains well above its pre-war level of 3.97%.

Overseas, Asian markets were mixed. South Korea's Kospi rose 1.6%, recovering from a 5.1% drop Monday that followed a record 17.9% surge Friday. Japan's Nikkei 225 edged up 0.3%, while Hong Kong's Hang Seng Index dipped 0.6%. Investors this week are watching for the July jobs report on Friday, with the Job Openings and Labor Turnover Survey due earlier as the first key labor market data.
