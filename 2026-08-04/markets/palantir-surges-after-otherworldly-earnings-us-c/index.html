Palantir surges after 'otherworldly' earnings, US commercial revenue soars

Financial & Markets desk

Palantir Technologies (PLTR) shares surged more than 27% on Tuesday after the company reported fiscal second-quarter earnings that beat analyst expectations and raised its full-year guidance, with CEO Alex Karp describing the results as 'otherworldly' in a call with analysts. The company posted adjusted earnings of $0.41 per share, exceeding the $0.35 consensus estimate, and revenue of $1.94 billion, compared with expectations of $1.8 billion. Revenue climbed 93% from about $1 billion a year ago, and net income rose to $1.07 billion, or 41 cents per share, from roughly $329 million, or 13 cents per share, in the same period last year.

The standout performance was driven by the U.S. commercial segment, where revenue surged 149% year-over-year to $764 million. U.S. commercial remaining deal value more than doubled to $6.24 billion, and total contract value hit a record $2.1 billion, up 153% from a year ago. U.S. government revenue also rose 90% to $809 million. The company closed 220 deals worth $1 million or more during the quarter, including 98 deals worth at least $5 million and 73 worth at least $10 million. 'Record highs across the board,' said chief revenue officer and chief legal officer Ryan Taylor on the earnings call.

Palantir raised its full-year revenue guidance to between $8.15 billion and $8.16 billion, up from prior guidance of $7.65 billion to $7.66 billion. The company now expects U.S. commercial revenue to exceed $3.42 billion in 2026, compared with earlier guidance of $3.22 billion. The strong results mark a sharp reversal from recent months: Palantir shares had fallen about 29% year-to-date prior to the earnings release amid broader concerns that the artificial intelligence software trade was losing momentum. The sell-off had pushed the stock's forward price-to-earnings ratio down to 84 times, from a four-quarter average of about 199 times, making it appear relatively cheaper by historical standards.

The surge extends a rally that began in 2024, traced by analysts to the 2023 launch of Palantir's AIP platform, which allows customers on its existing Gotham and Foundry platforms to connect large language models to their data. The product gained traction across end-market verticals, said William Blair analyst Louie DiPalma, who noted that the enterprise AI market represents a large total addressable market that Palantir is targeting alongside frontier labs like Anthropic and OpenAI. Citi analyst Tyler Radke called the quarter a 'standout' with a 'decisive reacceleration in U.S. Commercial demand,' while D.A. Davidson's Gil Luria, who has a Buy rating and $175 price target, said Palantir has several competitive advantages 'in the era of AI.'

Karp emphasized the role of 'sovereign AI' in the company's outlook, telling analysts, 'The sovereign AI revolution makes us very optimistic about the future.' In an interview with CNBC, he said the strong growth 'looks like this is going to go on for at least another 18 months.' Earlier this year, Palantir's Maven Smart System became an official Pentagon program of record, solidifying its role in U.S. military AI projects. Palantir also posted adjusted free cash flow of $1.22 billion, topping estimates of $1 billion. The company now has a mix of 22 Buy ratings, 9 Holds, and 2 Sells from Wall Street analysts.
