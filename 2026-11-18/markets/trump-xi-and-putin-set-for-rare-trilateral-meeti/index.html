Trump, Xi and Putin hold rare trilateral meeting at APEC in Shenzhen

Financial & Markets desk

U.S. President Donald Trump, Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin held a trilateral meeting Thursday on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) summit in Shenzhen, China, according to Beijing's official confirmation of the event billed as the 'China-Russia-U.S. Summit.' The meeting marked the first-ever gathering of the three leaders, though expectations for substantive breakthroughs were low given limited time for dialogue.

The trilateral encounter came after Xi hosted a welcome banquet Wednesday evening where Trump and Putin were seated just a few spots apart. Xi raised a toast, saying in Mandarin, 'At tomorrow's Leaders' Meeting, we will discuss measures to expand mutual opening up, explore pathways to innovation-driven growth, and identify opportunities to expand practical cooperation across various fields, so as to translate more initiatives into tangible results,' according to a CNBC translation. Trump did not deliver a toast of his own.

U.S. officials said ahead of the summit that Trump did not plan to raise allegations that former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang had an affair with a Chinese spy, a controversy that has gripped markets for the past 10 days. Instead, talks were expected to focus on implementing the existing trade agreement between the two countries. The U.S. and China in September agreed to lower tariffs on $30 billion worth of goods from the other country, though no effective date was specified. China has said it would only increase purchases of U.S. agricultural products if those tariffs were reduced.

The APEC summit comes amid heightened geopolitical tensions. China's relations with Japan have escalated since Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi's comments last November indicating Tokyo would support Taiwan if seriously threatened by Beijing's military. Takaichi is attending the summit in her first trip to China as prime minister. The Russia-Ukraine war continues to pressure global energy prices, and the Iran war is now in its ninth month. 'Across Asia, governments under mounting pressure to pick a side will be watching Shenzhen for signs of how much room they still have to maneuver,' said Reema Bhattacharya, head of Asia research at risk intelligence company Verisk Maplecroft. 'Most have little appetite for a region split into two technology camps.'

Tech issues were a central focus of the summit. Nvidia released quarterly results Wednesday morning Beijing time, boosting its outlook and sending shares higher in extended trading, following the resignation of founder Jensen Huang amid scrutiny of the company's China chip sales. Shenzhen-based Tencent is set to report quarterly earnings Wednesday evening. U.S.-based AI giant Anthropic is pricing its initial public offering this week, expected to list on the Nasdaq Thursday Nov. 19. Tesla and SpaceX CEO Elon Musk was expected to join Trump's delegation in Shenzhen but was not seen at the Wednesday evening banquet.

The APEC host city, Shenzhen, known as one of China's 'Silicon Valleys' and home to Huawei and BYD, is using the event to showcase local robotics startups. The summit also includes the APEC CEO Summit, where Malaysian Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim said 'the entire multilateral framework needs to change,' praising China's recognition of smaller countries. Neither Trump nor any other G7 leader took the stage at the opening event; Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney spoke later in the afternoon. Vietnam, which is hosting next year's APEC meetings, had its president meet with Xi earlier in the week.

Sources: CNBC
