Microsoft embeds Office apps inside Copilot, announces always-on agents

Tech / AI desk

Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella announced Tuesday at the company’s Ignite event that Word, Excel, and PowerPoint are now generally available inside Copilot, which he described as “the new OS for work.” The announcement inverts the traditional software model: instead of an application like Word being the primary tool with an assistant like Clippy to help use it, Microsoft envisions agents driving tasks while Office applications serve as plugin components. Users can now create documents, spreadsheets, and presentations directly in Copilot, with the resulting files remaining editable in their respective Office applications.

The rollout marks a major step toward the Copilot redesign Microsoft first announced in September. Alongside the Office integration, Microsoft introduced Copilot Autopilot, a “persistent, proactive agent for personal productivity” that operates as a cloud-based autonomous AI agent. Unlike single-task assistants, Autopilot can be authorized to work across services like email and calendars and complete assigned tasks as part of its role. It will use usage-based billing via Copilot Credits, though final pricing has not been announced ahead of general availability.

Also announced as generally available was Microsoft IQ, an intelligence platform that houses Work IQ, a context layer that helps agents understand a company’s communications, relationships, and ongoing work. Nadella described Work IQ as perhaps a company’s most valuable database: “It has a deep understanding of the people, their relationships to other people, your emails, your meetings, chats, projects that make up all the fabric of work.” Whether companies allow autonomous agents access to internal documents and messaging data will be determined by their own internal IT review.

The new Copilot integrations are backed by an organizational knowledge layer that draws context from emails, meetings, calendars, chats, and files. Nadella outlined the company’s vision for its new “frontier ecosystem,” with autonomous agents and new governance tools arriving alongside the Office integration. The announcements position Copilot as a central platform for work, with applications becoming components of the agent’s workflow.
