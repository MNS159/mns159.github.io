Grand Theft Auto VI launches to critical acclaim

Tech / AI desk

Grand Theft Auto VI, the latest installment in Rockstar Games' blockbuster franchise, was developed with a reported billion-dollar budget and roughly a decade of development. The game reimagines the iconic Vice City as a grand, crude, satirical criminal playground, rendered with technical polish and an extraordinary level of detail. According to Ars Technica's review, the game's massive map spans more than six miles from the northern wilderness to the southern keys, requiring up to 10 minutes to traverse by vehicle.

The core innovation of GTA VI is its dual protagonists, Jason and Lucia, a young criminal couple whose relationship drives the story. Jason is an ex-military meathead with a dull, affect-free demeanor, while Lucia is a vivacious and amiable Latina eager to seize opportunities. The game's writing explores Lucia's underprivileged youth and strained family relationships, adding depth to her character. Their partnership is reinforced by a simple relationship simulation system—a "heart level" that rises or falls based on obvious choices—and the game frequently shifts perspective between the two, though the lack of a co-op mode for missions is noted as a baffling omission.

The review highlights the game's signature juxtaposition of over-the-top action and mundane domestic life. Explosive gunfights and high-speed chases are counterpointed by lightly interactive moments such as grilling burgers, playing fetch with a dog, or assembling Ikea-style furniture. This contrast lends a surreal hyperreality to Vice City, with emotional peaks and valleys stretching across a gargantuan amount of content. The shooting mechanics remain familiar—cover-based combat with relatively unintelligent enemies—while driving is described as significantly more exciting, with a wide variety of vehicles and traffic to navigate.

The main storyline is divided into two halves. The first half involves the pair running errands for a rogues' gallery of one-note criminal caricatures, which can feel repetitive. The back half focuses on integrating Jason and Lucia into a professional criminal ring, with missions that build on each other through undercover work, casing targets, and tense heists that best showcase the dual-protagonist system. Outside the core plot, many side missions make the protagonists feel like side characters in their own story, and even dozens of hours in, some missions still feel like tutorials for new systems.

The game's humor retains the sophomoric style that defined earlier entries, with extended bits about kinky sex and phallic imagery, though some side stories offer sharper satire of crypto bros and multi-level marketing schemes. The review notes that the game's takes on 'wokeness' feel dated, without much in the way of thought-provoking statements on society. Parents are warned that the game earns a hard "M for Mature" rating, reflecting how standards have shifted since the series' earlier controversies.

Despite these criticisms, Grand Theft Auto VI is praised as a technical marvel and a world players will want to inhabit long after the main story ends. Dozens of side activities—including drone racing, rally races, and meditation spots—offer distractions that improve character stats or simply exist to be experienced. The review concludes that the game's ability to balance its two sides—criminal bombast and domestic routine—makes it all the better, with the relationship between Jason and Lucia providing an emotional core that is easy to root for, even as they get away with murder.

Sources: Ars Technica
