Kuwait and Bahrain repel Iranian attacks after US strike on Qeshm Island

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

Kuwait and Bahrain said they repelled Iranian air attacks overnight, marking the first such assault on Kuwait in more than two months and the fourth on Bahrain in six days. The attacks came after US warplanes struck an Iranian missile launch site on Qeshm Island in the Strait of Hormuz, according to US military officials. The Iranian army said its drones had targeted US Patriot air defence systems and drone and missile launch platforms at Kuwait's Ali Al Salem Air Base, which hosts US forces. Kuwaiti authorities reported no casualties or damage. Bahrain's military said its air defence systems had 'intercepted and destroyed several Iranian aerial attacks.'

The US said it carried out the strike on Qeshm Island 'with precision munitions' in response to Iranian 'aggression targeting US personnel and assets,' specifically an earlier Iranian attack on a US radar installation in Bahrain. Iranian media reported explosions on the island but cited a local official as saying they caused no damage to infrastructure. Iran's army commander-in-chief, Maj Gen Amir Hatami, warned that if 'the enemy repeats its mistake... we have the readiness and the plans to confront it and its supporters in a stronger, more crushing way and without geographic limitations.'

In a separate development, the Abu Dhabi National Oil Company (Adnoc) said a crew member was missing after one of its tankers was attacked in the Gulf on Tuesday. The rest of the crew were safely evacuated, and 11 were receiving medical care. The UK Maritime Trade Operations centre reported that a tanker was struck by an unknown projectile about 13 nautical miles north of Khasab, Oman, causing a fire that was extinguished. Iran said two oil tankers and a ship carrying liquefied natural gas had caught fire after hitting naval mines while sailing along an 'authorised route.'

The conflict began when the US and Israel attacked Iran in late February. Since then, Iran has retaliated by launching missiles and drones at Israel and Gulf Arab states hosting US military assets and has attacked dozens of commercial vessels in and around the Strait of Hormuz, through which a fifth of global oil and gas shipments usually pass. At least 25 seafarers have been killed across the Middle East since the start of the war, when the strait was effectively closed due to Iranian attacks and a US naval blockade. A preliminary US-Iran agreement to end the conflict and reopen the strait collapsed in June after Iranian attacks on shipping resumed and the US reinstated its blockade.

The recent exchanges of fire and deadlock in negotiations have raised fears of a major escalation in the nine-month-old war. Kuwait's foreign ministry condemned the drone attacks, saying they 'flagrantly violated Kuwait's sovereignty, directly threatened its security and stability, and constituted a grave breach of international law.' Qatar, which has mediated stalled indirect talks between Iran and the US, called the attacks 'a dangerous escalation that threatens to complicate efforts aimed at containing tensions.' Saudi Arabia's foreign ministry condemned the attacks on Kuwait and Bahrain 'in the strongest terms' and denounced the attack on the UAE tanker. Arab League chief Nabil Fahmy said the repeated attacks reveal 'a continuous escalation pattern that threatens to widen the circle of confrontation.'
