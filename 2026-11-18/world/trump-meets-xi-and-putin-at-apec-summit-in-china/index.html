Trump meets Xi and Putin at APEC summit in China

International & World desk

US President Donald Trump shook hands and exchanged greetings with Russian President Vladimir Putin before a group photo at the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) summit in Shenzhen, China. Xi Jinping personally greeted Trump on the airport tarmac, reciprocating the welcome Trump extended to Xi during his visit to the US in September. A trilateral meeting among the three leaders is scheduled for Thursday and is expected to last less than one hour, according to Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov. The Associated Press, citing an anonymous US official, reported that Trump also plans to hold a separate bilateral meeting with Putin on Thursday, which would be their first since talks in Alaska in 2025 failed to reach a deal on the war in Ukraine.

Before arriving in China, Trump was in Manila, where he met with Philippine President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. at the Malacañang presidential palace. During a meeting ahead of lunch, Marcos revealed he had arranged for fried chicken from the Filipino fast-food chain Jollibee to be delivered to the US delegation as a late-night snack, praising Trump for 'working very hard.' Trump told Marcos he knew his 'parents very well' and praised the palace, saying, 'I love what you’ve done here.' Trump also announced that the US had concluded a 'very big trade deal' with the Philippines, though he provided no details and Manila did not announce any agreement.

Xi Jinping told Trump he hoped their talks would help advance a 'constructive, strategic and stable relationship' between China and the US, based on 'respect, fairness and reciprocity,' according to China’s state news agency Xinhua. Xi also recalled Trump’s personal welcome at the airport during his visit to the US in September, saying China’s own welcome reflected its tradition of reciprocity. Earlier, Xi met with Putin and was quoted by Chinese state media as saying China-Russia relations are 'unshaken by storms and steady as a mountain,' setting 'an example of a new type of major-country relationship.'

On the sidelines of the summit, Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney challenged Putin’s earlier assertion that sanctions no longer work, telling reporters: 'Mr Putin can ask his financial institutions, he can ask his oligarch friends, he can ask his hockey players whether or not sanctions are a thing of the past. I think he’ll find out that they’re very real, they’re biting.' Mexican President Claudia Sheinbaum went viral on Chinese social media for taking an economy flight to the summit, while Trump’s visit came as a series of technical outages back in the US left his administration scrambling to calm the public. Trump had been nonchalant about the outages, saying from Manila, 'It’s fine,' and suggesting it was a consequence of being a nation that is 'big and powerful.'

The APEC summit in Shenzhen, China’s tech hub, opened with Xi calling for freer trade and greater cooperation on artificial intelligence. Trump arrived seeking to reinforce America’s status as the world’s leading technology power. It remains unclear whether North Korean leader Kim Jong-un will attend the talks, though Trump has floated the possibility. US Secretary of State Marco Rubio said the president was willing to engage but did not confirm whether a meeting had been set.
