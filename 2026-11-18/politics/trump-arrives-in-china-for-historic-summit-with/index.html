Trump arrives in China for historic summit with Xi and Putin

Politics desk
✓ Facts
  • President Trump arrived in Shenzhen, China on Wednesday for the APEC summit, where Xi Jinping personally greeted him on the airport tarmac, reciprocating Trump's September greeting of Xi at Joint Base Andrews.
  • Russian President Vladimir Putin arrived in Shenzhen earlier Wednesday and was greeted by senior CCP official Cai Qi rather than Xi personally; Putin held about two hours of talks with Xi before Trump's arrival.
  • Trump and Putin exchanged greetings at a reception Wednesday evening, their first in-person contact since the August 2025 Alaska talks on Ukraine, which did not produce a breakthrough.
  • A U.S.-China-Russia trilateral meeting between Trump, Xi and Putin is scheduled for Thursday, described as the first such three-way in-person meeting; Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said no more than an hour has been allocated for it.
  • A separate one-on-one Trump-Putin meeting is also scheduled for Thursday, confirmed by a U.S. official; potential topics across the meetings include the Ukraine war, nuclear weapons/proliferation, trade and artificial intelligence.
  • A senior U.S. official said before the trip that no formal denuclearization agreement is expected from the trilateral meeting, calling it an 'entry point' and 'first conversation.'
  • During Trump's trip, technical outages affecting Microsoft, the FAA, JPMorgan Chase and the NSA's communications network occurred in the U.S.; Secretary of State Marco Rubio said there was 'zero' evidence the outages were connected to foreign actors.
  • Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang was recently removed after admitting to an affair with a woman U.S. intelligence alleged was a Chinese agent, a claim China has denied.
○ Analysis

The Shenzhen summit marks the first in-person meeting between Trump and Putin since their inconclusive Alaska talks, and the first time the leaders of the U.S., China and Russia have met together in person — an unprecedented diplomatic configuration given China and Russia's status as America's principal geopolitical rivals. The choreography carries its own significance: Xi's tarmac greeting of Trump, mirroring Trump's earlier welcome of Xi in Washington, signals Beijing's interest in maintaining cordial relations even as trade tensions, AI competition and the Nvidia-linked espionage allegation complicate the relationship. Analysts and U.S. officials have tempered expectations, framing the trilateral session — limited to roughly an hour, per the Kremlin — as a preliminary conversation rather than a venue for substantive agreements on Ukraine or nuclear arms. Concurrent U.S. tech outages, while described by officials as unconnected incidents tied to routine security patching, created a backdrop of uncertainty just as Trump sought to project American strength and technological leadership abroad.

Steelman

Across all coverage, the consistent throughline is a gap between the summit's historic optics — an unprecedented three-way meeting and elaborate diplomatic choreography — and the modest, explicitly-acknowledged expectations for substantive outcomes, with even U.S. officials calling it merely a 'first conversation.'

Implications

Thursday's meetings will test whether personal diplomacy between Trump, Xi and Putin can translate into movement on Ukraine, nuclear arms control or trade, though officials on multiple sides have downplayed the odds of a formal breakthrough. Ukrainian President Zelensky's concern about being excluded from any Ukraine-related discussions, along with unresolved questions about AI competition and chip access highlighted by the Nvidia episode, suggest that outcomes from Shenzhen will likely be assessed less by immediate agreements than by their effect on the trajectory of U.S. relations with both Beijing and Moscow in the months ahead.
