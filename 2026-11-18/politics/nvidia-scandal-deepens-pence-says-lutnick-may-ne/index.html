Nvidia scandal deepens: Pence says Lutnick 'may need to go'; Nvidia won't reinstate Huang

Politics desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum
✓ Facts
  • Nvidia co-founder Jensen Huang stepped down as CEO amid allegations of extramarital affairs, including one with Alice Huang, a Chinese businesswoman U.S. intelligence reports allege has ties to China's Ministry of State Security
  • Nvidia chair Harvey Jones said the board 'does not intend to reinstate' Huang, after Huang said he would be 'honoured' to return as CEO
  • Nvidia reported results for the three months to Oct. 25: sales of $115.8bn (profits doubling to $65bn, data-center sales doubling to $107bn), with shares rising 4.8% in after-hours trading
  • Interim CEO Colette Kress said Nvidia is no longer shipping Hopper-based (H200) chips to China, without detailing why
  • The Trump administration this week imposed new export restrictions on Chinese firms accessing advanced U.S. chips via third countries such as Malaysia, Thailand and Brazil
  • Former Vice President Mike Pence said on CNN's 'The Source' that Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick 'may need to go' over his handling of Nvidia's China chip sales and the alleged honey-trap matter
  • Outgoing Sen. John Cornyn (R-TX) said 'the buck stops with [Trump]' when asked whether the White House contributes to a perception of corruption tied to the scandal
  • President Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping are scheduled to meet this week in Shenzhen
○ Analysis

The controversy sits at the intersection of corporate governance, U.S.-China tech rivalry, and domestic political accountability. Nvidia's advanced chips, especially the H200, have been central to a years-long debate over whether exporting cutting-edge AI hardware to China strengthens its military and surveillance capacity or merely cedes commercial ground to Chinese rivals. The Trump administration's 2025 decision to allow H200 sales was seen as a rare thaw in that standoff, making Huang's ouster over an alleged espionage-linked affair — and Beijing's furious denials — politically combustible just as new export curbs were announced and a Trump-Xi summit approached. For Nvidia, the business fundamentals remain strong: record sales and profits suggest the company's momentum is not immediately dependent on Huang personally, even as the board faces pressure over succession and the China-exposure fallout. For Washington, the episode has reopened bipartisan scrutiny of how export policy was set and enforced, with questions about oversight (Commerce Secretary Lutnick) and about the administration's broader posture toward China converging on a single scandal just as high-stakes diplomacy with Beijing unfolds in Shenzhen.

◐ Debate

Democratic

  • Rep. Ted Lieu (D) said the Trump administration had been '1,000%' too deferential to Nvidia, putting national security at risk
  • Rep. Zoe Lofgren (D) said Congress would likely move to further restrict AI chip sales to China

Republican

  • Former Vice President Mike Pence (R) said Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick 'may need to go' over his handling of the Nvidia matter
  • Sen. John Cornyn (R) said 'the buck stops with [Trump]' regarding perceptions of administration corruption tied to the scandal
Steelman

Despite differing motives, both parties are converging on the same underlying worry — that U.S. chip-export policy toward China has been shaped more by access and lobbying than security review, a concern now voiced by Republicans like Pence and Cornyn as well as Democrats like Lieu and Lofgren.

Implications

The scandal's immediate fallout — Nvidia's refusal to reinstate Huang, halted Hopper shipments to China, and new third-country export restrictions — will test whether the Trump-Xi summit can stay focused on trade rather than security disputes, while in Washington, bipartisan criticism of Commerce Secretary Lutnick and the administration's chip-export oversight could prompt congressional hearings or further legislative restrictions on AI chip sales to China.
