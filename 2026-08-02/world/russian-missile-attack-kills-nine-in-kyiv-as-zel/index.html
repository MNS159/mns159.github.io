Ukraine War

Russian missile attack kills nine in Kyiv as Zelensky warns air defenses overwhelmed

International & World desk

A Russian missile and drone barrage struck Kyiv before dawn on Saturday, killing at least nine people and wounding more than 30, Ukrainian officials said. The attack included 35 missiles and 185 attack drones, according to President Volodymyr Zelensky, who said only one of 27 ballistic missiles was intercepted because Ukraine is running out of Patriot air defense missiles — the only weapon capable of stopping such projectiles.

The bombardment hit five areas of the capital, setting fires and trapping residents in damaged buildings. The heaviest toll was in the Darnytskyi district, where seven people died and 14 were injured, including two children, Ukraine's state emergency service reported. In the Solomianskyi district, falling debris and a blaze partially destroyed a five-story apartment building; two people died and eight were injured, including two children. A police officer was among the dead. Lithuania's foreign ministry said its embassy in Kyiv sustained damage from nearby missile strikes.

Zelensky renewed his appeal for Western air defense supplies, writing on social media that "every package of ballistic missile interceptors saves the lives of our people" and that the shortage of interceptors "only encourages Russia to carry out such deadly strikes." The attack came two days after a separate Russian strike killed at least 10 people elsewhere in Ukraine and followed Zelensky's meeting with President Donald Trump in Washington, where he urged the U.S. to grant Ukraine a license to produce its own Patriot interceptors.

On Friday, Trump said the U.S. had not agreed to allow Ukraine to manufacture Patriot systems, calling it "a hard thing to give away that kind of technology" and expressing concern that Ukraine could someday use the missiles against the U.S. — though he acknowledged that was unlikely. Zelensky said he spoke by phone Friday with Vice President JD Vance and reiterated that air defense, particularly Patriot interceptors, remains a top priority.

Beyond Kyiv, a Russian airstrike killed one person and wounded 19 in the southern city of Kherson. Ukraine continued its own long-range campaign, with Zelensky saying Ukrainian drones struck three oil refineries in Russia's Bashkortostan region, roughly 1,000 miles from the front lines. Ukrainian drones also sank a sanctioned Russian container ship, the Yanina, in the Black Sea. Russia's state nuclear operator Rosatom, which owns the vessel's operating subsidiary, said all 17 crew members were rescued and that the ship was carrying only civilian goods, calling the attack "piracy and robbery."
