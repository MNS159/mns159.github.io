Ukrainian drones strike Russian oil refineries and Wildberries warehouse, killing eight

International & World desk · anchored on Reuters

Ukrainian drones struck multiple oil refineries and a Wildberries e-commerce warehouse across Russia overnight, killing at least eight people, regional governors said on Saturday. Ukraine’s General Staff confirmed it hit an oil refinery in Saratov, the Engels airbase, and an oil depot in Kaluga Oblast, part of a campaign to degrade Russia’s war-fighting capability. In Samara Oblast, a Wildberries warehouse in Novosemeykino was set ablaze; the company lists drone components and body armor on its website, and its banking arm was sanctioned by the European Union in July.

The Saratov refinery processes about 7 million metric tons of crude oil annually and supports Russia’s military-industrial complex, according to the General Staff. Engels airbase houses Tu-95MS and Tu-160 strategic bombers used to launch missile attacks on Ukraine. Saratov Oblast Governor Roman Busargin said two people were killed in the strikes there. Separately, President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Ukrainian drones struck three oil refineries in Bashkortostan, nearly 1,600 km (994 miles) from Ukraine, and that a sanctioned Russian container ship, the Yanina, sank in the Black Sea after being hit by drones. Russia’s state nuclear energy company Rosatom claimed the ship carried civilian goods.

The strikes came as Russia unleashed a massive ballistic missile and drone attack on Kyiv overnight, killing at least nine people and wounding 33, Ukraine’s state emergency service reported. A five-story residential building in the Solomianskyi district was damaged, with two deaths and eight injuries including two children. In the Darnytskyi district seven people were killed and 14 injured. Zelenskyy said Ukraine shot down only one of 27 ballistic missiles because of air-defense shortages and appealed for more Patriot systems. In Kherson, a Russian airstrike killed one person and wounded five.

Other developments over the weekend included the International Atomic Energy Agency reporting that the Russian-occupied Zaporizhzhia nuclear power plant lost off-site power for nearly two hours, the 23rd such incident since the full-scale invasion. In annexed Crimea, power cuts have forced residents to queue for hot meals; a local official in Massandra said her workers served roughly 200 people daily. Lithuania’s foreign ministry said its embassy in Kyiv was damaged by a Russian missile strike, with no injuries, and summoned the Russian embassy representative to protest. Lithuania’s foreign minister said that continued support for Ukraine and sanctions on Russia are needed to end the aggression.
