Trump halts strikes on Iran, says deal outline reached; US urges citizens to leave Middle East

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts slightly center-left

US President Donald Trump said Saturday that he had canceled planned strikes on Iran after being asked by Iran and other Middle Eastern countries to hold off, claiming that the "perimeters of a deal" had been agreed to. In a social media post, Trump said the emerging agreement would include the "Immediate, Complete, and Total OPENING OF THE HORMUZ STRAIT, and an end to Iran's nuclear threat." He added that Israel had agreed to join the US in the commitment to pause strikes, subject to "being able to rapidly make a DEAL."

Iran quickly pushed back on Trump's characterization. Iran's Mehr news agency, citing military officials, called the claim that Tehran had asked the US not to strike "a new lie." Iran's acting Defence Minister, Brigadier General Majid Ebn-e-Reza, said Trump's pullback comment was made "within the context of psychological operations and a war of calculations" and that Iran's armed forces "will neither be taken by surprise nor will we be passive." Iran's wartime military command had warned Saturday that US and other interests across the region could be attacked in tit-for-tat strikes if Washington launched threatened attacks against civilian infrastructure.

The US State Department on Saturday issued a security alert urging Americans across the Middle East to "consider departing, or be prepared to depart should there be escalation." The alert warned that "US diplomatic facilities, including outside the Middle East, have been targeted" and that "Iran and groups supportive of Iran may target other US interests overseas." US embassies in Bahrain, Iraq, Israel, Jordan, Kuwait, Lebanon, Oman, Qatar, Saudi Arabia and the United Arab Emirates reposted the alert, warning of "unexpected escalation."

The announcement came amid reports that Trump could have ordered fresh strikes against Iran as soon as this weekend. US media quoted officials saying the US and Israel were planning one of the harshest bombing campaigns to date against energy infrastructure targets in Iran. At a cabinet meeting Friday, Trump was heard saying, "We'll be hitting them [Iranians] very hard. At some point, they're going to say, 'We just can't take it anymore.'" On Thursday, the US military said it had completed a "heavy wave" of strikes against IRGC targets in response to Iranian attacks on US bases and ships. Iranian state media said three people, including a two-year-old child, were killed in those strikes.

The conflict, which began with US and Israeli strikes on Iran on 28 February, has escalated since a ceasefire in April fell apart in June. A memorandum of understanding reached that month partially reopened the Strait of Hormuz in exchange for lifting a US blockade on Iran's southern ports and a two-month relief from oil sanctions, but all provisions have since been rolled back. The IRGC has continued to strike or turn back ships attempting to use the strait. Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman spoke by phone with Trump before his announcement, stressing the need to prioritize dialogue to de-escalate tensions. Iran's Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi also spoke with his Saudi counterpart Saturday night. Other mediators including Pakistan, Qatar, Oman and Turkiye have been exchanging messages to nudge both sides toward an agreement.

In Tehran, some Iranians expressed skepticism. One resident told Al Jazeera she did not pay attention to the latest cycle of threats but was stressed that urban centers could be hit again. Iran's new supreme leader, Mojtaba Khamenei, who was selected in March after his father Ali Khamenei was killed in the opening salvo of the war, has not made any public appearance amid the ongoing battle over the Strait of Hormuz. A hardline member of parliament called for building underground "governance cities" to protect leaders from aerial bombardment. The UK Maritime Trade Operations reported two maritime incidents off Oman on Saturday, including one in which a tanker was struck by an unknown projectile, and Kuwait's army said it had detected and destroyed hostile drones targeting vital facilities.
