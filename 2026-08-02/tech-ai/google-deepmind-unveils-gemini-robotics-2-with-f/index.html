Google DeepMind unveils Gemini Robotics 2 with full body control

Tech / AI desk

Google DeepMind last week unveiled Gemini Robotics 2, the latest version of its vision-language-action (VLA) model, introducing intelligent whole-body control, advanced dexterity, and multi-robot collaboration. The updated model enables robots to reason through every movement, allowing a humanoid to walk, crouch, stretch, and manipulate objects to clean a cluttered room, and to team up with other robots to finish tasks faster. DeepMind also released Gemini Robotics ER 2, an embodied reasoning model that acts as an agent for communication and multi-step planning, and Gemini Robotics On-Device 2, a VLA optimized to run locally on robotic devices with fast adaptation to new robot embodiments using a few hours of data.

Gemini Robotics 2 expands physical AI from upper-body tabletop tasks to whole-body motions. When controlling Apptronik's Apollo 2 humanoid, the model processes an instruction such as 'put the watering can into the green bin on the bottom shelf,' then walks, picks up the can, steps to the shelves, and places it precisely. DeepMind noted that movement speed still needs improvement but called this an important step toward real-world tasks requiring whole-body coordination. The model also unlocks new dexterity across different end effectors: on the Apollo 2, it controls a five-fingered, 22-degree-of-freedom SharpaWave hand to tie knots or seal a ziplock bag, and on a Franka Duo platform it operates standard two-fingered parallel grippers for complex tasks like tight packing.

To manage complex multi-step tasks, Gemini Robotics ER 2 serves as the robot's high-level brain, processing user instructions, observing the room, reasoning about steps, coordinating with the VLA, and tracking progress. DeepMind said the update enables robots to reliably execute longer task sequences lasting several minutes and involving hundreds of decisions, with improved understanding of when tasks begin and end. The company also introduced multi-robot collaboration, allowing different types of robots to communicate and work together on workflows a single robot could not handle alone. Gemini Robotics ER 2 is now available on Google AI Studio and in private preview on the Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform; the VLA and On-Device models are available to early-access partners.

Gemini Robotics On-Device 2 is designed for applications with low connectivity, operating without network latency or internet. The model is natively multi-embodiment and inherits motion transfer techniques from Gemini Robotics 1.5. DeepMind claimed it can adapt to new bi-arm robot embodiments with just a few hours of adaptation time, typically using fewer than 200 examples, even for embodiments with drastically different shapes, sensors, and degrees of freedom.

DeepMind reaffirmed its commitment to safety, stating that Gemini Robotics 2 advances safety for navigating real-world uncertainty and collaborating alongside humans. The company introduced ASIMOV-Agentic, a new benchmark for agentic safety orchestration and uncertainty resolution that measures an embodied reasoning agent's ability to refuse unsafe tool calls from a VLA and to predict whether a task is possible, proactively requesting human intervention when uncertain. DeepMind said Gemini Robotics ER 2 is its safest robotics model to date in safety constraint following and human proximity benchmarks, capable of detecting when humans are nearby, triggering safety tool calls, and bringing the robot to a safe stop if someone approaches too closely—a key requirement in collaborative safety standards.
