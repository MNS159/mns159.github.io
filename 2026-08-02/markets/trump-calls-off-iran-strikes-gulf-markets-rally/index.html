Trump Calls Off Iran Strikes; Gulf Stocks Rise on De-escalation Hopes

Financial & Markets desk · anchored on Reuters

Gulf stocks rose, with de-escalation hopes and corporate earnings lifting sentiment, according to Reuters. Investor's Business Daily, in its futures coverage, said President Trump had called off Iran strikes, shifting market attention to how equities would react next.

The market was being pulled in two directions: investors were looking for signs of progress toward a US-Iran ceasefire deal, while also evaluating President Trump's progressively heated threats of escalation should Iran fail to reopen the Strait of Hormuz. Reuters described Iran headlines as pulling markets two ways, with escalation threats clashing with ceasefire talk.

The Strait of Hormuz is a strategically important waterway for global oil shipments, which gave the threat of closure outsized importance for markets. The apparent cancellation of strikes reduced the immediate risk of a broader conflict, helping Gulf stocks advance.

What happens next may hinge on official confirmation from Washington and on whether Iran moves to reopen the strait. Any shift between diplomacy and renewed military pressure is likely to drive the next market move.
