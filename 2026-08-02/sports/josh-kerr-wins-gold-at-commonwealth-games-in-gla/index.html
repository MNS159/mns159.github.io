Josh Kerr wins Commonwealth Games mile gold in Glasgow, then spends 45 minutes with fans

Sports desk

Josh Kerr won the men's mile at the Commonwealth Games in Glasgow on Saturday night, crossing the line in 3 minutes 54.12 seconds at Scotstoun Stadium. The 28-year-old Scot, who set a world record of 3:42.66 in London last month, held off Australian prodigy Cam Myers (silver) and former Olympic silver medallist Timothy Cheruiyot (bronze). His childhood friend and Scotland teammate Jake Wightman finished sixth.

The race was a physical, slow-paced affair with no pacers or wave lights. Kerr, who had emerged from fireworks spinning a bottle and allowed his rivals to sprint away before starting, was trapped in the pack for three laps. At the bell he burst to the outside, passed Myers and Cheruiyot with 250 metres to go, and celebrated by conducting the crowd during his final strides before wrapping himself in a Saltire.

After the medal ceremony, Kerr spent about 45 minutes taking selfies and signing autographs for fans. "I'm just trying to create a moment for them," he told BBC Sport Scotland. "There is absolutely nothing stopping any of them going out and doing what I've just done. I believe if you grow up in Scotland, you can be one of the best runners in the history of these distances." He added: "You've got to stay in your lane and realise I'm lucky coming into this championship healthy, ready to go and I'm in the best shape of my life. And to do that in front of a home crowd is very special."

Kerr, who grew up in Edinburgh and now lives in Albuquerque, New Mexico, said the Games meant "everything" to him. During the playing of Flower of Scotland on the podium, he repeatedly stopped singing to compose himself and wiped tears from his eyes. "To have Flower of Scotland playing versus the GB national anthem, it's just different," he said. "Not many times in my career I'll ever have that." He recalled watching the athletics at Hampden Park during the 2014 Commonwealth Games as a child, and said he wanted to inspire young Scots the way Sir Chris Hoy inspired him when Hoy spoke at his school assemblies.

Kerr had been candid about his goals for the summer: breaking the 27-year-old world record and winning Commonwealth gold. "This whole experience has been really fun for me," he said. "The city is buzzing with the Games and it's just nice to be a part of that and really feed off the city." Asked about being considered a sporting standard bearer for Scotland alongside Hoy and Sir Andy Murray, Kerr said: "It's an honour to be spoken in the same sentence as those two legends. I've got a lot of work to do to keep up with them."
