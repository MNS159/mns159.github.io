Dodgers acquire two-time Cy Young winner Tarik Skubal in blockbuster trade with Tigers

Sports desk

The Los Angeles Dodgers acquired two-time reigning American League Cy Young winner Tarik Skubal from the Detroit Tigers late Saturday night in a blockbuster trade ahead of the Aug. 3 trade deadline. The back-to-back defending World Series champions added the top starting pitcher available on the market, a rental who is set to become a free agent after the season. The move solidifies the Dodgers' bid for a third consecutive title, a feat not accomplished since the 1998–2000 New York Yankees.

In exchange, the Dodgers sent three prospects to Detroit: outfielder Zyhir Hope, right-hander River Ryan, and catcher Brady Smith. Hope, considered the headlining prospect in the deal, was originally acquired by the Dodgers from the Chicago Cubs in January 2024 as part of the trade for first baseman Michael Busch and reliever Yency Almonte. Ryan had come to the Dodgers from the San Diego Padres in an earlier deal, and Smith was a third-round pick in the 2023 draft. The connection to the Cubs was highlighted by The Athletic's Jim Bowden, who wrote: 'So the Dodgers acquired Zyhir Hope from the Cubs for Michael Busch and River Ryan from the Padres for Meatt Beatty and drafted Brady Smith in the 3rd round of '23 and turned them into Tarik Skubal. Got it.' Hope has pushed for a roster spot after a monster July in the minors, batting .372 with 10 home runs, six steals and a 1.184 OPS.

The trade was widely anticipated, as the Dodgers possess the prospect depth and financial resources to make such moves without crippling their long-term outlook. Skubal joins a rotation that already includes Yoshinobu Yamamoto, Tyler Glasnow, and Blake Snell, with Shohei Ohtani sidelined from pitching due to a balky knee. Over his past six starts, Skubal posted a 1.95 ERA, 0.78 WHIP and 12.2 strikeouts per nine innings. CBS Sports’ Scott White had predicted the outcome, noting that the Dodgers are 'the organization that's landed every top prize of the past few years, the one with farm system that never seems to run out and the financial firepower to make homegrown talent entirely optional.' The trade leaves the Dodgers poised to enter the postseason with a rotation of Yamamoto, Skubal, Glasnow and Snell once the latter two return from injury.

The Tigers, now in rebuilding mode, restock their system with three prospects, including Hope, who is expected to be ready for the majors next season, and Ryan, who is recovering from a hamstring strain but likely to make his debut for Detroit later this season. With Skubal moved, Detroit may now deal other rental starters such as Casey Mize, who has been scratched from a start to protect his trade value. For the rest of the league, the move underscores the competitive imbalance in baseball, as only the Dodgers could afford to part with top prospects for a rental without significant long-term pain. Other contenders now turn to remaining available starters, including Angels' Reid Detmers, Mets' Clay Holmes and Freddy Peralta, Blue Jays' Kevin Gausman and Shane Bieber, and Giants' Robbie Ray.
