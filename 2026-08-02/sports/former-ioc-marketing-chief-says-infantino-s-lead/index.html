Former IOC marketing chief: Infantino's leadership 'radioactive,' reign 'over' after FIFA investment plan backlash

Sports desk

FIFA president Gianni Infantino's leadership has become "radioactive" and his 10-year reign as head of world football "is over," according to Michael Payne, former head of marketing at the International Olympic Committee. Payne's comments to AFP come after a leaked FIFA plan to raise up to $4.2 billion through private investment — based on a $20 billion valuation of a new commercial subsidiary called the FIFA Forward Enterprise (FFE) — sparked a furious global reaction, leading Infantino to shelve the scheme on Saturday.

The proposed FFE was intended to run events such as the World Cup and Club World Cup and included one-time payments of $20 million to each of FIFA's 211 member associations, who each hold one vote. The plan, which Payne described as a result of "gross hubris," quickly drew opposition from major football confederations. European governing body UEFA said it had lost "confidence" in Infantino over what it called a "shabby deal," while CONCACAF — which includes the U.S., Canada, and Mexico, the three recent co-hosts of the World Cup — called for "a comprehensive reckoning with this presidency."

"I do not think we have ever seen such a fast fall from grace by a sports leader," said Payne, who helped modernize the IOC's brand and finances over nearly two decades. "One week ago, Infantino was riding high from delivering a very successful World Cup, and expected to run unopposed for election next year to continue his mandate as president of FIFA. Now Infantino will struggle to survive the month." Payne added, "I don't see how any amount of lobbying or 'persuasion' of the smaller countries can save him. His leadership has become radioactive, and if he stays there will be total civil war in global football, with breakaways by leading nations and boycotts. It's over."

Terrence Burns, a brand and marketing strategist who worked on two successful World Cup hosting bids, told AFP he had never seen "anything mismanaged and bungled so badly by people so smart." Burns said Infantino's position is "precarious" but not beyond repair, noting that in FIFA elections, confederations do not vote as a bloc. "It's the individual associations that receive development money from Zurich," he said. Burns identified Africa (CAF) and Oceania as potentially pivotal: "If CAF stays with him, he can lose Europe, Asia, and CONCACAF in public and still win in the hall. If CAF moves, it's game over."

Burns added that Infantino has lost "inevitability" — a key asset that kept him unopposed in 2019 and 2023. "I think that calculation changed this week, before the 18 November nomination deadline," he said. Meanwhile, UEFA president Aleksander Ceferin emerged as a winner, according to Burns: "He played it well. UEFA carried the fight collectively while he issued no personal statement at any point, which means he can now stand, or not stand, for the FIFA presidency without having spent anything on the argument. The boycott threat was aggressive and credible, which is a rare pairing."
