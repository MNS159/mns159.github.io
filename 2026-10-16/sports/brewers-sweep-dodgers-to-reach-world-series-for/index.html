Brewers sweep Dodgers to reach World Series for first time since 1982

Sports desk

The Milwaukee Brewers swept the Los Angeles Dodgers in four games in the National League Championship Series, sending the two-time defending World Series champions home and clinching their first World Series appearance since 1982. Thursday's Game 4 loss ended the Dodgers' run of eight consecutive postseason series wins dating back to 2024, a stunningly swift exit for a team that had been baseball's dominant club of the decade.

Shohei Ohtani, the Dodgers' two-way star, played through a difficult second half and postseason, and after the loss he addressed his health and whether he might need surgery. He was placed on the injured list in mid-September with right biceps inflammation and returned for the final week of the regular season, but he managed just a .630 OPS in eight playoff games. His second-inning home run in Game 4 was his first since Aug. 18 and his only extra-base hit of the postseason. Asked whether he might need surgery, Ohtani said through an interpreter, "Hard to tell at this point," adding that the team would analyze the situation in the offseason. He also said he plans to pitch in 2027: "Yes, definitely. Looking forward to having a better season next year."

The Dodgers' exit has put the roster under scrutiny. According to CBS Sports, Los Angeles fielded the oldest collection of position players in baseball this season when weighted by playing time, including Will Smith (31), Ohtani (32), Mookie Betts (34), Teoscar Hernández (34), and Freddie Freeman (37). The club could look to move on from some longtime contributors, including Teoscar Hernández, Enrique Hernández, Max Muncy, and bullpen arms such as Blake Treinen and Alex Vesia, in an effort to get younger and more athletic, though free-agent market dynamics and contracts complicate some decisions.

Another pressing question is the future of Tarik Skubal, the team's major trade-deadline addition. Skubal declined to discuss his plans after Game 4, saying, "I'm not going to comment on my future really at all. Today sucks, you know, and that's kind of where my head's at." The Dodgers have the financial resources to re-sign him, according to CBS Sports, but they already have Tyler Glasnow, Blake Snell, and Yoshinobu Yamamoto returning, with Ohtani expected back on the mound as well. Whether a new collective bargaining agreement or other spending constraints affect the club's ability to make a competitive offer remains an open issue.

For the Brewers, the sweep was a breakthrough after more than four decades without a pennant. The Dodgers, meanwhile, face the first real uncertainty of their recent dynasty, with an aging core, Ohtani's health questions, and a pivotal offseason ahead.
