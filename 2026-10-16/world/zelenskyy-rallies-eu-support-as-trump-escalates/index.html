Ukraine War

Zelenskyy rallies EU support as Trump escalates criticism over Ukraine's oil refinery strikes

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts slightly center-left

President Volodymyr Zelenskyy on Friday rallied European Union support in Brussels as his relationship with U.S. President Donald Trump deteriorated sharply. Trump, in a Newsmax interview, called Zelenskyy "not a good guy for us to deal with" and accused him of not wanting to settle the war. The U.S. president also branded Ukraine's renewed strikes on Russian oil refineries a "disgrace," saying they drive up global oil prices. Zelenskyy, standing alongside European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen and European Council President António Costa, said he was "only standing up for Ukraine's interests" and could not accept a recent U.S.-Russia diesel deal that he called an investment in prolonging the war.

The summit's agenda focused on funding for Ukraine and preparing for a fifth winter since Russia's full-scale invasion. Von der Leyen said the EU had supported Trump's proposed energy ceasefire but noted that Russia "did not respect it, not a single minute," and argued that sanctions on Russia "[had] to increase." Zelenskyy said Ukraine wanted the war to end "as quick as possible" but that Russia was not showing the same desire. Deadly attacks continued overnight: a Russian drone strike killed several people in Kharkiv, and about 270,000 households lost power after strikes in the western Volyn region.

Tensions between Washington and Kyiv have escalated over the diesel deal and restrictions placed on the Starlink internet network, which is critical for Ukraine's military. On Tuesday night, when the network was slowed, deadly Russian strikes hit Ukraine with no prior warning, residents said. Zelenskyy called the slowdown a "very dangerous and unpleasant surprise" and said speeds were still not fully restored. The Trump administration has previously warned it could withhold intelligence-sharing if Kyiv does not stop hitting Russian oil refineries.

At a news conference after the summit, Zelenskyy was asked by Sky News if he felt Trump was using him as a scapegoat for his own political problems. He remained silent for 15 seconds, then broke into a grin, prompting laughter from journalists. "This is my answer," he said. The Kremlin later agreed with Trump's assertion that Zelenskyy did not want to make a deal with Russia—the same accusation Kyiv has leveled at Moscow.

In a speech to EU leaders, Zelenskyy warned against allowing Russian oil and gas to "tempt the world into looking the other way" and called for a strong 22nd EU sanctions package. He laid out Ukraine's financial needs, saying the war would cost 155 billion euros next year and that Kyiv needs at least 70 billion euros more from the EU for defense, weapons, and resilience. He also thanked European leaders who he said were "trying to keep Europe and the United States together," expressing hope that most Americans still want to be allies. "At the end of the day, the American people will have their say," he said.
