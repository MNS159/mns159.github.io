Ukraine strikes Russian oil refinery after Moscow breaks energy ceasefire

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

Ukraine struck a major oil refinery in Russia's Ryazan region early Thursday, setting it ablaze, hours after President Volodymyr Zelensky said Kyiv would resume such attacks because Moscow had not honored a U.S.-brokered energy ceasefire. Russia's Defense Ministry said it intercepted four missiles and dozens of drones, with falling debris causing a fire at the refinery about 300 kilometers (180 miles) southeast of Moscow. A senior Ukrainian official, speaking on condition of anonymity, confirmed Ukraine had hit the refinery.

Zelensky said he had restricted Ukrainian strikes on Russian oil infrastructure for three days at Washington's request, while Russia continued striking Ukraine's energy facilities, leaving hundreds of thousands of people without power. The Kremlin never formally agreed to the ceasefire announced by President Donald Trump on Sunday. "We're done," Zelensky told the Financial Times, adding that he would now call off strikes on Russian oil infrastructure—which Kyiv says are vital to cutting revenue for Russia's war machine—only if the U.S. supplied more Patriot air defense missiles. Ukraine has faced a shortage of those U.S.-made defenses since the Iran war consumed stockpiles.

Zelensky accused the U.S. of withdrawing intelligence-sharing to pressure Kyiv into ending attacks and said Ukrainian officials had been told by U.S. counterparts, "If you will not stop [the strikes], we will block intelligence." He also said Washington imposed restrictions on Starlink internet terminals, which Ukraine relies on throughout its war effort to guide drones and protect its skies. Ukrainian defence sources later told the BBC that some Starlink functionality, specifically "operation while on the move" in the military Starshield service, had been restored, though it remained unclear whether restrictions on drone flight speeds had been fully lifted. Trump denied halting intelligence-sharing, while Starlink owner Elon Musk appeared to deny a technical glitch and suggested it was the Trump administration's doing.

In an interview with British journalist Piers Morgan, Zelensky said he felt betrayed by Trump's deal with Putin, the ceasefire announcement and the Starlink problems. "Really, yes, between us," he said. "We are losing a strong partner," he added, while acknowledging Trump might position himself as "in the middle." Italy's Defence Minister Guido Crosetto told the BBC that any U.S. restriction on intelligence-sharing would be "a complete mistake" because it would deny Ukraine the ability to defend itself. "Peace will be achieved when Putin will be convinced that there is a stalemate," he said, warning that if the U.S. takes resources away, "Putin will think he can win."

Zelensky also criticized a U.S.-Russian agreement last week to release 300,000 tonnes of Russian diesel to the U.S. and global market, saying it effectively invested in Russia's war effort. "The question will be: when will the next deal be? Maybe it will be gas. Definitely it will be gas," he told the Financial Times. Striking a more defiant tone, he said it was not up to him to mend U.S.-Ukraine relations given the events of the past week: "We didn't destroy any relationship." He met NATO chief Mark Rutte, who said they discussed "the importance of implementing an energy ceasefire as well as Nato allies' continuous support for Ukraine."

Amid the dispute, Russia continued large-scale aerial attacks on Ukraine. Zelensky said more than 850 drones had been launched at dozens of cities and communities over the past day, hitting 15 regions and leaving at least 13 people dead on Wednesday. In Kirovohrad, Ukraine's Emergency Services said three people were killed and six injured after nearly 360 drones struck during a 10-hour barrage—the region's largest attack since Russia's full-scale invasion began in 2022. Ukrainian air defenses intercepted 536 drones, but impacts were recorded at 31 locations. Zelensky wrote on social media that Russia had "deliberately escalated these attacks, taking advantage of the easing of sanctions and seeing attempts to pressure Ukraine."

In a separate development, seven people were missing after a drone attack set a chemical tanker on fire off Romania's coast, according to naval officials. Moscow said it had hit two cargo ships carrying military cargo heading to Ukraine's Odesa port, without naming them. Ukrainian officials were still working to clarify the reasons and circumstances behind the recent restrictions on U.S. support, including whether limits on Starlink drone operations had been fully lifted.
