Houthi missile attack on Yemen's presidential palace in Aden kills five

International & World desk · anchored on Reuters

Five people were killed and 16 wounded when Houthi rebels fired five ballistic missiles at the presidential palace in the southern Yemeni port city of Aden on Thursday, officials said, in a rare attack on the seat of the internationally recognised government. The missiles hit the al-Maashiq Palace, where Prime Minister Shaya al-Zindani and other ministers were unharmed, Information Minister Moammar al-Eryani told the BBC. Houthi military spokesman Yahya Sarea said the strike targeted what he called "the Saudi enemy's military operations headquarters in Aden", and Houthi-affiliated media said it hit a military operations headquarters led by Saudi officers as well as weapons depots at the palace.

Yemeni Health Minister Qasim Buhaibeh said the casualties included "those working to guard the site and civilian employees". Yemen's Information Ministry said two members of the prime minister's security detail were killed, while other reports citing government officials put the wounded at 10. Prime Minister Zindani condemned the attack as "cowardly" and a "continuation of their criminal behaviour", saying the government would continue to carry out its duties and that the assault would not weaken the resolve of its forces to continue operations against the Houthis on Yemen's south-western Red Sea coast and other front lines.

The attack came as the Houthis denied launching a missile at Saudi Arabia's Medina region on Thursday, after the Saudi-led coalition said it had intercepted a cruise missile over the area, home to one of Islam's holiest shrines. The Houthis have previously pledged not to attack airports in the holy cities of Mecca and Medina. Saudi authorities separately said a drone was destroyed over the oil-producing Eastern Province. The coalition said the Houthis have carried out 20 attacks this month on Riyadh's King Khaled International Airport, killing 17 people; a strike last Saturday killed 13 and wounded about 300, in the deadliest attack on Saudi Arabia in more than a decade. The UN Security Council condemned the Houthi attacks "in the strongest terms", calling strikes on civilian airports and other facilities "reckless".

Saudi Deputy Foreign Minister Rayed Krimly accused Iran of encouraging the escalation. "Iran continues to arm, train and support the Houthi militia in violation of UN Security Council resolutions," he told reporters, adding: "We think that the escalation by the Houthis at this latest stage of the conflict is encouraged by Iran." There was no immediate response from Iran's government. Tehran has previously denied supplying weapons to the Houthis and said it supports them only politically; Iran's foreign minister has said Tehran is ready to provide "any assistance necessary to facilitate the start of talks" between Saudi Arabia and the Houthis.

Aden has been the interim seat of the internationally recognised government since 2014, when the Iran-backed Houthis ousted the government from the capital, Sanaa. The conflict escalated in 2015, when a Saudi-led coalition of Arab states intervened to try to restore the government's rule. A four-year informal ceasefire began to unravel in July, when the Houthis announced a "maritime embargo" against Saudi Arabia and began launching missiles and drones at Saudi airports, oil facilities and tankers in the Red Sea in retaliation for what they called a Saudi blockade of their territory, and an air strike on Sanaa airport they blamed on the kingdom. The war escalated further in September, when the Houthis seized part of Yemen's south-western Red Sea coast near the Bab al-Mandab Strait, a chokepoint for global shipping, contributing to a rise in oil prices amid the wider war between the US, Israel and Iran. Last week, pro-government forces said they had regained control of territory overlooking the strait after a counter-offensive supported by Saudi air strikes. Yemen's health ministry says at least 1,282 people have been killed and 5,890 injured in the fighting since 6 August; the UN says another 201,000 people have been displaced and that there has been an alarming surge in suspected cholera cases.

Elsewhere in the region, Iran's military said an oil tanker caught fire in the Gulf of Oman on Thursday, south of the Strait of Hormuz, after trying to pass through a "prohibited" area; the US military had not commented. The incident came a day after Iran's Revolutionary Guards said a cargo vessel sank in the strait after striking a mine. Singapore's Maritime and Port Authority said the Singapore-flagged container ship GFS Pride was hit by "unknown projectiles" on Tuesday and sank — CNN cited expert assessment that a drone strike likely caused the sinking — while a second vessel, the GFS Giselle, was damaged but reached safe anchorage. The authority called the attacks "unprovoked, unjustifiable, and a breach of international law". According to the International Maritime Organization, the GFS Pride was one of only two ships sunk among 114 reported incidents in the Strait of Hormuz since the US launched its war with Iran in February.
