US envoys arrive in Moscow to revive Ukraine peace push; limited ceasefire begins

International & World desk · anchored on Reuters

Two of US President Donald Trump’s top envoys, Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner, arrived in Moscow on Saturday to revive stalled efforts to end the war in Ukraine, as both sides agreed to a three-day limited ceasefire. The envoys were met at Vnukovo Airport by Russian diplomat Kirill Dmitriev, according to Russian state media. They are scheduled to hold talks in Moscow before traveling to Kyiv on Sunday for their first visit to Ukraine since Trump returned to office, Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy confirmed.

Trump told reporters on Friday that the envoys were bringing a proposal to end the war, but efforts to broker a peace deal have so far failed to deliver a breakthrough. The visit comes as the conflict, now in its fifth year, has intensified with both sides escalating aerial attacks. Russia’s President Vladimir Putin ordered a three-day halt to strikes on the capital Kyiv starting at midnight Saturday, and Ukraine’s military also received a ceasefire order for the same period, covering ground, air, and sea operations, according to Ukrainian media. A separate local ceasefire was brokered by the International Atomic Energy Agency to enable repairs at the Russian-controlled Zaporizhzhia nuclear power plant, which has been without external power since August 20.

Despite the ceasefire announcements, Russian attacks continued overnight. In the Dnipropetrovsk region, a strike on a civilian enterprise in Kamianske killed five people and injured five others, Governor Oleksandr Hanzha said. The victims were employees at the site — a locksmith, an electrician, a plumber, and a machinist. Zelenskyy said Russia carried out “demonstrative” strikes on airports in Kyiv and Boryspil overnight, linking the attacks to the US envoys’ planned visit. “Clearly, these Russian strikes on the airports are a reaction to the discussions and preparations for the possibility of the US side using an aircraft to travel to Ukraine,” he wrote on social media. He added that Ukraine would adjust its operations regarding Russian airspace next week but pledged no threats to diplomacy.

Additional overnight attacks caused injuries in other regions. In the Odesa region, three people were injured, including a 17-year-old boy. In Mykolaiv, a 17-year-old girl was injured in a drone attack on a shopping centre. In the Kyiv region, two women were injured and hospitalized after a strike on a residential building in Boryspil, said regional military administration head Tymur Tkachenko. Russia’s Defense Ministry said it intercepted and destroyed 53 Ukrainian fixed-wing drones overnight, including several over the Moscow region.

The peace push has lost momentum as US attention has been focused on its war with Iran over the past six months, according to NPR. Previous talks brokered by the US and European allies ended without a breakthrough, with key disagreements remaining on territory and security guarantees for Ukraine. Trump attributed the failure to a “personality problem” between the warring sides, saying, “President Zelenskyy and President Putin truly hate each other.” The envoys’ visit follows a rare trip to Moscow by CIA Director John Ratcliffe just over a week ago, which Trump described as “semi-routine.”
