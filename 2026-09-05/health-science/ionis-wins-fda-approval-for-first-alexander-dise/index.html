FDA Approves Ionis Drug Zanvastro, First Therapy for Alexander Disease

Health & Science desk

The FDA has approved zilganersen, a treatment for Alexander disease that will be marketed as Zanvastro, making it the first authorized therapy for the ultra-rare neurodegenerative condition and Ionis Pharmaceuticals' first wholly owned neurology drug. The Thursday decision covers both children and adults. Ionis estimates about 300 patients in the United States have Alexander disease, which occurs in roughly one per one million to three million people worldwide.

Alexander disease is caused by mutations in the gene that encodes glial fibrillary acidic protein (GFAP). Normal GFAP supports astrocytes in the central nervous system, but mutated versions accumulate in those cells, leading to nerve damage that can cause seizures, developmental delays, muscle weakness, and increased pressure in the brain. Zanvastro is an antisense oligonucleotide that binds to and degrades GFAP pre-messenger RNA, reducing production of the disease-driving protein. It is administered by intrathecal injection every three months.

Approval was supported by a placebo-controlled pivotal trial of 49 participants. The primary endpoint was a 10-meter walk test at 61 weeks, in which treated patients achieved statistically significant and clinically meaningful stabilization of gait speed, while the control group showed deterioration. Blood tests indicated the drug engaged its target and modulated the underlying disease process. Adverse reactions were mild or moderate; the most common were vomiting, back pain, and cough.

Ionis will price Zanvastro at $285,000 per dose, or about $1.14 million per year before insurance rebates or discounts. William Blair analyst Myles Minter noted the price is higher than expected but does not anticipate pushback from payers given the unmet need in this ultra-orphan population; the bank models peak U.S. sales of $160 million in 2040. Chief Global Product Strategy Officer Kyle Jenne said the company is working to get the drug into distribution channels and that it will become available shortly.

Zanvastro is the third wholly owned Ionis drug to reach the market, following Tryngolza for familial chylomicronemia and severe hypertriglyceridemia, and Dawnzera for hereditary angioedema. Previously, Ionis partnered out its neuroscience drugs Spinraza and Qalsody to Biogen. In June, Recordati licensed ex-U.S. rights to the Alexander disease drug for $30 million upfront plus royalties, and will handle regulatory submissions outside the United States.

Ionis also received a priority review voucher with the approval, which can be used to accelerate review of a future rare pediatric disease drug or sold. CEO Brett Monia said during a conference call that the company is weighing its options and noted the voucher could apply to an asset in Ionis's pipeline, which currently includes eight medicines in clinical development. The most advanced wholly owned candidate is obudanersen, an antisense oligonucleotide in Phase 3 for Angelman syndrome, with data expected in the second half of 2027. Ionis reported a cash position of about $2.1 billion as of the end of the second quarter.
