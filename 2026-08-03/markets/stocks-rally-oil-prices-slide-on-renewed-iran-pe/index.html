Stocks Rally, Oil Prices Slide on Renewed Iran Peace Hopes; Yen Strengthens After Intervention

Financial & Markets desk · anchored on Reuters

Wall Street's main indexes rose more than 1% on Monday, the first trading day of August, as signs of de-escalating U.S.-Iran tensions sent oil prices sharply lower and boosted investor sentiment. The tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite led the gains, rising 2.02% to 25,886.13, followed by the S&P 500, which gained 1.29% to 7,586.46, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average, which added 0.98% to 52,998.45. Both the Dow and S&P 500 flirted with record highs during the session.

Oil prices fell sharply after U.S. President Donald Trump said he called off a fresh attack on Iran in the hope of sealing a quick deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz. U.S. crude dropped 6.07% to $79.53 a barrel, while Brent crude fell 4.88% to $83.64. Trump posted on Truth Social, 'Get to work, everybody, and get it DONE,' regarding a potential deal. However, Iran said on Monday there were no talks under way with the U.S. and no plans for any meetings. 'The sharp drop in oil prices, due to Trump's cancelling severe attacks against Iran and hopes of a diplomatic resolution, set the ball rolling this morning,' said Peter Cardillo, chief market economist at Spartan Capital Securities.

Optimism over earnings also underpinned equities. More than 300 S&P 500 companies have reported earnings so far, with roughly 85% beating expectations, according to LSEG data. Amazon.com rose 5.3%, pushing its market value above $3 trillion for the first time, helped by strong earnings and signs that the AI boom is driving demand for its cloud-computing services. Alphabet and Meta rebounded 4.7% and 6.7%, respectively, from steep declines in July. Apple, however, shed 1.6%. The AI-linked stocks Nebius and CoreWeave each gained over 13%.

In currency markets, the yen strengthened against the euro and dollar after Japan and the U.S. confirmed coordinated yen-buying intervention. Japan's finance ministry said it 'will not hesitate to take further action.' Trump said the U.S. was helping Japan prop up the yen as a sign of friendship and to help the world economy. The dollar weakened 0.44% against the yen to 156.87, while the dollar index rose 0.24% to 99.95. The euro was down 0.18% at $1.1506.

Investors also eyed a potential consolidation in the healthcare sector after a report said Bristol Myers Squibb and AstraZeneca held preliminary merger talks that could create one of the world's biggest drugmakers with a combined value of nearly $400 billion. AstraZeneca shares fell, while Bristol Myers gave up early gains to trade down 0.4%. The key report to watch this week is Friday's jobs report, expected to show further employment gains in July. On Monday, the ISM manufacturing purchasing managers' index rose to an expansionary 55.6%, up 2.3 percentage points from June. The earnings calendar includes reports from Palantir Technologies, SpaceX, Advanced Micro Devices, SanDisk, and Western Digital.
