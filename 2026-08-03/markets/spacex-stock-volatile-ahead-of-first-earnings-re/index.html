SpaceX Stock Volatile Ahead of First Earnings Report; Company Plans Louisiana Spaceport

Financial & Markets desk

SpaceX (NASDAQ: SPCX) is scheduled to report its first quarterly earnings as a public company after the market close on Tuesday, August 4, amid a volatile stock that has lost more than half its value from its intraday high and faces the imminent expiration of lock-up restrictions that could flood the market with additional shares. The stock closed Friday at $108.37, roughly 17% below its $135 IPO price and about 52% off its June 16 peak of $225.64, according to CNBC and Forbes. Options markets are pricing an implied move of roughly 15% following the earnings release, Forbes reported.

The earnings report arrives as the company, which integrated xAI and X in February 2026, now operates as a three-segment enterprise spanning space, connectivity and artificial intelligence, with AI consuming capital at a pace exceeding the other two businesses combined, according to Forbes. Consensus earnings estimates range from a loss of $1.26 per share to a profit of $0.33, reflecting uncertainty around AI investment and monetization. The company's market capitalization currently stands at $1.4 trillion, with a price-to-sales ratio in the 70s, billions of dollars in quarterly cash burn, and nearly twice as much debt as cash, CNBC reported.

Starlink remains the primary value driver, generating $11.4 billion in revenue and $4.4 billion in operating income during 2025, with operating income more than doubling year over year, according to Forbes. The connectivity business is SpaceX's only profitable segment, while its launch business is still losing money, CNBC reported. Investors are expected to focus on customer mix, pricing and margins across Starlink's five distinct businesses — residential, business, maritime, aviation and government — rather than headline subscriber additions, Forbes noted.

AI capital expenditures reached $7.72 billion in the first quarter of 2026 alone, Forbes reported. SpaceX's AI strategy, including its $60 billion acquisition of AI-coding startup Cursor expected to close in the third quarter pending regulatory approval, and its Grok chatbot — which has secured contracts with the U.S. Department of Defense but also sparked probes and lawsuits over deepfake porn — will be under scrutiny, CNBC reported. To generate revenue from its AI buildout, SpaceX has leased excess compute capacity, including a deal with Google worth $920 million per month and agreements with Anthropic and Reflection AI, CNBC said.

The company is also planning a new spaceport in Louisiana as Elon Musk seeks AI talent, according to Investor's Business Daily. The bullish case for SpaceX hinges on Starship, its next-generation fully reusable rocket designed to reduce launch costs and enable rapid expansion of the Starlink constellation and orbital AI compute program, CNBC reported. SpaceX completed its 13th test flight of the approximately 400-foot-tall rocket on July 24, but the booster's landing was not perfect, with only a subset of engines igniting before a 'hard splashdown,' the company said. SpaceX expects Starship to 'commence payload delivery to orbit' in the second half of this year, according to its IPO prospectus.

Weighing on the stock is the impending expiration of lock-up restrictions starting in the coming days, which will allow early investors to sell shares for the first time, CNBC reported. Short sellers have accumulated $8.3 billion in paper profits since the IPO, according to S3 Partners, which called it 'among the most aggressive and quickest bearish builds we have seen in a mega-cap name heading into its first earnings report post-IPO,' CNBC reported. Analysts at Bernstein recommend buying the stock with a $239 price target, writing that 'the quarterly results should not matter' and that what matters is 'the level of confidence projected by management regarding the company's growth path,' CNBC reported. Cantor analysts have a $246 price target, saying earnings 'can meaningfully alleviate some of those pressures,' while New Street Research initiated coverage with a $165 target and called the downturn 'an attractive entry point' for long-term investors, CNBC reported.
