Amazon Hits $3 Trillion Market Cap After Strong Cloud Earnings

Financial & Markets desk

Amazon shares surged roughly 4% to 5% on Monday, pushing the company's market capitalization past $3 trillion for the first time. The record came after the company reported better-than-expected second-quarter earnings last week, driven by a surge in cloud growth on the back of strong artificial intelligence demand.

Amazon Web Services posted $42.2 billion in revenue for the quarter, exceeding analyst expectations of $40.54 billion, according to CNBC. Total company revenue reached $200.61 billion, surpassing the $196.47 billion Wall Street had forecast. Adjusted earnings per share came in at $1.97, beating the consensus estimate of $1.82. The stock's gain marked its best single session since May 5, and shares are up more than 23% year to date.

CEO Andy Jassy told investors on the company's earnings call that capital expenditures are now projected to reach $220 billion this year, up from the $200 billion projected in February, citing rising memory prices tied to the AI buildout. "But even at that amount, we will still not have enough capacity to meet all the demand we have in 2026, and I believe this dynamic will also be true in 2027 too," Jassy said. "In fact, the demand we already have for 2028 is striking." AWS growth hit 36.7% in the quarter, its fastest in 18 quarters, Jassy stated. Amazon also said its AI and chip businesses now have annual revenue run rates of $25 billion.

Amazon joins Apple, Microsoft, Alphabet, and Nvidia in the exclusive group of companies that have ever reached a $3 trillion valuation, according to Yahoo Finance. Amazon reached the $2 trillion threshold for the first time in June 2024, meaning it took a little more than two years to add another trillion dollars in market value. Nvidia currently holds the top spot as the most valuable company in the world, with a market cap approaching $5 trillion.

The strong results from Amazon came alongside positive earnings from other cloud hyperscalers. Microsoft said it saw record cloud revenue, with its Azure business topping $100 billion in annual sales for the first time; Azure revenue grew 43% during the company's fiscal fourth quarter. Alphabet's Google Cloud reported 82% growth. The announcements provided breathing room for hyperscalers, which have faced ongoing questions about their prodigious spending on AI data centers and chips and when they will see healthy returns on those investments.
