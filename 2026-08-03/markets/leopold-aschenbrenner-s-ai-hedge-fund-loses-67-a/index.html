Leopold Aschenbrenner's AI hedge fund loses 67% in July after 4x leverage triggers $30B wipeout

Financial & Markets desk

Leopold Aschenbrenner’s AI-focused hedge fund, Situational Awareness, suffered a 67% decline in its portfolio in July, erasing approximately $30 billion from its peak value of $45 billion, according to multiple reports. The 25-year-old former OpenAI researcher had been up 439% in the first six months of 2026 and had posted cumulative gains of 1,551% since the fund’s launch in 2024, but a market correction combined with aggressive leverage led to the dramatic reversal.

The fund’s assets were concentrated in companies tied to the AI data center buildout, and Aschenbrenner also held short positions in legacy software names such as Adobe. When semiconductor stocks soared earlier in the year, the fund printed billions in gains. But volatility returned to the high-flying names, and market reports indicate that some of Aschenbrenner’s positions carried as much as four times leverage, meaning price moves were amplified fourfold. The resulting margin calls from brokers pushed the fund to the brink of liquidation.

On July 30, Situational Awareness narrowly avoided total collapse by striking a deal to sell $10 billion worth of its public stock to market maker Citadel, according to The New York Times. Aschenbrenner had frantically phoned Wall Street firms to raise cash in the days leading up to the transaction. The fund’s backers had included prominent tech figures such as GitHub CEO Nat Friedman and Stripe co-founders Patrick and John Collison.

In a letter to investors obtained by the Financial Times, Aschenbrenner took “full responsibility” for the losses, writing, “These were very expensive scars, but I am dedicated to ensuring they will be invaluable lessons for our organization and for myself as we move forward.” The fund did not respond to a request for comment by the time of publication. The episode underscores the risks of using high leverage in volatile sectors such as AI infrastructure, where rapid gains can just as quickly turn into catastrophic losses.
