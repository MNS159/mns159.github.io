Russian Embassy blames Ukraine for deadly Moscow restaurant bombing

International & World desk · anchored on AP
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts slightly center-left

An explosion at an upscale Italian restaurant in central Moscow on Saturday evening killed at least three people and injured 21, Russian authorities said, as the Russian Embassy in Rome blamed Ukraine for the attack. The blast occurred shortly before 8 p.m. local time on Kudrinskaya Square at the Balzi Rossi restaurant, located on the ground floor of one of the city's seven Stalin-era skyscrapers. Moscow Mayor Sergei Sobyanin described it as a "brutal terrorist attack" and promised that those responsible would be punished.

Russia's National Anti-Terrorism Committee said a woman carrying a homemade explosive device tried to enter the restaurant but was stopped by a security guard. The device detonated at the entrance, killing the woman, the guard, and one restaurant patron. The business daily Kommersant reported that the bomb, hidden in a gift box and containing about 1 kilogram (2.2 pounds) of explosives with metal balls for lethality, was probably detonated remotely and the woman may not have known what she was carrying. Russian news outlets suggested she was a courier delivering a package to a private function, and that a security guard prevented her from entering before the blast.

The Russian Embassy in Rome posted a statement Monday blaming "Kyiv terrorists" for the attack, saying they wanted to "scare the Italians working in Russia and to show them that they too are in the crosshairs and could become the scoundrels' next victims." The embassy added that the restaurant's Italian chef and his staff were unhurt thanks to the guard's "vigilance." The Russian Foreign Ministry reposted the statement without comment. There was no immediate comment from Ukrainian authorities.

Unconfirmed reports in Russian media said the attack targeted a senior military commander celebrating his birthday at the restaurant. Some identified the commander as Col-Gen. Alexander Chaiko, who led the failed 2022 assault on Kyiv and was recently appointed chief of Russia's aerospace forces. Authorities have not confirmed the guest list or named the intended target. Russia has blamed Ukraine for a series of bombings against senior military officials since its invasion of Ukraine in February 2022, including a shooting that wounded a GRU deputy chief in February and a car bomb that killed a lieutenant general in December of last year. In some cases Ukraine has denied involvement; in the December 2024 scooter-bomb killing of Lt. Gen. Igor Kirillov, Ukraine openly acknowledged responsibility.

The bombing occurred on the same night Ukraine launched drone strikes deep inside Russia, including an attack on an apartment block in the city of Engels that killed two people, and a strike on a Wildberries warehouse in Samara Oblast. Ukrainian authorities also reported Russian strikes: at least one killed and seven wounded in Kherson, seven passengers wounded in a drone strike on a minibus in Dnipro, and at least nine killed and 33 injured in Russian strikes on Kyiv earlier in the weekend. Meanwhile, Russian state television on Sunday morning gave minimal coverage to the Moscow restaurant blast, focusing instead on Russian strikes in Ukraine, paratroopers' and railway workers' days, and news about Donald Trump and Iran.
