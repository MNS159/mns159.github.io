Hundreds flee northern Ethiopia clashes as fears of renewed civil war mount

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

Hundreds of Ethiopians from the Tigray region have crossed into neighboring Sudan after intense fighting erupted over the weekend between Tigrayan forces and the Ethiopian federal army near the border town of Shererina, raising fears that a three-year-old peace deal could unravel. The clashes, which began Saturday morning and continued into Sunday, involved heavy artillery and drones, according to a member of the Tigray People's Liberation Front (TPLF). The death toll was not immediately clear, but Sudanese medical and local sources reported that civilians and wounded fighters fled across the border, and witnesses on the Sudanese side heard explosions, heavy gunfire, and aircraft throughout the fighting.

The confrontations mark one of the most serious breaches of the 2022 African Union-brokered peace agreement that ended a two-year civil war in northern Ethiopia, a conflict that left hundreds of thousands dead, according to an African Union estimate. That deal has not been fully implemented, and the TPLF has since pushed out the interim administration created under the accord, reasserting control over Tigray. Both sides have been massing forces along the border of northern Tigray for months, each accusing the other of seeking to trigger a new war.

The TPLF-led regional administration accused the federal government of launching an "open war" in the Shererina area, saying the offensive violated the peace agreement and was aimed at "subjugating and dividing" the people of Tigray. The federal government and army have not issued an official statement, but Minister Abraham Belay confirmed that clashes occurred. In a social media post, he accused the "TPLF clique" of starting the fighting and warned of "worse consequences" if the conflict continued, while also calling for dialogue, saying disputes "should be resolved through peaceful negotiations rather than war." He added, "Today, it is better to reach out for peace before destruction."

By Sunday evening, a TPLF member reported that the fighting had subsided, saying, "There is a de-escalation." However, the strategic sensitivity of Shererina, a small locality in western Tigray, adds to the risk. The area has been disputed between Tigray and the neighboring Amhara region since the civil war. There are also concerns that the fighting could become a proxy for the Sudanese civil war, with accusations that the warring parties in Sudan are backing opposing sides in Ethiopia.

The United Nations High Commissioner for Refugees (UNHCR) has expressed concern about the border tensions. Residents on the Sudanese side reported hearing explosions and sustained gunfire, and drone or artillery fire caused explosions in several large refugee camps at the border, though casualty numbers were not available. The situation remains fragile as the peace deal's implementation lags and the TPLF and federal authorities continue to trade blame.
