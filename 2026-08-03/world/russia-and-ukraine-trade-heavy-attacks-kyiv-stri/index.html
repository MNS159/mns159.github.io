Russia and Ukraine trade heavy attacks; Kyiv strikes Russian radar systems in Crimea

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts left

At least 17 people were killed as Russia and Ukraine exchanged heavy attacks on Monday, with Ukrainian drone strikes killing at least 14 in Russia and Russian strikes killing three in Ukraine, according to local officials. In Russia’s southern Krasnodar region, six people, including three children, were killed and nearly 40 wounded when drone debris struck the resort village of Arkhipo-Osipovka near Gelendzhik, Governor Veniamin Kondratyev said. In occupied Crimea, the death toll rose to four, with two others injured, according to Moscow-installed head Sergey Aksyonov. Acting Belgorod Governor Alexander Shuvaev reported four civilian deaths and 17 injuries across border settlements. In Ukraine, three people—including an emergency response worker and his eight-year-old son—were killed in a Russian strike on a petrol station in the Kryvyi Rih district of the Dnipropetrovsk region, regional Governor Oleksandr Hanzha said.

Ukraine’s military intelligence agency HUR said it used a Magura V7 uncrewed surface vessel to strike Russian radar systems in occupied Crimea in late July, the first attack of its kind. The operation targeted a command vehicle from the Podlet radar system and a Parol-4 identification friend-or-foe radar interrogator. “From now on, our Maguras threaten the Russian occupiers on land as well — this fundamentally shifts the fight into a new dimension,” HUR said. “Overall, our wartime operations prove that the invaders have no safe haven in Crimea, whether in the waters, the skies over the Black Sea, or on Crimean soil. Crimea is Ukraine.”

Ukrainian drone strikes also hit facilities of the Russian e-commerce giant Wildberries, which Kyiv accuses of playing a key role in Russia’s war effort. A drone struck a warehouse in the village of Khryastovo in the Vladimir region, and a separate attack targeted a Wildberries warehouse in the Samara region, about 800 km from Ukraine’s most advanced military positions, Governor Vyacheslav Fedorishchev said. No casualties were reported in those attacks. Ukrainian forces have struck about a dozen Wildberries sites since July 18, according to reports. Al Jazeera’s Audrey MacAlpine reported that independent watchdogs have found Wildberries sells dual-use items such as used military helmets, bulletproof vests, and fibre optic cables used in drones.

Russia’s Defence Ministry said its forces captured the settlements of Bilyi Kolodiaz and Uspenivka in the Kharkiv region of eastern Ukraine. Russian strikes in Ukraine killed two people in the Dnipropetrovsk region and at least one each in Zaporizhzhia and Kherson, according to regional authorities. A separate Russian strike destroyed a postal terminal in the Kharkiv region, killing a worker, the mail operator Nova Poshta said. Russia’s defence ministry also said it struck infrastructure and two vessels with military cargo in the Ukrainian port of Mykolaiv, as well as a vessel carrying military supplies in the Black Sea.

An EU naval mission led by Italy boarded a tanker from Russia’s so-called “shadow fleet” on Sunday, the second such operation in less than two weeks. Italy’s defence ministry said the Toa Payoh, a tanker subject to EU sanctions, was intercepted west of the Sicilian island of Pantelleria while sailing from Benin to Istanbul. The vessel had switched to a Cameroon registry only last week, a source familiar with the operation said. Maritime authorities launched the inspection to verify the tanker’s right to fly the Cameroon flag and the validity of its registration documents.

The exchange of fire has intensified as the war, now in its fifth year, shows no signs of slowing down. Civilian deaths have increased in recent months, although both sides deny targeting civilians. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy met US President Donald Trump at the White House last week, describing the meeting as “good,” and discussed bolstering Ukraine’s air defences with US Vice President JD Vance on Friday. However, Trump appears to have since backtracked on his promise to grant Ukraine a licence to manufacture Patriot interceptor missiles.
