MLB trade deadline tracker: Skubal to Dodgers leads flurry of moves as Aug. 3 deadline arrives

Sports desk

The first big domino of the 2026 trade deadline fell Aug. 1 when the Los Angeles Dodgers acquired left-handed ace Tarik Skubal from the Detroit Tigers in exchange for outfield prospect Zyhir Hope and right-handed pitching prospects River Ryan and Brady Smith, according to CBS Sports. The Skubal trade marked the most significant deal among a wave of transactions that accelerated as the 6 p.m. ET Monday, Aug. 3, deadline approached, with the muddled standings across nearly every division and wild-card race turning the deadline into a seller's market, CBS Sports reported.

Trades continued to pile up on deadline day. The Dodgers were also involved in an Aug. 3 trade for reliever Brent Honeywell, while other deals included the Miami Marlins sending outfielder Brenton Doyle to an undisclosed team for right-handed pitcher Mason Adams and infielder Carlos Vielma; the Colorado Rockies trading outfielder Jo Adell for catcher Jacob Cozart; the New York Yankees acquiring catcher Ben Rortvedt from the Tampa Bay Rays for right-handed pitcher Chayce McDermott; and the Cincinnati Reds acquiring left-handed pitcher Brent Suter from the Colorado Rockies for infielder Nacho Alvarez Jr., per CBS Sports. The Mets also traded left-handed reliever Brooks Raley to an undisclosed team for right-handed pitcher Luke Gabrysh and outfielder John Spikerman, while the San Diego Padres sent second baseman Luis Arraez and right-handed pitcher Caleb Kilian to the Chicago White Sox for right-handed pitchers Ramon Marquez and Marty Gair.

The New York Yankees were among the teams still weighing their catcher options as of Aug. 3, with Cincinnati Reds backstop Tyler Stephenson — a free agent after the season — viewed as a plausible fit, according to team and league sources granted anonymity to speak freely by The Athletic. Stephenson has been on a tear since Father's Day, hitting .353/.433/.600 with four home runs and nine doubles, but he is considered a fallback option for the Yankees, who would prefer a catcher with more offensive track record, such as the Colorado Rockies' Hunter Goodman or Baltimore Orioles' Adley Rutschman, The Athletic reported.

The Reds, sitting at 53-58 and 5.5 games out of the National League's third wild-card spot, have several trade chips on expiring contracts — including right-hander Brady Singer, first baseman/designated hitter Nathaniel Lowe, left-handed relievers Brock Burke and Caleb Ferguson, and third baseman/DH Eugenio Suárez — but each is considered a backup option by other clubs, per The Athletic. While teams have inquired about shortstop Elly De La Cruz and right-hander Hunter Greene — both under team control through 2029 — the Reds would require an "obvious and overwhelming overpay" to move either, according to team sources familiar with the organization's thinking who were not authorized to speak on the record, as reported by The Athletic. Outfielders JJ Bleday and Dane Myers, each with more team control, could also draw interest from teams needing outfield depth, but the Reds lack an immediate in-house replacement for either in 2027, The Athletic noted.

The deadline's uncertainty has left multiple teams in wait-and-see mode, CBS Sports reported, with clubs needing to determine whether they are genuine playoff contenders or merely beneficiaries of a mediocre first half. The Philadelphia Phillies, Miami Marlins, Boston Red Sox, and New York Mets each face pivotal decisions, with the Red Sox's 15-game winning streak having turned them into buyers while the Mets contemplate how deep a sell-off to conduct, according to CBS Sports.
