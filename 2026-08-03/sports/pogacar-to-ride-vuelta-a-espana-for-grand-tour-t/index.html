Pogacar to ride Vuelta a Espana for Grand Tour treble

Sports desk

Newly crowned five-time Tour de France champion Tadej Pogacar will race in this month's Vuelta a Espana in an attempt to become the ninth rider in history to win all three of cycling's Grand Tours. The 27-year-old Slovenian, who rides for UAE Team Emirates-XRG, is the runaway favourite for the three-week race after winning the Tour de France by more than six minutes in July and the Giro d'Italia by nearly 10 minutes in 2024.

This year's Vuelta begins in Monaco on 22 August and finishes in Granada on 13 September, featuring 21 stages that include time trials, sprints, rolling hills and high mountains. Pogacar, who finished third at the Vuelta as a youngster in 2019, said: 'I'm excited to say I'm going back to La Vuelta. It was my first ever Grand Tour back in 2019 and an amazing experience. Spain is a country I love to visit and race in, and I think the time is right to go back. The motivation is high to finish off the year in a good way and the Vuelta will be a big target. The team is strong and we hope to do good things.'

Pogacar became the joint most successful Tour de France champion of all time last month, alongside Jacques Anquetil, Eddy Merckx, Bernard Hinault and Miguel Indurain. A victory in Spain would make him only the ninth rider to win all three Grand Tours (Giro d'Italia, Tour de France, Vuelta a Espana). Merckx, Anquetil and Hinault (twice) all won La Vuelta during their careers.

Beyond his Grand Tour dominance, Pogacar has won almost all of cycling's biggest races, including the Tour of Flanders (three times), Milan-San Remo (once), the Road World Championships (twice), Il Lombardia (five times) and Liege-Bastogne-Liege (four times). This year he has won six of the seven World Tour races he has entered, finishing second at Paris-Roubaix in April. He is expected to win the Vuelta by an even bigger margin than his Tour de France victory over Belgium's Remco Evenepoel.

After the Vuelta, Pogacar is expected to try to defend his Road World Championships rainbow jersey in Montreal, Canada, just one week later. The sport's top talents in the modern era often prioritise the Tour de France at the expense of the Vuelta and Giro, but Pogacar, who shows no signs of fatigue or weakness, is taking on the rare treble bid.
