Iran denies direct talks with US as Trump threatens condemnation

Politics desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum
✓ Facts
  • US President Donald Trump said new talks aimed at ending the war with Iran would begin on Monday, and that he had called off 'massive' planned strikes on Iran to allow for this.
  • Iran said it is negotiating with Oman - not the United States - to reach 'an understanding' on a temporary route through the Strait of Hormuz to allow commercial shipping to resume, and stated it has no plans to negotiate directly with Washington.
  • After Iran's denial, Trump wrote on Truth Social that Iran's leadership was 'unbelievably duplicitous,' accusing it of requesting talks while publicly denying any US discussions.
  • Trump said the 'perimeters' of a deal had been agreed, including reopening the Strait of Hormuz, but said the strait is controlled by the US and that no ships would reach Iran 'unless a Deal, or Total Surrender, is accomplished.'
  • Trump said Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed Bin Salman, along with the UAE and Qatar, urged him over the weekend not to proceed with planned strikes.
  • Global oil prices fell after Trump's announcement Monday: Brent crude dropped 4.5% to $83.98, and US-traded crude fell 4.6% to $80.74.
  • Vessel-tracking data from Kpler showed 51 ship crossings through the Strait of Hormuz between 30 July and 2 August, a roughly 28% drop from the 71 crossings recorded in the preceding four days.
  • A ceasefire agreed in April collapsed last month; earlier US-Iran talks in Pakistan led by Vice President JD Vance in April also failed to produce a deal, and polling shows the war and its economic effects are broadly unpopular with Americans, including many Republicans, ahead of the November midterms.
○ Analysis

The Strait of Hormuz, through which roughly 20% of the world's crude oil and LNG normally flows, has become the central pressure point in the US-Iran conflict that escalated after strikes began on 28 February. Iran's repeated closures of the strait have driven oil-price volatility, and any reopening - even a temporary, Oman-brokered arrangement - has immediate market effects, as seen in Monday's price drop. The episode fits a recurring pattern in this conflict: Trump has repeatedly announced imminent breakthroughs, only for negotiations to stall or collapse, as happened after the April ceasefire and the Vance-led Pakistan talks. The dispute over who is actually negotiating - Trump implying direct US-Iran talks, Iran insisting it is dealing only with Oman as an intermediary - underscores the ambiguity that has characterized diplomatic efforts throughout the war.

◐ Debate

Democratic

  • Critics could point to the contradiction between Trump's claim of active negotiations and Iran's explicit denial of any US talks as evidence that the administration has overstated diplomatic progress or lacks a clear, verifiable process.
  • Given polling cited in the reporting showing broad American dissatisfaction with the war's handling and economic toll, opponents could argue the administration is pursuing continued brinkmanship - including threats of 'massive' strikes and talk of 'Total Surrender' - that is out of step with public opinion ahead of the midterms.

Republican

  • Supporters could argue that Trump's threat of 'massive' strikes and his hardline stance ('no Deal, or Total Surrender') created the leverage that brought Iran to the table via Oman and produced movement toward reopening the Strait of Hormuz.
  • The reporting notes that 'some conservative strategists' are alarmed by polling on the war's unpopularity ahead of the midterms, reflecting a split within the Republican coalition between those backing Trump's hawkish approach and those worried about the political costs of an unresolved conflict.
Steelman

The core uncertainty cutting across both sides is a credibility gap: Trump describes progress toward a deal while Iran publicly denies any direct talks are occurring, leaving outside observers unable to verify either side's account of where negotiations actually stand.

Implications

Attention will turn to whether Monday's talks - regardless of who formally participates - produce a verifiable reopening of the Strait of Hormuz and further easing of oil prices, or whether the pattern of announced-then-stalled negotiations repeats as it did after April's collapsed ceasefire. The unresolved question of direct US-Iran contact versus Oman-mediated diplomacy will likely shape both markets and domestic political pressure on Trump as midterm-related scrutiny of the war's costs continues.
