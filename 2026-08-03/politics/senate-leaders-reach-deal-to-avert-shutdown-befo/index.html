Senate leaders reach deal to avert shutdown before 2026 elections

Politics desk
✓ Facts
  • Current government funding expires at the end of Sept. 30, 2025, and a full government shutdown would begin Oct. 1 if Congress does not act.
  • Senate Appropriations Committee Chair Susan Collins (R-Maine) and Vice Chair Patty Murray (D-Wash.) announced a bipartisan continuing resolution (CR) deal on Sunday.
  • The Senate CR would keep federal funding at existing levels through Dec. 11, 2025.
  • A Senate procedural vote to advance the bill is scheduled for Monday, with leaders aiming for full passage before the chamber's August recess.
  • The House has already passed its own funding bill, which would extend funding through Dec. 4, and House members have left Washington for August recess, returning in early September.
  • The two chambers will need to reconcile differences between the Senate and House versions before any bill can become law.
  • The Senate bill includes provisions addressing SNAP and WIC food programs, disaster relief, and national security programs, along with restrictions on certain fund transfers and grant-cancellation authority.
  • Susan Collins is facing a competitive re-election campaign in Maine in the 2026 midterms.
○ Analysis

A continuing resolution, or CR, is a stopgap measure that keeps federal agencies funded at current levels when Congress has not finished passing full-year appropriations bills. This deal is designed to prevent a lapse in funding — a shutdown — that would otherwise begin Oct. 1, while buying lawmakers time to negotiate longer-term spending bills after the 2026 midterm elections, when election results could shift the balance of power shaping that next package. The measure still must clear both chambers: the Senate is moving quickly to pass it before its August recess, but the House, which passed a different version funding the government through Dec. 4, will need to reconcile its bill with the Senate's Dec. 11 timeline once it returns in September, meaning the agreement is not yet final law.

◐ Debate

Democratic

  • Patty Murray said the Senate bill 'rejects Trump's frivolous war spending wish list' and closes loopholes that would let the administration shift funds from existing programs to Border Patrol, which she said 'desperately needs reform, not more money.'
  • Murray and Chuck Schumer credited the bill with blocking a White House budget office proposal that would let Trump officials cancel grants 'at any time for any reason,' framing this as stopping the politicization of federal grants.
  • Rosa DeLauro, top Democrat on the House Appropriations Committee, called the Senate bill 'a clear improvement over the House Republican continuing resolution,' signaling House Democrats' preference for the Senate version in upcoming negotiations.

Republican

  • Susan Collins described the Senate stopgap as 'straightforward' and free of 'poison pills,' emphasizing it attaches only 'necessary adjustments' for SNAP, WIC, disaster relief and national security programs.
  • Collins said the bill preserves time for Congress to continue work on full bipartisan appropriations bills, which she called her 'strong preference' over relying on stopgap measures.
  • House Republicans passed their own version of the funding bill (through Dec. 4) before leaving for recess, setting up a negotiation with the Senate's Dec. 11 date that reflects differing House GOP priorities on the funding timeline.
Steelman

Both parties are publicly claiming the deal reflects their priorities — Republicans framing it as a clean, bipartisan stopgap that preserves the regular appropriations process, and Democrats framing it as a win that blocks specific White House spending and grant-cancellation powers — but the underlying mechanics show a modest, short-term extension that leaves the harder funding decisions, and the House-Senate reconciliation, unresolved until after lawmakers return in September.

Implications

The agreement still requires House action after its September return, where lawmakers must reconcile the chamber's Dec. 4 funding deadline with the Senate's Dec. 11 date; failure to align the two versions could revive shutdown risk later this year, and the stopgap's expiration in December sets up another funding deadline just weeks before the 2026 midterm campaign fully takes shape.
