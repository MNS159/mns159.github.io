Michigan cyclosporiasis outbreak approaches 14,000 cases

Health & Science desk

Michigan's cyclosporiasis outbreak has reached 13,909 documented cases, according to the Michigan Department of Health and Human Services, an increase from the 12,485 cases reported the previous week. The state is providing weekly updates, which now show 314 hospitalizations—up from 279—and two deaths, both reported earlier this month. The outbreak has been detected in 73 of Michigan's 83 counties, with Wayne, Oakland, Washtenaw, Ingham, and Genesee counties reporting the highest case numbers.

The outbreak, first announced by the state health department on July 1, is caused by the microscopic Cyclospora parasite, which spreads through contaminated food or water. Symptoms, which may appear two days to two weeks after exposure, include watery diarrhea, loss of appetite, weight loss, cramping, bloating, increased gas, nausea, and fatigue. On August 6, the state revised its earlier recommendations: residents no longer needed to buy whole heads of lettuce, remove outer layers, and wash and cook inner leaves, but were instead advised to follow basic food safety practices such as handwashing, separating potentially contaminated foods, and cooking to proper temperatures.

The outbreak has been linked to iceberg lettuce from central Mexico. On July 17, Taylor Farms de Mexico issued a voluntary recall of its iceberg lettuce products from that region. The U.S. Food and Drug Administration, along with the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention and state and local health departments, continues to investigate infections tied to that lettuce. The Michigan Department of Health and Human Services says data suggest the majority of contaminated produce is likely no longer available for consumption, but still advises residents to avoid recalled lettuce.

The CDC is also tracking the outbreak, reporting 13,895 cyclosporiasis cases nationally as of August 11. However, that count includes 10,455 cases not yet confirmed by lab testing, and the CDC notes its figures exclude probable cases, which may result in lower numbers than those from state health departments. The CDC is investigating a multistate outbreak linked to iceberg lettuce affecting 15 states: Arkansas, Illinois, Indiana, Iowa, Kansas, Kentucky, Michigan, Missouri, Nebraska, New Hampshire, North Carolina, Ohio, Oklahoma, Pennsylvania, and West Virginia. Michigan remains the hardest-hit state, with the CDC recording up to 6,999 cases.
