OpenAI reportedly disbands its preparedness team

Tech / AI desk

OpenAI has disbanded its preparedness team, the group responsible for assessing whether the company's AI models pose serious risks—such as going rogue or hacking another organization—and for developing ways to mitigate those dangers, according to a report from the Financial Times cited by The Verge.

The team was dissolved at the end of last month, according to the report. Responsibility for evaluating and mitigating risks has been divided by specific areas, such as bio and cyber threats, and moved into existing teams. The head of the preparedness team, Dylan Scandinaro, who joined OpenAI from Anthropic in February, will now focus on the implications of "recursive self-improving" AI.

The disbanding is the latest shakeup of OpenAI's safety teams as the company heads toward what is expected to be a massive initial public offering. Over the past few years, OpenAI has gradually dismantled its more safety-focused structures, dissolving its AGI readiness and superalignment teams.

Several key safety and ethics personnel have also left recently, including ethics lead Chloé Bakalar, chief futurist Josh Achiam, and head of safety Johannes Heidecke. The departures have drawn criticism from former employees; Jan Leike, who resigned from OpenAI in 2024, told the Financial Times that the company was ignoring safety in favor of creating "shiny products."

Sources: The Verge
