Kushner meets Netanyahu in Jerusalem after rare Hamas talks on Trump Gaza plan

International & World desk · anchored on Reuters
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts slightly center-left

Jared Kushner, U.S. President Donald Trump’s son-in-law and envoy, began a meeting Monday with Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu in Jerusalem, a day after holding a rare meeting in Egypt with Hamas leader Khalil al-Hayya that lasted more than two hours, according to a regional official and a Hamas official. In Egypt, Kushner convened with Qatari, Turkish and Egyptian mediators, accompanied by Nickolay Mladenov and former British Prime Minister Tony Blair. Israeli media reported that the Jerusalem meeting was underway.

The plan, drawn up by the U.S.-led Board of Peace, calls for Israeli troops to withdraw as Hamas gives up its weapons, with an international stabilization force to work alongside a new Palestinian police force to secure Gaza. Hamas would hand over weapons to a Palestinian technocratic committee meant to oversee daily operations in the territory, though that committee has not yet entered Gaza. Netanyahu rejected the plan last week, saying there would be no military pullout until Hamas is “genuinely” disarmed and objecting to the gradual disarmament and handover of governance to an international force. Hamas says it has accepted the plan but that implementation depends on Israel first withdrawing forces and halting attacks, and it has called on mediators and the Board of Peace to “compel” Israel to approve the road map. A Hamas official said the group is committed and waiting for mediators to start a 14-day negotiation period, which requires all parties’ approval; the official said al-Hayya demanded that Israel halt attacks and withdraw to the so-called “yellow line” dividing the territory before implementation can begin. That line has never been precisely defined, and Israeli forces now control about 60% of Gaza; in May, Netanyahu said the next step was to move to 70% control, with Israel “tightening the grip” on Hamas “from every direction.” Hamas, designated a terrorist organization by several countries and the EU, said on July 30 it would hand over its weapons to a new Palestinian governing body under the Board of Peace’s supervision, avoiding the term “disarmament.” Hamas has linked the surrender of its heaviest weapons to the creation of a Palestinian state, something Israel’s current government rejects. Kushner’s meeting with the Hamas leader was aimed at “translating the roadmap’s obligations into concrete, verifiable steps,” an official familiar with the matter said.

The foreign ministers of eight Muslim-majority countries—Egypt, Jordan, Qatar, Indonesia, Saudi Arabia, Turkey, the United Arab Emirates and Pakistan—issued a joint statement on Sunday condemning Israel’s rejection. They said rejecting the road map “constitutes an explicit refusal to proceed with the implementation of the Comprehensive Plan, and directly threatens to derail the extensive efforts exerted by US President Trump to bring an end to the war in Gaza and establish the conditions for lasting peace,” and that “Israel now bears responsibility for obstructing the efforts to bring peace in Gaza and the rest of the Occupied Palestinian Territory.” They called on the Board of Peace and the U.S. to take “immediate and concrete measures” to prevent any party from obstructing the plan’s implementation. Netanyahu’s Likud party responded by accusing Arab states of wanting to topple Netanyahu so centrist challenger Gadi Eisenkot and center-left leader Yair Golan “will give them everything they want. That will not happen.”

The Board of Peace was inaugurated in February 2026 and includes roughly 40 countries, including Israel and several Arab states but no Palestinian representatives, with Trump installed as chair for life. It was set up under a broader Gaza ceasefire reached between Israel and Hamas in late 2025. Last month, Trump announced an agreement for the disarmament of Hamas, and the board said it had reached a deal for the complete disarmament of Hamas and other armed groups. At least 1,260 Palestinians have been killed by Israel in Gaza since the ceasefire took effect. The ceasefire ended major military operations more than 10 months ago, but the enclave remains largely controlled by Israeli forces, and reconstruction and the deployment of international forces are on hold. About 2 million people live in Gaza. A meeting last year between Kushner, fellow envoy Steve Witkoff and Hamas leaders in Egypt helped clinch the ceasefire.

Mladenov and Blair were expected at Monday’s meeting with Netanyahu. The White House referred questions to the Board of Peace, which did not immediately comment; the Tony Blair Institute also did not comment. Netanyahu, who faces re-election on Oct. 27 with a coalition including ministers taking a hard line on Gaza, previously said Israel remained in talks with Washington despite its objections: “They have ideas; some of them are acceptable to us and some are not, and we know how to stand our ground on these matters.” It is not clear whether he will take decisive steps on the ceasefire before then. The anniversary of the Oct. 7, 2023, Hamas-led attack on southern Israel that sparked the war is also approaching.
