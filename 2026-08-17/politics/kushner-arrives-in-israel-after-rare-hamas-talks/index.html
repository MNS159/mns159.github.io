Kushner arrives in Israel after rare Hamas talks on Gaza plan

Politics desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum
✓ Facts
  • Jared Kushner, US President Donald Trump's Middle East envoy, arrived in Israel and is expected to meet Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu on Monday.
  • Kushner held talks in Egypt on Sunday with a Hamas delegation led by the group's new overall leader, Khalil al-Hayya — reportedly the first meeting at this level since last year's Gaza ceasefire agreement — alongside Board of Peace representative Nickolay Mladenov, former UK PM Tony Blair, and officials from Egypt, Qatar and Turkey.
  • Trump's Board of Peace said last month it had reached agreement on the 'complete disarmament' of Hamas and other Gaza armed groups; last week Israel rejected the US-backed 15-point plan, with Netanyahu stating the IDF 'will not carry out any withdrawal until Hamas is genuinely disarmed.'
  • Eight countries — Egypt, UAE, Qatar, Saudi Arabia, Jordan, Turkey, Pakistan and Indonesia — issued a joint statement on Sunday condemning Israel's rejection of the plan.
  • The Gaza ceasefire took effect on 10 October last year and remains formally in place, though the IDF has carried out near-daily strikes since then; more than 1,200 people have reportedly been killed since the ceasefire began, according to the Hamas-run health ministry (figures the UN considers reliable), including at least 300 children per UNICEF.
  • The war began with the Hamas-led attack on southern Israel on 7 October 2023, killing about 1,200 people and taking 251 hostage; Israel's subsequent military campaign has killed more than 73,000 people in Gaza, including 21,280 children, according to the territory's health ministry, and displaced roughly 1.9 million people (about 90% of the population), per UN estimates.
  • Israeli National Security Minister Itamar Ben Gvir publicly said he disagrees with Netanyahu on reducing Gaza operations and called for nightly 'targeted assassinations,' according to a podcast interview that aired 15 August, as reported by the Associated Press, Financial Times and Times of Israel.
○ Analysis

This story sits at the intersection of US-brokered diplomacy and Israeli domestic politics. Trump's 'Board of Peace' — an international body he leads that grew out of Gaza ceasefire efforts — has proposed a 15-point roadmap requiring Hamas disarmament, an Israeli troop withdrawal, and a transition to a new Palestinian civilian administration (the National Committee for the Administration of Gaza), backed by an international stabilizing force. Kushner's direct engagement with Hamas leadership is unusual because Hamas is designated a terrorist organization by the US and Israel, underscoring how far Washington is pushing to operationalize the deal. Netanyahu's rejection of the plan's sequencing — insisting on disarmament before any withdrawal — reflects both skepticism that Hamas will genuinely disarm and political pressure from his own coalition, including hardline ministers like Ben Gvir who oppose scaling back operations, as Netanyahu heads into an October election. Meanwhile, a bloc of Muslim-majority states, including the UAE (which normalized ties with Israel in 2020), has publicly pressured Israel to accept the US framework, signaling regional stakes beyond the US-Israel relationship.

◐ Debate

Democratic

  • Some Democrats have argued the scale of Gaza casualties (over 73,000 dead, including more than 21,000 children, per Gaza's health ministry) and continued Israeli strikes during a nominal ceasefire warrant firmer US pressure on Israel to accept the peace plan's withdrawal terms rather than deferring to Netanyahu's conditions.
  • Other Democrats, wary of a Trump-branded diplomatic vehicle, have questioned whether the 'Board of Peace' and direct US engagement with Hamas — an organization the US designates as a terror group — sets a problematic precedent, even while supporting the underlying goal of ending the conflict.

Republican

  • Many Republicans have backed Netanyahu's position that Hamas must be 'genuinely disarmed' before any Israeli withdrawal, framing this as a necessary security precondition given Hamas's role in the 7 October 2023 attack that killed about 1,200 people and took 251 hostages.
  • Some Republicans, including allies of the Trump administration's approach, view Kushner's rare direct talks with Hamas leadership as a pragmatic diplomatic breakthrough advancing Trump's plan, while more hawkish voices — echoing figures like Israeli minister Itamar Ben Gvir — remain skeptical of any negotiation with Hamas and favor continued military pressure instead.
Steelman

Both the plan's international backers and Israel's government say they want Hamas disarmed and the war ended — the dispute is almost entirely about sequencing and trust: whether disarmament must precede withdrawal (Israel's position) or whether withdrawal and governance transition can proceed alongside a disarmament process brokered by mediators (the US/Board of Peace and Hamas's stated position).

Implications

Kushner's Monday meeting with Netanyahu, alongside Mladenov and Blair, will test whether Israel can be moved off its insistence on disarmament-before-withdrawal, especially amid coordinated diplomatic pressure from eight Muslim-majority states including the UAE. With Netanyahu facing an October election and coalition partners like Ben Gvir pushing a harder line, and with strikes continuing despite the ceasefire, the durability of any near-term agreement will depend on whether Hamas takes verifiable steps toward disarmament and whether Washington can secure enforceable commitments from both sides.
