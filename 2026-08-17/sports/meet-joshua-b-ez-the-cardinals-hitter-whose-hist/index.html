Joshua Báez hits three home runs in historic MLB debut for Cardinals

Sports desk

CHICAGO — St. Louis Cardinals outfielder Joshua Báez made his major-league debut Saturday with three home runs in his first three at-bats, powering an 8-4 victory over the Chicago Cubs at Wrigley Field. The 23-year-old became the first player in MLB history to hit three homers in his debut, a feat that drew comparisons to Albert Pujols’ 700th career home run from manager Oli Marmol. Báez homered to center, left and right field off Cubs left-hander Matthew Boyd, connecting on a fastball, changeup and slider.

Báez, a second-round pick by the Cardinals in 2021 out of Dexter Southfield prep school in Massachusetts, signed for a $2.25 million bonus and was one of the youngest players in his draft class. He lost his father, Jose Manuel Báez, three years ago and dedicated his career to reaching the majors in his honor. After the game, Báez embraced his mother, Yris, on the field. Marmol made a point to hug her and acknowledge the support system that helped Báez reach the highest level.

The debut capped a long developmental journey. Báez struggled with strikeout rates as high as 39 percent in High A in 2024, prompting the Cardinals to place him on the development list and send him to the team complex in Jupiter, Florida, for a swing overhaul. Minor-league hitting coordinator Brock Hammit worked with Báez to optimize his swing path for contact. His strikeout rate eventually dropped below 30 percent, and he hit 20 home runs with 54 stolen bases across two minor-league levels in 2025, winning a championship with Double-A Springfield.

Cardinals scouting director Randy Flores said Báez’s power was evident when the team first scouted him in the summer of 2020, despite pandemic restrictions. Flores noted that Báez’s strength is so natural that he can drive the ball to all fields without selling out for pull-side power. Bench coach Daniel Descalso, who won a World Series with the Cardinals in 2011, said Báez’s ability to endure struggles and make adjustments was key to his development.

The Cardinals are in the midst of a roster transition under new top baseball executive Chaim Bloom, who was hired as an advisor before the 2024 season and elevated after last year’s 78-84 finish. Bloom has revamped the player development system, and a new core of homegrown talent — including Jordan Walker, Masyn Winn, Alec Burleson, Michael McGreevy, Iván Herrera and JJ Wetherholt — has emerged. Báez’s debut adds to that group. Marmol said Báez injects a personality and edge into the clubhouse that he has been eager to see at the major-league level.

In his second game Sunday, Báez went 0-for-5 with three strikeouts and was replaced for defense in the eighth inning of an 11-4 win over the Cubs. The Cardinals left Wrigley Field two games over .500 and within striking distance of a wild-card berth. Báez, who also worked as a DoorDash driver during his minor-league career, said he interpreted a bee that kept buzzing around his hat during Saturday’s game as a sign of his father’s presence.
