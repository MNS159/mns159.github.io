Von Miller to sign with Cowboys: Dallas continues EDGE overhaul

Sports desk

Eight-time Pro Bowl edge rusher Von Miller is expected to sign a one-year contract with the Dallas Cowboys, according to ESPN's Adam Schefter. The 37-year-old Texas native and his new team confirmed the agreement Sunday, Aug. 16, making the Cowboys the destination for one of the top remaining free agents on the market. Miller spent the 2025 season with the Washington Commanders on a one-year deal and has now been part of five franchises in six years.

Miller, a former first-round pick of the Denver Broncos in 2011, has 138.5 career sacks — the most of any active player and tied with DeMarcus Ware for ninth-most all time. Despite making just three starts in 17 games for the Commanders last season, he recorded nine sacks, his highest single-season total since 2018. He joins a Dallas EDGE unit that includes trade acquisition Rashan Gary, 2025 second-round pick Donovan Ezeiruaku, first-round rookie Malachi Lawrence, and James Houston.

The Cowboys' pass-rushing group has been in flux since trading Micah Parsons to the Green Bay Packers last summer. The team ranked 26th in sack rate in 2025 and surrendered more pass yards (4,521) than any NFL team. Owner/general manager Jerry Jones has since made a series of moves to rebuild the unit, including drafting Lawrence in the first round and Jaishawn Barham in the third round, and acquiring Gary via trade. Miller is expected to fill a role similar to that of Jadeveon Clowney in 2025, who served as a veteran rotational piece rather than a full-time starter.

Miller spent his first 10½ seasons with the Broncos, winning a Super Bowl and earning Super Bowl 50 MVP honors. He was traded to the Rams in 2021 and won another Super Bowl that season, then signed a six-year, $120 million deal with the Bills in 2022. After a pay cut in 2024, he was released and signed a one-year deal with Washington for 2025. With Clowney out of the picture this year, the Cowboys developed a clear need for a veteran presence in a young and still-deficient pass-rushing unit. If Miller can replicate his nine-sack production from last season, he could be instrumental in helping Dallas address its glaring defensive weakness.
