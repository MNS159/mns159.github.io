Amy Hunt wins fourth gold as GB storm to mixed 4x100m relay victory at European Athletics Championships

Sports desk

Amy Hunt won a historic fourth gold medal of the European Athletics Championships on Sunday as Great Britain’s team of Jeremiah Azu, Dina Asher-Smith, Zharnel Hughes and Hunt stormed to victory in the mixed 4x100m relay in Birmingham. Hunt became the first athlete to win four European golds at a single championships, while Asher-Smith claimed a record eighth European career title. The win capped a sensational seven days at Alexander Stadium in which Hunt also took the individual 100m and 200m crowns.

The 24-year-old’s path to this moment has been far from smooth. Seven years ago, as a 17-year-old, Hunt ran 22.42 seconds over 200m and broke the under-18 world record, also bettering the British under-20 record; a year later Vogue named her one of its “faces to define the decade.” She later began an English Literature degree at Cambridge, determined to prove she could be “an academic badass and a track goddess.” In 2022, after lining up against Elaine Thompson-Herah at an indoor meeting in Birmingham, she fully ruptured a tendon in her quadriceps, needed surgery and watched that year’s Commonwealth Games on crutches. She came back to win Olympic 4x100m relay silver and, in 2025, her first individual global medal in Tokyo.

Hunt’s week included 100m gold on the opening night, and after completing the individual sprint double she roared at the TV camera: “Are you not entertained?!” She had said before the championships that she wanted to become “iconic” and have it all, and afterwards reflected: “Four golds was the dream and perhaps dreams do come true. It’s so beyond the bounds of my dreams that I’m just euphoric. I can’t believe it’s happening. I think it will sink in later, maybe on the podium. I’m just so proud of myself and the way I’ve handled this week.” She is now among the frontrunners to be named BBC Sports Personality of the Year.

Coached by Marco Airale in Padua, Italy, Hunt has focused on improving a start that, at almost 6ft tall, remains her biggest area for development. Her major strength is top-end speed, and she finally broke the 11-second barrier in the 100m this season while lowering her 200m personal best to 22.08 seconds in winning world silver. Her 60m best now stands at 7.04 seconds, the eighth-fastest time worldwide this year. She also had to manage low blood pressure during longer efforts, which left her close to fainting at the London Diamond League in July; electrolytes and cold towels have helped ease those concerns, and she showed strength to hold off Asher-Smith and Ireland’s Rhasidat Adeleke in the European 200m.

Colin Jackson, the double world 110m hurdles champion, said Hunt now has to take on the best in the world. “To win a global 100m nowadays, you have to run around the 10.75-second mark. Amy knows that’s what she has to work towards and improving her start will get her towards that,” he told BBC Sport. “If she wants to win the 200m, she has to be around the 21.7, 21.8-second mark, and I think she’s really capable of doing that. She is a champion and she has to go and take on the best in the world.”

This season Hunt ranks 14th-fastest worldwide in the 200m and 25th in the 100m, while Asher-Smith’s British records of 21.88 and 10.83 seconds have stood since 2019. Hunt has already spoken of the big goal of repeating her sprint sweep at the 2028 Los Angeles Olympics, with next year’s World Championships in Beijing the nearer target on the track after the inaugural World Ultimate Championships in September. The coming task, Jackson said, is translating continental domination into glory on the sport’s biggest stages, as Asher-Smith did after her European treble in 2018: “Is it going to be easy? No. But this will fill Amy with confidence.”
