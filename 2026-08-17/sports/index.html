Tommy John, MLB pitcher who lent name to landmark surgery, dies at 83

Sports desk

Tommy John, the four-time All-Star pitcher whose name became synonymous with the revolutionary elbow surgery that extended his career by 14 seasons, died Saturday at his home in Bradenton, Florida, at age 83. The New York Yankees — one of six teams he played for during a 26-year major league career — confirmed the death in a statement. His agent, Mike Maguire, said John died peacefully at his home in Lakewood Ranch, Florida, surrounded by his wife, Cheryl, and family. John had battled bladder cancer and other ailments in recent years and had shared a farewell message to fans after missing the Yankees' Old-Timers' Day on Aug. 8.

Track & Field

Amy Hunt wins fourth gold as GB storm to mixed 4x100m relay victory at European Athletics Championships

Sports desk

Amy Hunt won a historic fourth gold medal of the European Athletics Championships on Sunday as Great Britain’s team of Jeremiah Azu, Dina Asher-Smith, Zharnel Hughes and Hunt stormed to victory in the mixed 4x100m relay in Birmingham. Hunt became the first athlete to win four European golds at a single championships, while Asher-Smith claimed a record eighth European career title. The win capped a sensational seven days at Alexander Stadium in which Hunt also took the individual 100m and 200m crowns.

Joshua Báez hits three home runs in historic MLB debut for Cardinals

Sports desk

CHICAGO — St. Louis Cardinals outfielder Joshua Báez made his major-league debut Saturday with three home runs in his first three at-bats, powering an 8-4 victory over the Chicago Cubs at Wrigley Field. The 23-year-old became the first player in MLB history to hit three homers in his debut, a feat that drew comparisons to Albert Pujols’ 700th career home run from manager Oli Marmol.
