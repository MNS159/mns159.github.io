Indonesian ferry capsizes in Java Sea; at least six dead, 130 missing

International & World desk

At least six people have died and 130 remain missing after an Indonesian passenger ferry capsized in the Java Sea in bad weather, authorities said Sunday. The Virgo Transport 8, carrying 213 passengers and 30 crew members for a total of 243 people, lost contact with its operator early Sunday while en route from Surabaya on the island of Java to Banjarmasin on the island of Borneo. Rescuers have so far pulled 107 people from the water, according to Indonesia's national search and rescue agency, Basarnas.

The vessel's last known position was about 150 kilometers (93 miles) south of Banjarmasin, where it reportedly encountered rough seas with waves reaching 2.5 to 3 meters (8 to 10 feet), officials said. The ship's captain issued a distress message reporting the vessel was listing before contact was lost at around 2 a.m. local time Sunday (18:00 GMT Saturday). Authorities received an initial distress report from the ship's operator, PT Virgo, at 4:10 a.m. The ferry had departed Surabaya at 10:33 a.m. on Saturday and was scheduled to arrive in Banjarmasin by 2 p.m. Sunday.

Indonesian Transport Minister Dudy Purwagandhi said the vessel capsized but had not sunk, based on helicopter surveillance. A joint task force involving Basarnas, the navy, maritime police, and other agencies has been deployed, with 280 personnel, multiple ships, and a helicopter searching the area. I Putu Sudayana, head of the Banjarmasin search and rescue office, said the operation was hampered by waves as high as 2.5 meters, calling it 'extremely risky for vessels.' The National Transport Safety Committee will investigate the incident.

Distraught relatives gathered at Trisakti Port in Banjarmasin and at Tanjung Perak Port in Surabaya, seeking news of loved ones. Rusmilawati, a resident of Banjarmasin, said eight of her relatives were on the ship returning from visiting tombs of Islamic scholars in Java. She last heard from one through a WhatsApp status that read: 'The waves are really frightening tonight.' Another relative, Fredy, said his sibling Stevie Manuhua worked aboard as a keyboard player and had not been heard from since the ship docked in Surabaya. Jenah, 26, told AFP she came for information about her older brother, saying, 'I couldn't contact him since last night.'

Ferries are a common and affordable mode of transport across Indonesia's archipelago of more than 17,000 islands, but accidents are frequent due to weak safety enforcement. Government data recorded 111 ship accidents between 2020 and 2026. Last month, a fire on a ferry off Madura island killed five people, and in July the KM Nurul Salsa sank off Selayar Island, leaving three dead and 14 missing. Sunday's accident comes just days after a fire on a Philippine ferry killed at least 76 people.
