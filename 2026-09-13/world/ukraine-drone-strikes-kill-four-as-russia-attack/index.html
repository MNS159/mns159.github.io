Ukraine drone strikes kill four as Russia attacks close to Poland border

International & World desk

At least four people were killed in Ukrainian drone strikes on Russian territory overnight as Moscow launched a massive wave of attacks near Ukraine's border with Poland, officials said Sunday. The Ukrainian General Staff reported that Russia fired 863 drones over the previous 24 hours, while the Russian Defense Ministry said its forces shot down 389 Ukrainian drones across the country, including over the occupied Crimean Peninsula and the Tatarstan region.

Two Russian drone attacks struck within hundreds of meters of Ukraine's border with NATO member Poland, disrupting traffic at the Dorohusk-Yahodyn crossing point. Polish border guards said the crossing briefly stopped working after a drone hit a truck about 0.8 kilometers (0.5 miles) from Poland but resumed operations by Sunday morning. Ukrainian authorities said a Russian drone also hit a gas station in the town of Yahodyn, setting fire to the building and several parked trucks. In a separate incident, a Russian drone struck the locomotive of a Warsaw-bound passenger train in Ukraine's Volyn region, two kilometers (1.2 miles) from the Polish border; no casualties were reported.

Ukrainian Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha condemned the strikes as "Putin's terror 'knocking' directly on the doors of the EU and NATO." The border crossings near Poland are critical for military and humanitarian aid deliveries to Ukraine. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky said Russia had fired more than 2,000 drones at Ukraine over the past week, many of them fast, jet-powered models that are harder to intercept.

On the Ukrainian side, the military said it struck the TANECO oil refinery in the Russian city of Nizhnekamsk, Tatarstan. Regional officials reported two civilians killed and 12 others wounded in that attack. Separately, the head of Russia's Rosatom nuclear corporation said two people were killed in a series of Ukrainian drone strikes that targeted fuel trucks near the Zaporizhzhia Nuclear Power Plant. In Ukraine's southern port city of Odesa, at least nine people were injured in a Russian overnight attack, according to regional military administration chief Serhiy Lysak.

In another incident linked to the heightened aerial activity, Lithuania closed Vilnius airport and scrambled a NATO fighter jet after a perceived drone sighting in its airspace, which was later determined to be birds. On the diplomatic front, Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said Sunday that three-way negotiations between Ukraine, Russia and the United States could take place in October, according to Russian state media. Zelensky said in an interview aired Saturday that he would be willing to meet Russian President Vladimir Putin at the G20 summit in Miami this December.
