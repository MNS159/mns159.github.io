Hot CPI data ramps up Fed rate hike odds and political pressure on Warsh

Financial & Markets desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

Consumer prices rose 3.4% in August from a year earlier, while average hourly earnings increased just 3.1% over the same period, according to Bureau of Labor Statistics data released Friday. Real average hourly earnings, adjusted for inflation, fell 0.1% from July and were down 0.3% year-over-year, reversing a period when wage growth had generally exceeded inflation. “A substantial number of Americans are worse off, their incomes are not keeping up with the price increases right now,” Heather Long, chief economist at Navy Federal Credit Union, told CNBC.

The renewed gap between inflation and wage growth is driven largely by energy costs: gasoline prices rose 3.9% in August alone, accounting for more than one-third of the consumer price index’s gain, and diesel touched $6 per gallon on Friday amid fuel supply disruptions from wars in Iran and Ukraine. Long tied the shift to the surge in energy prices after the war in Iran, and said she expects inflation to remain elevated and household budgets to remain squeezed. “It’s going to be tough for a long time,” she said, adding that consumer spending data already shows higher-income shoppers shifting to discount retailers like Costco and Aldi.

The inflation data has pushed market expectations for a Federal Reserve rate hike at this week’s meeting to roughly 86%, according to JPMorgan chief U.S. economist Michael Feroli. The Federal Reserve’s decision is scheduled for Wednesday at 2 p.m. ET, followed by a press conference from Chairman Kevin Warsh at 2:30 p.m. ET. Investor’s Business Daily reported that markets are largely pricing in a rate hike on Sept. 16.

The Fed meeting comes amid intense political pressure on Chairman Warsh, who took a forceful stance on inflation at his first Jackson Hole symposium last month. A commentary in The Spokesman-Review noted that former Fed economist Kenneth Rogoff advised the Fed to delay a rate hike until after the midterm elections to preserve independence, but argued that playing politics would undermine the Fed’s credibility. Warsh has already faced White House attacks, including an attempt to remove Fed Governor Lisa Cook that was blocked by the Supreme Court. “The Fed’s rate decision should reflect a wide range of data. The date of the election is not one of them,” the commentary concluded.

Beyond the rate decision, the week also includes manufacturing data releases: Empire state manufacturing numbers on Tuesday, followed by Federal Reserve industrial production and manufacturing production data on Friday. Rising crop prices, noted by MarketWatch, add to the inflation challenge beyond oil. With core PCE inflation above 3% every month this year and making little progress toward the Fed’s 2% target, the central bank’s credibility as an inflation fighter is at the center of the debate. All eyes are on whether Warsh will deliver a hike Wednesday to demonstrate that commitment.
