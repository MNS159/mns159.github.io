US accuses Chinese AI firms of 'industrial-scale' theft of trade secrets

Tech / AI desk · anchored on Reuters

US federal agencies on Tuesday accused Chinese artificial intelligence firms of conducting “industrial-scale” theft of trade secrets from American AI companies, alleging that major Chinese tech firms are using a technique called “distillation” to illegally extract proprietary capabilities from US frontier AI models. The advisory, issued jointly by the FBI, the National Security Agency, and the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency, singled out DeepSeek and Alibaba Group as having carried out “high-volume knowledge distillation campaigns” against US firms.

Distillation is a legitimate research technique in which a smaller AI model is trained on the outputs of a larger, more advanced model. But US officials said the scale and targeted nature of Chinese campaigns amount to stealing. “The sheer scale of these campaigns and their sophistication indicate that distillation is not a supplement to these companies’ AI model development, but the critical core of it,” the advisory stated. The agencies said China-based AI firms use a “gray market of proxies known as ‘transfer stations’” to bypass US companies’ geographic restrictions and safeguards.

The advisory adds evidence to long-standing complaints from US officials and AI executives. Anthropic, an AI company, separately alleged that three Chinese AI labs had conducted an “industrial-scale campaign” to extract capabilities from its model Claude, generating “over 16 million exchanges with Claude through approximately 24,000 fraudulent accounts.” Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent threatened consequences, writing on X: “When [People’s Republic of China] firms conduct covert, industrial-scale distillation attacks that cross the line into IP theft, sanctions and Entity List designations will be on the table.”

China rejected the accusations. A spokesperson for the Chinese Embassy in Washington said, “The U.S. side’s hyping of the so-called ‘distillation’ concept is deliberate attack on China’s development and progress in the AI industry. China firmly rejects it.” On Wednesday, Chinese Foreign Ministry spokesperson Mao Ning said China’s AI development reflects its self-reliance and strength, and urged the US to refrain from “unfounded accusations against or smearing China.” She added that both countries should strengthen cooperation.

The allegations come amid rising US-China tensions over AI technology. President Donald Trump has said AI will be among the discussion topics when he hosts Chinese leader Xi Jinping in Washington later this month. Separately, Google’s Threat Intelligence Group reported Tuesday that hackers are “increasingly targeting AI assets,” including proprietary models and source code, for espionage, extortion, and resource theft. Chinese open-weight AI models, which are highly customizable and less expensive, have grown in popularity.
