Brent crude tops $100 as Middle East conflict intensifies; stock futures fall

Financial & Markets desk

Brent crude futures breached $100 a barrel on Wednesday for the first time since July 24, rising 2.94% to $100.80 per barrel by 1210 GMT after touching an intraday high of $100.95. U.S. West Texas Intermediate crude climbed 2.76% to $95.60 per barrel, its highest level since early June. Stock futures declined in response: Dow Jones Industrial Average futures dropped 0.7%, S&P 500 futures fell 0.3%, and Nasdaq-100 futures edged 0.4% lower ahead of the open.

The price surge followed an escalation in the U.S.-Iran conflict. Overnight, U.S. Central Command announced that the military sank five Iranian crude oil tankers in what it described as a response to attempts by Iran to strike U.S. Navy carriers in the region. The strike was the second in as many days targeting Iran’s shipping sector. Meanwhile, Iran-backed Houthi rebels set Saudi energy facilities ablaze, threatening crude shipments via the Red Sea, a key alternative route to the Strait of Hormuz, where oil flows remain severely curtailed. A tanker carrying about 2 million barrels of Iraqi fuel oil was struck by a drone in Iraqi territorial waters, and several merchant vessels in the Gulf were hit by disabling fire, according to port officials and the British navy-linked UKMTO.

Market participants are pricing in a more prolonged conflict. “The move towards and back above $100 Brent is reflecting a market that increasingly has to change its view on how long the Middle East crisis will continue to curb supply from the region,” said Ole Hansen, head of commodity strategy at Saxo Bank. Hamad Hussain, senior climate and commodities economist at Capital Economics, said the key risk is whether the recent attacks on oil tankers lead to fewer ship-to-ship transfers in the Gulf of Oman. Since the war began on February 28, Brent has surged as high as $126.41, a peak reached on April 30. According to Rystad Energy, about 8 million to 9 million barrels per day flowed through the Strait of Hormuz in the week before a resumption of fighting on August 30, but flows have since fallen below 2 million barrels per day.

The oil rally stoked inflation fears and shifted expectations for Federal Reserve policy. Traders now see a 60% probability of a 25-basis-point rate hike at the Fed’s September meeting, up from earlier odds, according to CME Group data. Treasury yields hit fresh long-term highs. The Dow Jones Industrial Average lost over 600 points on Tuesday.

Elsewhere in markets, Apple (AAPL) shares were in focus ahead of the company’s annual iPhone launch event, where CEO John Ternus will take the stage for the first time. The U.S.-Canada trade war escalated as Canadian retaliatory tariffs on 700 U.S. goods took effect and the White House unveiled plans to ban Canadian dairy products, most alcoholic beverages, and motorcycles under Section 338 of the Tariff Act of 1930. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, speaking at SMU Cox School of Business, said the U.S. can grow its way out of the $40 trillion national debt if it achieves 3% annual growth, noting that the underlying economy is “very, very strong” and reaccelerating once the Iran conflict subsides.
