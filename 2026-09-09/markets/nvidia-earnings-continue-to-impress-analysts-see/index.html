Nvidia earnings beat expectations, guidance signals continued AI boom; CrowdStrike also surges

Financial & Markets desk

Nvidia reported earnings after the close on Aug. 27, 2026, that once again exceeded Wall Street estimates, sending shares up about 7% in the following session. The company posted 106% year-over-year revenue growth, a figure that Motley Fool contributors described as 'utterly unbelievable' for the world's largest company. More significantly, Nvidia guided for approximately 70% revenue growth in the coming year, far above the 45% analysts had expected, and indicated that growth would be even higher if not for supply constraints.

The guidance implies that the top five hyperscalers will increase capital spending from roughly $800 billion this year to $1.3 trillion next year, according to Nvidia's outlook. The company said it can satisfy only about 70% of the demand it sees for its products. While Nvidia's gross margins face some compression due to higher memory costs — the company is trading about three percentage points of its 75% gross margin for roughly double the revenue growth — the overall report was seen as overwhelmingly positive.

The earnings and guidance have broad implications for the AI infrastructure ecosystem. Among the winners highlighted by analysts are Credo Technologies (CRDO), a small GPU interconnectivity firm with $1.3 billion in revenue that has tripled in the last year and expects 80% growth; Onto Innovation (ONTO), which makes inspection tools for memory production; and IES Holdings (IESC), which designs electrical systems for data centers. On the losing side, HP was cited as a potential loser because rising memory costs driven by AI demand could squeeze its consumer PC business, while Garmin (GRMN) faces similar margin pressure from higher memory prices.

Separately, CrowdStrike reported a blowout quarter, with shares up 18% as of the podcast recording. Revenue grew 26% year over year, but the standout metric was net new annual recurring revenue (ARR), which surged 51% year over year — a company record and 17% above management's own guidance. The company's remaining performance obligation (backlog) reached just over $10 billion, up 49% year over year, while adjusted operating margin improved by 350 basis points. Management raised its full-year guidance, and analysts noted that AI is clearly fueling demand for cybersecurity rather than rendering it obsolete.
