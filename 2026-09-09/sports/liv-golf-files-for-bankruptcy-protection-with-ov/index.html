LIV Golf files for bankruptcy protection with over $45m owed to players

Sports desk

LIV Golf has filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection in the United States, listing at least $45m (£33m) in unsecured claims owed to 14 current and former players, according to court documents filed Tuesday in the federal district court of New Jersey. The filing comes after Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund (PIF) withdrew its multibillion-dollar funding in April, saying the "substantial investment required by LIV Golf over a longer term" was "no longer consistent" with its strategy. LIV Golf says it has found a new investor in BC Partners and intends to launch a restructured, player-owned league early next year.

The petition's list of the 30 largest unsecured claims is topped by two-time major winner Jon Rahm, who has an unsecured claim of $7.5m (£5.5m). Other prominent players among the top 30 creditors include Bryson DeChambeau ($5.7m, £4.2m), Dustin Johnson ($5.5m, £4.1m), Cameron Smith ($4.8m, £3.5m), and Tyrrell Hatton ($3.4m, £2.5m). Brooks Koepka, who left LIV to rejoin the PGA Tour in January, has an unsecured claim of $1.7m (£1.25m). A source familiar with the figures told BBC Sport the creditors list reflects "amount owed and not paid for Q3" of 2026, not the full contract values. LIV Golf estimates its assets at between $100m and $500m (£74m-£370m) and its liabilities at between $500m and $1bn (£370m-£739m).

Chapter 11 protection postpones a U.S. company's obligations to its creditors, giving it time to reorganise its debts or sell parts of the business. PIF is providing a bankruptcy loan of $49.6m (£36.6m) — called 'debtor in possession' (DIP) financing — to help fund the process. In a letter to fans, LIV Golf described the restructuring as a "court-supervised" process that "provides us with the time and framework to address previous financial obligations and complete a transaction that will make the league's next phase a reality." The letter added that the new league would be "built around a sustainable business model," with players set to receive equity and individual commercial rights returned to them. Prize money is expected to be lower than PGA Tour events but higher than DP World Tour events. Proposed changes include expanding field sizes to 75 players, introducing a cut, creating qualifiers, and having more teams that "embrace national identities."

Since LIV's controversial launch in 2021, PIF spent more than $5bn (£3.7bn), luring major winners with lucrative contracts and vast prize money. The 2026 season ended early amid uncertainty over the league's future. LIV Golf CEO Scott O'Neil told the BBC at the final event in Indianapolis last month he had "high levels of confidence" in achieving a "critical mass" of players for the new league. DeChambeau said at that event he felt there was "a lot of potential moving forward" and believed there was "something fun coming."

BBC Sport understands there is no obligation for players to sign on to LIV 2.0, regardless of previous multi-year contracts. Sources said contracts under the previous iteration will finish because of the court filing, with amounts owed addressed through the court process. It remains unclear when players would be able to enter discussions with other tours. Speaking Tuesday before the Irish Open, Rahm was asked about his future. "Yes and no. It hasn't really changed from my last interview in Indianapolis," he told BBC Sport. "There's just a lot of things in place, right? There's a lot of things that could happen and it's one of those things where time's gonna tell." He added: "I still have a contract with LIV 1.0 that I'm more than willing to fulfil, so like I said, time will tell."
