England take seven wickets as Pakistan collapse to 100-7 on first morning of third Test

Sports desk

England took seven wickets in the opening session of the third Test at Edgbaston, reducing Pakistan to 100-7 at lunch on a gloomy first morning. Joe Root won the toss and opted to bowl in Autumnal conditions, and the hosts’ four-pronged seam attack made immediate inroads against a touring side that is "clearly shot for confidence," according to former England captain Michael Vaughan.

Pakistan’s top five were all dismissed for single figures, the first time that has happened in the first innings of a Test match. Ollie Robinson opened the scoring with his 100th Test wicket, trapping Saim Ayub lbw for a duck; the ball nipped back into the left-hander and clipped the front pad. Jofra Archer then had Azan Awais caught at mid-wicket for nine, Robinson drew an edge from Abdullah Shafique to gully, and Archer removed captain Shan Masood via a mistimed shot to substitute Theo Wylie at mid-on. Josh Tongue later bowled Babar Azam for seven, had Ghazi Ghori lbw for four on debut, and broke Mohammad Imran’s off stump with a yorker.

Former Pakistan captain Ramiz Raja described the team’s performance as "pretty ordinary" and noted "mental fatigue and turmoil in the dugout." He added: "They have not been proactive at all. They have to have a plan. This is a flat pitch, it's not a demon-like pitch." Vaughan called the situation "embarrassing with everything they've had to cope with in the last week." Pakistan’s false-shot percentage in the session was 40%, the second-highest in the opening session of a Test since 2006.

The collapse continues a miserable series for Pakistan’s batting. Their second-wicket partnership has averaged only 5.6 runs across the three Tests (scores of 6, 13, 16, 7 and 14), putting them on course to break a record set by Australia in 1888 of an average of 16 for the fall of the second wicket. The PCB had made a raft of controversial changes before this match in an attempt to revive the side, but the early evidence suggests little improvement. Opener Saim Ayub, one of the new call-ups, was playing his first first-class match since October 2025.

England bowled with discipline on a pitch that CricViz rated a 5.7 for bounce inconsistency—higher than in recent years. Despite the scoreboard reading only 84-7 before the final wicket of the session fell, the morning’s events left Pakistan’s cause in Birmingham appearing forlorn. Play will resume after lunch with Saud Shakeel still at the crease, but with the tourists deep in trouble.
