Buccaneers sign Baker Mayfield to three-year, $165 million extension

Sports desk

The Tampa Bay Buccaneers and quarterback Baker Mayfield have agreed to a three-year, $165 million contract extension, paying $55 million per year, according to Adam Schefter and Ian Rapoport. The deal was reached Monday after months of negotiations that had generated little optimism for an agreement before the start of the season.

Mayfield had one year remaining on his current contract and would have been a candidate for the franchise tag next year had the sides not reached a new deal. The extension adds $30 million in guarantees to Mayfield’s contract for the 2026 season.

Mayfield, 31, was the No. 1 overall pick in the 2018 draft out of Oklahoma by the Cleveland Browns. After he played out the fourth year of his rookie contract, the Browns exercised his fifth-year option and traded him to the Carolina Panthers in exchange for a conditional 2024 fourth-round pick, with the Browns covering $10.5 million of his salary. The Panthers later cut Mayfield, and he was claimed off waivers by the Los Angeles Rams to finish the season. He signed a one-year deal worth up to $8.5 million with Tampa Bay in 2023 before inking a three-year, $100 million contract with the Buccaneers in 2024.

In the 2025 season, Mayfield appeared in all 17 games for Tampa Bay, completing 63.2 percent of his passes for 3,693 yards, 26 touchdowns and 11 interceptions, while also rushing for 382 yards and a touchdown on 55 carries.
