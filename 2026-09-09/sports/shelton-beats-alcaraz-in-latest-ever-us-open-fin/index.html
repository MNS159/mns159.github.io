Shelton beats Alcaraz in latest-ever US Open finish to reach semifinals

Sports desk

Ben Shelton outlasted defending champion Carlos Alcaraz in a five-set quarterfinal thriller that ended at 3:34 a.m. local time Wednesday, the latest finish in US Open history. The eighth-seeded American won 6-7 (5-7), 6-1, 6-3, 1-6, 7-6 (10-7) in four hours, 28 minutes on Arthur Ashe Stadium, after the night session was delayed by earlier matches. The win is Shelton's first career victory over a top-three player, snapping an 0-17 record against that tier, and sends him to his third major semifinal.

The match featured dramatic momentum swings. Alcaraz edged the first set in a tiebreak, but Shelton responded with a dominant second set, taking it 6-1 after winning nine consecutive games. He carried that form into the third set, going up two sets to one. Alcaraz, however, roared back in the fourth, breaking Shelton twice to force a decider. The fifth set stayed on serve until a 10-point match tiebreak, where Shelton prevailed 10-7, closing with an ace. Alcaraz committed 53 unforced errors in the match.

"That was a war, an epic match, so physical," Shelton said. "Carlos is one of the greatest champions in our sport already at 23 years old. It's ridiculous some of the stuff he comes up with sometimes — the most enjoyable match that I've had in my career, for sure." Alcaraz, who was playing his first tournament since a wrist injury sidelined him for more than four months, said: "It was close. So I'm just not going to be disappointed about not getting the win at the end. I'm just proud about myself, about how I fought. I leave the court with a smile, healthy, and this is what I needed."

Alcaraz entered the match with a 28-3 record at the US Open and an 18-match Grand Slam win streak, having won the Australian Open earlier this year. He had won 13 straight matches against Americans and 14 in a row against left-handers. Shelton, meanwhile, had lost all three previous meetings with the Spaniard. The victory marks the biggest win of Shelton's career and boosts the home hope of a first American men's singles champion since Andy Roddick won in 2003.

Shelton will face fellow American Frances Tiafoe in an all-American semifinal on Friday. Tiafoe earlier came back from two sets and a break down to beat Alex Michelsen. Shelton is now the second-highest seed remaining in the draw after world number two Alexander Zverev. "The city of New York that showed up tonight until 3am, that's what kept me going," Shelton said, adding he hoped his family and friends could get the day off from work.
