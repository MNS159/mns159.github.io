Houthi strikes on Saudi Arabia wound dozens, stoke Middle East war fears

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts slightly center-left

Houthi rebels fired dozens of ballistic missiles and drones at four Saudi Arabian cities early Tuesday, wounding 73 people and igniting fires at oil facilities in what Saudi officials called a serious escalation of the Middle East conflict. The attacks hit Abha, Jazan, Najran and Khamis Mushait, the Saudi-led coalition said, with the Energy Ministry reporting that operations at several oil facilities and utilities were temporarily suspended as firefighters battled the blazes.

The Houthis, who control most of Yemen's populated areas including the capital Sanaa, claimed responsibility for the barrage, saying it targeted an airbase in Khamis Mushait, an Aramco facility in Jizan, and targets in Abha and Najran. Saudi coalition spokesman Maj. Gen. Turki al-Malki said women and children were among the wounded and vowed that the coalition "will take all necessary operational measures to deter the terrorist Houthi militia with utmost resolve." Saudi Arabia's foreign ministry separately said it would "take all necessary measures to defend its sovereignty" and demanded a halt to the strikes on Saudi territory and oil tankers in the Red Sea.

The attacks shattered a United Nations-brokered truce that had largely held since 2022, ending years of full-blown conflict in which at least 150,000 people have been killed, according to UN estimates. The immediate trigger was a Houthi attempt to fly a plane from Tehran to Sanaa, which the Saudi-led coalition blocked with an airstrike on the airport runway. The Houthis responded with missile and drone attacks on Saudi Arabia and a blockade of Saudi shipping in the Red Sea, while Saudi-backed Yemeni government forces have launched counterattacks, claiming territorial gains in the provinces of Hodeida, Taiz and Baydah.

The renewed fighting has displaced about 18,500 people in Taiz and Hodeida, according to the International Organization for Migration. "Families are arriving with nothing: no shelter, no food, no way to know if it's safe to go back," IOM Yemen Chief of Mission Abdusattor Esoev said. The Houthis also blamed Saudi Arabia for an airstrike hours earlier on a prison in Yemen's northern Jawf province that killed at least seven people, including a child, and wounded seven others.

The escalation comes at a volatile moment for the region, with the war involving Iran having nearly halted shipments through the Strait of Hormuz, and the Saudi-Houthi fighting threatening to squeeze traffic through the Bab al-Mandeb strait, a critical route for oil and global trade. The Houthis, an Iran-backed group that is part of a regional alliance known as the "axis of resistance," have also gained prominence for attacks on Israel and Red Sea shipping since the war in Gaza began. The attack on Saudi Arabia was among the most serious yet, and Saudi authorities said the large number of wounded indicated a significant expansion of the conflict.
