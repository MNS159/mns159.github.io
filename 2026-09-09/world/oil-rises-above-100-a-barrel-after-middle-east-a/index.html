Oil Rises Above $100 a Barrel After Latest Wave of Middle East Attacks

International & World desk · anchored on AP

Oil prices surged past $100 a barrel on Wednesday for the first time since late July, as escalating hostilities between the United States and Iran, along with renewed Houthi attacks on Saudi energy facilities, stoked fears of supply disruptions. Brent crude, the global benchmark, touched $100.19 before settling back to $99.93, up 2.05% on the day. U.S. West Texas Intermediate crude rose 1.60% to $94.52 a barrel.

The latest spike follows tit-for-tat strikes that have intensified over the past week. The U.S. military attacked five Iranian oil tankers, sinking one—the M/T Riesco—in the Gulf of Oman, according to U.S. Central Command. Iran responded by launching missiles at a U.S. base in Jordan, most of which were shot down, and by attacking two U.S. vessels and eight oil tankers in the Strait of Hormuz. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said the situation was 'pretty straightforward,' adding, 'Iran continues to try to hit U.S. naval ships. And, for every time they do that or try to do that, they're going to lose tankers.'

Yemen’s Iran-backed Houthi movement also escalated the conflict, striking energy facilities and civilian infrastructure in Saudi Arabia on Tuesday. Saudi authorities said the drone and missile attack injured 73 people, caused fires at oil installations, and led to a temporary halt in operations. The Houthis accused Saudi Arabia of bombing a prison in al-Hazm two days earlier, killing 11 people.

The broader war began on February 28, when the U.S. and Israel launched attacks on Iran. Since then, Brent crude has fluctuated wildly, reaching a high of $126.41 a barrel on April 30. The conflict has effectively closed the Strait of Hormuz, which carries 20% of the world’s oil and liquefied natural gas (LNG). Before the war, Brent was priced at about $70 a barrel.

Rising oil prices have pushed up gasoline prices for motorists globally and fueled concerns that inflation will rebound, potentially prompting central banks to tighten monetary policy. The European Central Bank is expected to hike interest rates on Thursday, while the U.S. Federal Reserve will meet next week. Wall Street’s three main stock indexes posted small losses, and European stocks fell to one-week lows. Ipek Ozkardeskaya, a senior analyst at Swissquote, told Reuters that risk appetite remained weak due to rising oil prices from the war, and that 'summer was full of hope that a peace agreement could be achieved. This optimism is fading as we enter September.' Manish Kabra, a multi-asset strategist at Societe Generale, said $100 was a 'psychological threshold' and that crude would need to hit $150 to create a major drawback in demand.
