Novartis shares tumble after third drug trial setback in a week

Health & Science desk

Novartis shares fell as much as 10.5% in trading Tuesday after the company reported that its experimental drug del-desiran failed to improve muscle function in a late-stage trial for myotonic dystrophy type 1 (DM1), a rare neuromuscular condition. The failure is the third clinical trial setback for the Swiss pharmaceutical giant in a week, following the collapse of its pelacarsen cardiovascular drug and the suspension of eight trials of an experimental cell therapy after three patient deaths.

The global Phase 3 HARBOR study tested del-desiran, an antibody oligonucleotide conjugate therapy, in patients with DM1, a disease that causes progressive muscle stiffness and weakness. Novartis said the drug did not significantly improve patients' ability to open their hands faster compared with a placebo. The company stated it is evaluating the full dataset and will engage with health authorities to determine the most appropriate development path for del-desiran. Del-desiran was one of three therapies Novartis acquired when it bought Avidity Biosciences for approximately $12 billion last year.

The del-desiran failure comes days after Novartis announced that pelacarsen, an investigational drug designed to lower lipoprotein(a) levels, failed to reduce the risk of major adverse cardiovascular events in the Lp(a)HORIZON trial of 8,323 high-risk heart patients. Although pelacarsen lowered Lp(a) levels, the reduction did not translate into a statistically significant decrease in cardiovascular death, nonfatal heart attack, nonfatal stroke, or urgent coronary revascularization. Novartis Chief Medical Officer Shreeram Aradhye called the results 'not what we hoped for' but said they advance scientific understanding of Lp(a) lowering and cardiovascular outcomes.

Analysts cast doubt on Novartis's growth outlook following the setbacks. The company reiterated its target of 5% to 6% average annual sales growth through 2030, but Jefferies analysts said the target 'will likely be perceived as unattainable without further M&A, which is now questionable again.' Barclays analysts noted that del-desiran and pelacarsen had represented roughly $5 billion in risk-adjusted peak sales opportunities, and that the del-desiran failure in particular casts doubt on the $12 billion Avidity acquisition and the company's ability to offset upcoming patent expiries through mergers and acquisitions.

Novartis did report a positive result last week: a late-stage trial of remibrutinib showed 'a clinically meaningful delay in disability progression' in patients with relapsing multiple sclerosis. However, Jefferies analysts said confidence in that program remains uncertain without supporting data from the failed neuromuscular candidates, and that business development and M&A likely remain key to securing management's long-term growth aspirations. The stock decline put Novartis shares on track for their worst trading day since March 2020.
