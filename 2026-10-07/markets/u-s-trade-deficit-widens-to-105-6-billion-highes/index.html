U.S. Trade Deficit Widens to $105.6 Billion, Highest Since Pre-Tariff Era

Financial & Markets desk

The U.S. trade deficit widened sharply in August, hitting $105.6 billion amid an influx of goods tied to the artificial-intelligence build-out and the effects of import tariffs, the Commerce Department reported Tuesday. The figure marked a 13.7% jump from July and exceeded the Dow Jones consensus estimate of $102 billion.

Imports swelled 4.3% for the month, pushing the total imbalance to its steepest level since the all-time gap of March 2025, recorded just before President Donald Trump's 'liberation day' announcement of reciprocal tariffs against U.S. trading partners. Despite the monthly increase, the year-to-date deficit of $138.2 billion was down nearly 20% from the same period a year ago.

Oren Klachkin, financial economist at Nationwide, said in a note that 'rising prices overstate the moves, but nonetheless net trade is set to drag on Q3 GDP growth.' He added, 'We see this as a sign of strong domestic demand, not economic weakness.' Imports generally subtract from gross domestic product calculations, but if they reflect stronger demand and consumption, they can be offset elsewhere.

Following the report, Goldman Sachs cut its tracking estimate for third-quarter economic growth to 3.1%, down 0.3 percentage point from its prior estimate. The Atlanta Federal Reserve's GDPNow tracker lowered its estimate to 3.7%, down 0.1 percentage point from the last update.
