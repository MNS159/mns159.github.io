Stocks Slide as Treasury Yields Hit 24-Year High; Fed Minutes Awaited

Financial & Markets desk

U.S. stocks fell sharply on Wednesday, retreating from record highs as Treasury yields surged to their highest levels in more than two decades. The Dow Jones Industrial Average dropped roughly 500 points, or about 1.1%, while the S&P 500 declined 0.6% and the Nasdaq Composite fell 0.7%. The pullback came a day after the S&P 500 closed above 7,800 for the first time and the Nasdaq hit an all-time high, driven by bullish earnings estimates and gains in major technology stocks.

The sell-off was led by a renewed spike in bond yields. The benchmark 10-year Treasury note yield climbed more than 8 basis points to trade at 5.356%, its highest level since April 2002. The 30-year bond yield rose a similar amount to 5.725%, the highest since May 2002. The moves came ahead of a Treasury auction of $39 billion in 10-year notes and as investors awaited the release of minutes from the Federal Reserve's September policy meeting, when the central bank raised interest rates for the first time since 2023.

Higher yields weighed broadly on equities, with bank stocks particularly hard hit as investors worried that rising borrowing costs would crimp lending activity. Goldman Sachs and Citigroup each fell nearly 2%, while Bank of America, Wells Fargo, and JPMorgan lost about 1%. Technology stocks also came under pressure amid concerns that higher rates could slow the artificial intelligence buildout. CrowdStrike shares dropped almost 4%, while Palo Alto Networks and Meta Platforms fell more than 3% and 2%, respectively.

Energy costs added to the pressure. International Brent crude futures rose above $101 per barrel, while U.S. West Texas Intermediate crude hovered near $90. The spike was driven by renewed Houthi attacks in the Middle East and a tropical storm threatening the U.S. Gulf Coast, which prompted Chevron to evacuate nonessential personnel from offshore platforms. The rise in oil prices revived fears that inflation could remain stubbornly high, reinforcing the bond market's recent surge.

Investors are now focused on the Fed minutes, due at 2 p.m. ET, for clues on the central bank's future rate path. Traders are trying to determine whether September's rate hike was a one-off or a signal of further tightening. As of Tuesday, markets priced in roughly 20% odds of another hike at the Fed's October meeting. "With the level of rates where they are and the rise that we've seen, I think it's fair to characterize the fact that the margin for error has narrowed as it relates to earnings," said Mike Dickson, head of research and quantitative strategies at Horizon Investments, adding that earnings could "still carry the market higher."

On the earnings front, Constellation Brands fell 5% despite reporting better-than-expected results. The Corona and Modelo beer maker posted second-quarter net sales of $2.63 billion, above the $2.54 billion consensus, and adjusted earnings per share of $3.74, topping estimates of $3.55. The company cited a decline in off-premise sales at grocery and convenience stores, while on-premise sales at restaurants and bars grew. Constellation reaffirmed its full-year adjusted earnings forecast of $11.20 to $11.90 per share. Levi Strauss and Applied Digital are scheduled to report later.
