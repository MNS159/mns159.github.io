Inflation Expectations Jump to 3.9% as Dollar Rises Ahead of Fed Minutes

Financial & Markets desk · anchored on Reuters

Inflation expectations among U.S. consumers rose sharply in September, with the New York Federal Reserve's Survey of Consumer Expectations showing the median one-year outlook climbing to 3.9%, the highest level since May 2023. The reading, up 0.3 percentage point from August, comes as the Federal Reserve prepares to release minutes from its September policy meeting, when it raised interest rates for the first time since 2023. The U.S. dollar strengthened ahead of the release, with the dollar index rising 0.3% to 102.13, while gold prices fell 0.9% to $4,126.02 an ounce as a stronger dollar weighed on the precious metal.

The New York Fed survey also found that consumers expect household spending growth to hit 5.5%, also up 0.3 percentage point and the highest since May 2023. Longer-term expectations were more stable: the three-year outlook edged up 0.1 percentage point to 3.3%, while the five-year view held steady at 3%. Surging energy costs are a key driver of the inflation outlook; gasoline prices rose nearly 4% in August alone, and the survey showed consumers expect gas prices to rise 4.8% over the next year. Market-based measures also reflect inflation concerns, with the five-year breakeven rate near its 2025 high at 2.35%.

The Fed's September rate hike was its first since 2023, and projections released alongside the decision indicated further increases could occur before year-end as inflation remains above the central bank's 2% target. However, expectations for another hike at the Fed's October meeting have retreated following weaker-than-expected employment data and comments from policymakers signaling less urgency. According to CME FedWatch, the probability of a 25-basis-point hike in October stands at 21.6%, down from about 51% a week ago, while markets price in a 68.6% chance of a hike at the December meeting. Fed funds futures imply a rate of 5.58% in five years, compared with the current target range of 3.75%-4%.

Fed officials have offered mixed signals on the path ahead. New York Fed President John Williams said policymakers can afford to take their time evaluating where rates should be set, while Kansas City Fed President Jeff Schmid said the central bank needs to raise its policy rate further to lower inflation, even if higher long-term yields are weighing on some parts of the economy. Several Fed officials, including Christopher Waller, Neel Kashkari, and Alberto Musalem, are scheduled to speak later Wednesday. The central bank is also due to release consumer credit data, expected to show a decrease to $15 billion in August from $18.06 billion in July.

The dollar's gains were supported by rising oil prices, with Brent futures climbing back above $100 a barrel on concerns over supply disruptions from a storm threatening U.S. oil-producing regions and attacks by Yemen's Iran-aligned Houthis on Aden International Airport. The euro fell 0.4% to $1.1216, giving back some of its gains from Tuesday when French bonds rallied after far-right presidential candidate Marine Le Pen raised her target for spending cuts to €140 billion ($158 billion). The yen weakened 0.13% to 158.31 per dollar, even after a dovish Bank of Japan board member signaled support for interest rate increases. Central bank demand for gold continued to provide support to the market, with the World Gold Council reporting that central banks added 39 tonnes of gold in August, bringing the year-to-date total to 170 tonnes.
