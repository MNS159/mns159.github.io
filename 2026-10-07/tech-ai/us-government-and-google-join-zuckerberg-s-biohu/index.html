US government and Google join Zuckerberg's Biohub in $1.8B AI biology push

Tech / AI desk · anchored on Reuters

The US government and Google are joining forces with Biohub, the nonprofit biomedical research organization founded by Mark Zuckerberg and Priscilla Chan, in a $1.8 billion initiative to build artificial intelligence datasets for biology. Google DeepMind, Meta, and the AI drug discovery startup Isomorphic Labs are jointly investing $300 million into Biohub, as part of the broader effort to create AI models that could allow researchers to 'ask, predict, and answer biological questions digitally,' according to a press release cited by Reuters.

The initiative aims to develop a 'virtual cell' — an accurate predictive model of biology that would let scientists carry out simulations digitally, potentially accelerating the discovery of new ways to prevent and manage diseases. The US Department of Energy will invest more than $500 million over the next five years, while the National Institutes of Health will contribute datasets, repositories, and knowledge bases stemming from previous federal investment totaling over $500 million.

Biohub was founded in 2016 with the goal of combating diseases through the creation of a virtual cell. Alex Rives, Biohub's head of science, said in the press release: 'An accurate predictive model of biology could dramatically accelerate scientific discovery by enabling scientists to perform experiments digitally. The creation of a virtual cell is one of the most important challenges for the next era of science. It will require coordinated data generation efforts at a national and international scale, which is why these partners are coming together.'

The collaboration brings together private companies, a nonprofit research organization, and major US government agencies to tackle what they describe as a critical scientific challenge. The combined $1.8 billion in funding and data contributions is intended to generate the large-scale datasets needed to train AI systems capable of modeling biology with enough accuracy to replace some physical experiments with digital ones.
