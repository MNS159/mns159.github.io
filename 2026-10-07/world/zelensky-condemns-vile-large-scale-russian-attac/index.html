Zelensky condemns 'vile' large-scale Russian attacks that killed at least 24 across Ukraine

International & World desk

At least 24 people were killed and dozens more wounded in a wave of Russian missile strikes across central and eastern Ukraine overnight, Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky said Wednesday, calling it one of Moscow's 'most vile' attacks. Zelensky said 70 missiles were launched in total, hitting cities including Kyiv, Dnipro, Kremenchuk, Zaporizhzhia, Oleksandriya, and Pryluky in the Chernihiv region, where a five-story residential building was destroyed. He described the strike on Pryluky as a 'birthday gift' to Russian President Vladimir Putin, who turns 74 on Wednesday.

The heaviest toll was in Pryluky, where Zelensky said 18 people, including four children, were killed. Ukraine's State Emergency Service reported 50 people injured in that attack, including seven children. Two people were killed in Kyiv and two others in Oleksandriya, Zelensky added. Ukraine's national police released verified images of an unexploded Russian Iskander missile in a Kyiv district, and video showed rescuers digging through collapsed rubble in Pryluky.

Zelensky said one of the 'key targets' of the assault was Ukraine's energy infrastructure, following Moscow's earlier pledge to intensify such strikes in retaliation for Ukrainian attacks on Russian energy facilities. He renewed a call for allies to provide more air defense support, noting that Ukraine's military managed to shoot down only 'some' of the incoming missiles.

In the same 24-hour period, Zelensky said Ukraine struck four targets in retaliatory attacks on Russia's Perm, Samara, and Astrakhan regions, including two oil facilities and a training ground. 'There is also a response in the Black Sea,' he wrote, without providing details. On Tuesday evening, footage showed a large plume of smoke from an oil tanker off the coast of Sochi, Russia. The Russian transport ministry said unmanned boats had attacked the Liberian-flagged Aframax Rio tanker, and that the crew was rescued.

Separately, Bulgaria suspended a search for the missing crew of a civilian cargo ship that sank after being hit by a drone on Tuesday in the Black Sea, according to Vice-Admiral Rumen Nikolov of Bulgaria's Maritime Administration. Bulgarian Prime Minister Rumen Radev said a second vessel carrying grain was also hit within Bulgaria's exclusive economic zone, but its 18 crew members were rescued. Bulgaria has not attributed the attacks to any party. Moscow has not commented on the latest incidents involving the Black Sea shipping, though Zelensky accused Russia of being responsible for those attacks as well.

Zelensky's condemnation and plea for air defense underscore the ongoing urgency for Western support as Ukraine continues to face large-scale missile barrages while carrying out its own cross-border operations.
