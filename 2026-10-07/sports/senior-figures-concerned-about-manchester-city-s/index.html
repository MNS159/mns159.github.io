Senior figures concerned Manchester City could play Champions League despite relegation

Sports desk

Senior figures within English football are concerned that Manchester City could play in the Champions League next season even if they are relegated from the Premier League for breaching financial regulations, BBC Sport has reported. The concern centers on the possibility that City win the Champions League this season, which would provide qualification as titleholders regardless of their status in the English domestic pyramid. BBC Sport has been told there is no automatic mechanism to remove City from a European competition they qualify for on sporting merit.

An independent commission found City guilty of all charges related to inflating sponsorship revenue by £830m using sham contracts, as well as hiding other expenses worth an additional £90m. Without this, the commission said, City would have failed profit and sustainability rules in all nine seasons between 2009-10 and 2017-18. City continue to strenuously deny any wrongdoing, calling the outcome a 'Premier League conspiracy,' and last week lodged an appeal against the verdict.

The immediate issue surrounds the Uefa licence every club needs to play in Europe. Licences are issued by the Football Association, though the process is delegated to the Premier League. They are based on current information and do not take into account sanctions or breaches related to historical offences. One source said the issue of City's licence 'had been raised' and that a solution 'would require the sort of joined up thinking that's been a bit lacking so far.' A source added that 'licences are awarded on the basis of current financial performance, so the grounds to refuse one are unclear.'

If the verdict is upheld by the appeal board, City could face severe sanctions including heavy points deductions, a large fine, transfer embargoes, or even immediate expulsion from the Premier League. The sanctioning hearing has not yet been scheduled. While a points deduction could rule City out of qualifying by league position, qualification could still be achieved through cup competitions. If City win the FA Cup or Carabao Cup, that would provide a place in the Europa League or Conference League respectively.

European football's governing body cannot take any action while the Premier League disciplinary process plays out, but it is understood potential scenarios are likely to be discussed by the Uefa administration and the Uefa club financial control body (CFCB). Uefa may open a disciplinary case to find a way to remove City's eligibility, but City's offences are historical. In 2020, City successfully overturned a two-year European ban from Uefa at the Court of Arbitration for Sport (Cas) because the offences were 'time-barred' under Uefa's rules. If City are not successful in their appeal and win the Champions League, the CFCB will have to find a disciplinary measure to remove the club, but there is concern City could again win against Uefa through Cas.
