MLB Division Series Day 5: Freeman sets lefty HR record, Padres win without Miller, Dodgers take 2-1 lead

Sports desk

Day 5 of the MLB Division Series delivered a full slate of action and developments across both leagues. The Los Angeles Dodgers took a 2-1 lead over the Atlanta Braves with a 3-1 victory in Game 3, moving within one win of eliminating the Braves and advancing to their third consecutive National League Championship Series. The San Diego Padres avoided elimination in the NLDS by beating the Milwaukee Brewers 4-3 at Petco Park, forcing a Game 4 on Wednesday. In the ALDS, the Tampa Bay Rays hold a commanding 2-0 series lead over the New York Yankees after a 5-2 win in Game 2, with Game 3 set for Wednesday night at Yankee Stadium.

Dodgers first baseman Freddie Freeman made postseason history Tuesday night, hitting a solo home run in the top of the fourth inning against Atlanta left-hander Chris Sale to give Los Angeles a 3-0 lead. The blast was the eighth lefty-on-lefty home run of Freeman's postseason career, establishing an MLB playoff record for left-handed hitters against left-handed pitchers, according to Elias Sports Bureau. Freeman, who spent his first 12 MLB seasons with the Braves before joining the Dodgers, batted .288 with 16 home runs during the regular season and is a former National League MVP and World Series MVP.

The Dodgers made a notable lineup change for Game 3, moving Shohei Ohtani from the No. 2 spot to fifth in the batting order. Manager Dave Roberts had opened the door to the adjustment, saying, "Going further than moving him from one to two, I'm open to it." Ohtani went 2-for-8 in the first two NLDS games and chased 42.9% of pitches outside the zone this postseason. Former MLB player Trevor Plouffe criticized the move to fifth as "disrespectful," arguing Ohtani should bat fourth instead. Ohtani was receptive to the change and had joked during a September slump about telling Roberts to hit him seventh. The Dodgers' starting lineup for Game 3 was Mookie Betts, Freddie Freeman, Teoscar Hernandez, Will Smith, Shohei Ohtani, Miguel Rojas, Andy Pages, Kyle Tucker and Kike Hernandez.

The Padres won Game 3 without All-Star closer Mason Miller, who was placed on the postseason paternity list Tuesday afternoon after his wife went into labor with the couple's first child. MLB approved the roster change, and the Padres replaced Miller with reliever Wandy Peralta. Under postseason paternity rules, Miller can miss a minimum of one day and a maximum of three; when he returns, he can replace Peralta without being forced to sit out another series. Miller had a career-high 38 saves this season with a 1.67 ERA. Peralta, a 35-year-old lefty, made 65 appearances this season and went 3-3 with a 3.61 ERA. Miller is eligible to return for Game 4 on Wednesday. The Padres had lost Games 1 and 2 by one run each, and Miller blew the save in Game 2 when Jackson Chourio hit a walk-off single that drove in two runs for a 4-3 Milwaukee victory.

In the ALDS, the Rays have taken a 2-0 series lead over the Yankees with a 1-0 win in Game 1 behind Drew Rasmussen and a 5-2 victory in Game 2 behind Freddy Peralta. Game 3 is scheduled for Wednesday at 8 p.m. ET at Yankee Stadium, with Tampa Bay expected to start right-hander Nick Martinez (15-6, 3.10 ERA) and New York expected to counter with left-hander Max Fried (5-5, 2.80 ERA). The Yankees are a -164 favorite on the money line, with an over/under of 7 runs. SportsLine's computer model, which simulates every game 10,000 times, projects the over to hit 54% of the time and recommends betting over 7 combined runs.

Looking ahead, the Dodgers can eliminate the Braves with a win in Game 4 on Wednesday in Atlanta; if the Braves force a Game 5, it will be played Friday in Los Angeles. The Padres and Brewers continue their series with Game 4 on Wednesday in San Diego. The Yankees and Rays play Game 3 on Wednesday night, with the Rays seeking a sweep and the Yankees trying to avoid a 3-0 deficit.
