Nvidia stock slides as AI doubts persist despite Jensen Huang's return

Financial & Markets desk

Jensen Huang returned as CEO of Nvidia on Nov. 23 after resigning on Nov. 12 over reports of an affair with a woman U.S. intelligence reportedly believes has links to Chinese intelligence. His return sent Nvidia shares surging.

President Donald Trump told CNBC he intervened to bring Huang back, saying, 'We have to protect our geniuses, and he's clearly a genius.' Trump warned that Huang could hypothetically have taken a job in China, though he acknowledged 'never even a suggestion' that the founder was recruited by China.

The turmoil around Huang's future highlighted the U.S.-China AI race, which is about more than semiconductors. China has surpassed the U.S. by share of global AI talent in just three years, according to Carnegie Endowment research. 'Whoever builds and keeps that human layer will hold the rest of the stack,' said Winston Ma, an AI investor and adjunct professor of law at New York University. Chinese companies are 'very, very aggressive in getting good talent from the U.S.,' said Rachel Wu of Tidalwave Solutions, noting that a Meta engineer in Canada took an offer from a Chinese AI startup for a salary of 1.4 million yuan ($210,000) plus stock options.

Federal Reserve Chairman Kevin Warsh, who has convened an AI task force, acknowledged the outlook is 'moving rapidly and is deeply uncertain.' Markets may be rethinking the potential earnings upside and spending on AI buildout, while the adoption rate and economic effects on inflation and growth remain unclear. Warsh has pushed for the Fed to rely more on market signals than forecasts, but the Nvidia situation shows 'markets can be very short-term about the long-term signals they send,' according to CNBC's Steve Liesman.

Huang founded Nvidia more than 30 years ago as a gaming graphics chip company. The semiconductors later became used to mine cryptocurrencies and now train cutting-edge AI models. Trump said Huang 'has other chips in his brain that nobody else knows about.'
