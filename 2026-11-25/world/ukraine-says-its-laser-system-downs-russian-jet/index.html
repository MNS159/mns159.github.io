Ukraine says its laser system downs Russian jet-powered drone in world first

International & World desk

Ukraine announced on November 25 that its Blysk laser system had intercepted a Russian jet-powered Geran drone in combat for the first time, marking a milestone for experimental air-defense technology. The claim was made by presidential adviser Serhii "Flash" Beskrestnov, who said the system, deployed by the company Carmine Sky, was protecting a critical infrastructure facility.

Beskrestnov cautioned against overoptimism, stating: "It is still too early to talk about serial production of the laser. Tests are underway, during which lasers protect critical infrastructure facilities." The Blysk laser has a reported power output of four kilowatts and an effective range of up to one kilometer. Persistent cloud cover and fog over Kyiv had previously prevented the system from engaging Russian drones effectively, as lasers suffer in poor weather and cloud cover can disperse the beam.

The development comes as laser weapons are increasingly seen as a cheaper alternative to conventional air-defense missiles, with Israel's Iron Beam among the most prominent examples. Carmine Sky announced on November 19 that Ukraine's first air-defense laser had been deployed to help protect Kyiv from Russian drones, offering a potentially lower-cost method that requires electricity rather than more costly interceptor drones or missiles. However, precision tracking of agile drones with a laser remains challenging.

Beskrestnov said the technology is being developed under the "Private Air Defense" state program with support from the Brave-1 defense technology cluster. Launched by the Defense Ministry, the program authorizes companies to form air-defense groups under the Air Force's operational control. Currently, about 100 companies nationwide have joined the effort, with half forming active combat units using systems ranging from interceptor drones to automated gun turrets.

Ukrainian postal and courier company Nova Poshta, for example, has started its own private air-defense force and shot down Russian attack drones targeting their facilities, according to Vyacheslav Klymov, co-owner of the Nova group of companies. As of October 14, private air-defense units had shot down more than 60 Russian aerial targets, primarily drones, according to Ukraine's Defense Ministry. Then-Defense Minister Mykhailo Fedorov said of the initiative: "We created a model where the state, military and business work as one system."
