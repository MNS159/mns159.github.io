Poland to Establish No-Fly Zone on Ukraine Border, Urges NATO Allies to Follow

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

Poland announced on November 25, 2026, that it will create a no-fly zone inside its border with Ukraine and called on other NATO members to do the same along the alliance's eastern flank. Deputy Defense Minister Cezary Tomczyk said an ordinance is being prepared and should take effect at the end of December or the beginning of January. The move comes after repeated Russian drone and missile incursions into Polish airspace amid Moscow's ongoing war against Ukraine. Poland already strictly limits air traffic at the border; the new zone will cover several dozen kilometers of territory and provide additional support for the armed forces.

The announcement coincides with a series of military and diplomatic developments in the nearly five-year-old conflict. Ukraine's General Staff reported that its forces attacked the Russian Savasleyka air base in the Nizhny Novgorod region, damaging three MiG-31 jets, used to launch hypersonic missiles, and two Su-34 fighter-bombers, knocking them out of operation for some time. Russia did not immediately comment. Overnight, both sides struck energy infrastructure: Russian attacks shut down a thermal power plant and a gas production facility in Poltawa, causing power outages in five regions; Ukrainian drones hit at least two oil refineries in Russia's Samara region, killing one person and sparking large fires.

In Berlin, German Chancellor Friedrich Merz defended his government's expenditures on Ukraine aid during a budget debate, arguing that Russia wants more than Ukraine and is escalating in the Baltic region, Poland, the Black Sea, in space, and inside Germany. 'Anyone who doesn't see that doesn't understand the world around them, or they are pursuing entirely different aims,' Merz said, rebuking opposition parties—the Left Party and Alternative for Germany—that have called for ending aid. He stressed that by supporting Ukraine, Germany is protecting itself.

Russian officials reiterated their demand that European diplomats travel to Moscow to explain their intentions before they can participate in any peace negotiations. Foreign Policy Advisor Yury Ushakov said no Europeans have accepted the invitation, which President Vladimir Putin extended last month. Ambassador to Germany Sergei Nechayev called the reaction 'restrained,' claiming Europe is the impediment to stopping the war and that Moscow does not see the same 'honesty' from Europeans as from the Trump administration's representatives.

Allies remain divided over Ukraine's recent attacks on Russian ships off the coast of Libya. French Foreign Minister Jean-Noel Barrot defended Kyiv's actions as an exercise of its 'absolute right to self-defense,' noting Russia's ongoing aggression. Italian Foreign Minister Antonio Tajani, however, called it a 'mistake to violate Libya's sovereignty' and said Italy was unaware of Kyiv's plans. Tajani said he conveyed Italy's concern to Ukrainian Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha while reiterating Rome's support for Ukraine's sovereignty.

Separately, a German-Ukrainian woman accused of spying for Russia goes on trial in Berlin on Thursday. Prosecutors charge her with providing sensitive information to a Russian Embassy employee—the country's former deputy military attaché, who has since been expelled—and attempting to recruit individuals from Germany's Defense Ministry and arms industry. She was arrested in January 2026 and is said to have acted out of personal conviction, making contact with the Russian asset as early as October 2023.
