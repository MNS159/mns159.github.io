Leaked data shows over 700,000 Russian soldiers killed in Ukraine war

International & World desk

More than 700,000 Russian soldiers have been killed in the war in Ukraine, according to a new projection based on leaked official data from Russia's state statistics agency, Rosstat. The estimate, published by the exiled Russian outlet Meduza, projects a total of 720,000 military deaths, including those not yet recorded. The leak, released earlier this month by Belarusian and Ukrainian hackers, contains nearly 30 million death records from early 2021 through September 2026. Independent media including the BBC and Mediazona analyzed the data and arrived at a figure of at least 490,000 registered military deaths as of the end of September.

The Rosstat dataset, which the Kremlin acknowledged was obtained through a cyberattack but denied any protected information was leaked, includes ages, places of residence, places of death, and circumstances of death. Analysts filtered the records by location, cause of death, and demographic criteria to isolate military fatalities. They identified 425,174 entries with an explicit military-related cause of death, such as explosions or shrapnel during combat, and added 14,093 entries identified by employment records and 51,067 entries based on court rulings declaring soldiers dead after going missing for more than six months. The data was cross-checked against Mediazona's own named list of war dead, matching 95% of records, as well as official registries. Mediazona said fabricating a dataset of this scale would be 'impossible' and expressed confidence the data is genuine.

Meduza's projection of 720,000 deaths accounts for a significant lag in military death registrations, which has grown dramatically since 2024. The outlet used epidemiological methods and actuarial science tools, such as the 'chain-ladder method,' to estimate deaths that have occurred but have not yet appeared in the data. The BBC's own analysis of the leaked files found that at least 490,334 Russian soldiers have died since the start of the war. The BBC's separate named list, compiled from open sources, stands at 290,012 deaths, but military experts estimate that figure represents only 45-55% of the true death toll, which would range from 527,295 to 644,471.

The data reveals stark demographic and regional impacts. In 2025, 65% of Russian men aged 18-24 who died lost their lives in the war, and 59% of those aged 25-34. The average age of those killed rose from 34 in the first year of the war to 40 by 2025, as prisoners and contract soldiers entered the ranks. Russia's poorest regions in Eastern Siberia, such as Tyva, Buryatia, and Zabaykalsky Krai, account for a disproportionately large share of losses, with men there 20-25 times more likely to die in the war than residents of Moscow or St. Petersburg. At least 5,017 foreigners are among the dead, including 1,031 Uzbeks and 720 Tajiks. The BBC also identified 2,304 North Korean soldiers who died repelling a Ukrainian incursion into Russia in 2024, based on satellite imagery and official photographs of a new memorial in Pyongyang.

Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov dismissed the analysis on Wednesday, saying, 'The only department authorized to officially release data on casualties is the Ministry of Defense of the Russian Federation. I suggest disregarding any other data.' The Russian Defense Ministry has not officially updated its death toll since the early months of the war, when then-Defense Minister Sergei Shoigu put it at nearly 6,000. Ukrainian losses were last estimated by President Volodymyr Zelensky at about 50,000 in July 2026, though analysts consider that an undercount; an anonymous open-source website puts the figure at 115,952, and a Center for Strategic and International Studies assessment earlier this year suggested up to 150,000 Ukrainian soldiers killed. The war, now in its fifth year, shows no sign of ending despite pressure from President Donald Trump for Kyiv to negotiate.
