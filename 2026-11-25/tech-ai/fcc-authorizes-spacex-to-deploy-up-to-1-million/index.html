FCC authorizes SpaceX to deploy up to 1 million satellites for orbital data center project

Tech / AI desk

The Federal Communications Commission on Wednesday granted SpaceX conditional authorization to deploy a constellation of up to 1 million satellites under the company's "Starmind" initiative, designed to serve as orbital data centers. If fully deployed, the constellation would increase the number of satellites in orbit by roughly 50-fold. The decision marks the first approval the agency has granted for a full constellation of AI satellites, with other companies including Google and Blue Origin also pursuing similar concepts.

SpaceX has described Starmind as a way to "capture solar energy in space to power" AI capabilities on Earth, transmitting data using high-speed lasers that connect to its existing Starlink network. The company plans to produce chips for the satellites at an upcoming Terafab facility. The FCC authorization gives SpaceX a long regulatory runway: the company has seven years to deploy a single operational Starmind satellite, until November 2035 to deploy 100,000 operational satellites, and until November 2040 for the full constellation.

The authorization includes several conditions. SpaceX has proposed operating in six orbits ranging from 550 km to about 1,000 km. The company sought a waiver from FCC rules to allow satellites above 600 km to be disposed of into "Earth disposal orbits or heliocentric orbits" rather than burning up in Earth's atmosphere. The FCC deferred that request, saying SpaceX has not provided "sufficient information" about its disposal process plans.

FCC Chairman Brendan Carr said in a statement: "The United States is moving decisively to ensure that this breakthrough technology creates opportunity, drives investment, and delivers lasting benefits for the American people. This action opens a bold new frontier for the computing infrastructure needed to power America's leadership and growth." The decision, announced a day before the Thanksgiving holiday, represents a major regulatory victory for SpaceX and its founder, Elon Musk, who has allied himself with the Trump administration.

While the announcement clears a major hurdle to deploying data centers in space, it does not establish that orbital data centers are technically or economically viable. The FCC authorization appears designed to give the company wide latitude as it works through those remaining issues. Separately, Google has already taken its first steps toward putting compute into space with the launch of a "Project Suncatcher" AI server satellite last month, and Blue Origin will carry out the first dedicated heavy-lift launch for Project Suncatcher in 2029.
