Back-to-back measles deaths in US; tally now at 14

Tech / AI desk

The United States has recorded two more measles deaths in consecutive days, bringing the national death toll to 14 for the year, according to state health officials. Pennsylvania reported its ninth death on November 23 — an unvaccinated resident of Centre County — and Wisconsin reported its third on November 24, an adult in Lafayette County, following two earlier deaths in the state of an infant and a child. New York and Virginia have also reported deaths, including a 7-month-old baby in New York and a child between ages 0 and 4 in Virginia.

The national tally of measles cases is approaching 6,000, the highest since 1991. Transmission is widespread across 47 states and jurisdictions, with 43 outbreaks this year accounting for 96 percent of cases. Four states have case counts above 500; Pennsylvania leads with 1,638 cases as of November 23. Nearly 650 people have been hospitalized nationwide.

Measles is the most contagious virus known; it can linger in the air for up to two hours, and up to 90 percent of people who are not immune will become ill after an exposure. People are considered immune if they have been vaccinated or previously infected. With slipping vaccination rates nationwide, the number of susceptible individuals is growing.

Last week, international health authorities formally declared that the US had lost its measles-elimination status, meaning the virus is now endemic. To prevent measles from establishing a foothold, vaccination rates must be 95 percent or higher. According to the latest data, only 92.4 percent of US kindergartners received the measles, mumps, and rubella (MMR) vaccine in the 2025–2026 school year. Only 10 states meet or exceed the 95 percent threshold, and even in those states, pockets of undervaccinated communities remain.
