Tech sell-off deepens Friday, capping worst week since June for S&P 500 and Nasdaq

Financial & Markets desk · anchored on Reuters

U.S. stocks ended sharply lower Friday, capping a volatile week in which the technology sector suffered its steepest decline since June. The tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite dropped 2.1%, while the S&P 500 fell 1% and the Dow Jones Industrial Average slipped 0.4%. For the week, the Nasdaq lost 4.5% — snapping a monthlong winning streak with its worst weekly performance since late June — and the S&P 500 declined 1.95%. The Dow managed a 0 .7% weekly gain as investors rotated out of technology. The Cboe Volatility Index surged more than four points to a high of 22 .31 , its highest intraday level since June11 , while Brent crude touched$110 a barrel for the first time since May and West Texas Inter mediate crude neared$100 , adding to inflation concerns after this week’s hot price reports.

The sell-off deepened across software and semiconductor stocks, which had diverged for much of the year but fell in tandem Friday amid renewed scrutiny of artificial-intelligence spending and sensitivity to rising interest rates. Shares of the neocloud provider CoreWeave fell10 %, extending pressure this week as investors fretted over the company’s debt-heavy balance sheet;the stock had bounced Thursday after Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang voiced optimism. The broader AI trade remained under scrutiny, though Bank of America analysts led by Vivek Arya argued it is “resilient,” citing that “deployed AI infrastructure runs near100 % utilization rate”and that GPU rental rates have risen12 % to37 % year to date. The analysts acknowledged risks from a potential credit wipeout at a neocloud company and pressure from higher rates, but wrote: “We think the risk is priced in.”

Several individual stocks moved on earnings and corporate news. Aluminum producer Alcoa rose more than6 % after third-quarter earnings beat estimates, helped by a rebound in its alumina business. Tesla shares erased early gains to fall2 % after the EV maker unveiled its second-generation Roadster, which uses SpaceX-developed rocket-style thrusters and is described as the world’s first flying car, with production expected to begin in2028 . Netflix fell2 % after announcing a leadership shake-up: chief content officer Bela Bajaria is leaving, to be replaced by former Disney executive Peter Rice, a day after Netflix said it would cut roughly6 % of its workforce, about1 ,000 jobs. Trucking giant J..B. Hunt slipped3 % on mixed quarterly results;revenue exceeded estimates but adjusted earnings per share fell just short. Fox Factory tumbled9 % after CEO Mike Dennison stepped down and the company trimmed its full-year outlook. Moog rose4 % after Morgan Stanley upgraded the stock to overweight with a$448 price target. Independent Bank and Regions Financial each gained on earnings beats. Travelers fell4 % even after reporting a sizable earnings and revenue beat. Palantir fell7 %, erasing about$34 billion in market value and heading toward a correction from its record close on October9 . Moderna sank9 % as investors took profits and Goldman Sachs maintained a neutral rating while raising its price target.

MarketWatch noted that the S&P 500’s tech sector just had its worst week in a year and a half, while an equal-weighted version of the index cemented a second-straight weekly advance, signaling a broadening of market leadership away from mega-cap technology. The shift comes as the10 -year Treasury yield hovered near5 .10%. Earnings season continues next week with results from Tesla, Netflix, and Meta Platforms expected.
