White House press secretary Katie Zacharia exits after five days

Politics desk · anchored on Reuters
✓ Facts
  • Katie Zacharia served as White House press secretary for about a week before a White House official announced Friday she was "transitioning out" of the role; NPR, The Hill and CNBC described the tenure as five days, while Fox News put it at seven days, citing an Oct. 9 start date and an Oct. 13 first briefing.
  • Zacharia held her first and only press briefing on Tuesday, which lasted about 14 minutes and included questions from only three reporters.
  • On Fox News's "Hannity" Thursday night, Zacharia acknowledged she never obtained a U.S. law license, saying she had pre-registered for a bar exam before moving to China and "sat for it" so as not to have "withdraw status," despite having been referred to as an attorney by President Trump when he announced her appointment; Zacharia maintained a distinction between calling herself a "lawyer" versus an "attorney," saying past social media posts calling her an attorney were retweets that she had corrected.
  • On Friday, President Trump told reporters on the South Lawn that he asked Zacharia, "Is this for you?", said she had been treated "pretty nastily" by the media, and advised her "you should reconsider" continuing in the job, while saying he was not firing her: "I want her to have a good life."
  • A senior White House official confirmed to multiple outlets that Zacharia was "transitioning out" of the press secretary position.
  • Zacharia posted on X Friday night calling the job "an honor" and praising Trump as "a kind, wonderful, gracious, thoughtful, enthusiastic, and hardworking man."
  • Zacharia is a graduate of Pepperdine Caruso School of Law who worked at the Beijing-based law firm Zhong Lun roughly 2011–2013, and more recently served as a communications advisor for Trump Media & Technology Group and as a pro-Trump commentator on Fox News and Newsmax before becoming press secretary.
  • The press secretary post is now vacant again; predecessor Karoline Leavitt left the role roughly two months earlier, and the White House has not held regular press briefings since, with Trump frequently fielding reporters' questions himself.
○ Analysis

The episode marks one of the shortest tenures for a senior White House communications official in recent history, drawing comparisons to Anthony Scaramucci's 10-day stint as communications director in 2017. Zacharia's exit followed two compounding problems: a widely criticized debut briefing that was unusually brief compared with her predecessors', and unresolved questions about whether she could accurately describe herself as a practicing attorney. The turnover comes as the position has already sat largely dormant for press briefings since Karoline Leavitt's departure roughly two months prior, with Trump increasingly acting as his own spokesperson. It also lands at a politically sensitive moment, with Trump's approval ratings at lows for his presidency and Republicans working to shore up support ahead of the midterm elections, giving added weight to how the administration manages its public-facing communications operation.

Steelman

The episode is widely covered as a rapid, credential-driven collapse of a high-visibility hire, underscoring the practical risk of elevating media-friendly messaging figures into scrutiny-heavy spokesperson roles without the credentials or preparation to withstand sustained press questioning.

Implications

The White House will need to decide whether to name another press secretary or continue relying on Trump speaking directly to reporters without regular briefings, a pattern already in place for about two months. The episode may also prompt closer vetting of professional credentials for future communications hires, particularly as the administration heads into a competitive midterm campaign season under pressure from low approval ratings.
