Trump waives Russian sanctions passed by Congress

Politics desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum
✓ Facts
  • Trump signed a Russia sanctions package into law last month; it was posthumously dedicated to the late Sen. Lindsey Graham (R-S.C.) and is referred to as the Graham Act.
  • The law gave Trump broad powers to impose tariffs of up to 100% on imports from the top five purchasers of Russian oil and gas, most significantly China and India, and included sanctions on senior Russian officials (including Putin), Russian financial institutions, and Russia's 'shadow fleet' of vessels used to evade existing embargoes.
  • On Friday evening, two days before the sanctions were due to take effect, Trump signed an executive order waiving most of them.
  • Trump's executive order stated that suspending the sanctions was in the US 'national interest.'
  • Russian presidential envoy Kirill Dmitriev called the waiver 'an important and truly historic moment' in a post on X.
  • Rep. Don Beyer (D-Va.) criticized the move on X, calling it a 'surrender to Putin' that 'betrays our Ukrainian partners.'
  • Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky said the sanctions 'could have helped enormously' and accused the US of deciding to 'completely disregard' the law's intent.
  • Zelensky said Ukrainian and US negotiating teams spoke Friday and could meet again the following week.
○ Analysis

The waiver concerns a sanctions package Trump himself signed into law last month, which Congress designed to pressure the Kremlin economically—through potential tariffs on major buyers of Russian energy like China and India, and direct sanctions on Russian officials, banks, and oil-shipping networks. By issuing an executive order to block many of these measures just before they took effect, Trump stated the move was in the US 'national interest.' The move comes amid a broader rupture between Washington and Kyiv, following a separate controversial US deal to import Russian diesel (aimed at easing domestic fuel prices) and reports of disruptions to Ukraine's Starlink satellite access, both of which have fueled accusations that the administration is tilting toward Moscow at Ukraine's expense.

Steelman

The core tension is between a law Congress passed to constrain Russia economically and a president issuing an executive order to waive many of those sanctions, citing US national interest—leaving both the sanctions' deterrent effect and Washington's reliability as an ally to Kyiv in question.

Implications

The waiver is likely to intensify friction between the White House and both Congress and Kyiv, while Ukrainian and US negotiating teams are expected to meet again soon amid continued Russian strikes on Ukrainian energy infrastructure.
