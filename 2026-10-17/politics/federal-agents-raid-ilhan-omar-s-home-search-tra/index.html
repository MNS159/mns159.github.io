Federal agents raid Ilhan Omar's home, search trash in immigration fraud probe

Politics desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts slightly center-right
✓ Facts
  • Monday morning, Homeland Security Investigations (HSI) agents executed a judicially authorized search warrant on Rep. Ilhan Omar's Washington, D.C. home.
  • The Wall Street Journal reported agents seized Omar's personal cell phone, computers and passport during the search; sources told The Hill that investigators had earlier searched Omar's trash for DNA before the raid, a technique used when a search warrant is not available.
  • The immigration probe is examining whether Omar or her late father committed fraud on paperwork related to her 1995 arrival in the U.S. and her 2000 naturalization, including whether her age was accurately reported; the Journal reported the probe's goal is to determine whether she could be stripped of citizenship.
  • More than a dozen subpoenas have been issued in the immigration investigation, including to members of Omar's family.
  • On Tuesday, U.S. Attorney for the District of Columbia Jeanine Pirro confirmed her office is separately investigating discrepancies in Omar's congressional financial disclosures.
  • Omar's 2023 disclosure listed $15,000-$51,000 in assets tied to her husband's venture capital firm and winery; her May 2025 filing for 2024 listed $6 million-$30 million in the same assets; she later amended the filing to $18,004-$95,000, stating the businesses had zero value after liabilities and blaming an accounting error.
  • The National Legal and Policy Center filed a complaint over the disclosures in February 2025; in September the Office of Congressional Conduct voted 5-1 to recommend the House Ethics Committee dismiss the misconduct allegations.
○ Analysis

Rep. Ilhan Omar, D-Minn., is currently the subject of two distinct federal inquiries that converged publicly within days of each other: a Homeland Security Investigations criminal probe into whether she or her father committed immigration fraud decades ago, and a separate U.S. Attorney's office review of recent discrepancies in her congressional financial disclosures. The immigration probe is notable because its stated aim—denaturalization of a sitting member of Congress—would be a highly unusual legal outcome; legal experts cited in the reporting disagree on Omar's potential criminal exposure, with one former FBI agent noting she likely couldn't be held criminally liable for paperwork her father filled out while she was a minor, though she could still face denaturalization if her citizenship was obtained through materially false information. The financial disclosure matter stems from a watchdog complaint over a one-year spike and subsequent correction in reported assets tied to her husband's businesses, a matter the House's own ethics review process had already moved to dismiss before Pirro's office took it up. The timing—roughly three weeks before the November general election—has made the raid a flashpoint, with the search warrant requiring a federal judge's finding of probable cause, which legally establishes only that evidence related to the investigation was likely to be found at the location, not that any crime was committed.

◐ Debate

Democratic

  • Rep. Ilhan Omar stated the raid was 'an outrageous abuse of power and an unprecedented escalation in the weaponization of federal law enforcement to target political enemies,' and said she 'has done nothing wrong.'
  • Omar said Trump has 'relentlessly targeted' her for a decade with 'inflammatory rhetoric' and argued 'no president should be able to use the power of the federal government to intimidate his political enemies.'

Republican

  • President Trump said of the raid, 'I understand they're looking at her very carefully... I've always found her to be totally crooked, personally,' while adding 'that has nothing to do with me' and 'that's really up to the DOJ.'
  • Vice President JD Vance said the administration wants 'the law, the facts' to drive the matter rather than being 'like the Biden administration' in going after political opponents, while calling Omar 'a very unprecedented person' with 'disdain' for the country.
  • Oversight Project President Mike Howell argued that securing a federal search warrant in Washington, D.C. suggests investigators have strong evidence, saying 'they probably got the goods on her.'
Steelman

Vance and Howell argue the investigations should be allowed to proceed on the facts and the law, with Vance explicitly rejecting the idea of pursuing political opponents the way he accuses the Biden administration of doing. Omar and her defenders counter that the probes themselves—given their timing before the election and the House ethics committee's prior recommendation to dismiss the financial disclosure allegations—amount to the kind of political weaponization Vance says he wants to avoid.

Implications

The outcome of the immigration probe could set a rare precedent for denaturalization proceedings against a sitting member of Congress, while the separate financial disclosure investigation adds a second track of federal scrutiny that could outlast the House's own ethics review, which has already recommended dismissal.
