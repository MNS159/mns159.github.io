Smart Home

Apple launches HomeView smart display and Home Collection devices

Tech / AI desk

Apple this week entered the smart home hardware market with the introduction of the HomeView smart display and the Apple Home Collection, a line of devices including cameras, a thermostat, a smart door lock, and a video doorbell co-developed with LG Electronics and Schneider Electric. The HomeView, revealed in a three-minute ad showing a chaotic family morning routine, is a kitchen-focused smart display that uses Siri AI to pull up recipes, calendars, and messages. The Apple Home Collection starts at $200 per device and promises one-tap setup, tight integration, and data privacy.

The HomeView’s recipe function is designed to strip online recipes down to ingredient lists, step-by-step instructions, and timers, controlled hands-free by voice. While this simplifies cooking, it removes the ads, affiliate links, and contextual writing that many recipe writers rely on for income. Bettina Makalintal, senior reporter at Eater, said such tools “break apart that unspoken social contract with recipe developers and audiences.” The feature requires users to first open the recipe on an iPhone or Safari on the HomeView, guaranteeing at least one visit to the original page, but that’s less valuable than the repeated ad impressions from scrolling through a full page, the article notes.

Apple’s move comes after more than a decade of relying on third-party partners for HomeKit-compatible devices. Kaiann Drance, Apple’s vice president of product marketing for home, said the timing was driven by two technologies: the Matter open smart home standard, which provides ecosystem interoperability, and advanced Siri AI, which enables a more conversational experience. “With the magic of Siri AI, it comes together in a way that we haven’t been able to deliver before,” Drance said. The HomeView is positioned as a gateway device: once users try a smart home product and find it easy, Drance argued, they are more likely to add cameras or locks.

Apple is not the first company to streamline online recipes. Amazon’s Echo Show offers similar hands-free cooking with partner sites like Allrecipes, while Google previously had a step-by-step recipe feature on Nest Hubs but removed it in 2024 as “underutilized.” More recently, Google Search uses Gemini AI to amalgamate recipes directly on the search page, bypassing original sources entirely. Makalintal said the threat from Apple’s HomeView is “below AI summaries” because AI summaries are more “opaque” and “really erasing labor.”

Recipe writers face ongoing challenges from these tools. Jaya Saxena, co-founder of the food website Ravenous, said the complaints about recipe intros are overblown: “Nearly every food blog has a ‘jump to recipe’ button right up at the top, so you don’t actually have to scroll. So if you’re still out here complaining about these essays, you’re just an asshole.” Larger platforms like Allrecipes and NYT Cooking can cast apps to the HomeView using CarPlay-like technology, presenting content on their own terms, but independent writers lack that option. Many are turning to Substack and paywalls, though Makalintal noted that subscription fatigue among home cooks limits that model’s reach.

Saxena emphasized that quality recipes require significant time and expense. “A good, creative recipe that has been tested to ensure it really works takes time and effort and money … People should probably be more comfortable paying for that,” she said. Apple has not yet responded to questions about how the HomeView will handle paywalled recipes. The long-term impact may depend on adoption: unless the HomeView sells in large volumes, the recipe feature will reach far fewer users than Google’s AI summaries, which Saxena said is “more like a continuation of the threats they’ve already seen.”
