Ukraine expands strikes into Russia as war enters new phase

International & World desk

Ukraine has intensified attacks on Russian digital infrastructure, damaging three of the five major data centers powering Yandex, a Russian technology company, since October 8, disrupting services for millions of users across Russia and neighboring countries. The strikes have affected everything from online payments and ride-hailing to search and navigation, exposing vulnerabilities in Russia's domestic digital economy. The attacks come as Russian President Vladimir Putin declared that Moscow is establishing a non-negotiable “security zone” inside Ukrainian territory along the border, spanning 660 kilometers (410 miles), of which 70% is currently under Russian control, according to Kremlin spokesperson Dmitry Peskov.

The Yandex data center strikes have caused widespread disruption: users in St. Petersburg reported smart bulbs not working, a Moscow taxi driver said the Yandex Pro app malfunctioned, preventing orders and withdrawals, and a Moscow resident lost access to 1.5 million rubles (about $17,830) in her Yandex Pay account. Yandex said customers' money was safe and it was working to restore services. Ukrainian defense company Fire Point trolled Yandex on social media, responding to a statement from the tech giant's fintech division head with: "Dima, have you tried turning it off and on again." Kyiv has framed the attacks as retaliation for Russian strikes on Ukrainian data centers in September, which the Russian Defense Ministry described as a “digital detox.” Putin himself has acknowledged the impact of Ukrainian strikes, which Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky has called “long-range sanctions.”

The expanding Ukrainian strikes against Russian territory—including attacks on oil refineries and major online retailers Wildberries and Ozon—have led to fuel shortages and economic disruption. Meanwhile, Russia has continued its own devastating aerial campaign against Ukraine. On Friday, Russian attacks killed at least eight people and injured 60 across Ukraine, hit two shopping centers, and cut power to more than 1 million people after strikes on the energy grid, officials said. A drone strike on a food-processing facility in the northwestern Volyn region killed one and wounded eight, leaving 370,000 people temporarily without power. Separately, a Russian airstrike on Kherson killed an ambulance driver and injured 19, including a child. A new United Nations report said civilian casualties from Russia's invasion have climbed steeply: at least 328 civilians were killed and 2,105 wounded last month, an increase of 85% from the same month last year. The first two weeks of October were among the deadliest periods for civilians in years, said Danielle Bell, head of the UN human rights office in Kyiv.

Putin, speaking in military uniform during a visit to a command post, said the security zone inside Ukraine was in response to Ukraine's 2024-25 incursion into Russia's Kursk region, and that Russia does not plan to make this zone a subject for peace negotiations. The zone requires carving land in Sumy and Kharkiv regions, which Russia is not illegally claiming as its own. Concurrently, US President Donald Trump has put on hold a sweeping new sanctions package targeting Russia, waiving many sanctions and restrictions, citing national interest, while calling Ukraine's strikes on refineries “a disgrace” after a purported “energy ceasefire” failed to take hold. Ukrainian President Zelenskyy, meeting European leaders in Brussels, asked for another €70 billion ($79 billion) next year to fund defense, while saying Starlink satellite internet access for the military had not yet been full restored, with reports suggesting the order to throttle service came from within the White House.

The disruption hits at the heart of Russia's efforts to build a domestica controlled digital economy following Western sanctions. Across Russia, users have expressed war fatigue. A 31-year-old history teacher from St. Petersburg said after his smart bulbs failed because of the Yandex outage: “Every time you run into something like this, it's incredibly irritating. In my case, every time I just think about how sick and tired I am of (President Vladimir) Putn.” He added that people around him do not blame Ukraine, but instead think “when is all this finally going to end?” The Kremlin has placed the onus on private businesses to protect critical infrastructure from Ukrainian drones.
