Strait of Hormuz attacks hit record as US and Iran trade claims over control

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts slightly center-left

Dozens of ships were attacked in or near the Strait of Hormuz in October, marking the highest monthly tally since the start of the US-Israel war on Iran on February 28, according to the United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations (UKMTO). At least 12 vessels were confirmed attacked in the week ending October 16, with UKMTO warning it is struggling to accurately report the true number because most vessel owners refuse to report incidents. Of the 12 confirmed attacks, half continued to their destination or reached port, one sank, two were disabled, and three were damaged or had an unconfirmed outcome. Over the same week, UKMTO recorded 125 outbound transits and 129 inbound transits through the waterway, mostly dry bulk cargo.

The US and Iran each claimed control of the strategic waterway. US Central Command (CENTCOM) chief Brad Cooper said in a video message that despite Iranian attacks, 400 ships and 100 million barrels of oil moved through the Strait over the past week. "Let me be clear: The Strait of Hormuz is controlled by the United States and our regional partners, not Iran," he said, attributing the surge in attacks to "desperation" by Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC). Iran has not provided its own figures but says Washington is exaggerating numbers to project success. The IRGC increasingly reports that vessels are hitting naval mines set in the strait and says the waterway remains unsafe for vessels that do not coordinate with Iran's Persian Gulf Strait Authority. CENTCOM, however, says it has cleared the strait of mines and that vessels are being struck by projectiles. On Thursday, the US military said the container ship M/V GFS Pride was hit by an IRGC projectile, not a mine as Iran claimed; the ship sank while being towed to shore.

The conflict has severely disrupted global oil supplies. Before the war began, the Strait of Hormuz handled roughly one-fifth of global oil and natural gas flows. Oil flows through Hormuz were 11.6 million barrels per day (bpd) over the seven days to October 13, about 68 percent of the pre-war baseline of 17.1 million bpd, according to Kpler data. On Friday, Brent crude touched $110 a barrel for the first time since May amid ongoing attacks, exacerbated by Iran-aligned Houthi attacks on Saudi energy infrastructure and their capture of the Bab al-Mandeb strait. Goldman Sachs Research forecasts that benchmark Brent crude will trade at $90 per barrel in December 2026 and average $80 in 2027, but notes that spot prices include a nearly $25 risk premium due to potential escalation. The firm estimates Persian Gulf oil exports have rebounded to near their 2025 average since late September, including an estimated 4 million bpd of "dark" exports on tankers with transponders turned off.

The US military says its naval blockade of Iran has cost Tehran $25 billion in oil export income and that it recently seized $350 million in Iranian cryptocurrency financing. The US has refrained from responding militarily to the recent vessel attacks, but President Donald Trump has reportedly ordered the military to remain prepared for launching bigger attacks after the November 3 midterm elections, potentially including the use of the GBU-43/B Massive Ordnance Air Blast bomb, according to US media. Iran's acting Defence Minister Majid Ibn Reza said on Saturday that Iranian missiles will determine the war's future, vowing continued attacks. Diplomacy has not fully halted, with messages relayed by regional mediators including Pakistan and Qatar, but no breakthrough is expected soon.

Goldman Sachs maintains a base case that continued adaptation of Middle East supply will eventually reduce oil prices, but warns that renewed escalation poses significant upside risk. The oil market was nearly balanced in September, with demand exceeding supply by just 0.3 million bpd, a dramatic easing from a 7.2 million bpd deficit in March. Global oil demand in the third quarter of 2026 was down by 3.3 million bpd year-over-year, with weakness concentrated in China, the Middle East, and OECD Asia. The shock has accelerated behavioral changes such as increased use of electric vehicles in China, but analysts expect most of the demand gap to close by late 2027. The UKMTO also reported a new type of attack on Saturday: a tanker was hit by a rocket-propelled grenade from a small boat 78 nautical miles northeast of Saudi Arabia's Jubail, damaging the aft port quarter.
