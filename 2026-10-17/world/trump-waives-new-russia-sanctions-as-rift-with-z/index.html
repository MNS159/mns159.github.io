Trump waives new Russia sanctions as rift with Zelenskyy deepens

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts slightly center-left

US President Donald Trump on Friday issued an executive order waiving sweeping sanctions and tariffs targeting Russia’s energy revenues, two days before they were due to take effect under the Lindsey Graham Act. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy on Saturday called the move a boost for Russian President Vladimir Putin, saying, “A US sanctions blackout, easing pressure on the aggressor, financial rewards for Putin – all of this only encourages him to keep attacking.” The waiver came as Moscow intensified attacks on Ukrainian energy infrastructure, with Zelenskyy reporting that more than 15 critical power generation and transmission facilities were targeted overnight across multiple regions.

The sanctions law, passed by Congress and signed by Trump in September, aimed to impose tariffs of up to 100% on imports from the top five purchasers of Russian oil and gas – particularly China and India – and included sanctions against senior Russian figures including Putin, Russian financial institutions, and Moscow’s “shadow fleet” used to circumvent existing embargoes. Trump’s executive order suspended many of those provisions, citing the “national interest.” The law had given the president broad powers to levy such tariffs, which he now waived.

The deepening rift between Washington and Kyiv has been evident in recent days. Trump told Newsmax: “I think I find Zelenskyy to be more difficult to deal with than Putin.” On October 12, the US struck a deal to buy Russian diesel, a step back from previous sanctions. Zelenskyy also said Ukraine experienced “deep and unpleasant” disruptions to its Starlink satellite internet service, critical for battlefield communications, and complained that US-built missiles were being sent to the Middle East, leaving Ukraine “not a priority.” Speaking at an EU summit in Brussels on Friday, Zelenskyy urged European leaders to back Kyiv with nearly $80 billion in additional support for 2027. EU Council President Antonio Costa said Ukraine “remains a priority” and asked the European Commission to develop a funding proposal, but no concrete promises were made.

Russian envoy Kirill Dmitriev hailed the waiver as “an important and truly historic moment,” arguing the sanctions would have caused “serious damage first and foremost to the US economy and global markets.” US lawmakers, including Virginia Democrat Don Beyer, criticized Trump for “capitulating to Moscow” and defying congressional intent. Zelenskyy said the sanctions “could have helped enormously” and that the law was “supposed to impose truly effective pressure on Russia to bring this war to an end.” He added that Russia is “trying to cause a blackout in Ukraine – right before winter” and has expanded its attacks on the energy system to stretch Ukraine’s reserves and repair capacity. One person was killed and 19 injured, including a child, in a Russian airstrike on Kherson; Russian state media reported one woman killed by Ukrainian drone strikes in Russia’s Belgorod region.

Zelenskyy attended the EU summit amid what experts describe as the most difficult winter since the 2022 invasion. Olena Prokopenko, senior fellow at the German Marshall Fund, said Ukraine is “arguably entering the most difficult winter since the full-scale invasion.” Zelenskyy told reporters at the summit: “Putin wants to destroy us” and that Ukraine must prepare for a hard winter, but concluded: “We will keep living and fighting with dignity.” European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen promised the EU would “continue to lead” but urged international partners to step up. The Starlink slowdown, which Zelenskyy said had not been fully restored, remains a critical vulnerability, as the EU’s top diplomat Kaja Kallas noted that “in the short term Europe can’t replace this capability.”
