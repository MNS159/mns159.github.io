Google DeepMind nears launch of Gemini 4 AI model

Tech / AI desk

Google DeepMind is nearing the release of its next flagship artificial intelligence model, Gemini 4, according to the division's new chief, Koray Kavukcuoglu. In his first media appearance as head of DeepMind, at The Information's AI Agenda Live Summit on Wednesday, Kavukcuoglu said Gemini 4 has entered the early stages of post-training — the refinement phase where a base model is tuned for reliable performance — and that the company aims to launch the model "much earlier" than the end of 2026. "Our intention is to, like — as soon as possible — to release an early post-training output because we see the results and we are excited," he told The Information, adding that Google would "continue the fast-paced iterations" going forward.

Kavukcuoglu, who previously served as DeepMind's CTO, took over the top role in August after Demis Hassabis stepped down as DeepMind CEO. Hassabis, who now chairs the unit and serves as Alphabet's chief scientist, had said at the time that "great progress" was being made on Gemini 4. Kavukcuoglu noted that Google has already begun what he called its most ambitious pre-training run yet for Gemini 4, and that engineers are already using the model internally for the coding tool Antigravity. Safety testing and safeguards are still being worked on, he said.

Gemini 4 would be Google's first new flagship AI model since the Gemini 3 series launched in November 2025. Since then, OpenAI has released GPT-6 and Anthropic has released its Mythos series, both of which outperform Gemini 3, giving rivals a sizable lead. Google had planned to release a major Gemini 3.5 Pro update in June 2026, as announced by CEO Sundar Pichai at the I/O event in May, but the model never shipped. Kavukcuoglu explained that the company "took a little bit of a step back" to focus instead on introducing faster, less powerful Flash models. According to the Financial Times, the project missed three deadlines; Kavukcuoglu did not say whether Gemini 3.5 Pro has been officially canceled.

When asked whether he was concerned about Google falling behind rivals, Kavukcuoglu expressed confidence. "In my mind, it's a certainty that we are always gonna be at the frontier," he told The Information, citing his "insanely strong team." The pressure on Google has intensified with the launch of Meta's Muse AI agent earlier this month, which JPMorgan analysts said could become the most widely used consumer AI app since OpenAI's ChatGPT. Alphabet shares fell nearly 5% on Wednesday amid competitive concerns tied to Muse, closing at $334.98. Kavukcuoglu also noted that the model work is intended to help Google's chip business, as new models show hardware engineers how to design the "next two or three generations" of TPUs, putting Google in closer competition with Nvidia.

Separately, Google has rolled out two advanced text-to-speech models — Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS and Gemini 3.8 Flash-Lite TTS — described as the company's most expressive speech-generation tools to date. The releases are part of Google's broader Gemini Audio portfolio and are designed for developers, content creators, and businesses, with applications including large-scale dubbing, automated audio production, and conversational voice bots.
