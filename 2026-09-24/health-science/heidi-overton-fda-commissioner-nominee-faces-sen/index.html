Heidi Overton, FDA nominee, faces Senate confirmation hearing

Health & Science desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

Heidi Overton, President Trump's nominee to lead the U.S. Food and Drug Administration, appeared before the Senate Committee on Health, Education, Labor, and Pensions on Thursday morning at 10:30 a.m. in a confirmation hearing that is expected to examine her views on drug approvals, food safety, and the agency's public health direction. If confirmed, Overton, at 37, would become the youngest FDA commissioner in history.

In her opening statement, Overton plans to emphasize the need for speed and efficiency at the FDA to compete with China's accelerating biotechnology industry. "President Trump's direction for me in this role, should I be confirmed, is to 'go fast,'" Overton says in the statement, an advance copy of which was obtained by STAT. "Slow and prohibitive requirements for cutting edge therapies at the FDA means investment is increasingly flowing to China who has found ways to do things faster and cheaper." She also committed to continuing the administration's Operation Trialblazer, an initiative to speed up the clinical trial process, and said she would work with career professionals to optimize drug development.

Overton, a preventive medicine physician who worked in both Trump administrations, would take the helm of an agency that has weathered a turbulent 18 months of politicized decisions and staff departures, and is still trying to rebuild its workforce after the 2025 reductions-in-force cut its numbers by nearly 20%. She has drawn scrutiny from lawmakers and industry leaders for her relative lack of managerial experience and for her adherence to policies pushed by Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. Her role in shaping the administration's efforts to upend the childhood vaccine schedule has drawn criticism from Sen. Bill Cassidy (R-La.), the committee chairman.

The hearing, chaired by Cassidy with Sen. Bernie Sanders (I-Vt.) as ranking member, will test whether Overton's record and views align with the committee's priorities on drug pricing, food safety modernization, and the role of federal health regulators. Overton's background includes growing up in Gallup, New Mexico, where her father leads a charismatic church. She told a journalist in 2007, as a freshman at the University of New Mexico, that her dream was to become a doctor and return to her small hometown to practice medicine. Instead, she leveraged her medical degree, rural background, and "calm, reasoned" work style into becoming the youngest-ever nominee to lead the FDA.

The confirmation hearing occurs as the FDA faces questions about its direction under new leadership, with regional delegations and members already raising concerns about federal health programs and agency practices. Overton's confirmation will likely depend on whether committee members view her as capable of balancing competing demands from Congress, industry, and public health advocates, a test that will preview broader FDA priorities for the 119th Congress.
