Fury vs Joshua heavyweight fight set for December 11 in Cardiff

Sports desk

Anthony Joshua and Tyson Fury will finally meet in a long-awaited all-British heavyweight showdown on 11 December at Cardiff's Principality Stadium, with the bout to be broadcast globally on Netflix. The fight, more than a decade in the making, was officially announced after contracts were signed earlier this year, though a date and venue had remained unconfirmed until now. BBC Sport understands the main event could start in the early hours of Saturday morning UK time, with organisers considering a late ring walk to cater for American audiences; a start around 01:00 or 02:00 GMT would place the fight on the US east coast at 20:00 or 21:00. Joshua and Fury will come face-to-face at a news conference in London on Wednesday, 30 September.

The event is promoted as a collaboration between Saudi boxing powerbroker Turki Alalshikh's The Ring and UFC chief Dana White's Zuffa Boxing. Alalshikh said: "Two of the best heavyweights of this generation, finally in the ring together. This will be one of the biggest fights in boxing history. I am very happy to make this fight happen for the fans." White noted the fighters' combined record of 66 wins and 52 knockouts, adding: "On December 11, two of the biggest heavyweights of this generation finally collide - and the winner will take a significant place in heavyweight history." The 36-year-old Joshua posted "I am inspiring a generation" on Instagram; Fury, 38, had not commented. Joshua's promoter Eddie Hearn said, "Forever indebted to the UK public for their support of Anthony Joshua. We couldn't have got this over the line without you."

London's Wembley Stadium and New York's Madison Square Garden had been frontrunners to stage the fight, but both presented issues before Cardiff emerged as the alternative. The Principality Stadium has hosted late-night boxing before, including Joe Calzaghe's 2007 victory over Mikkel Kessler, which began at 01:00 GMT. Welsh Rugby Union and Cardiff Council officials welcomed the fight, saying it would reinforce the city's reputation for major events and bring a significant economic boost, though some operational details remain. There is precedent for UK fights scheduled around US television audiences: Ricky Hatton's 2005 win over Kostya Tszyu began at about 02:00 local time, and Calzaghe's 2006 bout against Jeff Lacy was staged at a similar hour. Mixed martial arts has gone later, with UFC events in Manchester starting main cards at 03:00 BST or later. Hearn has played down suggestions of a 03:00 GMT start.

The fight's announcement resolves years of failed negotiations and collapses. In 2020 the camps agreed the basis of a two-fight deal; in 2021 contracts were signed for an undisputed contest but Deontay Wilder's arbitration case forced Fury into a third fight with the American; and in 2022 Joshua accepted a 60-40 split to challenge Fury, but that attempt fell apart. Joshua had been pushing for the fight to take place in the UK, and his wish has been granted. The involvement of White, whom Hearn previously said he did not want in a promotional role, underlines the array of powerful figures who finally got the fight over the line.

The contest evokes the long-delayed heavyweight clashes of the past. As Yahoo Sports noted, Mike Tyson and Evander Holyfield signed for a 1990 fight only to finally meet in 1996, after prison, defeats and chaos; that wait proved worth it, with two memorable bouts. Joshua first called for a fight with Fury in April 2016, after winning a world title. The analysis suggests the fight may have arrived later than many wanted, but it can still leave a legacy: tens of thousands are expected to descend on the Principality Stadium for one of the biggest nights British boxing has staged in years, potentially inspiring a new generation of fighters. "Some fights are about more than purses, broadcast deals and site fees. This is one of them," BBC Sport analysis concluded.
