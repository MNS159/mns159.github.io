2026 Presidents Cup begins at Medinah; U.S. heavily favored

Sports desk

The 2026 Presidents Cup tees off Thursday at Medinah Country Club in Chicago, where the United States enters as a heavy favorite to extend its winning streak to 11 straight titles. Captain Brandt Snedeker’s American squad looks to defend home soil against Captain Geoff Ogilvy’s International team, which aims to pull off a major upset in the biennial match-play competition. The U.S. opens at -550 odds to win the cup, while the Internationals are long shots at +500, according to FanDuel.

Day 1 features five four-ball matches starting at 12:35 p.m. ET, with live coverage on Golf Channel. The pairings include world No. 1 Scottie Scheffler alongside Sam Burns vs. Min Woo Lee and Sungjae Im, and Cameron Young partnering Xander Schauffele vs. Hideki Matsuyama and Ryo Hisatsune. Other matches feature Justin Thomas with rookie Jackson Koivun against Corey Conners and Nico Echavarria, Wyndham Clark and Collin Morikawa vs. Ryan Fox and Adam Scott, and Chris Gotterup with Patrick Cantlay vs. Tom Kim and Si Woo Kim. Medinah’s No. 3 course, site of three U.S. Opens and two PGA Championships, underwent a full renovation in 2022 overseen by International captain Ogilvy.

The U.S. has historically dominated the Presidents Cup, amassing a 13-1-1 record since the event’s inception in 1994. The Americans won the 2024 edition at Royal Montreal 18½ to 11½, and their only loss came in 1998 in Australia; the 2003 competition ended in a tie. The U.S. roster features eight of the top 11 players in the Official World Golf Rankings, led by Scheffler (No. 1) and Young (No. 3). By contrast, the Internationals’ highest-ranked player is Si Woo Kim at No. 14, highlighting a significant talent gap.

CBS Sports experts overwhelmingly predict a U.S. victory, with projections of 17-13 or 17.5-12.5. Patrick McDonald and Robby Kalland both picked Sam Burns and Cameron Young as potential U.S. MVPs, noting Burns’ perfect 3-0-1 record in 2024 and Young’s match-play prowess from the Ryder Cup. For the Internationals, Min Woo Lee and Si Woo Kim are seen as key figures, with Lee’s aggressive style and Kim’s leadership essential if the visitors hope to keep the score close. Rookies Jacob Bridgeman and Chris Gotterup are highlighted as breakout candidates for the U.S., while Justin Thomas’s recent form raises questions despite his strong Presidents Cup record.

Among the surprise predictions, McDonald suggested Thomas might struggle given a subpar iron season, while Kalland forecast that 20-year-old debutant Jackson Koivun could clinch the winning point in Sunday singles, citing his extensive college match-play experience. The question hanging over the week is whether the Americans can maintain their Presidents Cup dominance — a level they have not replicated in the Ryder Cup — or if the Internationals can snap a losing streak that stretches back nearly three decades.
