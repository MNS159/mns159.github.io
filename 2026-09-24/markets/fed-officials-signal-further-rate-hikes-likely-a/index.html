Fed officials signal further rate hikes likely as inflation pressures persist

Financial & Markets desk · anchored on Reuters

Federal Reserve officials signaled Wednesday and Thursday that further interest-rate increases are likely as inflation pressures persist, with market pricing pointing to a possible hike at the central bank's October meeting. Fed Governor Michael Barr said Wednesday that "further policy adjustments are likely to be needed to ensure inflation comes down to target in a timely fashion," and New York Fed President John Williams said Thursday it would be "reasonable" to expect another rate hike by the end of the year.

The comments came as fresh data showed the U.S. economy expanding strongly but also generating new price pressures. S&P Global's flash purchasing managers' indexes for September put services activity at 58.7, the highest in 59 months; manufacturing at 56.7, a 53-month peak; and the composite at 58.4, a 62-month high. Any reading above 50 signals growth. The firm said its overall inflation measure hit its highest level since October 2022, driven by higher fuel and transportation costs and rising wages. "Firms' input costs have meanwhile jumped in September at the steepest rate for four years, with fuel and transport costs spiking higher thanks to the rise in oil prices seen during the month, which will add further to the upward pressure on selling prices and inflation in the coming months," said Chris Williamson, chief business economist at S&P Market Intelligence.

The PMI surveys also showed a strengthening labor market, with companies reporting they need more workers to handle order backlogs. Job growth rose at a rate not seen since June 2022, according to S&P, with service-sector employment expanding at the fastest pace since June 2002 and manufacturing employment at its highest since February 2021. The Fed last week raised its benchmark rate by a quarter percentage point to a target range of 3.75%-4%. Barr, who supported the move along with the other 11 FOMC voters, called it an "important action" and said the committee was "out of position" on rates "and we made an adjustment in the right direction."

Other officials have voiced similar views. St. Louis Fed President Alberto Musalem and Boston Fed President Susan Collins, neither of whom votes on the FOMC this year, both said earlier this week they see a need for further hikes. Collins warned there was "an increased likelihood" that inflation will remain "notably" above the Fed's 2% target. Recent data suggests the economy remains strong while inflation runs above 3%.

Williams, speaking at the London Macro Policy Forum, said investor sentiment suggests "it's likely that another rate hike may be appropriate by the end of the year." "That seems to me a reasonable way of thinking about it. But we have to see. We're going to collect the data and do what we did between July and September," he said. He also said the time for explicit forward guidance is "over," echoing Fed Chairman Kevin Warsh, who has said the central bank will refrain from directly signalling its intentions at coming meetings.

In response, traders aggressively boosted their bets on an October move. CME Group's FedWatch tool put the probability of a hike at the Oct. 27-28 FOMC meeting at 77.5% on Thursday, up from around 53% on Wednesday; it had already jumped to 73% after Barr's remarks and the data earlier Wednesday. Treasury yields soared, with the 2-year note, considered the most sensitive to Fed policy expectations, climbing more than 13 basis points to 4.9%. Of the 18 Fed participants who submitted projections last week, only two did not expect another increase this year, though there had been some speculation the committee might wait until after the November midterm elections and then hike in December.
