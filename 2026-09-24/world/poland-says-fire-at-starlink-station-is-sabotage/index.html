Poland says fire at Starlink station is sabotage as Denmark warns of rising Russian threat

International & World desk

A fire at a Starlink satellite station in central Poland was a deliberate 'act of sabotage,' Polish Deputy Prime Minister Krzysztof Gawkowski said, as Denmark's intelligence service issued a new threat assessment warning of escalating hybrid warfare from Russia. The fire broke out Wednesday night in Wola Krobowska, in the Masovian Voivodeship province, at a state-owned station that transmits internet traffic to Ukraine and uses SpaceX systems via Starlink terminals. Gawkowski told Polish public broadcaster TVP Info that the attack was 'deliberately designed to disable the station... disrupting internet access for various institutions, including the Ukrainian army,' and that 'many indications suggest' it fits into a 'new Russian doctrine of attack.' The Kremlin has not commented. Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk said he disliked 'labelling something as sabotage versus an accident without 100% certainty, but it looks bad.' The station remains operational.

Denmark's intelligence service said it expects Russia to escalate hybrid warfare in the coming months. While an attack remained 'highly unlikely,' it warned of 'a low but growing risk' that Russia could launch a limited military attack against NATO countries. Hybrid warfare refers to non-military methods such as cyberattacks, sabotage, or misinformation, often carried out by proxies, with Russia consistently denying involvement despite growing evidence. In recent weeks, several European NATO members have said Russia may be preparing attacks to test their commitment to supporting Ukraine, which Moscow denies. Earlier this month, Germany blamed Russia for an attempted drone attack on Leipzig/Halle Airport, and France ordered a plan to protect critical infrastructure and defence industry sites.

On Wednesday, Poland also reported that a Russian military helicopter briefly violated its airspace, forcing it to scramble fighter jets. Russian President Vladimir Putin has repeatedly said Moscow does not plan to attack NATO states, and Foreign Minister Sergei Lavrov told the UN Security Council on Wednesday there would be no pause in the war. Lavrov earlier said that if Europe attacks Russia, 'it will be a completely different war [from that in Ukraine], and it will be very short.' Putin launched a full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022, and Moscow currently controls about 20% of Ukrainian territory, including Crimea, annexed in 2014.

In Ukraine, Kharkiv and Kyiv experienced the worst of Russia's attacks overnight into Thursday. In Kharkiv, six people were killed and twelve injured in a Russian strike on a farm in the village of Stara Hnylytsia, with Ukraine's state emergency services saying the victims were agricultural workers sorting vegetables, including four women. In Kyiv, two people were killed and eight injured, with more than 50 civilian facilities damaged, including a maternity building. The hospital's director said a woman gave birth to a baby boy in a shelter during the attack, adding: 'Even on such a terrible night, life goes on.' Russia's defence ministry said it had hit Ukrainian defence facilities, logistics and telecom centres, as well as vessels used by the military.

Moldova's president said four Russian drones crossed into its airspace, with at least one exploding, while Romania's defence ministry said one drone crashed in its territory. Russian aerial attacks have become more devastating due to new jet-powered drones that are harder to shoot down. Speaking at the UN General Assembly in New York on Wednesday, Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky said, 'we need ballistic interceptors to protect lives' and called on the US to help. He said Putin needed to be stopped from 'spreading this evil further,' adding: 'Putin is always looking for someone who can help him kill more, kill more people for every kilometre of land.' Zelensky said Ukraine would seek to target the Russian energy sector over the winter.

Meanwhile, Russian presidential envoy Kirill Dmitriev headed to the US to meet Trump administration officials, including Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner, according to Reuters, citing two sources. Lavrov told the UN Security Council there would be no pause in the war.
