Teen rocker Nene Royal becomes first Thai winner of America's Got Talent

International & World desk

Nene Royal, a 16-year-old rocker from Thailand, has won the 21st season of America's Got Talent, becoming the first Thai contestant to take the title. The finale aired Tuesday night in California, where Nene performed a rendition of Seven Nation Army by the American rock duo The White Stripes. The win comes with a $1 million prize (£755,000).

Nene, whose real name is Rattikarn Amloy, beat nine other finalists including magician acts, canine performers, and a robot dance crew. Her victory was secured after a viral audition clip — in which she belted out Zombie by The Cranberries — racked up 20 million views, making her an early fan favorite. On Wednesday night, before the results were announced, she also shared the stage with Linkin Park to perform the band's 2024 rock song The Emptiness Machine.

After being crowned winner, an emotional Nene broke down in tears, kneeling on the ground. 'I'm very happy and emotional right now,' she said, thanking everyone for her support. In Thailand, fans who were unable to watch the live broadcast on NBC followed via YouTube livestream; distress turned to celebration when word of her historic win spread despite the livestream being cut during the results announcement.

The Thai government joined in celebrating the win. Prime Minister Anutin Charnvirakul said in a statement that Nene had 'brought honour to Thailand' and 'instilled pride in the Thai people.' The government's public relations department added that 'what makes this win special is that Nene carried her Thai identity with confidence onto one of the world's biggest stages,' noting that she is 'a young girl from Phuket with a guitar and a big dream.'

Nene began playing guitar at age seven and once dreamed of becoming a touring musician. In her hometown of Phuket, a tourist hub known for its beaches, she used to busk at Naka Weekend Market. Piyarat Na Songkhla, a 43-year-old tour guide in Phuket who has seen Nene perform since childhood, told the BBC: 'She's so adorable. I feel so elated and happy for her. It's unbelievable that she has gone this far. So proud of her.' The market's Facebook page posted a tribute, saying 'everyone at Naka Market is so proud to see you shine on one of the biggest stages in the world.' Kajonkiet International School in Phuket, where Nene studies, also congratulated her and posted a video of students and staff cheering her final performance.
