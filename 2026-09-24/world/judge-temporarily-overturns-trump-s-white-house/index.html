Judge temporarily overturns Trump's White House media ban

International & World desk

A federal judge has ordered the Trump administration to immediately reinstate access to the White House for three news organisations—CNN, MS NOW and Politico—that were barred last week by President Donald Trump. The ruling, issued by Judge Timothy Kelly, requires their access to be restored for 14 days, stating the ban 'likely violated their constitutional due process rights.' Some reporters from the outlets said they had been turned away on Thursday morning, though it was unclear whether all reporters would be readmitted.

Judge Kelly ruled that CNN, Politico and MS NOW were 'likely to succeed' in showing the White House had not exercised 'adequate due process' in implementing the ban. He cast doubt on the Trump administration's argument that the bans were motivated by national security concerns, noting the president made no mention of that in his initial announcement. The ruling added: 'The record lacks factual support for Defendants' contention that the revocation of Plaintiffs' hard passes will in fact protect national security or that national security will be endangered if the Court orders their passes reinstated while this litigation proceeds.'

In its defence of the ban, the Trump administration cited a number of articles published by the outlets, including some about construction at the White House. The judge said that 'in most cases' the stories the White House took issue with were written by journalists who 'did not even hold card passes'—and in the remaining cases, the administration did not demonstrate 'reason to believe that the reporter's possession of the hard pass was integral to the stories identified.' The administration also cited declarations from defence department officials, but the judge said they had 'no direct knowledge of how reporters operate in the White House, or about any of the facts at issue here.'

When President Donald Trump announced the ban on Friday, he accused the three news outlets of writing 'fake news' and 'fictions and lies' in their coverage. The three outlets launched a legal challenge after their reporters were denied access last weekend. On Monday, major US TV networks pulled out of White House pool duties in protest. Forty-nine media organisations—including CBS News, The New York Times and Fox News—filed a legal brief in support of the lawsuit.

Ted Boutrous, the lawyer representing the news outlets, told CNN: 'This is a strong ruling vindicating freedom of the press, due process and the rule of law.' The White House has yet to comment on the ruling, and lawyers acting for the president may take further legal action. Trump frequently criticises news organisations, and his administration has launched legal action against a series of outlets, including the New York Times, the Wall Street Journal and the BBC.
