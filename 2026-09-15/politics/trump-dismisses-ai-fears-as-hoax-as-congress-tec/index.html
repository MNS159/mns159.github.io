Trump dismisses AI fears as 'hoax' as Congress, tech leaders push for regulation

Politics desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum
✓ Facts
  • President Trump called AI safety fears a "hoax" and rejected calls for stronger safeguards, per BBC.
  • Trump said Sunday, "We're leading China in AI... whoever wins AI, wins," adding the U.S. can add "guardrails" but dismissing warnings as coming from "negative forces," per NPR.
  • Anthropic co-founder Jack Clark told the BBC a third-party-verifiable "kill switch" may need to be mandatory for the AI industry.
  • Leaders at Anthropic, OpenAI and Google called over the weekend for a coordinated industry slowdown in AI development, according to NPR.
  • Sen. Bernie Sanders (I-VT) and Rep. Greg Casar (D-TX) are introducing legislation to ban development of "superintelligent AI" and create a federal agency for oversight; Casar cited a Data for Progress poll finding roughly 70% of Americans support an immediate ban on superintelligent AI, with about 25% opposed.
  • Sanders and former Trump adviser Steve Bannon are scheduled to appear together at the Future of Life Institute's "Pro-Human Assembly" in Washington, D.C., on Tuesday to press Congress on AI safeguards.
  • House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) said AI companies, not Congress, should be responsible for policing safety and warned that swift regulation risks losing the AI race to China; the House is slated to vote on the Ratepayer Protection Act addressing data-center-driven energy cost spikes.
  • Sen. Jon Ossoff (D-GA) said Trump is unlikely to regulate AI because of his family's investments in the industry, pointing to 1789 Capital, where Donald Trump Jr. is a partner.
  • New York state lawmaker Alex Bores launched a $30 million effort called "Who Decides" aimed at unifying Democrats on AI regulation, per Politico.
○ Analysis

The story centers on a widening gap between the Trump administration's embrace of rapid AI development—framed as a national-security race against China—and a growing chorus, spanning tech insiders, lawmakers and advocacy groups, warning that unchecked AI poses existential or safety risks. That warning gained unusual weight when executives at Anthropic, OpenAI and Google themselves called for a coordinated slowdown, prompting Trump to dismiss such concerns as exaggerated or a "hoax." Meanwhile, congressional action remains stalled: House GOP leadership favors industry self-policing over legislation, even as bipartisan and cross-ideological figures—including Sanders, Casar, and even Bannon—organize public pressure campaigns and draft bills targeting the most extreme AI risks (so-called "superintelligence"). The debate is complicated by the fact that AI's economic and national-security stakes (the China race, energy costs from data centers) intersect with unresolved safety questions, leaving Congress divided over whether to prioritize speed or caution.

◐ Debate

Democratic

  • Rep. Greg Casar (D-TX) says superintelligent AI could become uncontrollable and weapons-capable, and argues Congress must create a federal safety agency and pursue international agreements, including with China, rather than rely on voluntary industry pledges.
  • Sen. Jon Ossoff (D-GA) says Trump is unlikely to regulate AI because of his family's financial ties to the industry through 1789 Capital.

Republican

  • House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) says AI companies, not Congress, should be responsible for policing safety, and warns that rushing regulation risks the U.S. losing the AI race to China.
  • President Trump says AI safety fears are a "hoax," that the U.S. is leading China in AI and must keep winning, and that concerns raised by critics are driven by "negative forces."
Steelman

The clearest cross-cutting signal in this story is that AI regulation is scrambling normal partisan alliances—illustrated by Sanders and Bannon sharing a stage—suggesting the fight is less Democrat-vs-Republican than insiders/accelerationists vs. a bipartisan coalition of safety-focused lawmakers, advocates and even some industry leaders.

Implications

Expect near-term flashpoints including the House's data-center energy bill vote, the release of the Sanders-Casar superintelligence ban text, the Future of Life Institute's Pro-Human Assembly, and AI diplomacy discussions at the upcoming UN meeting—each testing whether growing public and cross-partisan pressure can overcome Trump's and Speaker Johnson's preference for industry self-regulation and a competitive posture toward China.
