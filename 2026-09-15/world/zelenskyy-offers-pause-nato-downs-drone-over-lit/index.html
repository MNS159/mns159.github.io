Ukraine War

Zelenskyy offers pause in attacks if Russia spares infrastructure; NATO downs drone over Lithuania

International & World desk · anchored on Reuters, AP

President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Ukraine is ready to halt attacks on Russian territory if the United States can secure a commitment from Russia to stop hitting Ukrainian infrastructure. His comments came after US President Donald Trump claimed, without providing details, that Ukraine and Russia had agreed to stop targeting each other's energy sites. Zelenskyy said Ukraine's halting of attacks would depend on the US ensuring that Russia is "genuinely ready to stop this war aimed at destroying life, and to do so for real and for the long term," and only then would Ukraine take "de-escalatory steps." He proposed that partners secure an agreement with Russia to halt attacks on critical infrastructure, accusing Moscow of targeting food, energy, and other basic facilities "that sustain life."

The offer came as both sides continued air attacks. Russian strikes on Kyiv killed one person and wounded seven since the start of Tuesday, Ukraine's State Emergency Service said, with two more people killed in separate attacks in Sloviansk and Kramatorsk. Russia's Ministry of Defence said its forces shot down 222 Ukrainian drones overnight. Ukrainian drones attacked an industrial facility in Russia's southeastern Samara region, damaging the site and several residential buildings, according to Russian state news agency TASS, which cited Regional Governor Vyacheslav Fedorishchev as saying about 40 drones were shot down. Ukrainian media said the targeted facility was an oil refinery. A "massive" Ukrainian drone attack sparked fires in the Russian city of Taganrog, damaging two apartment buildings, three private homes, an educational institution, agricultural warehouses, and a site belonging to Russian e-retailer Ozon, according to Regional Governor Yury Slyusar. Zelenskyy said Kyiv's forces had targeted a drone production facility in the city.

Separately, NATO fighter pilots shot down a drone that entered Lithuania's airspace overnight, Lithuanian President Gitanas Nausėda said. Authorities said the drone likely entered southern Lithuania, near the second-largest city of Kaunas, from neighboring Belarus shortly after midnight on Tuesday. The origins of the drone are yet to be determined, a spokesperson for the Lithuanian national crisis management center said. It is thought to be the first time a NATO jet has shot down a drone in Lithuanian airspace. Drone alerts sounded across the capital Vilnius and surrounding region, with phones warning residents to "find a safe place" and "wait for the all clear." Lithuania's Ministry of Defence said its armed forces and Italian pilots serving in the NATO Air Defence Mission "showed a swift and responsible response." The drone debris, scattered widely, will be collected and examined to determine who it belonged to, whether it was armed, and whether it was launched deliberately or strayed off course.

Nausėda wrote on X: "With Russia intensifying its aggression against Ukraine, such readiness is vital for our region. Together with our NATO Allies, Lithuania will defend its airspace." UK Defence Secretary Wes Streeting said: "Thanks to the swift and professional actions of Lithuanian and Italian forces, NATO once again demonstrated our readiness to protect our airspace. We stand united, around the clock, in defence of every inch of NATO territory." Moscow has not commented on the incident. Lithuania shares a large part of its southern and eastern border with Belarus, a key Russian ally. Drone sightings were reported in Lithuania on Sunday, with the crisis management center later saying an object had "likely" entered from Belarus and traveled across the country before entering Russian airspace; that incident did not trigger air threat alerts.

Poland also announced "aviation operations" in its airspace on Monday night "due to the activity of Russian Federation's jet-powered unmanned aerial vehicles conducting strikes on Ukrainian territory," describing the actions as "preventive" and aimed at "securing airspace and its protection, especially in areas adjacent to threatened regions." Military drone incursions have become increasingly common across Europe since Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine. NATO jets have scrambled multiple times to shoot down stray Ukrainian drones that crossed into Estonia and Latvia this year; Ukraine has blamed the stray drones on Russian electronic warfare designed to change Ukrainian drone flight paths. Lithuania, Estonia, and Latvia all joined NATO in 2004, becoming signatories to Article 5, which states that "an armed attack against one NATO member shall be considered an attack against all members."

In a related incident, Denmark accused a Russian warship of firing two emergency flares at a Danish military helicopter during a "routine" photographic operation in the Baltic Sea on Monday. The Danish armed forces said one flare passed close to the Fennec helicopter, which was tracking a Russian frigate in international waters off Gedser. Denmark summoned the Russian ambassador, with Prime Minister Mette Frederiksen branding the action "reckless" and designed to "intimidate and divide." Foreign Minister Lars Lokke Rasmussen called the incident "completely unacceptable" and accused Russia of "gradually shifting the boundary for what they consider acceptable behaviour." Russian Ambassador Vladimir Barbin said Moscow would "thoroughly investigate" but accused the Danish helicopter of "provocative actions," saying it was "not the first time" Danish helicopters had carried out dangerous maneuvers near Russian warships. Danish Defence Minister Jeppe Bruus told Reuters the frigate's crew made no attempt to contact the helicopter.

Meanwhile, Ukraine and Russia signaled readiness to resume trilateral talks with the US, with Ukrainian presidential office head Kyrylo Budanov saying "We're preparing. For October," and Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov saying "We do not rule out such a possibility. We are talking about the foreseeable future." US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner visited Moscow and Kyiv in early September to revive trilateral talks. The last round took place in January and February 2026 in Abu Dhabi and Geneva without producing a breakthrough. Few expect a breakthrough, but Ukraine is seeking partial de-escalation to help it get through the winter, as Russia's repeated attacks on energy infrastructure have strained heating and electricity systems, leaving thousands of households without power for the past three winters.
