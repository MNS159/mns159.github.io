Politics Foreign Policy

US-Iran tensions persist as administration signals possible Strait of Hormuz deal

Politics desk

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said Tuesday that a deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz to normal shipping could be reached "today or tomorrow," citing ongoing talks with Iranian officials.

The dispute centers on whether U.S.-Iran diplomacy over the Strait of Hormuz — a critical global oil-shipping chokepoint — is producing a real deal or merely another temporary pause in a conflict that has cycled through broken ceasefires since June. The Trump administration, through Bessent and the president himself, describes talks as imminent and productive, envisioning a two-phase process that first restores shipping and later addresses Iran's nuclear program. Iran's government publicly denies negotiating directly with Washington, framing its only confirmed engagement as a narrower shipping arrangement brokered by Oman, while regional mediators like Qatar describe broader diplomatic efforts underway. Compounding the uncertainty, unexplained attacks on shipping continue even as talks are reported, and separate allegations that Russia and China are aiding Iran — through satellite intelligence or weapons transfers, both denied by Moscow and Beijing — raise questions about outside actors shaping the conflict's trajectory. Analysts cited in the reporting, including Ali Vaez and Jonathan Schanzer, describe a stalemate in which neither Washington nor Tehran has strong incentive to make the concessions needed to end hostilities, since each can tolerate the current standoff longer than a decisive settlement.

Oil Prices Fall as US Hints Hormuz Deal Near; Iran Focuses on Oman Talks

International & World desk · anchored on Reuters

Oil prices fell to a three-week low on Tuesday after senior U.S. officials expressed optimism that a deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz could be reached within days. Brent crude, the global benchmark, dropped nearly 5% to under $80 a barrel, while U.S. West Texas Intermediate fell more than 5% to $76 a barrel, according to the BBC. The declines come as the market weighs the possibility of supply disruptions easing through the strategic waterway, which handles roughly one-fifth of the world's daily oil and liquefied natural gas supplies.

Financial & Markets desk · anchored on AP, Reuters

US company's AI lets Ukraine's cheap kamikaze drones track targets on their own

Tech / AI desk

Ukrainian drone operators have destroyed many Russian armored vehicles on the ground and even military helicopters in midair using $400 Shrike drones with explosive payloads. Now thousands of those drones are getting upgraded with an AI system capable of autonomously tracking and homing in on moving targets. In mid-July, the Ukrainian military began receiving Shrike drones made by the Ukrainian company SkyFall, equipped with AI-powered autonomy hardware and software developed by the US company Auterion. The companies plan to deliver 50,000 drones equipped with Auterion's Skynode S strike kits in the coming months.
