2026 MLB playoffs set: Wild Card Series previews and predictions

Sports desk

The 2026 Major League Baseball postseason field is set after the regular season concluded Sunday, with the Wild Card Round beginning Tuesday. Eight teams will compete in best-of-three series, all hosted by the higher seed, while four clubs—the Tampa Bay Rays, Cleveland Guardians, Milwaukee Brewers and Los Angeles Dodgers—earned first-round byes and will await the Division Series. The Dodgers enter as the betting favorites at +200 on DraftKings Sportsbook, seeking a third straight World Series title behind a pitching staff that led MLB in fWAR and a deep rotation of Yoshinobu Yamamoto, Tarik Skubal, Tyler Glasnow and Blake Snell.

In the National League, the Philadelphia Phillies face the Atlanta Braves in a series defined by the Phillies' rocky path to the postseason. Philadelphia clinched the third wild-card spot only on the final day, forcing them to use co-ace Zack Wheeler in Game 162 and leaving their rotation alignment uncertain. Jesús Luzardo, recently activated from shoulder inflammation, is expected to start Game 1 opposite Braves ace Chris Sale, while Game 3—if necessary—could see Wheeler on short rest or Aaron Nola. The Chicago Cubs visit the San Diego Padres in a matchup pitting the Cubs' league-leading offense—built around likely unanimous NL MVP Pete Crow-Armstrong—against a deep Padres bullpen. The Padres' rotation depth is a question, with ace Michael King set for Game 1 and Nick Pivetta a less certain option for Game 2.

In the American League, the Boston Red Sox travel to the Bronx to face the New York Yankees, with the Yankees' ability to score without Aaron Judge the central question. Judge has been sidelined since Sept. 16 with a calf injury, and even if activated, his effectiveness is uncertain. The Yankees' offense without Judge has a .719 OPS, and while the rotation—led by Cam Schlittler—is excellent, the lineup may struggle against a Red Sox staff that includes Sonny Gray, Payton Tolle and Ranger Suárez. The other AL series pits the Chicago White Sox against the Houston Astros, who at 81-81 have the worst record of any playoff team in MLB history and were outscored by 32 runs. The Astros ranked 27th in rotation ERA and 17th in bullpen ERA, while the White Sox offense ranked fifth in runs scored, making Houston a vulnerable favorite despite being the higher seed and division champion.

Among the teams receiving byes, the Brewers (+600) posted the best record in baseball but have a history of postseason struggles, leading some analysts to question whether their offense can handle elite October pitching. The Rays (+800) rely on star Junior Caminero and a recent resurgence from Freddy Peralta, who has a 3.25 ERA since being traded from Milwaukee. The Guardians (+1200) and Dodgers (+200) round out the bye teams. Notable longshots include the Red Sox (+1200), who benefit from a favorable bracket and a strong bullpen, and the Cubs (+1700), whose pitching depth is a concern after losing Cade Horton to Tommy John surgery. The Yankees (+850) face uncertainty around Judge, while the Brewers (+600) are listed as a team to fade despite their regular-season success.

The Wild Card Series begin Tuesday, with each series carrying high stakes: the Phillies must overcome a disjointed rotation, the Astros need to outperform their regular-season metrics, the Yankees require offensive production without their MVP, and the Padres must navigate rotation questions against a potent Cubs lineup. The winners advance to face the bye teams in the Division Series, with the World Series scheduled to conclude in late October. Betting markets reflect the Dodgers as clear favorites, but the compressed format of the wild-card round leaves ample room for upsets.
