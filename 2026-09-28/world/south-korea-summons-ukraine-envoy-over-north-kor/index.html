South Korea summons Ukraine envoy over North Korean POW transfer

International & World desk · anchored on Reuters
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

South Korea has summoned Ukraine's acting ambassador, Andrii Vieshkin, after President Volodymyr Zelensky publicly announced the transfer of two captured North Korean soldiers to Seoul, a move Seoul says was supposed to remain secret. Vice Foreign Minister Park Yoon-joo lodged a formal complaint and expressed "strong regret" over the disclosure, which President Lee Jae Myung called "greatly disappointing" and a "highly sensitive issue" impacting national security.

Zelensky announced the transfer at the UN General Assembly last Wednesday, stating the soldiers had been captured in Russia's Kursk Oblast and were "not easy to capture alive." The two North Koreans, among thousands deployed to support Russia, had earlier told local media they wanted to be sent to South Korea. Seoul confirmed the transfer but sharply criticized Zelensky for allegedly breaking an agreement to keep it secret, partly to protect the soldiers' families and because Kyiv had also wanted secrecy to avoid criticism that it had given up a chance to exchange them for Ukrainian prisoners of war.

The dispute escalated when Zelensky's office claimed no non-disclosure agreement existed. Seoul demanded an apology for what it called "announcing a falsehood" and said Ukraine's actions had seriously damaged trust between the two nations. Lee's office explained that Ukraine had indicated the soldiers would be mentioned in Zelensky's address, but not the fact of their transfer. Ukraine's foreign ministry declined to comment when approached by the BBC.

Lee faced heavy domestic criticism after the disclosure, with the South Korean opposition accusing him of covering up the transfer to appease Pyongyang. Lee, whose party takes a conciliatory approach to North Korea, has been making efforts to reopen peace talks with little success. South Korea is technically still at war with North Korea, as the 1950-1953 Korean War ended with an armistice, not a peace treaty.

Seoul has been sympathetic to Ukraine since Russia's full-scale invasion but has refused to break its longstanding policy of not providing lethal weapons to any country engaged in active war. After North Korean troops were deployed to support Russia in 2024, Kyiv stepped up pressure on South Korea to supply anti-air missiles. In July, Seoul announced a $100 million assistance package to Ukraine, expanding earlier non-lethal aid including helmets, personal armour, and medical equipment. Russia's foreign ministry has warned it would have "no choice but to retaliate" if South Korea provides arms to Ukraine, without specifying the action it might take.
