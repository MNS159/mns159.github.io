SpaceX's Starship reaches orbit for first time

International & World desk

SpaceX's Starship, the largest rocket ever built, reached orbit for the first time on Monday, with mission control announcing 'Starship is orbital' to cheers during a livestream. The uncrewed spacecraft launched from the company's base in southern Texas, carrying 26 of the latest Starlink satellites to join approximately 11,000 older models already in orbit. It was the 14th full-scale launch of the 124-meter (407-foot) rocket in three years.

The flight nearly failed after one engine shut down too soon, but other systems performed well and flight controllers decided after several tense minutes to proceed. The spacecraft was intended to circle the globe at an altitude of 275 kilometers (170 miles) six times over nearly 10 hours before splashing down in the Pacific Ocean near Chile. The first-stage booster was not recovered, instead dropping into the Gulf of Mexico within minutes of liftoff.

Designed from the start to be fully reusable, Starship's earlier test flights ventured no farther than the Indian Ocean, often crashing in flames or briefly skimming space. SpaceX salvaged the last flight's spacecraft from the Indian Ocean in July and modified the new Starship's heat shield based on hands-on inspection. Founder Elon Musk has stressed caution, saying at a business summit earlier this month that if the spacecraft breaks apart over land and rains debris onto people, 'our popularity would diminish very rapidly.'

Starship's orbital debut is a critical step toward its role in NASA's Artemis program. The Artemis III mission, as soon as next summer, will feature a triple-launch docking exercise involving an Orion capsule, Jeff Bezos's Blue Moon lander, and Starship. Subsequent moonshots, beginning with Artemis IV no sooner than 2028, will alternate between Blue Moon and Starship. Musk originally developed Starship for Mars but now plans to focus on the moon and use it to haul satellites by the truckload, phasing out the Falcon 9 rocket within several years. A second Starship launch site is nearing completion at Florida's Kennedy Space Center, and a third is planned for Louisiana.
