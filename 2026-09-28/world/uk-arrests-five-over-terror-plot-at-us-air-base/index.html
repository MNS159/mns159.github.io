UK arrests five over terror plot at US air base; Iran denies role

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts slightly center-left

British counterterrorism police arrested five British nationals on Sunday morning near RAF Fairford, a UK air base used by the United States to launch strikes on Iran, on suspicion of preparing a terrorist act and explosives offences. The men, aged 23 to 25 and from London, were detained after three white vans were spotted heading toward the base in Gloucestershire. Police evacuated 85 homes in the nearby village of Whelford, and army bomb-disposal experts used a robot to examine the vehicles, which remained at the scene as of Monday.

Assistant Commissioner Laurence Taylor of UK Counter Terrorism Policing said the five were released on bail Monday afternoon “following extensive interviews and inquiries,” adding that “this does not mean that the investigation is over” and that they are subject to stringent conditions on movement and contact. They had initially been held on suspicion of explosives offences and later rearrested under the Terrorism Act. Residents remained displaced Monday morning, housed in a local leisure center, and the vans had not yet been moved.

Iran’s embassy in London denied involvement, calling the accusations “unfounded and malicious speculations” that fuel “Iranophobia propaganda” and reserving the right to take legal measures. A source familiar with the investigation told Al Jazeera an Iranian-linked motive is considered the most likely, but British police have not officially linked Iran to the arrests. Authorities are also exploring possible connections to Russian sabotage or Islamist plots. The Islamic Revolutionary Guards Corps had stated in July that Fairford was a “legitimate target” after the UK authorized the US to use the base for what then-Prime Minister Keir Starmer called “defensive” operations against Iranian missile sites.

US President Donald Trump said the suspects were “under view for a long time” and “looking to do big damage to our fort,” praising cooperation with British authorities. British Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband said the incident is “a reminder of the threats our country faces” and that the UK will “act against the proxies of Iran.” He had met Iran’s foreign minister at the UN the previous week and warned that Britain would not tolerate hostile activity on its soil. Prime Minister Andy Burnham was due to chair a COBRA emergency meeting Monday as the investigation continues, with UK intelligence services reporting a 50% increase in national security cases involving hostile states in recent months.
