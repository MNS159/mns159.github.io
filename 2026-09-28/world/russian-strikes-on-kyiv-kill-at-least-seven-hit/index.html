Russian strikes across Ukraine kill at least seven, hit National Academy of Sciences in Kyiv

International & World desk

Russian forces launched a wave of drone and missile strikes across Ukraine on Monday, killing at least seven people and wounding more than 50 others, officials said. In the capital, Kyiv, a direct hit by a jet-powered drone struck the National Academy of Sciences of Ukraine in the Shevchenkivskyi district around 1:30 p.m. local time, killing a woman and injuring six people, according to the State Emergency Service. The building was engulfed in flames and surrounded by rubble as firefighters worked at the scene.

Elsewhere, a drone struck an office center in the central city of Dnipro during working hours, killing three people and wounding at least 12, local authorities said. In the northeastern city of Kharkiv, a Russian strike on a nine-story apartment building wounded 43 people, including 11 children, Mayor Ihor Terekhov reported. Four people, including two teenage girls, were hurt when a drone hit a residential area in the Chernihiv region on Sunday evening, said regional military administration head Viacheslav Chaus. Overnight drone attacks on the wider Kyiv region killed four people and wounded eight, according to officials.

Russia's Defense Ministry said its forces struck two data centers in Kyiv, gas stations in the capital, a distribution hub of the Nova Poshta postal service in Odesa, and two cargo ships in the Black Sea and the port of Chornomorsk. Moscow claims it targets only military-related sites, but the attacks regularly hit civilian areas. Russian aerial bombardments have continued around the clock, aiming to wear down Ukrainian resistance and cripple the economy.

Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov indicated Monday that the chances of a swift peace settlement were bleak after recent Ukrainian attacks. 'Ukraine will have to pay a price for the actions that have taken place in recent months,' Peskov told reporters. 'That is happening right now.' On Saturday, Russian Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov told the U.N. General Assembly that 'Europe is doing everything it can to thwart peace talks.'

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy announced that Ukrainian forces had struck two defense plants in Russia's Tula and Voronezh regions, as well as oil facilities in the Krasnodar region. Ukraine's long-range drones have repeatedly targeted Russian fuel depots and industrial plants. On the front line, Ukraine's Third Army Corps said it had recaptured another 51 square kilometers (20 square miles) from Russian forces in the Donetsk region as part of Operation Vivaldi, bringing the total to 176 square kilometers (68 square miles) since the offensive began. The corps said it used converted Soviet-era BTR-60 armored personnel carriers as kamikaze vehicles and ground robots to break through Russian defenses. Russia's Defense Ministry said its troops captured the villages of Novovodian in Donetsk and Ulanove in Sumy. The claims could not be independently verified.

Ukraine's Minister of Education and Science, Andrii Butenko, condemned the strike on the academy, saying, 'By destroying educational and scientific infrastructure, Russia is attempting to intimidate children, parents, educators, and scientists, and to disrupt the educational process.' Kyiv Mayor Vitali Klitschko reported that Russian attacks on the capital on Monday had left 19 people, including three children, injured. The strikes come as Ukrainians prepare for another expected winter onslaught targeting the country's energy network.
