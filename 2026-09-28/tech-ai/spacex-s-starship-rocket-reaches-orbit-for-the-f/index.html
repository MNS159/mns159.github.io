SpaceX's Starship reaches orbit for first time, deploys Starlink satellites

Tech / AI desk

SpaceX's Starship rocket reached Earth orbit for the first time early Monday morning, launching from South Texas in a milestone flight that included an engine failure, a brief call-off, and an early end to the mission. The upper stage of the mega-rocket lost one of its six Raptor engines shortly after separating from the Super Heavy booster, and the company initially called off the attempt before deciding minutes later to keep pushing. Starship ultimately reached orbit and successfully deployed all 26 new Starlink satellites it was carrying.

The flight did not go exactly as planned. SpaceX had intended for the upper stage to orbit Earth six times over about nine hours, but roughly 1.5 hours into the flight the company decided to bring the vehicle back early. "Flight teams will deorbit Starship safely to our pre-cleared area of the Pacific Ocean after 3 hours in orbit," SpaceX wrote on X. The Ship stage dispensed all 26 of its V3 Starlink satellites during the flight and was able to connect to all of them; they are expected to begin servicing Starlink customers in the next few weeks.

SpaceX also made progress recovering the Super Heavy booster. After separating from the upper stage a few minutes into the flight, the booster returned to the Gulf coast and performed a simulated landing over the water. The company said it was the cleanest simulated landing it has performed with the V3 version of Starship, which began testing in May. This was the third test of Starship V3, with flights coming about two months apart.

Reaching orbit is a critical milestone that could allow SpaceX to use Starship for regular commercial missions and move closer to making the mega-rocket fully reusable. CEO Elon Musk is orienting the entire company around Starship, which is central to SpaceX's plans to operate moon missions for NASA and to far-out ideas such as putting data center satellites in orbit. The V3 Starlink satellites deployed on this flight are more powerful than earlier versions used to build SpaceX's constellation of more than 10,000 satellites; the company has said one full Starship launch of V3 satellites can add as much capacity to the Starlink network as 20 flights of the V2 mini versions flown on the Falcon 9 rocket.

This was the second Starship launch since SpaceX went public in the largest IPO ever. The company's stock jumped more than 1% in early trading Monday but fell into the red after SpaceX decided to end the first orbital mission early. Musk has said he wants Starship, the most powerful rocket ever built, to eventually fly thousands of times per year. To support that goal, he recently signed a deal with Louisiana to build a new Starbase launch complex that dwarfs the existing one in Texas; the company has said it plans to invest $100 billion in that facility, with construction starting next year.
