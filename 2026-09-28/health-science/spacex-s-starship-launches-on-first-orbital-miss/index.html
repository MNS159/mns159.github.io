SpaceX's Starship Reaches Orbit for First Time on Texas Test Flight

Health & Science desk

SpaceX launched its Starship spacecraft from its Starbase facility at Boca Chica Beach in Texas at 7:48 a.m. CT, sending the 171-foot-tall vehicle into orbit for the first time on its 14th flight. The Super Heavy booster ignited all 33 of its rocket engines to lift the spacecraft. Starship separated from the booster two-and-a-half minutes after launch and continued into space. One of Starship's six engines failed during ascent, but about 22 minutes into the flight, the team determined it was safe to continue to orbit.

SpaceX had planned a 10-hour flight with six orbits around Earth at an altitude of approximately 170 miles, but mission managers reduced it to three hours after launch. Previous Starship flights were suborbital; this mission was the first to reach orbital speeds of about 17,500 mph. The launch profile included provisions for Starship to abort its orbital trajectory and return safely to Earth if needed. During the flight, SpaceX deployed 26 Starlink V3 next-generation satellites into its constellation of thousands of internet satellites that provide broadband access worldwide.

The Super Heavy booster is designed to be reusable by returning to its launch site and being caught by the launch tower. For this flight, however, it simulated a landing offshore in the Gulf of Mexico while SpaceX tests upgrades to the booster's software and hardware. No one was on board. Starship later splashed down in the Pacific Ocean north of Hawaii; SpaceX ultimately aims to recover and reuse the spacecraft. Three of the deployed Starlink satellites carry cameras to capture data on Starship's heat shield, a key system for surviving reentry and enabling spacecraft reusability.

SpaceX is betting on Starship to drive its growth. In filings ahead of the company's IPO in June, SpaceX stated, "Any failure or delay in the development of Starship at scale or in achieving the required launch cadence, reusability and capabilities thereafter would delay or limit our ability to execute our growth strategy." Starship is the only vehicle capable of launching the larger V3 version of Starlink satellites. SpaceX announced it will use Starship for future launches from Florida's Space Coast, after launching its final Starlink mission from Florida using the Falcon 9 rocket last month.

Starship is also central to NASA's plan to return humans to the moon. The agency selected SpaceX to develop Starship as a human landing system to transport astronauts to the lunar surface for future Artemis missions. NASA plans to test the ability to dock its Orion crew capsule with Starship, as well as a separate commercial lander from Blue Origin, during Artemis III, slated for launch late next year. SpaceX also announced plans to build a $100 billion facility in Louisiana with dozens of launch pads, aiming to begin Starship launches from Starbase, Louisiana, in 2029.
