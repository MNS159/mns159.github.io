FDA approves AbbVie's once-daily Parkinson's pill Juvmo

Health & Science desk

The U.S. Food and Drug Administration on Friday approved Juvmo (tavapadon) for Parkinson's disease, developed by AbbVie. Juvmo is a selective D1/D5 receptor agonist, the first such therapy available in the United States, and is indicated for use with or without levodopa, positioning it for both early and later stages of the disease. AbbVie has set a list price of $4,900 per 30-day supply, or $58,800 per year.

The approval was supported by results from the Phase 3 TEMPO program, which included three pivotal trials. In TEMPO-2, patients taking Juvmo showed significant improvements in motor and daily living complications compared with placebo, the study's primary endpoint. TEMPO-3 demonstrated a significantly better total “on” time—periods of good symptom control and patient function—versus placebo, and TEMPO-1 met its primary endpoint of improving motor function. AbbVie filed for FDA approval in September 2025 after the successful readouts.

Juvmo originated from AbbVie's $8.7 billion acquisition of Cerevel Therapeutics, which closed in August 2024. The deal's centerpiece had been emraclidine, a next-generation antipsychotic that failed a mid-stage schizophrenia study in November 2024, causing AbbVie's shares to drop 12%. With Juvmo's approval, the company converts another late-stage pipeline asset into a commercial opportunity, diversifying its revenue base as neuroscience becomes a more important growth driver, according to BMO Capital Markets.

AbbVie Chief Commercial Officer Jeffrey Stewart described Juvmo as having "nothing else like it in the marketplace," noting that unlike other Parkinson's treatments—including levodopa—Juvmo is less likely to cause sedation or "sleep attacks." AbbVie expects to make Juvmo available in the U.S. next month, according to BMO. Jefferies equity analyst Alyna Shamsi noted that Parkinson's has been a difficult field for pharmaceutical development due to late diagnosis, slow progression, and varied biological presentation across patients.

The approval addresses a sizable market opportunity: AbbVie's Chief Commercial Officer said during the company's Q2 earnings call in July that Juvmo could access a $5 billion market. The Parkinson's treatment landscape has seen recent setbacks, including BioVie's murky mid-stage readout and the discontinuation of Biogen and Denali's investigational therapy BIIB112 after it failed to slow disease progression.
