Nvidia Announces Record $150 Billion Buyback; Stock Rises

Financial & Markets desk

Nvidia's board authorized an additional $150 billion in share repurchases, the company said Monday, bringing its total remaining buyback authorization to $235 billion and sending shares up almost 3% on the day. Nvidia said the increase is the largest in history and expects to complete the program through fiscal 2028; it is currently in the third quarter of its fiscal 2027.

The announcement builds on a $80 billion repurchase authorization with no expiration date that the board approved in May. Nvidia bought back a combined $39 billion of stock during the first two quarters of fiscal 2027, nearly matching its repurchases for all of fiscal 2026, and roughly $34 billion in fiscal 2025. The company has committed to returning at least 50% of its free cash flow to shareholders through buybacks and dividends; in May it also raised its quarterly dividend to 25 cents a share from a penny, though the yield remains below 0.5%.

CNBC's Jim Cramer, who had been urging Nvidia to return more cash and earlier this month called for as much as $500 billion in buybacks, welcomed the move. "This is a very significant buyback," he said Monday. "I think if they're active and in there every day, it will change the trajectory of the stock." Cramer pointed to Apple's long-running buyback strategy, noting that former Apple CFO Luca Maestri "would buy every day," would double buy on a dip and triple it if it dipped again; Apple bought back more than $800 billion of stock during Tim Cook's 15 years as CEO, reducing its share count by roughly 40%. "If Nvidia follows the same active strategy, then you're going to see this stock advance dramatically," Cramer said.

The buyback comes even as Nvidia's financial performance has far outpaced its stock. Adjusted earnings per share have more than doubled in back-to-back quarters, and FactSet projects Nvidia's earnings to grow 94% in fiscal 2027, an acceleration from 60% growth in the prior year. Yet this year Nvidia is the seventh-worst performer in the 30-stock iShares Semiconductor ETF, which is up 86% including Monday's intraday move. That disconnect is what led Cramer to push Nvidia to deploy more of its cash stockpile toward repurchases.

Wall Street projects Nvidia to generate roughly $440 billion in free cash flow over its next six unreported quarters, according to FactSet, the period in which Nvidia said it expects to complete the $235 billion buyback. "We're going to generate a lot of cash in the coming years, and every single year, as we generate more cash, we'd like to be able to return it back to shareholders," Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang said Monday on CNBC's "Squawk Box."

Nvidia has also used its financial strength to support the broader AI ecosystem through investments in and financing arrangements with customers and other companies building AI infrastructure, deals that have fueled concerns about so-called circular financing. The company's AI chips can be redeployed if a supported customer runs into trouble, and Nvidia has enough firepower to ramp up buybacks without sacrificing the investments needed to maintain its AI leadership. Cramer said the $150 billion authorization falls short of the $500 billion he called for, but called it a major step in the right direction that strengthens the case for owning the stock. (Jim Cramer's Charitable Trust is long NVDA and AAPL.)
