Big Tech earnings week ahead: Meta, Tesla, Netflix and others report as AI-boom costs weigh on markets

Financial & Markets desk · anchored on AP

The third-quarter earnings season accelerates this week, with 70 S&P 500 companies reporting, led by a cluster of Big Tech names including Meta Platforms, Tesla and Netflix. The reports come after both the S&P 500 and the Nasdaq Composite fell last week as traders rotated out of technology stocks over concerns about mounting debt from the artificial-intelligence build-out, while markets also contend with high inflation and continued geopolitical tensions. Meta, reporting Wednesday after the close, will release its first quarterly results since launching AI agent Muse on Sept. 8; its stock has shot up more than 15% since then, and analysts are broadly bullish, with LSEG data showing expected earnings per share growth of 460% from a year earlier. Tesla, also reporting Wednesday after the close, is expected to post a 14.5% drop in earnings per share and nearly a 1% decline in revenue, with Wolfe Research citing a "tough quarter for Auto stocks" and eyeing a potential SpaceX merger and the robotaxi business as possible positives; Tesla's third-quarter deliveries fell 2% from a year earlier but still topped estimates.

Netflix reports Tuesday after the close, with analysts expecting 40% earnings growth from the year-earlier period, according to LSEG. But the streaming giant's stock has fallen the trading day after each of its last five earnings reports, by an average of 6.9%, including a 7.3% drop after its July 2026 report; the streak runs from July 2025 through July 2026. Netflix also last week announced plans to lay off about 1,000 employees, or 6% of its workforce, and replaced its chief content officer. Barclays analyst Kannan Venkateshwar wrote that while Originals make up more than 60% of Netflix's titles, many viewers still see it "as a service for library content," adding, "The issue may not be Netflix itself but rather that the world around Netflix has changed and Netflix hasn't really changed enough with it."

Other major reports include General Motors, also Tuesday before the bell, where analysts expect nearly a 30% jump in earnings per share but flat revenue; JPMorgan analysts maintained their overweight rating but lowered their price target, citing a likely cautious 2027 outlook and affordability concerns after vehicle sales declined 5.5% in the quarter. United Airlines and American Airlines both face high fuel costs; United is expected to show revenue growth above 15% but declining EPS, while American is expected to post top-line growth of 18% as it pushes premium seating — CEO Robert Isom said last month that 30% of seats generate half of the airline's revenue. AT&T reports Wednesday, with expected earnings growth of nearly 11% but slower revenue growth of 3.2%, after a SpaceX spectrum acquisition sparked a telecom sell-off. Procter & Gamble reports Thursday, with analysts expecting revenue growth just shy of 2% and a 5.4% decline in earnings; Citi maintained a buy rating, citing potential upside in the U.S. and China. Also reporting Wednesday after the close are IBM, Texas Instruments, Lam Research and SAP, while Intel reports Oct. 29. Monday has no major tech earnings.

The week's data calendar is light: weekly jobless claims arrive Thursday and S&P Global's flash PMIs for October come Friday. Federal Reserve officials will not steer sentiment, as the central bank's quiet period covers every trading day ahead of its Oct. 27-28 meeting. Semiconductors have been under pressure, with the iShares Semiconductor ETF (SOXX) down 11.8% over the previous week. The earnings season got off to a strong start last week: about 45 S&P 500 companies have reported, with 85% exceeding earnings estimates, according to FactSet, though the big banks were mixed — Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley and Citigroup beat on earnings and revenue, Wells Fargo was mixed, and JPMorgan Chase and Bank of America missed on both lines.

Overall, the reporting period could shift investor attention toward corporate fundamentals after recent market volatility, according to JPMorgan strategists led by Mislav Matejka, who expect third-quarter earnings growth to broaden beyond artificial-intelligence-related companies across U.S. and European equities. Consensus estimates from IBES show S&P 500 earnings per share up 24% for the quarter, while FactSet and LSEG point to growth of 30%. Historical patterns suggest big post-earnings moves for several of this week's reporters: Meta beats estimates 88% of the time but fell 8% after its last topline miss; Tesla has missed in two of the last four quarters and posts a topline beat only 58% of the time, having averaged a 7.8% move in either direction after each of its last eight reports; Netflix has fallen after five straight reports; and both GM and American Airlines beat estimates 89% and 88% of the time, respectively.
