Zelenskyy says Starlink restrictions still hamper Ukraine's drone defenses; Rubio confirms Starshield request under discussion

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts slightly center-left

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Saturday that Ukraine is still unable to use its interceptor systems against Russian drones because of speed restrictions imposed on Starshield, the military-grade version of Elon Musk's Starlink satellite service. Speaking in an online group chat with reporters at the EU Summit, Zelenskyy said: "Unfortunately, we can't use Starlink or Starshield technology on our territory today to shoot down jet-powered Shaheds. They fly, and we can stop them with aircraft and other air defense, but we don't use Starlink for that, unfortunately."

Zelenskyy said Kyiv was in dialogue with U.S. officials and had been told the restrictions were linked to technical issues, but that "nobody there is answering our military or the Ministry of Defense now." He described the effect in detail: "Here (in Ukraine) Starlink works up to a speed of 161 km/h, then shuts off. In the EU it works at 180-plus and works constantly. Here it shuts off in the second minute after 161 km/h and stops working. We lose both drones and connectivity." Ukrainian officials had said last week that restrictions on Starlink terminals slowed some drones on the battlefield.

U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio, speaking to reporters at Joint Base Andrews in Maryland, confirmed that Starshield is "not at this moment available to Ukraine," and said Kyiv's request for access to the system to conduct deep strikes inside Russia is "under discussion." He stressed the distinction between the two services: "Starlink is a commercial product, okay? And so it's regulated and handled as a commercial product. Starshield is a defence product, a military product." Rubio rejected Zelenskyy's suggestion that the disruption was deliberate, saying, "No one at the White House ordered anyone to slow down internet or slow down speed so that certain drones couldn't use it," and adding that the service had reportedly been restored to its previous level. He also said, "I don't know what Zelenskyy said, I'm not sure Zelenskyy knows what he's talking about, but it wasn't deliberate."

Starlink has been central to Ukraine's communications, reconnaissance and drone operations since Russia's full-scale invasion in 2022. According to the Center for Security Studies at ETH Zurich, around 50,000 Starlink terminals were in use in Ukraine in 2025, enabling troops to communicate when infrastructure is destroyed, relay orders, and receive drone footage. The system became vital after the KA-SAT satellite system Ukraine previously favored was targeted in a cyberattack hours before the invasion. While Starlink and Starshield are based on the same technological infrastructure, Starshield is designed specifically for governmental and military use, offering deeper encryption, and SpaceX says it is primarily used by U.S. defense agencies and other government partners.

The speed restriction has direct consequences for fast-moving Ukrainian drones, some of which travel at up to 300 km/h; if the connection is lost, live video can no longer be transmitted and drones effectively fly blind. Rubio said the United States had not ordered Starlink speeds to be reduced and that the service was back to what it was before. Separately, Zelenskyy criticized the Trump administration's decision to waive sanctions on Russia and countries that buy its oil and gas. Europe has alternatives to Starlink such as Eutelsat/OneWeb, but their performance in wartime conditions has not been tested on a large scale, and Ukraine also relies on U.S.-provided reconnaissance data, which U.S. limits on access would compound.

Rubio said the U.S. government was not responsible for the outage, alleging that Ukraine's military has never had access to Starshield and has only been using Starlink. "Starshield is a contract with the U.S. military, it has nothing to do with Ukraine," he said. "I know Ukraine wants access to Starshield, they do not have Starshield now." Musk has said Starshield systems are controlled by the U.S. government, whereas Starlink is a commercial service operated by SpaceX.
