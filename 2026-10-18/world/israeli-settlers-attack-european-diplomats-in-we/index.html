Israeli settlers attack European diplomats in West Bank; EU, US condemn surge in violence

International & World desk · anchored on Reuters, AP
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

A delegation of European diplomats was attacked by masked Israeli settlers on Saturday in the occupied West Bank, drawing condemnation from Belgium, France, the European Union and the United States. The diplomats — from Belgium, France, Poland and the Catholic Church — had traveled to the largely Christian village of Taybeh near Ramallah to assess the situation on the ground after near-daily settler attacks on Palestinians trying to bring in their olive harvest. Video footage showed masked settlers confronting the convoy; reports indicate stones were thrown. A car from the Belgian consulate had a window smashed, and a French mayor, Bernard Baudoux, was assaulted. Baudoux told AFP he was kicked, beaten with iron bars and thrown to the ground. “It’s the gratuitous nature of the violence that stuns me,” he said. “The whole world needs to know what’s happening there.”

Belgium’s foreign minister, Maxime Prevot, condemned the attack and said Israel had a responsibility to bring those responsible to justice and ensure the safety of diplomats, journalists and Palestinians. He added that Belgium would continue to press for EU sanctions against those responsible for the violence. France’s foreign minister, Jean-Noël Barrot, called on Israeli authorities to “urgently halt this criminal violence and guarantee the safety of civilian populations.” Poland’s foreign affairs ministry also called for a “firm response” from Israeli authorities and an end to “the spiral of violence in the West Bank.” The EU condemned the attacks, urged Israel to abide by international and human rights law, and called for an end to the expansion of settlements. A spokesperson said the bloc expects “full accountability as well as effective measures” to stop the continued violence against Palestinians and journalists.

The attack came a day after Israeli settlers injured several Palestinians and Heidi Levine, a Washington Post photographer, in a separate assault in another West Bank village. US Secretary of State Marco Rubio called the attack on Levine “disturbing” and “not a good look,” and urged Israel to “crack down” on the violence. The US ambassador to Israel, Mike Huckabee, described the assault as a “despicable act of terror carried out by masked cowards” and said the assailants must face “prosecution to the fullest extent of the law.”

The Israel Defense Forces (IDF) condemned what they called “the severe act of violence” and said they were investigating. According to a preliminary inquiry, no IDF soldiers were present at the scene at the time of the attack. Israeli Foreign Minister Gideon Sa’ar condemned the violence by what he called “Israeli rioters” but noted that the visit was not coordinated with the IDF in advance, which could have helped safeguard the visitors. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu’s office urged “caution before drawing conclusions,” suggesting the reports could be part of a “coordinated campaign to smear Israel and the Jewish residents of Judea and Samaria during the annual olive harvest.” The office said Israeli police and forensic investigators had launched an investigation to establish the facts and identify those responsible.

Critics argue that mere condemnations without consequences will not deter settler violence, which they say flourishes within a system of occupation, settlement expansion and impunity. Ahmed Najar, a Palestinian political analyst, told Al Jazeera: “Western governments continue to arm, fund and diplomatically protect Israel while treating settler attacks as isolated acts by a few extremists. But this violence flourishes within a wider system of occupation, settlement expansion and impunity.” Sven Kuhn von Burgsdorff, former EU representative to the occupied West Bank and Gaza, said the “lame protests” issued by European chancelleries are “pathetic” because they are not followed by concrete punitive measures. He noted that at the last Foreign Affairs Council meeting in Brussels on October 12, EU ministers were still unable to decide on sanctioning violent settlers or their enablers.

Israeli settler attacks during this year’s olive harvest season have already surpassed 2025 totals, according to Al Jazeera. On Saturday alone, settlers injured six Palestinians as Israeli forces arrested farmers during attacks across the West Bank. Four women and a child were among the injured in Ma’in village in the Masafer Yatta area, south of Hebron, according to the Palestine Red Crescent Society. In Deir Ammar town, west of Ramallah, Israeli forces opened fire on farmers picking olives, forcing them to leave their land. The violence has prompted calls from the EU, the US and European governments for Israel to ensure the safety of civilians, journalists and diplomats, and to hold perpetrators accountable.
