France

French police arrest about 1,800 as protests turn violent across country

International & World desk

French police arrested about 1,800 people on Saturday as some 550,000 demonstrators took to the streets in nationwide protests that were largely peaceful but turned violent in parts of the country, leaving burnt-out cars, shattered shopfronts and looted stores across Paris and other cities. Protesters, including students, trade unionists and Yellow Vest activists, were demonstrating against rising fuel prices, the high cost of living, social inequality and deteriorating public services such as schools. France's interior ministry said more than 500 police officers, gendarmes and firefighters were injured in the unrest.

Violence unfolded in Paris and cities including Marseille, Lille, Lyon, Limoges and Saint-Étienne. In the capital, riot police fired tear gas and water cannon at protesters, charged crowds and were seen striking people with batons; groups set fires in streets, torched vehicles and gutted at least one restaurant, and the BBC witnessed fighting among some groups. Designer stores including Ralph Lauren, Gucci and Versace had their glass frontages smashed, and the Retail and Trade Federation said about 1,500 shops and retailers were damaged. Looting was reported in Paris, Marseille, Lille, Nice and Bordeaux, according to AFP. In Lille, a McDonald's was set on fire and an Apple store was looted; in Marseille, protesters used a metal fence as a battering ram to enter the city hall; and in Lille, protesters also broke into and vandalized the city hall. Clashes were reported near the Gare du Nord train station and in the Saint-Denis area on the outskirts of Paris.

The interior ministry said the arrests followed "targeted acts of violence" from "hooded individuals" in cities including Paris, Marseille, Rennes, Toulouse and Angers, involving "attempted looting, confrontations with law enforcement, the setting of fires to street furniture and the targeting of seats of power and media outlets." The windows of French broadcasters CNews and Europe 1 in western Paris were broken, and damage was reported in Grenoble, Avignon, Pau, Montpellier and Valence. In Lyon, the city's tourist office was set alight; Mayor Grégory Doucet called it "a tragedy" for a "very iconic, well-known historic building," and police said "hostile individuals" prevented firefighters from tackling the blaze. In Limoges, the 143-year-old town hall was "largely lost" after being deliberately set on fire, with parts of the building collapsing; Prefect Maurice Barate blamed a "wild horde." In Saint-Étienne, Mayor Régis Juanico said a young man "had his hand blown off" by a small explosive, adding, "Such a tragedy should never have happened."

Paris residents described the aftermath as akin to war. "It's like we had a war," Mehmet Ozeden told AFP. Resident Anita GrasIt said it was "terrifying" to see young people "smashing everything, with total impunity," while another resident, Maryse, said looters were "proudly carrying supermarket baskets filled with goods and laughing about their thefts." A shop owner near the Place de la République whose ransacked store he watched on CCTV told the BBC that looters spent over an hour and a half inside without police appearing, and said he did not believe they were genuine protesters. CCTV footage aired by France's Channel 2 showed masked teenagers helping themselves to items in a mobile phone shop. On Sunday, shopkeepers swept up glass and rubbish trucks cleared debris as graffiti reading "tax the rich" and "eat the rich" was daubed on stores; the BBC also reported the Good Vibes bakery's glass front was shattered and the Nicolas wine store had every bottle either stolen or smashed, leaving a lingering smell of alcohol.

The protests began late last month as student demonstrations over school conditions, including teacher absences, overcrowded classrooms and run-down buildings, with demands for greater investment. They widened as trade unionists and remnants of the Yellow Vests ("Gilets jaunes") movement joined; that movement staged weekly protests from November 2018 to June 2020, initially against fuel price rises and later against the cost of living and economic inequality. Hard-left France Unbowed (LFI) leader Jean-Luc Mélenchon has backed the student protests and marched in a demonstration on Tuesday, and the government has accused LFI of "pouring fuel on the fire of widespread public discontent," a charge the party denied. Prime Minister Sebastien Lecornu visited officers in Paris on Saturday evening, saying "It's a miracle the toll wasn't higher"; he put the arrests at about 1,200 and said 150 police officers had been injured.

Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez said that while demonstrating was a fundamental right, "several thousand far-left individuals" committed "outrages" determined to target public buildings and confront police, later joined by "delinquents who looted and ransacked numerous businesses." He also said 110,000 people took part in demonstrations in Paris alone and that 110 marches had occurred nationwide. President Emmanuel Macron condemned the violence on X, writing: "Those who have broken, attacked the prefectures as well as businesses, looted even our town halls, will be prosecuted and punished." Police had banned rallies around the Élysée presidential palace, the prime minister's office and the Champs-Élysées, as well as a march through central Paris. DW correspondent Jack Parrock reported that most people on the streets "were teachers, nurses, students, and workers, who want no part in the unrest" but expressed "a collective rage against a government they don't feel is listening to them," while police appeared to "show some restraint" and try to de-escalate tensions.
