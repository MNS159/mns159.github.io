Russia-Ukraine War

Ukraine launches massive drone attack on Russia, hitting St Petersburg oil terminal

International & World desk

Ukraine launched one of its largest drone attacks of the war on Russia overnight Saturday, striking the St. Petersburg Oil Terminal and other targets in and around Russia's second-largest city. The attack killed at least one person and wounded five others, according to St. Petersburg Governor Alexander Beglov. Fires broke out at the oil terminal, the Ruchyi oil depot, and industrial warehouses in the Kolpino district, while a logistics center belonging to e-commerce company Ozon and a data center operated by social network VK were also hit. Russia's Defense Ministry said it shot down 1,015 Ukrainian drones over 15 regions, and local officials reported that about 20,000 people were left without electricity after transformer substations were struck. Pulkovo Airport was temporarily closed, and 12 apartment buildings and two homes were damaged in the city.

The strikes came as Kyiv defied U.S. President Donald Trump's demand that Ukraine halt attacks on Russian energy infrastructure. Trump had announced an "energy ceasefire" a week earlier, saying both sides had agreed to stop striking each other's energy facilities. Ukraine paused its strikes for three days after the announcement but accused Russia of continuing its attacks and then resumed operations earlier this week. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky said on Sunday that since Trump declared the ceasefire, Russia had deliberately attacked Ukraine's energy and oil and gas sectors 76 times. "Last night alone, there were strikes on substations in the Vinnytsia, Zhytomyr, Khmelnytskyi, and Odesa regions," he wrote on X, adding that tens of thousands of families remained without power. Russian strikes on Ukraine overnight killed at least two people and injured a dozen others, according to Zelensky. Over the past week, he said, Russia had attacked Ukraine with nearly 2,700 drones, 1,700 aerial bombs, and 66 missiles, most of them ballistic, with energy facilities the main target.

The attack on St. Petersburg underlined the deteriorating relationship between Kyiv and Washington. Trump has called Ukraine's strikes on Russian oil facilities a "disgrace" and repeatedly pressed Zelensky to stop them. On Friday, the Trump administration waived a sweeping new sanctions package passed by Congress that would have expanded measures targeting Russia's oil and gas industry, and suspended sanctions on imports of Russian diesel as part of a deal Trump struck with Russian President Vladimir Putin. Zelensky criticized the move, saying it amounted to the U.S. investing in Russia's war effort and encouraging Putin to keep fighting. The White House said the waiver was a 60-day pause to analyze the impact of sanctions on the global economy. The decisions drew criticism even from some U.S. Republicans.

Zelensky also said that Ukraine's access to the Starlink satellite internet system, crucial for guiding its drones, had been restricted. He said that at speeds above 161 kilometers per hour, Starlink shuts down and Ukraine loses the drone, and that the restrictions also prevented Ukraine from using the system to shoot down Russian drones. "The restrictions are in effect on Ukrainian territory, but they do not apply within the European Union," he said. "In other words, this is a planned operation … to restrict us, cut us off and prevent us from defending ourselves." The Trump administration denied restricting the service. Kyiv also said it was now facing Russian ballistic missile attacks without alerts from allies, raising concerns that the U.S. might further limit intelligence sharing.

Both Russia and Ukraine have intensified strikes on each other's energy infrastructure as the war enters its fifth year. Russia says it hits targets used by Ukraine's military, but the United Nations noted that the first two weeks of October had been the deadliest for Ukrainian civilians in years. Russian strikes on energy facilities have left hundreds of thousands without power, raising fears of another freezing winter. Zelensky said energy workers and repair crews were working around the clock to restore electricity. The attack on St. Petersburg, which Russian state media called the largest on the region in four years, demonstrated Ukraine's willingness to keep striking deep inside Russia despite U.S. pressure, as Kyiv argues that targeting Russian oil facilities limits the main revenue stream for Moscow's war effort.
