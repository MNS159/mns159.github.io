Trump waives Russia sanctions; bipartisan backlash grows

Politics desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum
✓ Facts
  • President Trump signed an executive order Friday night suspending sanctions on Russia that were set to take effect Sunday.
  • The sanctions stem from the Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act, a law Trump signed last month after the Senate passed it 86-11.
  • Secretary of State Marco Rubio said the suspension amounts to a 60-day pause to let the administration assess the economic impact of the sanctions.
  • The law authorized tariffs of up to 100% on countries importing large amounts of Russian oil and gas, including top buyers China and India, and targeted senior Russian officials, financial institutions and Russia's 'shadow fleet' of tankers.
  • Congress is on recess until after the Nov. 3 midterm elections, limiting any immediate legislative response to Trump's order.
  • Rep. Gregory Meeks introduced legislation Friday aimed at blocking a separate Trump-brokered deal to import Russian diesel.
  • Sen. Lindsey Graham, who championed the sanctions bill, died earlier this year.
  • Ukrainian officials reported at least nine people killed and about 60 injured in Russian strikes since Friday, including an ambulance driver killed in Kherson.
○ Analysis

The dispute centers on a sweeping Russia sanctions law that passed Congress with overwhelming bipartisan support and was designed to choke off funding for Moscow's war in Ukraine by targeting its oil exports, financial institutions and shipping networks. Trump's decision to suspend enforcement just before the law's effective date marks a sharp reversal from the legislative intent, and it comes alongside other recent moves — a U.S. deal to import Russian diesel and reported disruptions to Ukraine's Starlink access — that have fueled concerns in Washington and Kyiv about the administration's posture toward Moscow. With lawmakers in recess until after the midterms, Congress has no immediate mechanism to challenge the executive order, leaving the suspension's 60-day review period as the near-term determinant of what, if any, sanctions take effect.

◐ Debate

Democratic

  • Rep. Don Beyer (D-Va.) accused Trump of capitulating to Moscow and turning his back on Ukraine and Zelensky.
  • Sen. Elizabeth Warren (D-Mass.) said Trump is 'thumbing his nose' at Congress and 'enabling Vladimir Putin to continue his war of aggression,' urging colleagues to reverse course.
  • Rep. Gregory Meeks (D-N.Y.) said Trump is 'cozying up to Putin and striking self-enriching deals instead of standing with Ukraine,' calling the related diesel deal 'corrupt.'

Republican

  • Sen. Thom Tillis (R-N.C.) called the waiver 'beyond irresponsible' and said the administration is 'capitulating to a Russian dictator who illegally invaded Ukraine.'
  • Sen. John Cornyn (R-Texas) called the move 'reckless' and said 'Lindsey would be appalled,' referencing the late Sen. Lindsey Graham.
  • Sen. Darline Graham (R-S.C.) said she would prefer to keep sanctions pressure on Russia and looks forward to discussing it with Trump.
Steelman

Despite sharp partisan rhetoric, lawmakers across both parties who spoke out agree on the substance: the sanctions law passed with near-unanimous support and should be enforced, suggesting the disagreement is less about policy toward Russia than about whether the executive branch can unilaterally override a bipartisan congressional mandate.

Implications

With Congress in recess until after the midterms, the 60-day review period cited by Secretary Rubio will likely determine whether any of the suspended sanctions are eventually reinstated, and lawmakers' ability to formally challenge the waiver — or related moves like the Russian diesel deal — will depend on legislative action once they return to Washington.
