FDA Approves Replimune Melanoma Therapy Tudriqev After Two Rejections

Health & Science desk · anchored on Reuters

The Food and Drug Administration on Aug. 6 granted accelerated approval to Replimune’s engineered viral immunotherapy Tudriqev (vusolimogene oderparepvec-wtpg, formerly RP1) in combination with Bristol Myers Squibb’s Opdivo (nivolumab) for adults with unresectable or metastatic cutaneous melanoma whose disease progressed on prior anti-PD-1 therapy. The list price is $450,000 per course of therapy before customary rebates and discounts, Replimune said. The approval marks a reversal for the Massachusetts-based biotech after two prior complete response letters from the FDA.

The accelerated approval is supported by data from the Phase 1/2 IGNYTE trial. Among 91 efficacy-evaluable patients, the combination achieved a 24.2% objective response rate and a median duration of response of 14.1 months. The recommended dose of RP1 is 1 mL per cm of the largest tumor dimension, up to 10 mL across all treated lesions per dose, given as intratumoral injections every two weeks for eight consecutive doses. The starting concentration is 10⁶ plaque-forming units (PFU) per mL at week 1, followed by 10⁷ PFU per mL for subsequent doses. Nivolumab is administered intravenously starting at week 3.

The regulatory path was marked by intense debate. The FDA had previously rejected the drug twice, and former FDA Commissioner Marty Makary defended one of those decisions. The FDA’s own reviewers raised concerns that, without a comparator arm, the contribution of RP1 could not be isolated from Opdivo, and they questioned the drug’s systemic effect since it is injected directly into tumors. At an advisory committee meeting on July 30, external advisers voted 10 to 3 in favor of approval, agreeing that the response rate remained notably above historical experience in this patient population even after excluding certain patients. Replimune resubmitted its biologics license application in October 2025 with additional analyses, and the FDA accepted the resubmission under a Class II timeline. A second complete response letter was issued in April 2026, and a third resubmission was accepted in June 2026.

Replimune CEO Sushil Patel said in a statement that the approval is "a transformative moment for Replimune, marking years of pioneering research to bring Tudriqev to patients desperately in need of new treatment options for advanced melanoma." Because the approval was granted under the accelerated pathway based on surrogate endpoints, continued approval is contingent on verification of clinical benefit in a confirmatory trial, which the sources identify as the ongoing Phase 3 IGNYTE-3 study. Leerink Partners analysts projected peak sales of $618 million on an un-risk-adjusted basis, below the Wall Street consensus of $971 million on a risk-adjusted basis, citing operational uncertainties after Replimune laid off most of its staff earlier this year. Most treatment-related adverse reactions observed in the trial were Grade 1 and 2 and transient.
