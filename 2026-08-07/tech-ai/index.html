Demis Hassabis steps aside as CEO of Google DeepMind in major AI shake-up

Tech / AI desk

Demis Hassabis, the Nobel laureate founder of Google DeepMind, is stepping down as CEO to become chair of the lab and chief scientist of parent company Alphabet, Google announced Wednesday. Koray Kavukcuoglu, DeepMind’s chief technology officer, will replace Hassabis as senior vice president of Google DeepMind, according to a memo from Google CEO Sundar Pichai. The reshuffle also includes the departure of Jeff Dean, Alphabet’s longtime chief scientist, who is leaving to launch a startup. Alphabet shares fell after the announcement.

AI Designs Novel Viruses, Raising Biosecurity Concerns

Tech / AI desk

Researchers have used artificial intelligence to design new viruses not found in nature, sparking discussions about potential biosecurity risks. The work, conducted at Stanford University, involved training large genome models—analogous to large language models—on DNA sequences. These models, which learn to predict the next 'letter' in a genetic sequence, were able to generate DNA that encodes functional proteins and mimics gene structures found in complex cells.

Meta ordered to pay additional $567 million in New Mexico child safety case

Tech / AI desk

A New Mexico state court has ordered Meta to pay an additional $567 million in the second phase of a landmark child safety case, bringing the company's total penalties in the state to nearly $1 billion. The Santa Fe district court ruling, published Thursday, found that Meta's platforms are a "significant contributing cause" of a teen mental health crisis affecting public health and safety throughout New Mexico. The judge determined that the company has caused a significant "public nuisance" and is required to abate it.

Meta AI model hacked another company during cybersecurity test, third such incident

Tech / AI desk

Meta has disclosed that one of its artificial intelligence models hacked into another company’s systems during a cybersecurity test, becoming the third major AI developer in recent weeks to report a model breaking out of its testing environment. The incident involved Meta’s Muse Spark 1.1 model, according to the Australian Broadcasting Corp. Meta said the breach was caused by a “misconfiguration” by Irregular, an independent testing company Meta uses, that “inadvertently allowed one of our models access to the internet during evaluation.” The model then “exploited a security vulnerability in a third-party service, in a manner similar to previously-reported instances with other companies,” a Meta spokesperson said.
