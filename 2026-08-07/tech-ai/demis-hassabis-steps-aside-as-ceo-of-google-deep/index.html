Demis Hassabis steps aside as CEO of Google DeepMind in major AI shake-up

Tech / AI desk

Demis Hassabis, the Nobel laureate founder of Google DeepMind, is stepping down as CEO to become chair of the lab and chief scientist of parent company Alphabet, Google announced Wednesday. Koray Kavukcuoglu, DeepMind’s chief technology officer, will replace Hassabis as senior vice president of Google DeepMind, according to a memo from Google CEO Sundar Pichai. The reshuffle also includes the departure of Jeff Dean, Alphabet’s longtime chief scientist, who is leaving to launch a startup. Alphabet shares fell after the announcement.

Hassabis said in a staff note that artificial general intelligence is “close at hand” and that he wanted to “focus on the big picture” and “help influence what is to come.” He will advise Kavukcuoglu and dedicate time to Isomorphic Labs, a drug-discovery company he co-founded. A Google spokesperson said the transition “changes absolutely nothing” regarding frontier AI safety. Other changes include DeepMind’s chief AI readiness officer, Lila Ibrahim, now reporting to senior Google executive James Manyika, and some safety teams potentially moving under Google’s president of global affairs, Kent Walker, though people familiar with the matter said no final decision has been made. Comms, legal and marketing teams from DeepMind will merge with Google’s. DeepMind co-founder Shane Legg will continue reporting to Hassabis.

The restructuring comes as Google struggles to keep pace with rivals OpenAI and Anthropic. Its Gemini frontier model has faced repeated delays, and top talent is leaving: John Jumper, a Nobel-winning scientist who worked with Hassabis on AlphaFold, departed earlier this year for Anthropic. Hassabis had been spending less time on Gemini in recent months, according to two people with knowledge of his focus, and more on safety, AI governance, and government engagement, including attending the G7 summit. Kavukcuoglu had already been leading Gemini development for at least a year, according to Semafor, and Hassabis was absent from many day-to-day Gemini meetings. In a December interview with CNBC, Hassabis described the competitive environment as “ferocious” and acknowledged that an ideal scientific approach to AGI was at odds with the “race dynamic” among companies and nations.

The changes expose a central tension in Google’s AI strategy: Hassabis, a scientist who prizes breakthroughs like AlphaGo and AlphaFold, wants to use AI to transform science, while Alphabet must commercialize quickly to justify capital spending that could reach $205 billion this year. Sebastian Mallaby, author of a book about DeepMind, wrote on X that Hassabis’s move “is a return to that side of his work” focused on safety and oversight. One DeepMind employee expressed concern that moving safety, responsibility and ethics teams deeper into Google could reduce their influence. The reorganization is seen as an effort to accelerate product delivery while keeping Hassabis’s strategic vision as the lab races toward AGI.
