International & World Ebola Outbreak

Ebola outbreak in DRC surpasses 4,000 cases, reaches displacement camps, officials fear mutation

International & World desk

The Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC) has surpassed 4,000 confirmed cases and 1,800 deaths, making it the second-largest Ebola outbreak on record, and has now reached displacement camps in the conflict-affected province of Ituri, according to health officials and UN agencies. The Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention (Africa CDC) said on Thursday that 3,973 cases and 1,801 deaths had been confirmed as of August 4, with the total now exceeding 4,000. The outbreak, caused by the Bundibugyo strain of the virus, was first reported on May 15, though officials suspect it may have been spreading since January.

The virus is spreading at a rate six to eight times faster than any previous Ebola outbreak, Africa CDC Director General Dr. Jean Kaseya told reporters on Thursday. He said the level of severity of the Bundibugyo outbreak is unprecedented, and that he and World Health Organization (WHO) Director-General Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus plan to launch studies to check if the virus is mutating. 'As Dr. Tedros shared with me yesterday, it's time for the WHO and Africa CDC to conduct studies to check that there is no issue, additional issue, or maybe if the virus is not mutating, because the level of severity of this Bundibugyo outbreak is unprecedented,' Kaseya said. More than two-thirds of Ebola deaths are occurring in the community rather than in treatment centers, and in a Médecins Sans Frontières (MSF) treatment center in Bunia, 90% of admitted patients do not appear on contact-tracing lists, indicating high levels of untracked transmission.

AI Designs Novel Viruses, Raising Biosecurity Concerns

Tech / AI desk

Researchers have used artificial intelligence to design new viruses not found in nature, sparking discussions about potential biosecurity risks. The work, conducted at Stanford University, involved training large genome models—analogous to large language models—on DNA sequences. These models, which learn to predict the next 'letter' in a genetic sequence, were able to generate DNA that encodes functional proteins and mimics gene structures found in complex cells.

Demis Hassabis steps aside as CEO of Google DeepMind in major AI shake-up

Tech / AI desk

Demis Hassabis, the Nobel laureate founder of Google DeepMind, is stepping down as CEO to become chair of the lab and chief scientist of parent company Alphabet, Google announced Wednesday. Koray Kavukcuoglu, DeepMind’s chief technology officer, will replace Hassabis as senior vice president of Google DeepMind, according to a memo from Google CEO Sundar Pichai. The reshuffle also includes the departure of Jeff Dean, Alphabet’s longtime chief scientist, who is leaving to launch a startup. Alphabet shares fell after the announcement.

FDA Approves Replimune Melanoma Therapy Tudriqev After Two Rejections

Health & Science desk · anchored on Reuters

The Food and Drug Administration on Aug. 6 granted accelerated approval to Replimune’s engineered viral immunotherapy Tudriqev (vusolimogene oderparepvec-wtpg, formerly RP1) in combination with Bristol Myers Squibb’s Opdivo (nivolumab) for adults with unresectable or metastatic cutaneous melanoma whose disease progressed on prior anti-PD-1 therapy. The list price is $450,000 per course of therapy before customary rebates and discounts, Replimune said. The approval marks a reversal for the Massachusetts-based biotech after two prior complete response letters from the FDA.

Senate panel votes to hold Anthony Fauci in contempt of Congress

A Senate committee voted along party lines Thursday to hold Anthony Fauci, the former director of the National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases, in contempt of Congress for refusing to answer questions during a hearing last week about the origins and federal response to the COVID-19 pandemic. The Homeland Security and Governmental Affairs Committee voted 8-7, with all Republicans in favor and all Democrats opposed. The contempt resolution now moves to the full Senate, where it would be subject to a filibuster; if passed, the Department of Justice would decide whether to prosecute Fauci on a federal misdemeanor charge that carries a penalty of up to $100,000 in fines and one to 12 months in prison.
