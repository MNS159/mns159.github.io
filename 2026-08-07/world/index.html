Middle East Defense

Saudi Arabia, Turkey, Pakistan sign mutual defense pact amid Middle East turmoil

International & World desk · anchored on Reuters

Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Pakistan signed a joint defense agreement on Friday, pledging that an armed attack on any one of the three countries would be regarded as an attack against them all. The pact, named the "Mecca Joint Defence Agreement," was announced during a meeting in Mecca between Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman, Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdogan and Pakistani Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif, according to Pakistan's Ministry of Foreign Affairs.

Ebola Outbreak

Ebola outbreak in DRC surpasses 4,000 cases, reaches displacement camps, officials fear mutation

International & World desk

The Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC) has surpassed 4,000 confirmed cases and 1,800 deaths, making it the second-largest Ebola outbreak on record, and has now reached displacement camps in the conflict-affected province of Ituri, according to health officials and UN agencies. The Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention (Africa CDC) said on Thursday that 3,973 cases and 1,801 deaths had been confirmed as of August 4, with the total now exceeding 4,000. The outbreak, caused by the Bundibugyo strain of the virus, was first reported on May 15, though officials suspect it may have been spreading since January.

Iran-US Tensions

Iran Signals Strait of Hormuz Deal Near; Parliament Considers Barring US, Israeli Ships

International & World desk

Iran and Oman have agreed on coordinates for shipping routes through the Strait of Hormuz, and Tehran says a deal to reopen the waterway is "on the verge of being finalised," according to reports cited by Al Jazeera and DW. The agreement on coordinates was reached Thursday, though the strait remains closed to most commercial traffic, with data from maritime analytics firm Kpler showing that only six commercial vessels crossed the route on Monday, down from seven the day before, according to Reuters. Meanwhile, Iran's Fars news agency reported that an Iranian parliamentary committee is reviewing a preliminary bill that would bar US, Israeli and other "hostile" vessels from transiting the Strait of Hormuz.

Yemen Conflict

Houthi attacks kill dozens in Yemen, wound 11 in Saudi Arabia as war escalates

International & World desk · anchored on AP

Houthi forces in Yemen launched a wave of missile and drone strikes on Thursday that killed at least 30 government soldiers and wounded 15 others in the deadliest attack in the country's civil war in years, while a separate Houthi strike on southern Saudi Arabia wounded 11 civilians, including a young child, a senior Saudi official said. The attacks targeted military camps and gatherings in the government-held provinces of Hadramout and Marib, according to Yemeni government sources. On Friday, the Houthis fired at least 12 additional missiles at the same provinces, and Yemen's government air defenses downed a number of Houthi drones over Marib.
