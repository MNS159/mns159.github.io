Yemen Conflict

Houthi attacks kill dozens in Yemen, wound 11 in Saudi Arabia as war escalates

International & World desk · anchored on AP

Houthi forces in Yemen launched a wave of missile and drone strikes on Thursday that killed at least 30 government soldiers and wounded 15 others in the deadliest attack in the country's civil war in years, while a separate Houthi strike on southern Saudi Arabia wounded 11 civilians, including a young child, a senior Saudi official said. The attacks targeted military camps and gatherings in the government-held provinces of Hadramout and Marib, according to Yemeni government sources. On Friday, the Houthis fired at least 12 additional missiles at the same provinces, and Yemen's government air defenses downed a number of Houthi drones over Marib.

The Thursday strikes struck at 2 p.m. local time, hitting camps belonging to the Homeland Shield Forces, the Emergency Forces, and the First Military Region in Hadramout, according to Al Jazeera's Yemen affairs editor. The Homeland Shield Forces, formed in 2022 with Saudi backing, number tens of thousands of troops and guard the Marib-Hadramout frontier and the Saudi border. The Emergency Forces, created by presidential decree in late 2025 under Saudi-led coalition supervision, secure land borders and anti-Houthi fronts. Casualties were high because troops had gathered for a morning training exercise, the First Emergency Division Command said. Houthi military spokesperson Yaya Saree claimed in a broadcast statement that the attacks killed “hundreds” and “destroyed a large number of camps and depots,” alleging that the strikes were a response to “a massive Saudi military mobilisation.”

The escalation marks a major turning point in Yemen's conflict, which began in September 2014 when the Houthis seized the capital Sanaa, prompting a Saudi-led coalition to intervene in March 2015. A UN-sponsored truce largely halted large-scale fighting in April 2022, but both sides have recently expressed a desire for a decisive military battle. The renewed violence follows the US-Israel war on Iran that began in late February, in which the Houthis, key allies of Iran, have become increasingly involved. The group last month announced a naval blockade on Saudi ports and launched attacks in the Bab al-Mandeb Strait. The Houthis have also expanded cross-border attacks on government forces, and analysts say the risk of all-out war is no longer far-fetched.

Reactions have underscored the deepening crisis. A senior Saudi official, speaking on condition of anonymity, told Reuters that intelligence suggests the Houthis and Iraqi armed groups, under the supervision of Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC), may launch new attacks on energy infrastructure, ports, and airports. Sheikh Othman Majali, a member of the Saudi-backed Presidential Leadership Council, said there can be no prospect of peace “as long as the project of the terrorist Houthi militia continues to threaten Yemen and the region,” and that peace can only be achieved by “confronting and toppling the militia.” The Houthi group said in a statement Thursday it would not “tolerate” any Saudi military presence inside Yemen’s borders and claimed a “legitimate right to confront any hostile moves.”

Analysts warn that the room for restraint is narrowing. Fuad Mussed, a Yemeni researcher, said the Houthis have taken a final decision to enter an open confrontation, with a message of “escalation both inside Yemen and beyond.” Ahmed Nagi of the International Crisis Group said that while containment is still possible, “the problem is that each new escalatory move makes that much more difficult.” Adel Dashela, a researcher at the Sanaa Center for Strategic Studies, said Marib is once again emerging as the focal point, with all indications pointing to an imminent ground war. “A full-scale eruption across all battlefronts would make it difficult for the group to contend with government forces,” he said, adding that such a war “would not resemble previous conflicts; rather, it would be far bloodier.”
