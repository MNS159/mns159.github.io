Ebola Outbreak

Ebola outbreak in DRC surpasses 4,000 cases, reaches displacement camps, officials fear mutation

International & World desk

The Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC) has surpassed 4,000 confirmed cases and 1,800 deaths, making it the second-largest Ebola outbreak on record, and has now reached displacement camps in the conflict-affected province of Ituri, according to health officials and UN agencies. The Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention (Africa CDC) said on Thursday that 3,973 cases and 1,801 deaths had been confirmed as of August 4, with the total now exceeding 4,000. The outbreak, caused by the Bundibugyo strain of the virus, was first reported on May 15, though officials suspect it may have been spreading since January.

The virus is spreading at a rate six to eight times faster than any previous Ebola outbreak, Africa CDC Director General Dr. Jean Kaseya told reporters on Thursday. He said the level of severity of the Bundibugyo outbreak is unprecedented, and that he and World Health Organization (WHO) Director-General Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus plan to launch studies to check if the virus is mutating. 'As Dr. Tedros shared with me yesterday, it's time for the WHO and Africa CDC to conduct studies to check that there is no issue, additional issue, or maybe if the virus is not mutating, because the level of severity of this Bundibugyo outbreak is unprecedented,' Kaseya said. More than two-thirds of Ebola deaths are occurring in the community rather than in treatment centers, and in a Médecins Sans Frontières (MSF) treatment center in Bunia, 90% of admitted patients do not appear on contact-tracing lists, indicating high levels of untracked transmission.

The UN refugee agency (UNHCR) confirmed on Friday that at least five of Ituri province's 69 displacement camps have reported Ebola cases, with 19 internally displaced people infected and five killed. The provinces of Ituri, North Kivu, South Kivu, Haut-Uele and Tshopo, where cases have been confirmed, host around 4.4 million internally displaced people. UNHCR warned that camps often lack water and sanitation infrastructure, and many residents distrust health services, creating conditions that allow Ebola to spread. No cases have been reported among refugees or asylum-seekers, but UNHCR said that could threaten transmission beyond the DRC.

In response, Africa CDC announced plans to scale up every aspect of the response, including shifting from contact tracing to active case search, with community health workers going door to door looking for patients. Officials also plan to begin using the antiviral drug remdesivir on a compassionate-use basis, after neighboring Uganda reported success treating its limited number of cases with remdesivir, recording 20 cases with only two deaths. Additionally, Africa CDC plans to start dispensing an existing Ebola vaccine for the Zaire strain, after limited testing showed that while people vaccinated against the Zaire strain experienced some symptoms, there were zero deaths among those vaccinated. Dr. Placide Mbala Kingebeni, Africa CDC director of research, said any initial rollout would be a relatively small trial. 'Seeing the expansion of this outbreak, only the public health measure will not be enough to quickly control or stop this outbreak,' he said.

The outbreak is centered on the conflict-affected mining province of Ituri, but has spread to Nord-Kivu, Sud-Kivu, Haut-Uele and Tshopo. Cases have been logged in 51 health zones, up from nine in May. Kaseya said contact tracing is insufficient, with only 10 contacts identified for every Ebola patient, when about 40 would be expected. He said a point has been reached where anybody in the hardest-hit city of Bunia or the worst-affected region of Ituri should be considered potential contacts. Meanwhile, authorities near the capital Kinshasa continued screening more than 300 people traveling on a river boat after the death of a passenger with Ebola-like symptoms. Kaseya said the boat came from Mongala province, where no Ebola cases have been reported, but authorities decided to screen all passengers to minimize risk. The WHO's Tedros, who visited Kinshasa, tweeted that 'we all agree that we must urgently and massively scale up all our efforts across all pillars of the response and by all partners.'
