FDA rejects Summit Therapeutics' lung cancer drug ivonescimab after trial misses survival endpoint

Health & Science desk · anchored on Reuters

The U.S. Food and Drug Administration has rejected a treatment for a type of lung cancer developed by U.S.-based Summit Therapeutics and Chinese drugmaker Akeso, the companies announced Sunday. The drug, ivonescimab, was intended for patients with pretreated EGFR-positive non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). In a complete response letter (CRL), the FDA said the drug could not be approved because it did not help patients live longer, despite evidence that it slowed the worsening of the cancer.

FDA reviewers concluded that the absence of a survival benefit “does not support the positive benefit-risk profile in support of approval.” The decision was a regulatory setback for Summit, which oversees development of ivonescimab outside of China, though the outcome was not entirely unforeseen by industry observers.

The rejection follows a clinical trial that missed a key survival goal. While the drug showed an ability to delay disease progression, the agency's action applies specifically to the proposed use of ivonescimab in combination with chemotherapy for previously treated EGFR-mutated NSCLC.
