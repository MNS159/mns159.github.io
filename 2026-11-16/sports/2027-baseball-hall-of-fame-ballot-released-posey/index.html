2027 Baseball Hall of Fame ballot released; Posey, Lester among newcomers

Sports desk

The Baseball Writers' Association of America released the 2027 Hall of Fame ballot on Monday, featuring 13 holdovers and 14 newcomers, including former San Francisco Giants catcher Buster Posey and left-handed pitcher Jon Lester. The full ballot, in alphabetical order, consists of Bobby Abreu, Jake Arrieta, Jay Bruce, Mark Buehrle, Asdrúbal Cabrera, Starlin Castro, Wade Davis, Brett Gardner, Cole Hamels, Félix Hernández, Torii Hunter, Jon Lester, Andrew Miller, Dustin Pedroia, Andy Pettitte, Buster Posey, Alex Rodriguez, Francisco Rodríguez, Jimmy Rollins, Pablo Sandoval, Ervin Santana, Kyle Seager, Joakim Soria, Chase Utley, Omar Vizquel, David Wright and Ryan Zimmerman. Newcomers to the ballot are in bold.

Players must receive votes on at least 75% of ballots cast to be inducted into the Hall of Fame with the 2027 class. Those who receive at least 5% of the vote can remain on the ballot the following year, for a maximum of 10 voting cycles. Players who fall below 5% or exhaust their 10 years are removed from the BBWAA ballot, though they may still be considered by Era Committees. The results will be announced on Jan. 19, with induction ceremonies scheduled for the summer in Cooperstown.

Buster Posey, who retired with a .302 batting average and 1,500 career hits, is widely considered a likely first-ballot inductee. He was the National League MVP in 2012 and the backbone of the Giants' three World Series championships in 2010, 2012 and 2014. Posey started 56 postseason games behind the plate, and 23.2% of those games were shutout victories; his 13 postseason shutouts are the most by a catcher in MLB history. He retired at age 34 after a resurgent 2021 season in which his .889 OPS helped lead the Giants to a franchise-record 107 wins. If elected, Posey would have the fewest career hits (1,500) of any position player elected by the writers since Ralph Kiner (1,451) in 1975, and would be the first player elected by the writers with fewer than 6,000 plate appearances since Jackie Robinson in 1962.

Among other newcomers, Jon Lester, who helped pitch the Boston Red Sox and Chicago Cubs to World Series titles, is seen as having a strong case but faces an uphill battle given how similar starting pitchers have been treated in recent voting. Brett Gardner, a stabilizing presence on 10 Yankees postseason teams, is also on the ballot for the first time. Other first-time candidates such as Jake Arrieta, Andrew Miller, Ryan Zimmerman and Kyle Seager are expected to fall short of the 5% threshold and be one-and-done on the ballot.

Returning candidates include Chase Utley, who has steadily increased his vote share from 28.8% in his first year to 59.1% last year, and is positioned to potentially gain induction or come close. Félix Hernández, the 2010 AL Cy Young Award winner, more than doubled his support from 20.6% in his first ballot to 46.1% last year. Andy Pettitte, on the ballot for the ninth time, received 48.5% last cycle. Alex Rodriguez, in his sixth year, rose to 40% last year, but his ties to PEDs continue to hinder his candidacy. Omar Vizquel is on his 10th and final ballot, having received 18.4% last year. Other holdovers include Bobby Abreu (30.8%), Jimmy Rollins (25.4%), Dustin Pedroia (20.7%), David Wright (14.8%) and Torii Hunter (8.7%).

In addition to the BBWAA vote, a separate Contemporary Baseball Committee will vote on Dec. 6 on a non-player ballot featuring managers, umpires and executives whose most significant impact came since 1980. The candidates are Sandy Alderson, Dusty Baker, Bruce Bochy, Joe Maddon, Lou Piniella, Brian Sabean, Ted Turner and Bill White. The 2027 Hall of Fame class could include both Posey and Utley, according to one prediction, with the committee possibly adding Bochy, Baker or Sabean.
