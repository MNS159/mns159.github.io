Five EU states plan migrant return hub in Rwanda from 2027

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts slightly center-left

Five European Union countries — Germany, Austria, the Netherlands, Denmark and Greece — announced on Monday a plan to open a migrant return hub in Rwanda next year, aiming to send people with no legal right to stay in Europe to the East African nation. The pilot scheme, expected to host about 100 people initially, will apply only to single men who have exhausted all legal avenues to remain, the Dutch government said. Each of the five nations may send roughly 20 migrants, diplomatic sources told the AFP news agency.

Under the plan, deportees will be able to either integrate into Rwandan society — gaining rights of residence, education, healthcare and labour market access — or voluntarily return to their country of origin, according to statements from the participating governments and Rwanda's interior minister, Vincent Biruta. The facility will be an open centre, not intended for long-term housing, and will not apply to people pursuing asylum claims, unlike a scrapped 2022 British deal that sought to send asylum seekers to Rwanda for processing.

The scheme is the first to use a recent EU reform allowing the creation of return hubs outside the bloc. In a joint press conference in Vienna, Austrian Chancellor Christian Stocker said the five countries wanted to relieve European asylum systems and stop traffickers arranging deadly Mediterranean crossings. Danish Prime Minister Mette Frederiksen called it a "game changer" that would "break down the business model of human smugglers." German Interior Minister Alexander Dobrindt said return hubs offer "a positive approach for people without the right to stay" whose home countries do not cooperate in repatriation.

Human rights groups strongly condemned the plan. In a joint statement, 47 aid, trade union and rights organisations urged the five countries to abandon it, warning it would leave vulnerable people at risk of serious human rights violations. Human Rights Watch senior Africa researcher Clementine de Montjoye said Rwanda has a "well-established record of repression of dissent, arbitrary detention, ill-treatment in detention" and support for armed rebels in neighbouring Democratic Republic of Congo. The independent EU Agency for Fundamental Rights said any host country must respect fundamental rights and that unaccompanied children should not be sent to a return hub, a condition the Dutch government confirmed.

The United Nations' International Organisation for Migration (IOM) said it was in discussions with some EU countries on plans for return hubs. The UK government, which scrapped its own Rwanda deal in 2024, said it would watch the scheme "with interest." According to the European Border and Coast Guard Agency, irregular border crossings into the EU fell 29% in the first 10 months of 2026 compared with the previous year, though arrivals on the Western African route to the Canary Islands rose sharply in October. The five countries said concrete negotiations with Rwanda will begin for implementation, with the hub expected to be ready to accept transfers in 2027.
