Nvidia leadership crisis and earnings loom as AI stocks slide

Financial & Markets desk · anchored on AP, Reuters

Nvidia faces a pivotal moment next Tuesday when interim CEO and CFO Colette Kress delivers the company's third-quarter earnings report, the first major test of investor confidence since founder Jensen Huang resigned Thursday amid a widening scandal over alleged chip smuggling to China. The company's stock has posted its longest losing streak since 2012, closing the week at $162.85 per share, down from a $350 price target maintained by Cantor Fitzgerald as recently as early October. Yorkville Ives lowered its own price target to $225 from $300 after Huang's exit but maintained a buy rating, with partner Dan Ives arguing Nvidia remains "the clear leader in GPU chips" with a strategic roadmap already built around its next chip families and physical AI.

The leadership crisis has reshuffled the AI trade, with the tech-heavy Nasdaq posting its best week since August. AMD finished the week up nearly 13%, and Broadcom posted similar gains. Roundhill Investments CEO Dave Mazza said on CNBC's "ETF Edge" that other AI players are positioned to fill Nvidia's chip demand if investors lose trust in the company. "If we get into an environment where people can't trust … Nvidia, they're going to look for other sources for these chips because they need them," Mazza said. His firm's Magnificent Seven ETF (MAGS) ended the week with a gain of less than 1%, while the Roundhill Neocloud ETF (NCLD) was up over 1%, though it remains deeply in the red, down 39% since its August 2026 launch.

The turmoil comes as Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick faces questions over his ties to Nvidia and his former firm, Cantor Fitzgerald, which is now run by his sons Brandon and Kyle Lutnick. Cantor hosted Huang and other Nvidia executives for investor meetings in late September, and its analysts wrote on Oct. 3 that they continued to view Nvidia as "the best risk/reward in our coverage." Yet between September 2025 and March 2026, the number of Nvidia shares Cantor reported holding fell by 49%, from 3 million to 1.78 million, with their value dropping from $652 million to $310 million. The decline coincided with White House and intelligence officials pressing Lutnick to crack down on Megaspeed, a Singapore-based company suspected of funneling Nvidia chips to China — pressure Lutnick repeatedly resisted, according to Axios.

A White House official said President Trump maintains "complete confidence" in Lutnick, but cracks in administration unity emerged Friday when Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent told CNBC's "Squawk Box" that the departments of Treasury, Defense and State and the Office of the U.S. Trade Representative all favored putting Megaspeed on the entity list for months, adding, "You will have to ask [Commerce] about their internal processes." Richard Painter, former chief White House ethics lawyer for President George W. Bush, said that while there is no proof Lutnick shared nonpublic information with his sons, "the appearance doesn't look great." Cantor said its Nvidia positions reflected in securities filings were entered into in the ordinary course of business in connection with client swap transactions and that it has had only "immaterial stand-alone proprietary positions" in the company.

The broader AI investment landscape is also being shaped by the Democratic Party's Nov. 3 election gains, which gave the party control of Congress. Ives estimates the "blue tidal wave" could mean 10% to 20% of planned data centers won't materialize, but he argues demand still outstrips supply four to one, making existing compute capacity more valuable. "The game is going to change for Big Tech. It will slow it down. But there's no overbuild, no bubble concerns. And I view that as actually a positive thing," Ives said. Mazza noted that hyperscalers may continue to benefit because they have the compute available, and it becomes more valuable if demand persists. Nvidia's earnings report Tuesday will be the first opportunity for Kress to reassure the market, with the company stating, "Our focus remains on serving our customers, partners, developers and shareholders."
