Fed's Warsh Warns AI Boom Could Disappoint, Task Force Weighs Risks

Financial & Markets desk · anchored on Reuters

Federal Reserve Chairman Kevin Warsh on Monday highlighted the potential economic benefits of artificial intelligence while warning that the emerging technology also could lead to widespread job losses or fail to deliver on its lofty expectations. Speaking in San Francisco before a blue-ribbon task force he assembled to examine productivity and employment, the central bank leader stressed that AI 'will have significant impact on the economy, with important implications for monetary policy down the road.' The conference, hosted by the Federal Reserve Bank of San Francisco, was part of a task force Warsh set up to evaluate AI and its impact on the economy's efficiency.

In his prepared remarks, Warsh used the term 'super intelligence' instead of artificial intelligence, following an executive order by President Donald Trump requiring federal agencies to use that term. He described it as 'an entirely new force in economic life and society' that has already begun to transform economic activity. Warsh noted that AI could lead to productivity gains that could accelerate real economic growth beyond its current range while keeping employment near full capacity. On the downside, he said 'less positive' outcomes include job displacement through automation that could raise unemployment.

At the same time, he outlined a third scenario where AI could simply 'disappoint.' 'After significant capital investment and stock market gains, expected returns could turn down and equity valuations could fall, leading to an economic slowdown and a more notable rise in unemployment,' he said. 'In time, the economy would recover, but without the productivity gains.' Warsh added that Fed staff has begun evaluating different scenarios for how AI's impact may play out. The potential for AI advancement has driven stock market valuations higher and led to companies raising capital to continue competing.

Warsh did not address AI's impact on inflation, a top concern of many of his Fed colleagues. Last week, several policymakers expressed concern that AI is fanning inflation because the buildout of AI infrastructure has sharply raised demand for computer chips, electronic components, electricity and copper. Those increases, in turn, have lifted the cost of smartphones, computers and other consumer electronics. Smartphone prices rose last month at the fastest pace on record, the government said last week. In addition, Fed Vice Chair Philip Jefferson said Friday that if AI does accelerate economic growth, it could lead to higher interest rates over time, even if inflation declines.

Monday's conference featured discussion panels with business leaders, economists and academics. The agenda included panels on the pace and scope of SI adoption, how SI will reshape the economy, and what happens when businesses adopt SI. The final session was a fireside chat that included Warsh and task force leader and AI advocate Marc Andreessen, co-founder of Andreessen Horowitz, along with Gina Raimondo, co-chair and CEO of RAISE US and former U.S. Secretary of Commerce. Warsh created four other task forces addressing topics such as Fed communication, data reliance, balance sheet policy and inflation frameworks. The chairman noted that this was the first visit he has made to a regional bank.
