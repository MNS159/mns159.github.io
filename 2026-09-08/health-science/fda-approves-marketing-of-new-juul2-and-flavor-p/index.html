Reynolds starts selling fruit-flavored Vuse Pro vapes without FDA authorization

Health & Science desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

Reynolds American has begun selling four new fruit-flavored Vuse Pro vapes — peach, berry, watermelon, and fresh mint — at a GetGo gas station in suburban Ohio and in select other states, even though the products have not been authorized by the Food and Drug Administration. The company's move comes after the FDA softened its enforcement guidance in May, a change that critics say effectively invites manufacturers to put unauthorized e-cigarettes on the market.

Under U.S. law, new nicotine and tobacco products cannot be sold until authorized by the FDA, and only 48 e-cigarettes have met that standard so far. The agency issued its first authorization for fruit-flavored vapes in May, saying it was confident that age-gating technology from the company Glas would reduce risks to youth. Under the FDA's new enforcement policy, the agency says it will not take action against unauthorized fruit-flavored vapes if manufacturers have pending applications that the FDA has accepted and have provided sufficient data on whether the product is 'appropriate for the protection of public health' regarding youth and nonsmokers.

The decision prompted sharp criticism from public health experts. Mitch Zeller, a former head of the FDA's Center for Tobacco Products, said the guidance is wrongheaded 'both as a public health matter, but also as a legal matter.' Cristine Delnevo, director of the Rutgers Institute for Nicotine and Tobacco Studies, warned that if major manufacturers conclude that compliance offers little advantage, it 'could encourage broader noncompliance across the marketplace.' Tobacco giant Altria sued the FDA last week over the timeliness of its review process.

Reynolds American, owned by British American Tobacco, highlighted the planned rollout of the Vuse fruit flavors in its midyear results presentation; a slide notes that 'Regulatory and enforcement actions support U.S. New Category growth.' Company spokesperson Luis Pinto said the sale of the flavored pods is 'consistent with applicable state regulations' and is paired with enhanced youth access prevention measures, including ID requirements and purchase limits. Some states, including California, Massachusetts, New York, and New Jersey, have bans on flavored vapes.

The FDA's May policy was released about a week after Reynolds American donated $5 million to a super PAC backed by President Trump and company executives dined with the president at his golf club, according to The New York Times. During the lunch, Trump reportedly called then-FDA Commissioner Marty Makary, Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr., and CMS Administrator Mehmet Oz about e-cigarette regulation. A coalition including the Campaign for Tobacco-Free Kids and the American Academy of Pediatrics filed a federal lawsuit in July, arguing the enforcement policy violates the 2009 Tobacco Control Act.

Youth vaping has fallen sharply since 2019, when 20% of middle and high school students reported using e-cigarettes; the most recent government survey put that rate at 5.9%. Among young adults aged 19 to 30, however, vaping reached a record 19% last year, according to a University of Michigan survey. Supporters of flavored vapes say they help adult smokers quit, while Delnevo noted that 'the question is whether a specific product, marketed in a specific way, produces a net public health benefit.' Zeller said the FDA's current approach will create confusion for smokers trying to choose among authorized products, illicit disposables like Geek Bar, and now unauthorized Reynolds vapes, and warned that more manufacturers may follow suit: 'Why wouldn't they?'
