AstraZeneca COPD drug tozorakimab reduces exacerbations by about 30% in late-stage trials

Health & Science desk

AstraZeneca’s experimental COPD treatment, tozorakimab, reduced dangerous flare-ups by about 30% across all patients in two Phase 3 trials, positioning it as a potential new option for the millions of people with the debilitating lung condition, according to data released Tuesday and presented at the European Respiratory Society’s annual conference in Barcelona. The results were also published in the New England Journal of Medicine. The drug, an injectable monoclonal antibody, is the first biologic to show efficacy in treating COPD symptoms, according to Dr. Frank Sciurba of the University of Pittsburgh, speaking in an AstraZeneca press release.

In the trials, patients already taking standard-of-care inhaler regimens were randomized to receive either 300 milligrams of tozorakimab once every four weeks via injection or a placebo. The drug reduced moderate and severe COPD exacerbations — sudden worsening of the disease requiring medical care, such as severe cough or breathlessness — by 29% to 34% in former smokers and 29% to 30% among current and former smokers combined. Aside from injection-site reactions, tozorakimab was well-tolerated without significant side effects, AstraZeneca reported.

Tozorakimab is a first-in-class monoclonal antibody that binds interleukin-33, a protein released by damaged cells that triggers inflammation in conditions like COPD. The trials included participants not eligible for other recently approved COPD treatments, which could give AstraZeneca a large segment of the market if the drug is approved. Analysts and the company have projected that “tozo” could reap billions in annual sales, both in COPD — the third-leading cause of death globally — and in other conditions including asthma.

COPD, a group of lung diseases that worsen over time, affects nearly 16 million U.S. adults and is one of the top 10 leading causes of death in the country, according to the CDC. The most common types are emphysema and bronchitis. Tobacco smoke is the main cause, though as many as one in four patients have never smoked. There is no cure; treatment involves lifestyle changes and medications. In 2021, more than 138,825 people died from COPD, per the American Lung Association.

Tozorakimab has been accepted for priority review by the FDA as an add-on maintenance drug for adults with COPD, meaning the agency will expedite its review for potential approval. AstraZeneca is also investigating the drug for asthma and lower respiratory tract disease. “Today's groundbreaking tozorakimab results, from two replicate trials, set a new standard for COPD treatment outcomes in a broad population of patients,” said Sharon Barr, executive vice president of BioPharmaceuticals R&D at AstraZeneca, in a statement. “AstraZeneca has validated the novel approach of targeting the two forms of IL-33 to both decrease inflammation and disrupt the cycle of mucus dysfunction. With our FDA Priority Review, we look forward to bringing this treatment to patients as quickly as possible.”
