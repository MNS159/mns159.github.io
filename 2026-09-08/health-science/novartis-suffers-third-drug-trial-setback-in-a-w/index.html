Novartis shares tumble after third drug trial setback in a week

Health & Science desk

Novartis shares fell as much as 10% in early trading Tuesday after the company announced that its experimental drug del-desiran failed to improve muscle function in a late-stage trial, marking the third clinical setback for the Swiss pharmaceutical giant in a week. The stock was last seen down 8.9%, on track for its worst trading day since March 2020, just days after hitting a record high of CHF132.68.

The Phase 3 HARBOR study tested del-desiran in approximately 150 patients with myotonic dystrophy type 1 (DM1), a rare, progressive neuromuscular disorder that causes muscle stiffness and weakness. Novartis said the drug did not meet its primary endpoint of statistically significant improvement versus placebo on video hand opening time (vHOT), a measure of the hand's ability to relax after squeezing. The company noted there were indications of an effect on other criteria and said it is evaluating the full dataset and will engage with health authorities to determine the most appropriate development path.

The del-desiran failure follows two other setbacks within days. On Monday, Novartis said its cardiovascular drug pelacarsen failed to reduce the risk of major cardiovascular events in a late-stage trial, sending shares down 3.2%. Last week, the company paused eight clinical trials of an experimental cell therapy, rap-cel, after three patient deaths. Del-desiran is one of three antibody oligonucleotide conjugate therapies Novartis acquired through its roughly $12 billion purchase of Avidity Biosciences last year.

Novartis said it still expects sales to grow 5% to 6% annually on average through 2030, but analysts expressed skepticism. Jefferies analysts wrote that the growth target 'will likely be perceived as unattainable without further M&A, which is now questionable again.' Barclays analysts noted that del-desiran and pelacarsen represented about $5 billion in risk-adjusted peak sales opportunities, but more importantly, del-desiran had been a test case for the Avidity deal; its failure casts doubt on the acquisition and Novartis's ability to offset upcoming patent expiries through mergers and acquisitions. Vontobel had estimated peak sales of $3 billion for del-desiran and has removed that forecast from its valuation model.

Novartis did have a positive result last week: a late-stage trial of remibrutinib showed a 'clinically meaningful delay in disability progression' in patients with relapsing multiple sclerosis. However, Jefferies analysts said it will be challenging to gain confidence in that program without del-desiran and another Avidity asset, del-brax, whose Phase 3 data are not expected until 2028. 'As a result, [business development] & M&A likely remain a key part of the story for securing management's longer-term mid-[single-digit] growth aspiration, with management's ability to conduct larger deals now likely to remain under scrutiny,' they wrote.

Shreeram Aradhye, president of development and chief medical officer at Novartis, said in a statement: 'Developing therapies for a complex disease like [myotonic dystrophy type 1] remains challenging, and setbacks are part of scientific progress. As we continue to evaluate the full HARBOR dataset, we remain committed to identifying the most appropriate development path for the del-desiran program and advancing innovative approaches for people living with [myotonic dystrophy] and other serious neuromuscular diseases.' Zurich Cantonal Bank said the setback has led to a loss of confidence in the entire portfolio of drug candidates acquired through the Avidity takeover.
