Totti turns down Italy and Roma roles to join basketball club

Sports desk

Francesco Totti, the 2006 World Cup winner who spent his entire 25-year playing career at Roma, has turned down positions with the Italian national team and his former club to become an adviser to Maxima, a new Roman basketball team. Totti, who will turn 50 on September 27 — the same date Maxima play their first Serie A match — was officially presented on Tuesday, saying he was convinced within 10 minutes of meeting Maxima's owner, California lawyer Paul Matiasic.

Matiasic bought the sporting licence of the Brescia club and used it to set up a new team in Rome. 'He isn't just a figurehead ambassador or celebrity, he's a genuine part of our project,' Matiasic said. 'I'm not joking when I say he's our captain, because he understands Rome better than any of us.' Totti, who played 785 games and scored 307 goals for Roma, leading them to the 2001 Scudetto, said he had been in talks with the new president of the Italian football federation, Giovanni Malago, who had appointed Roberto Mancini as national coach, and had also met with Ryan Friedkin, son of Roma owner Dan Friedkin, about a role at his old club.

"I've had opportunities in the world of football, but I've never really been convinced. I also met the president Malago, we had a good chat, but Paul, was more convincing, and more direct too," Totti said. 'It's a bit strange to find myself here. I come from a different world, but when I met Paul – whom I didn't know – it took him just 10 minutes to convince me.' He added: 'Why did I choose basketball over football? Because they made me feel valued and part of the project.'

Maxima are one of two clubs in Rome vying to take part in the planned NBA Europe project, which the NBA aims to launch in 2027 with 12 targeted cities: Rome, Milan, Paris, Lyon, London, Berlin, Madrid, Barcelona, Manchester, Munich, Athens and Istanbul. Rome has not had a top-flight basketball team since 2020 but will have two this season. The other Roman club — Roma SPQR, using the licence of the former Cremona team and part-owned by Los Angeles Lakers point guard Luka Doncic — will join Maxima in Serie A.

Matiasic carefully avoided addressing the subject of an NBA franchise on Tuesday, but both Roman clubs have their sights set on one.
