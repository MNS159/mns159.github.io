Ukraine War

Russian strikes kill at least two in Kyiv as three-day truce ends

International & World desk

Russian airstrikes killed at least two people and wounded 10 others in Kyiv early Tuesday, Ukrainian officials said, as a three-day mutual pause in strikes on the Ukrainian and Russian capitals expired. Kyiv authorities said air defenses were activated and residents were urged to seek shelter during the overnight drone and missile attack. Ukraine's air force said it downed 142 drones, 31 of 32 cruise missiles and two ballistic missiles, but did not disclose how many ballistic missiles Russia fired.

The assault damaged civilian infrastructure at 16 locations across at least five Kyiv districts — Darnytskyi, Holosiivskyi, Podilskyi, Shevchenkivskyi and Solomianskyi — and hit residential buildings, including a five-storey apartment block in the Solomianskyi district where residents were trapped and had to be rescued. Vehicles caught fire in the Podilskyi district, where a blaze broke out in garages and cars, and people sheltered in metro stations, a routine precaution during the war. Drones also struck buildings in Boryspil and Fastiv, on the capital's outskirts, according to Ukraine's emergency services.

The pause in capital strikes had been announced by Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky and Russian President Vladimir Putin to facilitate a visit by US President Donald Trump's envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner, their first to Ukraine since Russia's full-scale invasion began in February 2022. Russia's state news agency TASS confirmed the three-day suspension Putin ordered expired on Monday evening local time. After talks in Moscow lasting more than three hours, Kremlin aide Yury Ushakov called the meeting "highly useful," while Witkoff said after seeing Zelensky: "We've had a very meaningful discussion, substantive, important, and we are very encouraged, and we look forward to continuing this as long as it takes." Zelensky said Monday he expected the war to continue into winter, called for a trilateral meeting with Russia and the US, and said officials from Ukraine, Europe and America would meet in the near future. A White House official told Reuters the next steps would be announced in the coming weeks.

Ukrainian Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha condemned the attack. "The moment the US president's peace envoys left, [President Vladimir] Putin launched another massive, horrific attack against Kyiv," he said on social media. "His ballistic missiles speak louder than his words on diplomacy." Kyiv is suffering more daily air raid alerts than at any time since Russia's 2022 invasion, according to BBC Verify analysis.

In Russia, officials reported Ukrainian drone attacks overnight. Regional Governor Roman Busargin said civilian infrastructure was hit in Saratov, nearly 1,000km (621 miles) from Moscow, injuring several people. At least one person was killed in Starodub in the Bryansk region, according to acting Governor Yegor Kovalchuk, and at least two people were killed in the Crimean Peninsula, Moscow-appointed Governor Sergei Aksyonov said. Local officials also reported at least two more deaths in Ukrainian drone attacks in Russia's Kursk and Bryansk regions. Al Jazeera said it could not independently confirm the reports, and Ukraine gave no immediate comment.

In other developments, Britain committed £100 million ($135 million, €116 million) to an air defense package for Ukraine — including Patriot air-defence missiles, large-caliber artillery rounds and drones — announced Tuesday ahead of a Ukraine Defense Contact Group meeting co-chaired by UK Defence Secretary Wes Streeting and German Defense Minister Boris Pistorius. Streeting said: "We will stand shoulder to shoulder with Ukraine until they win. Ukraine's frontline is the frontline for freedom everywhere and our support will not waver." Separately, Ukraine's Prosecutor General Ruslan Kravchenko resigned late Monday after anti-corruption agencies raided his office, denying involvement in allegations that an official in his office led a network protecting fraudulent call centers and laundering money. Poland's Lublin Airport also resumed operations after a temporary closure linked to a military operation responding to the Russian missile strikes; Polish authorities said no airspace violations were recorded.
