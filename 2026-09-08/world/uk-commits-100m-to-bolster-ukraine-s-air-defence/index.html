Ukraine War

UK pledges £100m in Patriot missiles and air defence for Ukraine ahead of winter; Europe plans additional Patriot purchases

International & World desk

The United Kingdom has committed £100 million (€116 million) to bolster Ukraine’s air defences ahead of winter, including the purchase of Patriot missiles through a NATO scheme that allows European allies to buy American weapons. The package, announced by Defence Secretary Wes Streeting, also includes large-calibre artillery rounds and drones. The weapons will be delivered via the Prioritised Ukraine Requirements List (PURL) mechanism before winter, helping Ukraine protect critical national infrastructure, civilians and its armed forces from Russian attacks.

The UK did not specify how many Patriot interceptors the contribution will buy, but the number is likely to be a fraction of the 300 missiles President Volodymyr Zelenskyy has said Ukraine needs, each costing about $3 million (£2.6 million). Ukraine currently lacks enough Patriot interceptors to stop Russian ballistic missile attacks, which have killed civilians and destroyed food and logistics warehouses over the summer. Separately, Europe is also set to purchase Patriot systems from the United States to shield Ukraine this winter, according to Bloomberg.

The announcement follows visits to Kyiv by the UK prime minister and defence secretary, where they heard directly from Zelenskyy about the importance of additional support. The UK has committed to providing over 120,000 drones to Ukraine this year, with 25,000 delivered to date, including interceptors, reconnaissance and attack drones. It recently provided its millionth round of large-calibre artillery ammunition and will deliver tens of thousands more long-range rounds in coming months with funding from Britain, Germany, Denmark, Lithuania, Netherlands, Norway and Sweden. Additionally, the UK agreed to declassify blueprints for British-made components of the SCALP/Storm Shadow missile to enable local assembly lines in Ukraine.

Streeting co-chaired his first meeting of the Ukraine Defence Contact Group, a 50-nation alliance, with German counterpart Boris Pistorius on Tuesday. The virtual meeting, also attended by U.S. Undersecretary for Defence Policy Elbridge Colby, discussed further military aid as the war approaches its fifth anniversary. British Prime Minister spoke to U.S. President Donald Trump about the conflict on Monday, agreeing on the need for a ceasefire to prevent more loss of life. U.S. envoys held talks in Kyiv on Sunday, described as encouraging but with no breakthrough. Zelenskyy said it appeared the war would continue into winter.

Streeting said: ‘Both the prime minister and I heard loud and clear on our first visits to Kyiv the importance of supporting Ukraine through this winter, as Putin prepares to use winter as a weapon by continuing barbaric attacks against civilian and energy targets. We promised to mobilise allies and provide more support wherever we can. Today, we are delivering on that, strengthening Ukraine’s defences and, alongside 50 allies, converting commitment into capability. We will stand shoulder to shoulder with Ukraine until they win. Ukraine’s frontline is the frontline for freedom everywhere and our support will not waver.’
