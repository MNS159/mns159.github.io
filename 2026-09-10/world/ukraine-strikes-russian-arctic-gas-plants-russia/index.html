Ukraine strikes deep into Russia's Arctic gas heartland; Russian attacks kill 7 in Ukraine

International & World desk

Ukrainian drones struck deep into Russia's Arctic north on Wednesday, sparking a fire at an industrial facility in Novy Urengoy, a city in the heart of Russia's most important natural-gas producing region, according to Russian officials and news agencies. Regional Governor Dmitry Artyukhov said Russian air defenses repelled the attack but falling drone debris caused a fire at an industrial site, without identifying the facility. He reported no casualties. Ukraine's Special Operations Forces (SOF) confirmed the strike, calling it the deepest of the war at more than 3,000 kilometers (1,860 miles) from the border, and said it successfully hit two gas plants: Novy Urengoy and Purovsky in the Yamal-Nenets region.

Artem Zhoga, the Kremlin's envoy to the Urals, said it was the first strike ever to hit the Arctic part of the region. The Yamal-Nenets Autonomous Okrug produces about 80% of Russia's natural gas, according to Interfax data. Ukrainian defense manufacturer Fire Point appeared to claim involvement, posting online: 'Guess who covered more than 3,300 km and successfully hit the target.' The attack was part of a broader Ukrainian drone assault that also targeted the Black Sea port of Novorossiysk, where Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said a naval base, oil terminal, military port facilities, and warships were struck. Russian authorities said four people, including a child, were killed and 29 wounded in Novorossiysk.

Russia retaliated with its own long-range attacks across Ukraine. In Mykolaiv, southern Ukraine, a Russian strike killed four people and wounded at least 19, according to acting Governor Heorhii Reshetilov. A drone attack on a shopping center in the northeastern city of Sumy killed three people and wounded at least 14, including three children, said local officials. The Russian Defence Ministry confirmed the Mykolaiv strike but said it targeted a drone warehouse, and also claimed hits on the port of Chornomorsk and two vessels near Odesa. Additionally, Russian drones struck the Starokozache border crossing between Ukraine and Moldova, killing two civilians and wounding three, while a separate attack on Kyiv killed one and wounded 14, according to Reuters.

The escalation came as U.S. President Donald Trump said he spoke with Russian President Vladimir Putin and suggested both sides may be prepared to negotiate. 'We had a great conversation. He is wanting to make a deal,' Trump told reporters on Wednesday, adding that Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy might also be willing. Trump pointed to personal animosity between the two leaders, calling them 'two people that hate each other.' His comments followed a weekend diplomatic push by U.S. envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner, who traveled to Moscow and Kyiv but achieved no breakthrough. Russia resumed major missile and drone attacks on Kyiv shortly after the American delegation left, killing five on Tuesday.

A European intelligence source told Fox News Digital that the simultaneous diplomacy and military escalation reflects a dilemma for Western governments: 'You have to talk, you have to explain' to prevent misunderstandings, but Russian officials can interpret outreach as 'Western weakness.' The source said Putin's emphasis on Russian military progress in Ukraine appeared aimed at projecting strength to Washington. The United Nations reported that Russian attacks killed or wounded more Ukrainian civilians in July than in any month since March 2022, with 437 killed and over 2,600 wounded. More than 16,800 Ukrainian civilians have been killed and over 51,000 wounded since the full-scale invasion began in February 2022. Moscow denies deliberately targeting civilians.

Ukraine's SOF described the Yamal strike as a historic first, saying the targeted area had been considered a 'safe rear zone' for Russia. The facilities process millions of tonnes of condensate per year from gas fields in the region, which Gazprom once exported to Western Europe until relations broke down after the war began. The region's gas resources are estimated at 26.5 trillion cubic metres, enough to cover global demand for more than six years. The attack underscores Ukraine's escalating long-range drone campaign, which Zelenskyy has said will make Russian airspace 'completely unsafe' for the duration of the war.
