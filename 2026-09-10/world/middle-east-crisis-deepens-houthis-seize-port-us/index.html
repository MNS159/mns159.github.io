Middle East Conflict

Houthis seize port, US strikes five Iranian tankers, oil tops $100 as Middle East crisis deepens

International & World desk · anchored on Reuters
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts slightly center-left

The United States destroyed five Iranian oil tankers in the Gulf of Oman and near Iran's Kharg Island on Tuesday, Central Command announced, as oil prices surged past $100 a barrel amid escalating conflict across the Middle East. The strikes bring to eight the number of Iranian crude tankers the US military has hit since Saturday in retaliation for Iranian attacks on US forces. On Monday, Iran retaliated by targeting US troops stationed at an air base in Jordan, using cluster munition ballistic missiles, according to the Institute for the Study of War (ISW).

In Yemen, Houthi forces are testing the limits of Saudi restraint with intensified missile and drone attacks on civilian and economic sites in Abha, Khamis Mushait, Jizan and Najran, injuring dozens of civilians and causing fires and temporary disruption at energy facilities, Al Jazeera reported. The Houthis also continue to threaten maritime traffic in the Red Sea and the Bab al-Mandeb strait, a critical shipping lane. The attacks come as fighting intensifies along Yemen's western coast, where the Houthis are seeking to consolidate military positions overlooking the Red Sea.

The Houthi campaign presents Saudi Arabia with a difficult strategic equation, according to analysts cited by Al Jazeera. Riyadh has spent years trying to exit direct military involvement in Yemen and pursue a diplomatic settlement, but repeated attacks on Saudi territory and energy infrastructure are testing its restraint. Saudi Arabia has strengthened its air and missile defenses since the war's early years, but the Houthi arsenal now includes ballistic missiles, cruise missiles and increasingly sophisticated drones. Even when most incoming weapons are intercepted, relatively inexpensive systems can impose disproportionate defensive costs and create uncertainty around airports, industrial facilities, border communities and energy infrastructure, the report said.

On the economic front, the turmoil has pushed diesel prices in the US state of Idaho to nearly $6 a gallon, with AAA Idaho citing the escalating conflict between the United States and Iran and fighting between the Houthis and Saudi Arabia over the Bab al-Mandeb shipping lane as the main drivers. "As long as those areas are supply chain chokepoints, crude oil prices will be under tremendous pressure," AAA Idaho public affairs director Matthew Conde said. The average price for regular unleaded in Idaho reached $4.76 a gallon, up 14 cents in a week. Nestle's CEO said the Middle East conflict is driving inflation and higher supplier costs, Reuters reported.

Iran has launched a concerted effort to more aggressively target US naval forces in recent days, likely seeking to degrade the United States' ability to enforce a naval blockade on Iranian ports, according to ISW analysis. Since September 4, Iran has fired ballistic missiles at a US aircraft carrier, Navy destroyers and at least one Marine vessel, with US officials describing some attacks as "close calls." US officials warned that Iran may be using "more sophisticated weapons," possibly the solid-fueled Qassem Basir medium-range ballistic missile with a maneuverable reentry vehicle, which can help evade air defenses. US officials also raised concerns that Russian or Chinese targeting assistance—including intelligence and satellite imagery—may have enabled Iran's recent attacks.

Iran appears to be targeting stationary commercial vessels in the Persian Gulf and Gulf of Oman to deter further US strikes on Iranian oil tankers, ISW reported. The IRGC Navy issued an evacuation warning to tanker crews near Kuwaiti and Bahraini ports hosting US forces and threatened to attack crews "at anchor or at the ports." Iran is also framing its retaliation against vessels that do not comply with Iranian policies in the Strait of Hormuz as enforcement of its own "sanctions regime" to legitimize illegal activities against commercial shipping, ISW noted.
