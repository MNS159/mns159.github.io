IAEA Board Refers Iran to UN Security Council for Nuclear Non-Compliance in Historic Vote

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

The Board of Governors of the International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA) on Wednesday approved a resolution accusing Iran of failing to comply with its nuclear nonproliferation obligations and referring the matter to the United Nations Security Council (UNSC). The vote, the first of its kind in 20 years, saw at least 23 of the board's 35 member countries in favor; Russia, China and Niger opposed the measure. The resolution was sponsored by the United States, the United Kingdom, France and Germany.

The text expresses 'grave concern' over Iran's 'continued non-compliance' with its commitments under the Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty (NPT), which forbids Iran from producing nuclear weapons and requires IAEA safeguards verification. The IAEA has not had access to key nuclear facilities—including Fordow, Natanz and Isfahan—since the US and Israel launched a 12-day war against Iran in June 2025. The agency estimates Iran holds about 400 kilograms (882 pounds) of highly enriched uranium, potentially enough to manufacture 10 nuclear weapons if enriched further. IAEA Director General Rafael Grossi told the board on Monday that the lack of information and inability to conduct verification at these facilities is 'a matter of serious proliferation concern.'

Iran condemned the resolution as politically motivated and baseless. 'This resolution was passed under political pressure from the US and its allies and in our view has no basis,' said Reza Najafi, Iran's representative to the UN in Vienna. He argued that Iran has been unable to provide inspectors safe access due to ongoing military operations and persistent threats. Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi accused the sponsors of a pretext: 'They attack Iran's safeguarded nuclear facilities, disrupt the normal verification process, and then use that very disruption as a pretext to pass a resolution.' Iran's UN mission said the situation was caused by 'the criminal acts of aggression by the U.S. and Israeli regime.'

The diplomatic escalation comes more than six months into the US-Israeli war against Iran, amid heightened tensions in the Strait of Hormuz, where the US and Iran have traded attacks on vessels. The IAEA's referral to the UNSC is unlikely to result in further action, as Russia and China—both veto-holding permanent members—are expected to block any Western-backed measures. Ali Vaez of the International Crisis Group called the resolution 'an exercise in futility.' The EU, in a statement, accused Iran of suspending cooperation and urged it to provide updated declarations and allow inspections to resume.

On the same day as the vote, US President Donald Trump renewed threats to target Iran's 'Pickaxe Mountain' underground complexes near the damaged Natanz facility, claiming the US had observed 'a little activity' there. Trump reiterated his assertion that Iran seeks a nuclear weapon, despite the IAEA having found no proof of a systematic effort to build one. Grossi told CNN, 'We did not have any proof of a systematic effort to move into a nuclear weapon.' The US launched its current military campaign saying Iran was on the verge of making a bomb—a claim not backed by the IAEA or US intelligence.

The IAEA board's previous criticism of Iran dates to the early 2000s, leading to UNSC sanctions in 2006. Iran remained in compliance with the 2015 Joint Comprehensive Plan of Action (JCPOA) until the US withdrew in 2018 and reimposed sanctions, prompting Tehran to intensify its nuclear program. In June 2025, the board declared Iran non-compliant shortly before the US-Israeli attacks. Tehran insists on its right to pursue civilian nuclear technology under the NPT and says it will cooperate with the IAEA only 'to the extent that circumstances and safety permit.'
