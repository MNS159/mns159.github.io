Russian jet-powered drones escalate Ukraine war, delay Zelensky flight

International & World desk

Russia fired its new jet-powered drones against Ukraine every day throughout August, BBC Verify analysis of Ukrainian Air Force data has found, escalating the use of a weapon that Kyiv's air defences are struggling to intercept. The upgraded munitions can cruise at more than 300mph — significantly faster than older models and beyond the top speed of Ukraine's interceptors. Ukrainian military intelligence says some of the turbojet engines powering the drones are sourced from China. The growing use of jet-propelled drones prompted President Volodymyr Zelensky to condemn what he called an unfolding 'horror' over Kyiv's skies.

Ukraine's military reported that jet-powered drone attacks grew from 350 in June to 1,600 in July and 2,800 in August. In five of the nightly attacks in August, these drones outnumbered the older, slower models. 'The reason for turning to jet-powered drones is that they are much faster than the previous generation and it makes it much more difficult for Ukraine to intercept them,' said Dr Marina Miron, an expert on military technology at King's College London. The speed has prompted a new arms race: on 10 August, Ukraine’s deputy head of military intelligence, Maj Gen Vadym Skibitskyi, said his forces needed new interceptor missiles 'capable of matching [jet-drones'] speed'.

On Wednesday, a Russian drone incursion delayed Zelensky's flight from Chisinau, Moldova, where he was preparing to travel to Oslo for the funeral of King Harald V. Moldovan government spokesman Dumitru Ciorici told the BBC that Moldova closed its airspace after noticing a Russian drone had entered from Ukraine. Zelensky’s plane was left on the runway for about an hour before takeoff was authorised once the drone had come down and exploded some 160 km from the airport. Norwegian Prime Minister Jonas Gahr Støre initially said the flight was 'almost hit' by a drone, but a Ukraine source told AFP the risk had been 'exaggerated'. The Kremlin said there had been no threat to the jet.

The escalation comes as NATO 'military aviation' assets were deployed in Polish airspace overnight in response to Russian jet-powered drone attacks on targets in western Ukraine. Poland’s Armed Forces Operational Command said the operations were preventive and aimed at ensuring the safety of Polish airspace. Ukraine’s air force said Russia launched 149 drones into the country overnight, around half of which were jet-powered, along with decoy drones. Ukrainian defenses downed 128 drones, with impacts reported across 13 locations. In Kyiv, a drone hit a gas station, injuring three people; in Sumy, two people were killed and 23 injured in a strike on a shopping mall. Russia’s Defense Ministry said it also targeted two dry cargo ships heading to Odesa and claimed to have downed 448 Ukrainian drones overnight across 13 Russian regions.

Earlier this year Ukraine was intercepting 94% of long-range drones, according to Zelensky, but the newer models have reduced effectiveness. Ukraine's Air Force data showed a drop in interceptions of ballistic missiles up to the end of July, and in August Kyiv mostly stopped publishing interception figures. Four Ukrainian companies have reportedly started testing missiles capable of shooting down the new Geran models, and the new defence minister, Yevhenii Khmara, has tasked them with producing 'thousands of units' as quickly as possible. Meanwhile, satellite images appear to show Russia building a new drone launch base at Tsymbulova in the Oryol region, around 109 miles from the Ukrainian border, which has become a key centre for drone attacks since becoming operational.

The proliferation of jet-powered drones comes as BBC Verify found Russia is now striking Ukraine with complex attacks involving hundreds of drones and missiles about once a week. Analysts say the increase is driven by Moscow's shift to combining a greater variety of munitions with expensive ballistic missiles that are extremely difficult to intercept. 'Long-range one-way attack drones are basically a poor man's cruise missile,' said John Hardie of the Foundation for Defense of Democracies, noting that the line between drones and cruise missiles has become 'blurred'. However, Conflict Armament Research emphasised that jet-drones have shorter range, smaller warheads, and are more susceptible to jamming than cruise missiles. Ukraine has also struck back: its Special Operations Forces announced a 'record hit' on a Russian gas processing plant in the Yamalo-Nenets district, more than 3,000 km into Russia.
