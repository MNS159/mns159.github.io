Five dead, 86 missing after ferry fire off Philippines

International & World desk

At least five people have died and 86 remain missing after a fire broke out on a passenger ferry off the coast of the Philippine island of Palawan on Wednesday evening, the country's coastguard said. The MV June Aster was carrying 117 passengers and 17 crew members from Manila to the tourist destination of Coron when the blaze was reported at 18:45 local time (10:45 GMT), about 1.2 nautical miles (2.2 km) from Coron's Marcilla village. Forty-three people have been rescued, including two Chilean nationals.

Survivor Roland Lerma, a construction worker from Leyte province, told the BBC that as the fire spread he slipped and was trampled by other passengers before he crawled, found his footing, and leapt into the sea. 'When I hit the water, I ditched all my possessions … so that I could float,' he said. Lerma floated on his back for about two hours before rescuers arrived. 'If the rescue had been delayed, I would definitely have died last night,' he said. A first responder described the scene as 'really heartbreaking'.

The coastguard said preliminary interviews with survivors suggest the fire started in the cargo hold after a loud explosion. The ship was carrying several motorcycles, an electric car, a forklift, a pick-up van and other goods. The blaze was still burning as of 11:00 on Thursday, and thick smoke, strong currents, extreme heat and the risk of explosions hampered search and rescue efforts. Drone footage released by the coastguard showed smoke still rising from the blackened vessel. The coastguard said it was using drift-modelling tools to identify areas where survivors might have been carried by currents, and air assets were on standby.

Families of the missing expressed anguish. Anelyn Magbanua, whose husband Marlon was on the ferry returning to Coron after visiting their children in Manila, told the BBC she had heard no news. 'I have been crying and praying since last night,' she said. Mae Lorraine Galera-Navoa, her husband Elyzer Navoa and their three-year-old daughter Hiraya Joy were also among the missing, family member Rai Garcia told the BBC. Garcia and his family made a poster appealing for information. Others posted on the Philippine Coast Guard's Facebook page asking for news of loved ones.

Atienza Shipping Lines, which owns the ferry, expressed 'profound sadness' and said it was cooperating fully with authorities. The cause of the fire remains under investigation.

Maritime accidents are common in the Philippines, an archipelago of more than 7,600 islands where inter-island ferries are a cheap and vital mode of transport. Past disasters have been linked to overcrowding, rough weather, poor maintenance and lax enforcement of safety rules. In 2023, a fire on the MV Lady Mary Joy 3 killed 31 people off the country's south-western tip. With 86 still missing, this ferry disaster could prove to be among the worst in the Philippines in recent years.
