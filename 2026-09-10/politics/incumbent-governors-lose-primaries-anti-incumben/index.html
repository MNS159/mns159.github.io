Incumbent governors lose primaries; anti-incumbent anger crosses party lines

Politics desk
✓ Facts
  • Rhode Island Gov. Dan McKee lost the Democratic gubernatorial primary to Helena Foulkes, making him the first sitting U.S. governor to lose a primary since Kansas Gov. Jeff Colyer lost to Kris Kobach in 2018.
  • Foulkes, a former CVS Health executive and current Costco board member, had previously lost to McKee by a few percentage points in the 2022 Democratic primary; the 2026 race was a rematch.
  • The primary campaign centered on McKee's handling of the Washington Bridge, which was closed over structural concerns in December 2023 and remains under reconstruction, versus Foulkes' record at CVS during its handling of opioid prescriptions.
  • Providence Mayor Brett Smiley lost the Democratic primary to State Rep. David Morales, 27, a democratic socialist endorsed by Sen. Bernie Sanders and Rep. Ro Khanna, by a 54%-46% margin.
  • Sen. Jack Reed, 76, won his Democratic primary for a sixth term, defeating progressive challengers Connor Burbridge and Luis Muñoz; Reed's campaign reported about $3.4 million in receipts through Aug. 20 compared with roughly $25,000 for Burbridge.
  • The Republican gubernatorial primary between Elaine Pelino and Aaron Guckian remained too close to call as of early results.
  • Foulkes advances to the November general election against the GOP primary winner and independent candidates Ken Block, Jasjit "Jay" Gotra and Christopher "CD" Reynolds.
  • Morales advances to face Republican nominee Dave Talan, 77, in the November mayoral election; Republicans hold no seats on the Providence City Council and Kamala Harris carried the city by 51 points in 2024.
○ Analysis

Rhode Island's 2026 primary season saw two prominent Democratic incumbents denied renomination, fitting a broader national pattern described by Politico of anti-incumbent sentiment crossing party lines, compounded by redistricting changes in some states. In Rhode Island specifically, a sitting governor and a sitting big-city mayor were both denied renomination by their own party in the same election cycle, while a longtime U.S. senator fended off progressive primary challengers to win renomination. The governor's and mayor's races were shaped by distinct local controversies — a bridge closure and infrastructure management for McKee, housing costs and a vetoed rent-cap measure for Smiley — while Reed's campaign reported a significant fundraising advantage over his primary opponents.

Steelman

Across these races, the common thread is that voters showed a willingness to remove or seriously challenge sitting officeholders this cycle, echoing Politico's observation that anti-incumbent anger has crossed party lines nationally this year.

Implications

With Foulkes now favored in the general election in heavily Democratic Rhode Island, and Morales advancing to face Republican nominee Dave Talan in a city where Republicans hold no council seats and Harris carried by 51 points in 2024, and the GOP gubernatorial primary outcome still pending, attention will shift to whether the anti-incumbent trend seen here recurs in other states' remaining primaries and whether it affects general-election outcomes in November.
