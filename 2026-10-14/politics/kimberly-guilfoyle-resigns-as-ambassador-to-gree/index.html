Kimberly Guilfoyle resigns as ambassador to Greece amid misconduct allegations

Politics desk
✓ Facts
  • White House press secretary Katie Zacharia told reporters Tuesday that the administration had asked Ambassador Kimberly Guilfoyle to resign; Guilfoyle posted her resignation on X about two hours later.
  • Since late September, The Wall Street Journal has reported a series of allegations against Guilfoyle, including that she threatened a host-country official, accepted improper gifts and services, promoted the business interests of a Greek construction and energy company, let its lobbyist into private diplomatic meetings, and asked a Trump donor to pay a $100,000 credit card bill in exchange for promised access to administration officials.
  • The British tabloid Daily Mail separately reported that Guilfoyle had alienated staff at the U.S. Embassy in Athens.
  • Guilfoyle denied all allegations and said she would 'vigorously defend' her name, reputation and integrity; her lawyers, David Schwartz and Jesse Binnall, said the disputed payment was for prior speaking engagements and that her private-plane travel followed State Department guidance.
  • President Trump said he had not personally spoken with Guilfoyle before her resignation and said 'I didn't like what I read'; the AP, citing a senior U.S. official, reported that Trump and Secretary of State Marco Rubio were 'extremely pissed off' about the reports.
  • Rubio, speaking in Athens last week, declined to confirm or deny the allegations and criticized reporters for treating sourced reports as 'gospel.'
  • A second U.S. official said the State Department's inspector general's office was reviewing the allegations, though it was unclear whether a formal investigation had been opened.
  • House Foreign Affairs ranking member Rep. Gregory Meeks, Senate Foreign Relations ranking member Sen. Jeanne Shaheen, and House Oversight ranking member Rep. Robert Garcia, all Democrats, called for an investigation into Guilfoyle's conduct.
○ Analysis

Guilfoyle, a former Fox News host and California prosecutor once married to Gavin Newsom and later engaged to Donald Trump Jr., was serving as Trump's ambassador to Greece, a post of growing strategic importance given Greece's role in energy transit and Eastern Mediterranean security. Her departure follows weeks of reporting on alleged financial and ethical improprieties, capped by the administration itself requesting her resignation rather than her stepping down unprompted. The episode also surfaces a broader structural trend noted by a former State Department union head: the share of U.S. ambassadors who are career Foreign Service officers has fallen from roughly 60% historically to about 5% under Trump, with most posts now going to political allies, donors or supporters rather than career diplomats.

Steelman

Across the reporting, the throughline isn't partisan but structural: a political appointee with no diplomatic background faced detailed, documented allegations of blending personal financial interests with an ambassadorship, prompting even her own administration to seek her removal rather than defend her — a case study in the risks of the sharp rise in non-career ambassadorial appointments.

Implications

The State Department inspector general's review, if formalized, could determine whether Guilfoyle faces further consequences, while Democrats' calls for investigation may keep the matter alive in congressional oversight.
