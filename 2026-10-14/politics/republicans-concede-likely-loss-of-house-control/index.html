Republicans concede likely loss of House control in midterms

Politics desk
✓ Facts
  • Politico reports that top Republicans have privately conceded that House control is "all but gone," even as GOP-aligned groups work to limit their losses.
  • The Democratic-aligned Senate Majority PAC is scaling back ad spending in the Georgia and North Carolina Senate races as Republican spending there slows, with Sen. Jon Ossoff (GA) and former Gov. Roy Cooper (NC) increasingly seen as favored.
  • The GOP's Senate Leadership Fund has increased spending for Sen. Pete Ricketts in Nebraska after previously boosting spending in Kansas and South Carolina.
  • The Congressional Leadership Fund, a House GOP leadership-aligned PAC, is redirecting money away from purple-district incumbents Rep. Tom Kean (NJ) and Rep. Mariannette Miller-Meeks (IA) toward GOP incumbents in more Trump-favorable districts, including Reps. Monica De La Cruz (TX) and Rob Wittman (VA).
  • Democrats have pulled back ad spending in a red-leaning Arizona House district, while Republicans continue heavy spending to try to flip the Maine seat being vacated by Rep. Jared Golden.
  • The Guardian identifies competitive Senate races in New Hampshire (John Sununu vs. Chris Pappas), Kansas (Roger Marshall vs. Adam Hamilton), and Minnesota (Michele Tafoya vs. Peggy Flanagan), along with House races in Florida's 13th, Wisconsin's 1st, Alabama's 2nd, New Mexico's 2nd, Texas's 34th, and New Jersey's 7th districts, plus the Nevada governor's race (Joe Lombardo vs. Aaron Ford).
  • Cook Political Report rates the New Hampshire Senate race a tossup.
○ Analysis

The reporting centers on where the two parties are choosing to spend money in the run-up to the midterms. The pattern described here — Democratic money retreating from Georgia and North Carolina as those Senate races tilt toward Democratic candidates, and House GOP money shifting from suburban "purple" districts to safer Trump-won turf — is consistent with Politico's report that senior Republicans no longer expect to hold the House. At the same time, The Guardian's list of lower-profile Senate, House, and gubernatorial races shows that both parties still see a meaningful number of contests as competitive, meaning the overall midterm picture is not settled even if the macro trend favors Democrats.

Steelman

The reporting shows campaign money flowing out of races seen as decided and into a narrower set of contests, including some, like Kansas or Alabama's 2nd district, where the traditional partisan lean doesn't match the apparent competitiveness described by The Guardian.
