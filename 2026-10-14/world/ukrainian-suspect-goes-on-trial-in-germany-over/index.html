Ukrainian suspect goes on trial in Germany over Nord Stream pipeline sabotage

International & World desk

A Ukrainian man, Serhii Kuznetsov, went on trial in Hamburg on Wednesday, accused of playing a leading role in the sabotage of Russia's Nord Stream gas pipelines in 2022. Prosecutors have charged him with a war crime, alleging he ordered a team of divers and an explosives expert to attack civilian infrastructure. Kuznetsov, who appeared relaxed and smiled in court, denies all charges.

Kuznetsov’s defence lawyer, Nicola Canestrini, told the BBC outside the courtroom that after four years of investigation, prosecutors had found no witnesses, forensics, DNA, or fingerprints. The defence also argued that because Kuznetsov, as a soldier, is bound to secrecy, he cannot receive a fair trial — noting that he is unable to disclose his whereabouts or potential alibi witnesses if he was on a secret operation. They further contended that his extradition was flawed because the war crime allegation was not mentioned at the time of his arrest.

The Nord Stream pipelines — three of four gas lines — were destroyed by explosives on 26 September 2022 in the Baltic Sea off the Danish island of Bornholm. German investigators later focused on a yacht called the Andromeda, rented from the port of Rostock, and said they found traces of subsea explosives aboard. Kuznetsov was arrested in August 2025 at a hotel near Rimini, Italy, under a European arrest warrant, and extradited to Germany late last year.

In their indictment, German prosecutors allege the sabotage plot was conceived 'acting by order of Ukrainian state agencies.' Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky has consistently denied any involvement. Oleksandr Merezhko, chair of the Ukrainian parliament’s Foreign Affairs Committee, called the German account 'one of the versions,' adding that Nord Stream 2 was 'nothing but a tool used by Russia' to prepare for war.

The trial is diplomatically sensitive because Germany is one of Ukraine’s closest allies. Stefan Meister of the German Council on Foreign Relations said successive chancellors tried to depoliticise the case by leaving it to the judiciary. A second Ukrainian suspect, Volodymyr Zhuravlov, is also wanted in connection with the sabotage; Croatia approved his extradition last month, though the timing remains unclear. The Hamburg trial continues.
