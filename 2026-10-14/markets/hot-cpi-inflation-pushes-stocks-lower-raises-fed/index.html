Hot CPI inflation pushes stocks lower, raises Fed rate-hike bets

Financial & Markets desk · anchored on Reuters

U.S. stocks fell and Treasury yields jumped Wednesday after the September consumer price index came in hotter than expected, prompting investors to raise bets on further Federal Reserve interest-rate hikes. The broad S&P 500 was down 0.6%, the Nasdaq Composite shed 0.8%, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average fell about 32 points, or 0.1%. The 10-year Treasury yield rose more than 7 basis points to 5.293%, and the 30-year yield climbed over 4 basis points to 5.609%.

The Labor Department's Bureau of Labor Statistics reported that the consumer price index — a broad measure of goods and services costs — rose a seasonally adjusted 0.6% in September, putting the 12-month inflation rate at 3.7%. Economists surveyed by Dow Jones had expected a 0.6% monthly gain and a 3.6% annual rate. Stripping out volatile food and energy costs, the core CPI rose 0.3% on the month and 2.6% year over year, both slightly above the 0.2% and 2.5% economists had forecast. The annual headline rate was 3.4% in August, while the core rate was 2.4%.

Surging energy costs were again the primary driver of inflation. The energy index jumped 5.2%, with gasoline prices soaring 9.1% — accounting for about half of the headline CPI gain, according to the BLS. Over the past year, gasoline has risen 34.2% and fuel oil 74.2%. Food prices were flat on the month and up 2.5% from a year ago, while shelter prices increased 0.4% and used car and truck prices rose 0.7%. Apparel prices increased 0.3% on the month and are up 3.4% annually. The average U.S. gasoline price was $4.34 a gallon, versus $3.08 a year earlier, according to AAA, and U.S. West Texas Intermediate crude futures rose about 1% to around $95 a barrel.

Following the report, futures traders raised the probability of a rate hike at the Federal Open Market Committee's Oct. 27-28 meeting to about 54%, according to the CME Group's FedWatch tool. Odds of a rate increase before year-end rose above 96%, with a near 50-50 chance of a half-percentage-point move by December. The September CPI is the final inflation reading FOMC officials will receive before their next meeting; the BLS will release September producer price index data on Thursday. 'CPI came in hot and it wasn't just fuel prices, as core inflation ran at a 3.7% annualized pace in September and 3.2% over three years,' said Sonu Varghese, chief macro strategist at Carson Group. 'The only surprise is that investors keep getting surprised by broad-based inflation. And we've yet to see the full impact of higher diesel prices and the AI boom.'

The inflation data lands after the Fed in September approved its first interest-rate hike in more than three years, a quarter-point increase that took the benchmark funds rate to a target range of 3.75%-4%. Inflation as measured by the CPI has exceeded the Fed's 2% goal since March 2021, spiking following U.S. and Israeli strikes against Iran. While supply shocks are often viewed as temporary, Fed officials fear the longer the war continues, the greater the chance the price spike becomes embedded in expectations. Prices also face pressure from President Donald Trump's tariffs and demand tied to the artificial-intelligence build-out. Several policymakers have said the Fed need not rush another hike, but most expect one this year, and market pricing implies at least two more increases in 2027. The Fed's preferred inflation gauge — the Commerce Department's personal consumption expenditures price index for September — is due later this month.

In corporate news, Bank of America shares fell 7% after the bank reported third-quarter results that included a more than $1.2 billion federal penalty tied to allegations it failed to report over $5 billion in 'suspicious' transactions and violated anti-money laundering and sanctions laws; the fine reduced third-quarter earnings by 17 cents a share and revenue missed estimates. Morgan Stanley's stock traded nearly 2% lower. Chip stocks extended recent declines, with Micron Technology down 1%, Advanced Micro Devices down 3%, and Intel and Sandisk each losing 2%. The market's reaction suggested businesses face a higher bar during third-quarter earnings season, especially in tech, where semiconductors are expected to be the largest contributor to year-over-year earnings growth. Meanwhile, the Fed's 'beige book' survey of regional economic conditions was expected later Wednesday.
