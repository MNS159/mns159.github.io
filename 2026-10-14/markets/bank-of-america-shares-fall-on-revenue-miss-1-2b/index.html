Bank of America shares plunge 8% on revenue miss and $1.2 billion Treasury penalty

Financial & Markets desk

Bank of America Corp. shares fell 8% in early trading Wednesday after the bank reported third-quarter revenue that missed analyst estimates and disclosed a $1.2 billion penalty from U.S. regulators over failures to report suspicious transactions. The bank posted earnings per share of 91 cents, compared with the 1.10 expected by LSEG, while revenue came in at $30.12 billion against the $30.69 billion consensus. Profit declined 17% to $6.93 billion, with the regulatory fine reducing per-share earnings by 17 cents.

The revenue miss of about $570 million was driven largely by weaker-than-expected results from Wall Street trading operations. Fixed income trading revenue fell 17% to $2.55 billion, approximately $450 million below the StreetAccount estimate, as the bank cited weakness in mortgages, municipals and credit activity amid a sharp rise in interest rates. Equities trading revenue rose 10% to $2.51 billion, but that also fell short of analysts’ forecasts by nearly $300 million. Combined bond and stock trading revenue declined 5%, contrary to CEO Brian Moynihan’s previous guidance that trading revenue would be roughly flat.

Investment banking revenue dropped 11% to $1.8 billion, roughly in line with expectations and Moynihan’s earlier guidance. Net interest income, a closely watched measure of lending profitability, rose 7.9% to $16.6 billion, slightly above the StreetAccount estimate of $16.53 billion. Wells Fargo analyst Mike Mayo described the bank's capital markets results as 'easily worst in class' for the quarter.

The $1.2 billion fine, announced Tuesday, stems from U.S. regulators’ determination that the bank failed to report billions of dollars in suspicious transactions. The penalty raised questions about risk-management controls at the second-largest U.S. bank by assets. On a conference call with analysts, JPMorgan analyst Vivek Juneja noted the sharp share decline and pressed Moynihan on earlier assurances that compliance costs would be contained. 'Clearly you've had a huge charge this morning,' Juneja said. 'It's not that you can't absorb a billion… But obviously it's a reputational and confidence issue.'

CFO Alastair Borthwick attributed the bond trading shortfall to the sharp rise in interest rates during the period, saying, 'It just happens when rates go up 85 basis points in a quarter, that tends not to be good for' credit and mortgage trading businesses. Moynihan, who took the helm in 2010 and spent years resolving mortgage-related abuses from the financial crisis, now faces renewed scrutiny over whether additional risk-management issues remain undiscovered.

The disappointing report adds a sour note to an otherwise routine bank earnings week. On Tuesday, JPMorgan Chase, Goldman Sachs, Citigroup and Wells Fargo posted mixed results, with JPMorgan missing earnings and revenue estimates for the first time in years.
