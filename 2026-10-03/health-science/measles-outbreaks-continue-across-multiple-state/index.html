Measles outbreaks spread in multiple states; Pennsylvania reports fifth death, New York and Ohio see new cases

Health & Science desk

Measles outbreaks continue to expand across multiple states, with Pennsylvania reporting a fifth death from the disease, according to STAT. The full scope of the outbreak in Pennsylvania and nationwide remains unknown because only confirmed cases are reported; as of Wednesday, the state had 943 confirmed cases, about a quarter of the national total for 2026. Approximately 20% of infected people require hospital care, but some cases never come to the attention of public health authorities, particularly in areas with large Amish populations where vaccination rates are low and medical care may not be sought unless illness becomes severe.

In New York, Lewis County Public Health reported Friday evening that 10 more people had been diagnosed with measles, all unvaccinated. Officials said all 10 cases are linked to a case reported the previous Saturday, which was the first measles case recorded in the county since the state began providing county-by-county data in 1994. Sick residents who are in the infectious period are in isolation and will remain so until they are no longer contagious. Health officials said the overall risk to the public remains low for people fully vaccinated or born before 1957, who are presumed to have natural immunity.

In Ohio, Hancock Public Health confirmed one measles case in Findlay, with the infected individual having visited multiple locations while infectious. Potential exposure sites include NWO Urgent Care on September 29 between 2:30 p.m. and 5:30 p.m., and Blanchard Valley Hospital Emergency Department on the same day between 3:30 p.m. and 6:30 p.m. The agency urged anyone who was at those locations during those times to fill out a close contact investigation form and monitor for symptoms for up to 21 days.

Wisconsin has also seen significant spread, with the Milwaukee Journal Sentinel reporting that cases have reached 243 and that the count could be an undercount. Nationwide, the total number of confirmed measles cases stood at 943 in Pennsylvania as of Wednesday, representing about a quarter of the national total for 2026.

Measles is an acute, highly contagious viral disease. Early symptoms typically appear 7 to 14 days after exposure and include high fever (often above 101 degrees F), cough, runny nose, and red, watery eyes. A red, blotchy rash usually appears 3 to 5 days after initial symptoms, starting on the face and spreading downward. The MMR vaccine given within 72 hours of exposure, or immune globulin given within 6 days, can prevent or reduce severity. Unvaccinated individuals, young infants, pregnant women, and immunocompromised people are at highest risk for severe complications.

"It is much worse than is being claimed," Paul Offit, director of the Vaccine Education Center at the Children's Hospital of Philadelphia, told STAT. "I think we don't know how bad." Health officials urge anyone who develops symptoms to call ahead before seeking medical care and to limit contact with others to prevent further spread.
