Congo Ebola outbreak death toll surpasses 4,000 after treatment center burned

Health & Science desk · anchored on AP

Congo’s Ebola outbreak has killed more than 4,000 people since it was declared in eastern Congo on May 15, the Health Ministry announced Thursday, as the country continues to grapple with the fastest-growing Ebola epidemic in history. The latest figures show 4,018 deaths among 8,300 recorded cases. The outbreak is on track to exceed the deadliest on record — West Africa’s 2014-2016 epidemic, which killed 11,000 people — according to the World Health Organization.

Efforts to contain the virus suffered a setback this week when soldiers searching for weapons burned an Ebola-hit displacement camp that housed a transit center for infected patients in Kigonze, on the outskirts of Bunia, forcing 19,000 people to flee. “When we lose a center, that means we lose capacity, we lose investment, and we have to start again,” said Julien Harneis, the U.N. senior Ebola coordinator. “And above all, it means that people do not get access to the care that they really need.” Creating such treatment centers has been a struggle, requiring money and staff that are in short supply.

Meanwhile, some health workers at the outbreak’s epicenter in Ituri Province gathered to demand payment for wages owed them. Protesters, including surveillance team members, held placards reading “No money, no data!” and “No payment for months.” Some workers have not been paid for August and September, said Biongi Beiza, a protester in Bunia. “There is no explanation from the administration,” he said. Front-line workers have gone on strike several times since the outbreak began, further hampering containment.

An aid worker with Médecins Sans Frontières (Doctors Without Borders) who was infected with Ebola while working in Congo arrived in the Netherlands late Thursday on a special medical flight and was transferred to a high-level isolation unit at Leiden University Medical Center. “The human cost of this crisis is unprecedented in the DRC, with communities facing the largest Ebola disease outbreak ever recorded in the country,” the group said. At least 50 health workers have died after contracting the virus.

Local authorities have deployed mobile laboratories to speed detection. Test results that used to take 72 hours can now be obtained in six hours, said Dr. Lota Kalubi at a treatment center in Butembo. Michel Ngimba of the National Institute of Biomedical Research said the labs can be moved quickly to remote or emergency locations, such as Katwa. The number of newly confirmed cases has fallen — from about 850 cases and 470 deaths per week in mid-July to an average of 490 cases and 230 deaths in September — but Africa CDC said the decline may be due to insecurity and challenges in gaining community cooperation. The virus has accelerated in some areas even as it has slowed in others.

Insecurity and community resistance remain major hurdles. A Congolese politician was beaten to death earlier this week in an emerging hot spot after defending government measures to deal with the outbreak. Authorities last month launched a village-centered approach working with local leaders to educate people about the virus, but those efforts face residents who dismiss Ebola as a hoax. Deep distrust of the government — over its past handling of Ebola outbreaks and chronic corruption — further complicates the response.
