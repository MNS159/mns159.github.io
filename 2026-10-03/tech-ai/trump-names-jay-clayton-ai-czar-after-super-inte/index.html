Trump names Jay Clayton AI czar after 'super intelligence' order

Tech / AI desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts slightly center-left

The White House this week signed a new executive order rebranding artificial intelligence as "super intelligence" and secured a "morally binding" safety pledge from major tech CEOs.

The executive order, signed September 29, mandates all government agencies to refer to "AI" and "artificial intelligence" as "SI" and "super intelligence," stating that today's frontier systems "do much more than imitate or automate discrete aspects of human intelligence" and "increasingly represent not merely artificial intelligence but a new super intelligence." The same week, the White House gathered nearly every major tech CEO — including Mark Zuckerberg, Jeff Bezos, Elon Musk, and Anthropic's Dario Amodei — to sign an AI safety pledge that Trump called "morally binding."

The rebranding has triggered a surge in registrations for the .si domain name, which is associated with Slovenia — First Lady Melania Trump's home country. Registry SI, the official organization managing Slovenia's domains, reported a 2,199% increase in .si domain purchases in September, with 11,000 new addresses recorded on September 30 and nearly 13,000 in the past 24 hours. Web hosting platform Hostinger said .si is now its second most popular domain extension, with about 4,300 registered on September 30 and 5,400 on October 1; more than three-quarters of all .si domains registered since September 23 came in those two days, and more than half the buyers were from the U.S. and India.

Despite the surge, only around 3% of .si domains on Hostinger are explicitly related to AI, with most being "unclassified." Klaudijus Januitis, Hostinger's head of domains, said he expects demand to keep growing but does not see .si replacing .ai anytime soon, calling it a "more affordable second option." On Hostinger, .si costs about $12 a year, while .ai costs $90 with a two-year commitment. "Slovenia will not see what Anguilla saw," Januitis said, noting that Anguilla charges a premium for .ai that goes directly to the government, whereas .si fees go to the registry. "For Anguilla, .ai became a second economy. For Slovenia, .si is a nice bonus."

Some founders are already buying .si domains to protect their brands or as speculative investments. Kim Than, CEO of Genius PR, said he bought the .si version of his company name "to protect the brand in case this takes off like .ai," adding that "a few founder friends are buying too 'cause we think it's the new AI boom." He noted that domains can be tokenized and traded on the blockchain, though he is unsure if .si is supported yet. "Who knows how far it goes, but I'm happy to take a bet," he said. Meanwhile, Wix said it has not seen a "meaningful uptick" in .si registrations, while GoDaddy does not yet host the .si domain and Squarespace had not responded as of publication.
