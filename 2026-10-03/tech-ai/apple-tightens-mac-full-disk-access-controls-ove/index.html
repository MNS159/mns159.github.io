Apple tightens Mac full-disk access controls over AI agent risks

Tech / AI desk

Apple announced Friday that it is introducing new controls for macOS's Full Disk Access permission, citing risks posed by increasingly capable and autonomous AI agents. The change follows a controversy in which Inc. columnist Jason Aten reported that Meta's AI agent Muse had accessed the contents of his private Apple Messages without his explicit permission. Apple said the new controls are designed to ensure that users who wish to grant an app Full Disk Access can do so only with "very explicit user action."

Full Disk Access is a macOS system-level permission that gives an app access to a user's entire system, including files, mail, messages, and browsing history. Apple said the feature was originally designed to allow backup apps to function properly, but that it "largely sidesteps" other privacy controls. In a blog post aimed at developers, Apple stated: "Some developers are using Full Disk Access in ways that could put users at risk, exposing everything on their systems…without users' full knowledge and understanding." The company added: "As AI agents become increasingly capable and autonomous, the risks associated with this level of access will grow substantially."

The announcement comes two weeks after Aten wrote that Meta's Muse app sent him an unsolicited notification referencing a thread between him and a co-worker over Apple Messages. Aten said he never granted Muse permission to read his messages and had assumed they were off-limits. Meta CTO David Singleton responded that Muse requires two opt-in privileges to access Messages: macOS system-level Full Disk Access and a Messages connector setting within the app. "The Messages integration in the Muse Mac app is opt in," Singleton said. "Your Muse can only read Messages content if macOS system-level Full Disk Access is granted and the Messages connector is enabled."

Mac security expert Patrick Wardle questioned Singleton's denial, telling Ars Technica: "From a technical point of view, with FDA (full-disk access), any (non-root file), is readable, browsing history, browser cookies, chats, etc etc etc." When asked how Muse could not read messages when it had Full Disk Access while every other app with that privilege could, Meta PR's only response was to requote Singleton's statement. Meta spokesperson Andy Stone also pushed back on Aten's report, saying access to Messages is "entirely opt-in" and requires enabling both Full Disk Access and the Messages connector.

Apple did not specify when the new controls will roll out. The company did not immediately respond to requests for comment from The Verge or TechCrunch. The decision to tighten Full Disk Access also follows a Wired report that a flaw in ChatGPT's Mac app could have allowed hackers to access sensitive data, according to TechCrunch.
