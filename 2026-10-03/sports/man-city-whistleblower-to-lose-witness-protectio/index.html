Man City whistleblower loses witness protection; trail of inflated transfers examined

Sports desk

The Portuguese computer hacker who leaked documents that triggered the Premier League investigation into Manchester City is set to lose his witness protection, despite his fears of assassination. Rui Pinto, founder of Football Leaks, posted on X that the decision to terminate his participation in the programme on 10 October has put him in a 'life-threatening situation,' leaving him no alternative but to relocate to an undisclosed location because of 'multiple death threats' and 'the serious risk of an assassination attempt.' Pinto, 37, was convicted in Portugal of illegal access to data and attempted extortion and given a four-year suspended sentence; he wore a bulletproof vest during his first trial in 2020.

Pinto released a tranche of confidential football documents in 2018, including emails and financial records relating to Manchester City, which were shared with German magazine Der Spiegel and used for investigations into the club's finances and alleged breaches of financial fair play rules. Last week, an independent commission found City guilty of all charges relating to breaches of the Premier League's financial regulations between the 2009-10 and 2017-18 seasons, a verdict the club has appealed. Pinto's lawyers said the end of his witness protection leaves him at heightened risk, noting legal proceedings from the leaks, including the case against City, remain ongoing. Pinto has set up a fundraising page to help him 'survive this serious situation,' which he claims was 'created by the Portuguese State.'

The commission found that City had 'inflated' its accounts by more than £900m during the period under scrutiny. The club spent roughly £1.2bn on players between 2009 and 2018 (about £900m net spend), more than any other club in that time. That outlay was vital to City becoming England's most successful club and would have been heavily reduced had they not knowingly broken the rules, the commission concluded. The money flowed to 46 different clubs across 19 national league systems. Just over a quarter of City's total transfer outlay went to Premier League clubs; they also paid significant sums to clubs in La Liga, the Bundesliga, Ligue 1, and the Portuguese league.

Among the clubs receiving the highest fees from City were Wolfsburg (for Kevin de Bruyne), Monaco (for Benjamin Mendy and Bernardo Silva), and Arsenal (from whom City bought four players, the most of any club). For some clubs, the fees were the highest they had ever received at the time. However, the money did not always work out beneficially: Wolfsburg dropped from second to eighth in the Bundesliga after losing De Bruyne, and Monaco went from winning Ligue 1 and reaching the Champions League semi-finals to nearly being relegated within two years. Sell-on clauses also funnelled money to clubs not directly involved in the transfers, such as Queens Park Rangers earning roughly £9m from Raheem Sterling's move from Liverpool, and Barnsley taking about £7m from the John Stones transfer.

The independent commission's verdict and the club's appeal have drawn attention to the broader impact of City's spending. The Premier League investigation was triggered by Pinto's leaks, and his loss of witness protection adds a new dimension to the ongoing legal and financial fallout. City have confirmed their appeal against the guilty verdict. Pinto has also turned his attention to FIFA president Gianni Infantino, repeating allegations from 2018 that Infantino intervened to help City and Paris St-Germain avoid UEFA financial fair play sanctions. Infantino's position has recently come under increased pressure following controversial plans to sell stakes in FIFA competitions to private investors.
