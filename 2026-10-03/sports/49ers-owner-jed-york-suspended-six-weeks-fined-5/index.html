49ers Owner Jed York Suspended Six Weeks, Fined $500,000 for Prostitution Arrest

Sports desk

The NFL announced Friday that San Francisco 49ers owner Jed York has been suspended from attending six games and fined $500,000 for violating the league’s personal conduct policy, stemming from his arrest in an Ohio prostitution sting in August. The suspension was reported by Mike Garafolo of NFL Network.

York, 46, was arrested in East Palestine, Ohio, on suspicion of engaging in prostitution. Prosecutors later amended the charges to disorderly conduct and possessing criminal tools, to which York pleaded no contest. The league’s statement noted that York has already stayed away from San Francisco’s first three games of the season, which will count toward his suspension. He will be eligible to resume attending games after Tuesday, October 20.

In a statement released Friday, York said: “Today, the NFL concluded its review, and I accept its decision in full. To my family, to the players, coaches, and employees of the San Francisco 49ers, to our fans and partners, I am deeply sorry. This was an inexcusable mistake and I take full responsibility. What I did does not reflect my values or the man I strive to be for my family. While I fell short of the model I want to set for my sons, I now hope to show them the importance of owning up to one’s failures, and that even our worst moments teach us to be better. Leading the 49ers is a privilege, and one I don’t take lightly. Your trust will have to be earned back over time, and I look forward to that work ahead.”

York joined the 49ers in 2005 as director of strategic planning for his family-owned team. He was promoted to vice president of strategic planning before becoming CEO in December 2008, when his parents transitioned to co-chairmen. He currently serves as principal owner and CEO, a role he has held since 2024. During his 17 years as CEO, the 49ers have posted a 147-129-1 record with eight playoff appearances and three Super Bowl appearances.
