Verstappen Takes First 2026 Pole in Bahrain; Red Bull Upgrade Praised

Sports desk

Max Verstappen secured his and Red Bull's first pole position of the 2026 Formula 1 season with a dominant qualifying performance at the Bahrain Grand Prix, held at the Sepang International Circuit in Malaysia. Verstappen, whose last pole and victory came at the 2025 season finale in Abu Dhabi, topped all three qualifying sessions and posted a fastest lap of 1:35.130, 0.298 seconds ahead of Ferrari's Lewis Hamilton. Red Bull's Isack Hadjar qualified third but will start eighth due to a five-place grid penalty for exceeding his permitted number of engine parts, promoting championship leader Kimi Antonelli of Mercedes to third on the grid.

The result marks a significant turnaround for Red Bull, which started the season off the pace—the car was on average more than 0.5 seconds per lap slower than Mercedes in the first half of the year. An aerodynamic upgrade centered on a new floor, introduced at the last race in Azerbaijan, proved transformative at the demanding Sepang track, which features long corners and a rough surface in tropical heat. Verstappen said: 'It's been an incredible result for us. Looking at the season, how it started for us and the progression that we have made. At some tracks we were kind of close, but now I think this weekend it was very positive for us. We have been adding bits to the car and they have worked. It's simple as that.' Team-mate Hadjar, who missed three races after the summer break with a broken wrist sustained while boxing, added: 'I jumped in a car that's way better than when I left it in Budapest. The last two weekends have been surprising.'

Hamilton, starting from his equal best grid position of the year in second, acknowledged the challenge ahead. 'It's so hot here. That's made it a massive challenge for the cars, the tyres, the mechanics in the garage. Because the tyres are sliding, it's so hard to find a good balance and we made one change and we finally hit the sweet spot,' he said. Hamilton added that his mindset for Sunday's race was to 'just try my best to hold on to him tomorrow.' Verstappen has dominated the weekend so far on Formula 1's return to Sepang for the first time since 2017, and the front-row pairing revives memories of their no-holds-barred title battle in 2021.

Mercedes brought their first major aerodynamic upgrade since the Canadian Grand Prix in June, but it has not yet delivered the expected step forward. Antonelli, who under-performed slightly in qualifying due to an unusual tyre strategy and a track-limits violation, said: 'With this new package, obviously the window of the way the car operates is different. So, we're trying to understand in which window the car performance is best. Our high-speed performance has been very strong this weekend, but in the low-speed we lacked.' Team-mate George Russell could manage only eighth fastest, 0.741 seconds off the pace, and said: 'It's the most demanding circuit on the tyres, so we don't know exactly why we didn't have the pace. The others have caught up and overtaken [us].' McLaren, tipped by Russell as potential frontrunners, also struggled; Lando Norris was sixth, 0.627 seconds behind.

The race on Sunday is expected to be dominated by tyre degradation in stifling heat, with the possibility of rain. Antonelli noted that track position and strategy will be critical, saying: 'Tomorrow might be two stops, three stops. We don't know. But the only thing we know is that it's going to be roasting hot out there.' Elsewhere, Fernando Alonso, who recently confirmed he will stay in F1 for at least another year in 2027, gave Aston Martin their best qualifying of the year with 12th place, 1.524 seconds off the pace. Alonso said: 'It was definitely one of the best qualifying of the year. It seems this circuit is a little better for our package and let's see tomorrow if we can take advantage of this.'

Off the track, a separate controversy emerged over driver cooling vests. Mercedes opted not to use the FIA-approved active cooling gear at the Bahrain Grand Prix to stay under the budget cap, and Russell described driving the upgraded W17 in the 60°C Sepang heat as 'sitting in an oven.' Verstappen, who has routinely refused to use the system and previously called the mandatory heat-emergency components 'ridiculous,' dismissed Russell's complaints. 'You shouldn't make a fuss,' Verstappen said, suggesting the issue is one of physical conditioning rather than a financial or engineering problem.
