Flydubai co-pilot used axe in cockpit attack, identified as Hamam al-Hammami

International & World desk

The co-pilot of a Flydubai flight from Dubai to Tel Aviv attacked the captain with a crash axe and tried to take control of the aircraft, the United Arab Emirates prosecutor general said, as the plane carrying more than 170 people plunged 17,000ft (5,200m) two and a half hours into its journey. Passengers overpowered the attacker, and two reserve pilots safely landed the plane in Tabuk, Saudi Arabia. The co-pilot has been identified by multiple media outlets as Hamam al-Hammami, an Omani national, though there has been no official confirmation.

Captain Smit Machchhar, an Indian national, was stabbed in the attack. He later described lying injured on the cockpit floor as the aircraft descended. 'When I was down, I could feel that the aircraft was plummeting, and I knew this is the last chance to do something,' he told Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi. 'I told myself to go for one last push to open the door from inside.' Once the door was opened, passengers rushed to help. An Israeli woman, Tali Manes, had raised the alarm after hearing a commotion from the cockpit. Her husband, Zvika, along with three other men—Yaniv Hayun, Dr Shota Musayev and Asaf Rajuan—entered the cockpit. Hayun described putting the attacker in a chokehold and pulling him out, then pulling the plane's controls. The aircraft had first signalled a general emergency (squawk 7700) before switching to squawk 7500, the code for unlawful interference or hijacking.

Al-Hammami's background has come under scrutiny. According to reports, Omani authorities had previously barred him from flying over concerns about alleged extremist views, and he was removed from flight training at Oman Air before being reassigned. He completed a three-year distance-learning course in aviation management at Buckinghamshire New University in the UK, graduating in 2023; the university said he did not undertake any pilot training there.

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu told Fox News that after being subdued, the attacker 'calling out to the aircrew 'kill me, kill me', so it's clear he was suicidal—that's not a question.' Netanyahu added that the attacker 'underwent Islamist radical indoctrination' and that the stabbed pilot and passengers 'managed to prevent a 9/11 disaster'. The UAE has begun investigating whether the incident was linked to terrorist activity. Machchhar, who was airlifted to Abu Dhabi, is reported to be in stable condition. 'I am not looking at this wound like a wound because that would make my recovery even more difficult,' he said. 'I am confident that the recovery will be there and I will become fit and healthy again.'
