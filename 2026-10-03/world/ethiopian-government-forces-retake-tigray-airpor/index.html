Ethiopian government forces retake Tigray airport as rebels retreat from Mekelle

International & World desk

Ethiopia’s federal army and pro-government fighters have recaptured the main airport in Mekelle, the capital of the northern Tigray region, with Tigrayan rebels seen retreating from the city, multiple unnamed sources told Reuters and AFP on Saturday. The Alula Aba Noga Airport, about 10km (6 miles) east of the city centre, was one of three airfields seized by rebel forces at the start of the fighting on September 23. A local journalist told AFP that TPLF militants and leaders were leaving Mekelle, though federal forces had not yet been seen inside the city. Residents expressed fears of looting and lawlessness, with one saying, 'I am worried that lawlessness could spread in the city, and I am also afraid for my life.'

The fighting, which erupted last week between Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed’s government and the Tigray People’s Liberation Front (TPLF) and its allies, has shattered the fragile Pretoria Agreement peace deal signed in November 2022. The conflict has spread across multiple fronts in Tigray, Amhara and Afar regions. In Amhara’s North Wollo area, the Ethiopian military reported about 270 Tigrayan fighters killed and more than 200 wounded. The Tigray Peace Force, an armed group aligned with the federal government, captured the town of Alamata near Tigray’s southern border, a key supply hub. Fighting in Afar threatens supply routes from Djibouti, through which most Ethiopian imports pass. Outside the north, an explosion was reported early Thursday at the Ministry of Defence in Addis Ababa, marking an apparent spillover into the capital.

The humanitarian toll is mounting. At least 52 civilians have been killed, and Doctors Without Borders (MSF) reported a surge in wounded in the north. Amnesty International said on Friday it had received 'credible reports of destruction of civilian objects through shelling and air strikes, intensified forced conscription, and other acts that may constitute violations of international humanitarian law and international human rights law.' Amnesty’s regional director for East and Southern Africa, Tigere Chagutah, urged the African Union’s Peace and Security Council to 'urgently convene an emergency session on the situation in Ethiopia,' warning that 'fears of regionalisation of the conflict are on the rise.'

The conflict is taking on an international dimension. On Thursday, Eritrea severed diplomatic ties with Ethiopia, accusing Addis Ababa of 'pursuing an agenda of incessant hostility' and planning to invade Eritrea to secure access to the Red Sea coast. Eritrea denied involvement in the Tigray conflict, while Ethiopia has accused Eritrea, Sudan and Egypt of backing the rebels—accusations Khartoum and Cairo deny. TPLF leader Debretsion Gebremichael said in a video that 'the war is regional and it is a war to control the Red Sea.' Analysts fear the fighting could draw in neighbouring countries, with Laura Hammond of SOAS University of London telling Al Jazeera that 'the risks that conflict will spread to other countries … are already playing out.' Media access in Ethiopia is heavily restricted, and there have been significant communications blackouts. Neither the TPLF nor the government has commented on the latest developments on the ground.

Sources: Al Jazeera
