Colts release quarterback Daniel Jones after lackluster performance

Sports desk

The Indianapolis Colts released quarterback Daniel Jones on Friday, a day after a 26-3 loss to the Houston Texans on Thursday Night Football. Jones completed 12 of 27 passes for 144 yards with an interception in the defeat, which dropped the Colts to 3-8. Head coach Shane Steichen announced the move and declared that Anthony Richardson will be the starter moving forward.

Jones signed a two-year, $88 million contract extension with the Colts in March that included $50 million fully guaranteed at signing. For the 2026 season, he appeared in 11 games, completing 58.4 percent of his passes for 1,974 yards, seven touchdowns, and 13 interceptions. According to Over the Cap, Jones has now earned over $158 million in career salary between the Giants and the Colts. The release creates $49.49 million in dead money and a $30.31 million cap hit for Indianapolis, per OverTheCap.

Jones, 29, was selected by the New York Giants with the sixth overall pick in the 2019 NFL Draft out of Duke. He replaced Giants legend Eli Manning as the starter in Week 3 of his rookie season. After a tumultuous tenure marked by turnovers and injuries, the Giants rewarded him with a four-year, $160 million contract in spring 2023 following a playoff appearance. He was benched after a 2024 loss to the Carolina Panthers in Munich, requested his release, and landed with the Minnesota Vikings before signing with the Colts as a free agent in 2025.

Jones initially played well for the Colts, but his performance declined after a loss to the Pittsburgh Steelers and his 2025 season ended early with a torn Achilles. This season, he failed to throw for multiple touchdowns in any game and never finished a game with more touchdowns than interceptions. According to CBS Sports' Doug Clawson, among 678 quarterbacks who started each of their team's first 11 games over the last 45 years, Jones is the only one who never had a single game with more passing touchdowns than interceptions.

The Colts turned down trade interest from the Vikings and Ravens ahead of this year's deadline, according to Adam Schefter. Minnesota instead acquired Kirk Cousins, while Baltimore brought in Jacoby Brissett. Just 11 days before the release, Tom Pelissero reported that the Colts informed Jones he would not be traded. Jones will now be subject to the waiver wire; any team claiming him would take on his remaining $2 million salary for 2026 and $38 million in non-guaranteed money for 2027.

Richardson, the No. 4 overall pick in the 2023 NFL Draft, entered Thursday's game with seven minutes left and did not complete either of his pass attempts. He will make his first start since the 2024 season when the Colts host the New York Giants on Nov. 29. Steichen had told reporters after Week 10 that Jones would remain the starter, and ESPN's Jeremy Fowler reported earlier in the week that Steichen was "not motivated" to play Richardson unless absolutely necessary.
