President Trump Posthumously Pardons Pete Rose

Sports desk

President Donald Trump announced Thursday that he has granted a posthumous pardon to baseball legend Pete Rose, who died in September 2024 at age 83. In a post on Truth Social, Trump wrote, "I have just given a Complete and Total Pardon to the Late, Great Pete Rose, a true Baseball SUPERSTAR & LEGEND." Trump also praised Rose's record of 4,256 career hits and 17 All-Star selections, though he incorrectly described the total as "4,256 Hits in a Season." Rose accumulated the hits over his 24-year MLB career; the single-season record is held by Ichiro Suzuki, who had 262 hits in 2004.

Rose was permanently banned from baseball in 1989 after an investigation found he bet on the Cincinnati Reds while playing for and managing them from 1985 to 1987. He admitted to betting on baseball in his 2004 autobiography. In 1990, Rose pleaded guilty to two counts of filing false tax returns, failing to report $354,968 over four years. He was sentenced to five months in prison, fined $50,000, and ordered to perform 1,000 hours of community service, which he carried out as a gym teacher's assistant with inner-city children in Cincinnati. He also repaid the Internal Revenue Service.

Rose's MLB reinstatement came posthumously in May 2025, when Commissioner Rob Manfred removed all deceased players from the league's permanently ineligible list. Manfred acknowledged that Trump partially played a role in that decision. The reinstatement makes Rose eligible for the Baseball Hall of Fame; the earliest he could be inducted is 2028, under the "Classic Baseball" category for contributions before 1980. The next vote is scheduled for December 2027 at MLB's Winter Meetings.

A 17-time All-Star and three-time World Series champion, Rose spent 19 seasons with the Reds, winning World Series titles in 1975 and 1976, and added a third championship with the Philadelphia Phillies in 1980. He was National League Rookie of the Year in 1963, NL MVP in 1973, and World Series MVP in 1975. He won three NL batting titles and holds the record for most games played (3,562). As manager of the Reds from 1984 to 1989, he compiled a 412-373-1 record. The Cincinnati Reds inducted Rose into their own hall of fame in 2016 and retired his No. 14.

In his Truth Social post, Trump also criticized Rose's exclusion from the Hall of Fame, writing that Rose and former pitcher Roger Clemens were "treated terribly" and adding, "Politics, I guess!" The White House did not immediately respond to a request for comment from Fox News Digital. Rose's absence from the Hall of Fame has long been debated; in what is believed to be his last interview before his death, Rose told KTLA, "I was absolutely 110% wrong for what I did … and that's bet on baseball games, and now you're punished for the rest of your life."
