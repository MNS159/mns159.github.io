Trump, Xi and Putin hold first trilateral summit at APEC, release peace declaration

International & World desk

The leaders of China, Russia and the United States held an unprecedented trilateral summit Thursday on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) summit in Shenzhen, China, and issued a joint peace declaration that made no mention of the ongoing wars in Ukraine and Iran. Chinese President Xi Jinping, U.S. President Donald Trump and Russian President Vladimir Putin met for about one hour and 20 minutes. Trump told the two leaders, 'We are really making history,' while Xi described the talks as 'fruitful' and Putin called them 'open and candid.'

The declaration affirmed that 'a nuclear war cannot be won and must not be fought,' language previously used by the five permanent U.N. Security Council members, but stopped short of proposing a framework for denuclearization. Xi called for nuclear weapons to 'be completely prohibited and thoroughly destroyed over time to make the world free of [them].' The document also prioritized 'promoting UN reform so that it may respond more effectively to global challenges,' increasing the production and supply of refined petroleum products, and protecting diplomatic immunity. It stated that 'the international criminal court [ICC] and other similar institutions must not abuse their jurisdiction'—a clause benefiting Putin, who faces an ICC arrest warrant.

Analysts described the summit as largely symbolic. 'Overall, very little of substance seems to have been accomplished … This summit appears to have been all about the symbolism and prestige value,' said Prof. William Hurst of the University of Cambridge’s Centre for Geopolitics. Putin pushed back, saying, 'It is a serious collaborative work.' Hurst added, 'This was no Yalta moment, but it was certainly a solid public demonstration of Russia’s (and Putin’s) continuing relevance and power.' Patricia Kim of the Brookings Institution said, 'The real test will be whether the three countries follow through on their stated commitment to discuss nuclear risk reduction and preventing nuclear proliferation.'

European allies voiced indignation at the optics. Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk said, 'Mr Putin is not our friend and will never be.' Danish Prime Minister Mette Frederiksen called it 'very incomprehensible' that Trump would meet Putin. Trump and Putin had held a separate bilateral meeting earlier, their first since August 2025, where Trump called Putin his 'friend.' Trump and Xi also held their third bilateral meeting of the year, during which Xi described U.S.-China relations as 'generally stable and positive' but cautioned Washington over Taiwan, saying the U.S. 'must keep doing the right thing to avoid major setbacks.' Xi also called for enhanced communication on AI to 'ensure that AI remains under human control at all times.'

Separately, Trump faced accusations of falling asleep during Xi’s speech at the main APEC summit. Video showed Trump appearing to close his eyes and tilt his head downward for roughly 10 seconds. White House communications director Steven Cheung denied the claims, stating Trump was 'looking at papers in front of him' and accusing media of lying. Administration officials have previously attributed similar apparent drowsiness to blinking or listening intently, with Secretary of State Marco Rubio testifying he had 'never seen' Trump fall asleep.

Despite the historic nature of the first trilateral meeting, the declaration’s broad commitments and lack of breakthroughs led many analysts to view the summit as a footnote rather than a turning point. 'Beijing has little interest in taking on the diplomatic risks of brokering an actual peace agreement in Ukraine or resolving the conflict with Iran,' said Patricia Kim. The Guardian’s analysis concluded that the meeting 'will be consigned to a smallish footnote in any future volume of the 21st century,' with each leader gaining tactical benefits but no major substantive outcomes.
