Nvidia's Huang resigns amid affairs; California subpoenas firm over China ties

Tech / AI desk

Former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang resigned last week after revelations of multiple extramarital affairs, including one with a Chinese national whom U.S. officials believe is a spy. California Attorney General Rob Bonta announced Thursday that the state has served an investigative subpoena to Nvidia, probing whether the company or Huang facilitated the transfer of advanced GPUs or confidential information to China and whether investors were misled. “The conduct of Nvidia and its former CEO raises bright red flags, including about the ways in which the company may have misled investors,” Bonta said in a statement.

The California investigation centers on Huang’s relationship with Alice Huang (no relation), an executive at Singapore-based AI infrastructure firm Megaspeed. Megaspeed purchased $4.8 billion worth of Nvidia chips over the past two years and has been separately investigated by the U.S. and Singapore over suspected illegal chip transfers to China; the Justice Department has since taken over that federal probe. Nvidia’s board has hired an outside law firm and an independent slate of directors to assess the damage, while the U.S. Congress is preparing to subpoena Huang, and the SEC is said to be launching a civil inquiry into Nvidia’s disclosures.

Beyond the China-linked affair, the New York Times reported that Huang also had an extramarital relationship with Nvidia vice president of healthcare Kimberly Powell, who reported directly to him. Reuters then reported a third affair with another unnamed Nvidia employee. The Times also detailed a long pattern of alleged abusive management: Huang reportedly told subordinates that because Nvidia’s wealth made monetary motivation impossible, humiliation was the only lever. He was said to have told one executive his mother should have aborted him and threatened to rip another employee’s arms off. A former HR chief, Michael Winston, threatened to sue in 2005, calling Huang’s conduct the most abusive he had seen; Nvidia paid him over $1 million. Huang’s spokesman called the allegations fabrications.

The board accepted Huang’s resignation on November 12, appointing CFO Colette Kress as interim CEO. Chairman Harvey C. Jones said the company has no intention of reinstating Huang, despite a public petition from thousands of employees and public calls from Elon Musk, Anduril founder Palmer Luckey, and Y Combinator President Garry Tan for his return. In his first public statement since resigning, Huang said he had seen an “outpouring of calls” for his return and would be “honored” to come back if the board deemed it in the best interest of shareholders and the company. Separately, Bloomberg reported that Huang appeared “broken and shattered” when he surrendered his role, and the Wall Street Journal reported he had described Kress as “a deer in the headlights” during a presentation this summer.

Nvidia reported quarterly earnings on November 17 that beat expectations, with $115.8 billion in revenue, up 103% year-over-year. Kress said the company is on track to exceed full-year guidance and that customers and partners remain “100% behind us.” However, Nvidia announced it will stop selling its H200 chips in China, ending participation in a controversial loophole in U.S. export bans. Meanwhile, Democratic lawmakers have opened an inquiry into the Trump administration’s handling of Nvidia chip sales to China, noting that Trump was briefed about Huang’s relationship with a purported spy last fall but then granted permission for H200 sales in December 2025 in exchange for a cut of export proceeds. Huang has agreed to testify before the Senate Banking Committee before Thanksgiving; the closed-door briefing is expected to be recorded and transcribed.
