Google DeepMind says AGI threshold near, calls for preparation

Tech / AI desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

Google DeepMind has published a report arguing that artificial general intelligence (AGI)—machines that match humans across nearly all cognitive tasks—is coming within reach, and that the field must now seriously consider the subsequent development of artificial general superintelligence (ASI). The company warns that AI could continue to advance at its current pace or accelerate, with profound implications for science, the economy, and society at a scale much greater than AGI alone.

DeepMind defines ASI as a system that consistently outperforms large groups of human experts on tasks that could take them years, using fewer resources and less time—for example, making the scientific progress of an entire research field in a fraction of the time or achieving technological developments comparable to what large companies produce in a year. The report frames general intelligence as a continuum, with human-level AGI as one point and ASI further along, noting that the theoretical upper limits of intelligence are well understood but that the vast space between human-level AI and those limits is largely uncharted.

A key driver of progress, according to the report, is what DeepMind calls 'cognitive industrialization'—the commoditization of cognitive work driven by rapid improvements in hardware manufacturing, algorithmic efficiency, and compute hardware investment. Over the past decade, global effective compute stock has grown by at least tenfold per year, a factor of 10 billion over ten years. Meanwhile, the compute demand for running an AI workload has fallen by about fourfold per year; a task costing $1 billion today could cost just $1,000 in ten years. Extrapolating these trends, DeepMind estimates that the effective population of today’s AI agents could scale up more than a thousandfold by 2030 and more than a billionfold within a decade.

The report outlines four potential pathways from AGI to ASI: scaling models and data (though it notes bottlenecks such as data exhaustion and diminishing returns on model scale); technological paradigm shifts beyond today’s transformer-based architecture; improving coordination among multiple AGI instances to create 'group agents' or virtual economies; and recursive self-improvement, where AI systems automate improvements to future generations of AI. DeepMind emphasizes that simply scaling up today’s human-level AI in speed and number might itself bridge the gap to weak ASI, but that missing capabilities—such as the invention of genuinely new concepts—may require deeper breakthroughs.

DeepMind stresses that preparation for this potential shift requires mapping the possible roads from AGI to ASI and building rigorous, reliable ways to track progress. While the outcome is far from certain, the report argues that with AGI now within reach, the question of what comes after can no longer be postponed.
