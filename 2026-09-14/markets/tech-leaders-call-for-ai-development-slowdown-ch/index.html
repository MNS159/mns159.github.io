Tech leaders call for AI development slowdown; China and Trump push back

Financial & Markets desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

A group of prominent U.S. artificial intelligence executives, including Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei, OpenAI CEO Sam Altman, and Elon Musk, called over the weekend for the industry to slow the pace of AI research, citing safety risks. The appeal was met with immediate pushback from both China and U.S. President Donald Trump, who warned against ceding strategic advantage in what they described as a global AI arms race. AI-related stocks fell on Monday, with SoftBank, a major OpenAI investor, dropping 10% in Japan, while shares of Microsoft, Amazon, and Google parent Alphabet showed mixed results in morning trading.

In a weekend essay, Amodei urged peers to adopt common safety standards, cooperate with authoritarian governments, and place third-party safety monitors inside AI companies. Altman called the proposal a 'great idea,' and Musk posted 'Dario's right.' However, Amodei also acknowledged that any slowdown would be limited by the lead U.S. companies hold over 'authoritarian regimes, chiefly the Chinese Communist Party,' warning that if the U.S. slows by more than that lead, 'unpaced CCP-associated projects will pull ahead, creating significant national security risk.' Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella split the difference, warning that third-party safety officials could compromise customer data and trade secrets.

China's Foreign Ministry spokesperson Guo Jiakun on Monday dismissed the U.S. executives' call as 'fear mongering,' saying that 'confrontation, competition will just disrupt the process of global AI governance.' Separately, China's Minister of State Security, Chen Yixin, published an article on Sunday calling for acceleration of an AI security risk prevention and control system, describing AI as 'the main battleground for global technological competition and a new arena for strategic rivalry among major powers.' President Xi Jinping, speaking at the BRICS summit in New Delhi, said China will take the lead to foster AI collaboration and development among developing countries.

President Trump, during a trip to Ireland, rejected the slowdown calls, telling CNBC: 'Look, we're leading China in AI... and, frankly I want to keep it that way because whoever wins, AI wins.' His stance directly countered a push by former Senator Joe Manchin, who urged Trump to use an executive order to freeze AI-company IPOs until federal safeguards are in place. In Congress, House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries called for 'decisive action' without a timetable, while Speaker Mike Johnson urged caution.

Helen Toner, executive director of the Georgetown Center for Security and Emerging Technology and a former OpenAI board member, told CNBC's 'Squawk Box' that the best estimates suggest China's top AI models are 'something like six to nine months behind the very best U.S. models.' She added, 'If that means that we slow down and they just rip past us because they're pretty close behind — that's a problem.' Toner noted it is unclear how much of China's advancements are 'fast following,' and that if the U.S. frontier slows, 'perhaps China also slows somewhat.' Analysts at Citigroup warned that if AI model developments slow, earnings revisions could follow, potentially pulling the rug out from under a stock market that has been heavily carried by AI-related gains. Despite the safety debate, Microsoft reported that its Azure cloud business crossed $100 billion in annual revenue with 43% growth, and its commercial backlog surged 84% to $678 billion, helping explain why markets largely shrugged off the safety calls on Monday.
