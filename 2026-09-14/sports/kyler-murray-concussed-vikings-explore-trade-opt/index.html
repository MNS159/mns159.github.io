Kyler Murray concussed, Vikings explore trade options for quarterback

Sports desk

Minnesota Vikings quarterback Kyler Murray was placed in the NFL's concussion protocol after a late hit to the head during the first quarter of Sunday's season-opening 39–22 win over the Green Bay Packers. Murray scrambled and slid, then took a direct helmet hit from Packers safety Javon Bullard, who was flagged 15 yards for unnecessary roughness. Murray completed 3 of 5 passes for 18 yards and an interception, adding nine rushing yards, before being helped to the locker room. He was ruled out for the remainder of the game.

Carson Wentz replaced Murray and finished 13 of 19 for 133 yards and three touchdowns. The Vikings’ depth chart now lists Wentz as the starter with J.J. McCarthy as the backup. Head coach Kevin O’Connell said he spoke with Murray after the game, noting, "He seemed to be doing pretty well, but I wouldn't medically quote me on that… We'll take it day-by-day." With no clear timeline for Murray’s return, the Vikings are considering emergency trade options to bolster their quarterback room.

One reported proposal, floated by FanSided’s Cody Williams, would send second-year receiver Tai Felton and a 2027 third-round pick to the San Francisco 49ers for backup Mac Jones. The 49ers reportedly want to keep Jones but could be swayed by a strong offer; the Vikings hold an extra 2027 third-round pick (acquired via the Jonathan Greenard trade), giving them flexibility to part with a Day 2 selection without affecting long-term draft capital. Another proposal, from Heavy, suggests the Vikings trade for Dallas Cowboys backup Sam Howell in exchange for a fifth-round pick. Howell has thrown for 4,139 yards, 22 touchdowns and 23 interceptions in 20 career appearances (18 starts) but signed only a one-year, $2.5 million contract this offseason.

Murray, the No. 1 overall pick in the 2019 draft, signed a one-year deal with Minnesota this spring after the Arizona Cardinals released him. Before the season, Murray spoke about his competitive drive and referenced F. Scott Fitzgerald's 'The Great Gatsby,' explaining that the "green light" represents the aspirations he wants to accomplish in life. "I love 'The Great Gatsby,'" he said. "I damn near watched it every day in high school. Would turn it on at night and go to sleep. ... It stuck with me."

Elsewhere in the NFC, Packers head coach Matt LaFleur said the off-field incident involving running back Josh Jacobs has not changed his opinion of the player, adding he is "confident in Jacobs’ off-field character" but will not comment further until the league completes its investigation. Meanwhile, Steelers quarterback Aaron Rodgers, speaking about his former team, acknowledged that some people in the Packers organization might not welcome him back after his retirement, but said he doesn't let the ending "skew any of the good times."
