Clippers owner Steve Ballmer accepts NBA penalties in salary cap circumvention case, apologizes to fans

Sports desk · anchored on Reuters

Los Angeles Clippers owner Steve Ballmer announced Sunday night that the team will comply with the NBA's penalties for salary cap circumvention involving Kawhi Leonard, reversing his earlier vow to fight the punishment. In a statement posted online, Ballmer apologized to Clippers fans, employees and fellow NBA owners for the "distraction and distress" the matter caused, saying, "I accept responsibility as principal owner." He confirmed the team has paid the $30 million fine and is "moving forward" despite "disagreements concerning the findings in the report."

The NBA's punishment, handed down 11 days before Ballmer's statement, includes the forfeiture of five first-round draft picks, a one-year suspension for Ballmer, a one-year unpaid suspension for president of business operations Gillian Zucker, and a six-month unpaid suspension for president of basketball operations Lawrence Frank. The penalties stem from the league's investigation into the Clippers' efforts to circumvent the salary cap in their dealings with Leonard. Ballmer had previously called the investigation a "witch hunt" and a "heavily biased investigation," but the team ultimately chose not to fight the discipline through the courts.

The decision to accept the penalties could open the door to reducing them, according to league precedent. In 2000, the Minnesota Timberwolves were stripped of five first-round picks for salary cap circumvention involving Joe Smith, but the NBA later returned two of those picks after the team accepted the punishment and demonstrated compliance. Whether the Clippers receive similar relief remains uncertain, particularly because the Justice Department has opened a criminal investigation into the Clippers' dealings with Leonard, and because the Clippers were already penalized in 2015 for cap circumvention involving DeAndre Jordan.

Ballmer's announcement came hours before the NBA's Board of Governors meeting, which runs Monday to Tuesday, and could clear the way for the long-awaited finalization of the Leonard trade to the Toronto Raptors. The deal, agreed upon in late June, would send Brandon Ingram, Gradey Dick, two unprotected first-round picks, two first-round pick swaps and a second-round pick to the Clippers. The Raptors paused the trade on July 9 due to concerns about the NBA's findings and potential penalties. League sources said the delay was primarily due to the Clippers needing to restructure their front office, and a source close to Leonard said the trade could be completed in the coming days.

The Clippers' acceptance of the penalties also comes amid scrutiny of other potential cap circumvention. Last offseason, the team signed Bradley Beal to a two-year, $11 million contract using the taxpayer mid-level exception; Beal declined his player option and re-signed for a 20% raise, the maximum allowed under his Non-Bird Rights, while the team traded younger and healthier guard Bennedict Mathurin to the New Orleans Pelicans. The NBA is not known to be investigating the Beal contract, but it bears resemblance to the Gary Trent Jr. contract with the Milwaukee Bucks, which the league is examining. The Clippers have a history of signing veterans to minimum deals and later rewarding them, though previous cases like Nicolas Batum and Reggie Jackson were paid in line with market value.

In his statement, Ballmer said, "This has been a very difficult time for everybody associated with the Clippers, and for that, I have sincere regrets." He added, "Team owners should support, not distract," and expressed confidence that the team will "compete at our highest level and be an organization our fans will be proud of." The decision to accept the penalties, rather than pursue a protracted legal battle, aligns with recent court precedent favoring internal league resolution, and it leaves open the possibility of regaining some draft picks at the commissioner's discretion.
