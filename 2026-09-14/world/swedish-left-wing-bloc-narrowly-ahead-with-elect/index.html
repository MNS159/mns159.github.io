Swedish left-wing bloc narrowly ahead with election too close to call

International & World desk

Sweden's general election remained too close to call on Monday, with the left-wing bloc marginally ahead by approximately 20,000 votes as more than 90% of ballots were counted. Preliminary counts from Sweden's electoral authority projected the left bloc, led by the Social Democrats' Magdalena Andersson, would finish with a three-seat majority over the rival four-party coalition led by incumbent conservative Prime Minister Ulf Kristersson.

With 95% of votes reported, the left-wing bloc held 176 mandates (seats) in the Riksdag, compared to 173 for the right-wing bloc. Sweden uses a proportional representation system, and a party needs at least 4% of the vote to enter parliament. Counting will resume on Wednesday to include early votes submitted late and votes from abroad that did not arrive by election day. In 2022, more than 220,000 votes were counted in that later tally; only about 28,000 votes currently separate the blocs, meaning Wednesday's count could determine the outcome.

The left bloc comprises the Social Democrats, Left Party, Greens and Centre Party. The right bloc includes Kristersson's Moderate Party and three other parties, supported by the hard-right Sweden Democrats (SD), which have neo-Nazi roots. Turnout dropped to approximately 80%, down four percentage points from the 2022 election. The Social Democrats were on track for their worst result in more than a century with 28%, while SD was losing support for the first time since entering parliament in 2010. Other left-bloc parties increased their support.

Kristersson said early Monday that it remained unclear who would form the next government. 'The voters have had their say, but we still don't have a final result. Which government will lead Sweden in the coming years is still an open question,' he told supporters. Andersson urged patience but added that if the current results hold, 'Sweden will have a new government.' After casting her vote Sunday, she framed the choice as whether Sweden should have 'a government entirely dominated by the Sweden Democrats, something that has never happened in Sweden before' or a government led by her that would steer the country in 'a new spirit of co-operation.'

The Sweden Democrats were founded by Nazi sympathisers in the 1980s and were shunned by other parties until 2022, when they became the country's second-largest party after campaigning on violent crime and immigration. The Social Democrats have been a major force in Swedish politics since World War Two. National security, gang crime and welfare spending dominated the election campaign. No major media outlet had declared a winner as of Monday morning.
