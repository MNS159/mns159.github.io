Pennsylvania reports third measles-related death amid outbreak

Health & Science desk

Pennsylvania officials have reported the state's third measles-related death this year, the death of a 40-year-old unvaccinated woman in Jefferson County on Saturday. Jefferson County Coroner Greg Furlong announced the death in a Facebook post, calling it "measles-related." The woman was not identified; Furlong said no more information about the case would be released. The first two measles-related deaths in Pennsylvania occurred in August and involved infants, according to state and local officials.

As of Friday, Pennsylvania had recorded 676 measles cases across 37 counties, with 124 hospitalizations and four cases among vaccinated individuals, according to the state Department of Health. The outbreak has prompted a clash between state and federal officials over how to count the deaths. The U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention’s website currently states: "The National Center for Health Statistics (NCHS) does not currently have any death records from 2026 that indicate measles is the underlying cause." Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. had previously called the announcement of the first two deaths "premature" and suggested it "may even have been altogether fabricated by one of the Governor's hopeful staffers."

In a statement Sunday, CDC Director Erica Schwartz said the agency had not been notified by Pennsylvania officials of the latest death and suggested the state had not accepted repeated offers from the CDC for "on-the-ground measles outbreak support." Pennsylvania's first two deaths were a newborn boy who died of a ruptured spleen—with measles listed as a "significant condition" but not contributing to the rupture, according to Lancaster County Coroner Dr. Stephen Diamantoni—and a 6-week-old girl born with Amish lethal microcephaly who died directly from measles. Neither infant was old enough to be vaccinated.

Jefferson County Chief Deputy Coroner Jason Stiver said in a statement that "while cases of severe illness remain uncommon, this tragedy highlights that measles should not be taken lightly." The coroner's office emphasized that measles can lead to severe complications at any age. The CDC has reported more than 3,200 confirmed measles cases in the United States this year; no other state has announced a measles-related death in 2026. The National Center for Health Statistics said it will update its records as it receives and reviews death records.
