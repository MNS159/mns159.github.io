Jensen Huang's return as Nvidia CEO sparks massive stock market rally

Financial & Markets desk

U.S. stocks surged on Monday in their best day since April 2025 after Nvidia reinstated Jensen Huang as CEO, reversing a steep sell-off that had pushed the major averages into correction territory. The S&P 500 jumped 4.7%, the Dow Jones Industrial Average rose more than 1,400 points (about 3%), and the Nasdaq Composite rallied nearly 6.5%. The rally was led by Nvidia, whose stock soared 31% — its biggest one-day gain since May 2003 — adding more than a trillion dollars in market capitalization, a record for any public company, according to Bespoke Investments.

The move came after Nvidia's board announced it had reinstated Huang as president, CEO and board director, effective immediately. Board chair Harvey Jones said, "The Board believes Jensen is the right person to lead NVIDIA, and that his reinstatement is in the best interests of our stockholders, employees, partners and customers." President Trump said he spoke with the board and "convinced" members to bring Huang back after he was forced to resign following reports of affairs, including one that intelligence officials had previously leaked to the media as potentially involving a Chinese spy.

The relief rally followed a brutal week that saw the S&P 500 and Dow enter correction territory. The S&P 500 alone tumbled more than 9% between Huang's initial departure on Nov. 12 and Friday's close. Wall Street experts were divided on whether the bounce marks a lasting bottom. Mark Gibbens, founder of Gibbens Capital Management, said, "I think the AI trade is back on. I certainly believe this establishes a bottom and a floor in the AI trade and the recent market weakness." Art Hogan, chief market strategist at B. Riley Wealth Management, noted that futures were already higher Sunday night before any news of Huang's return, suggesting the market was oversold. "Sometimes you just hit that crescendo where you know the market gets oversold, and I would argue that we'd be higher today, even without the Nvidia news," he said.

But other strategists urged caution. Jay Woods, chief market strategist at Freedom Capital Markets, said the rally may fizzle because "people will pull back and still have the same questions we had before he was replaced." Nomura cross-asset strategist Charlie McElligott warned that much of the move was driven by "mechanical" buying — short sellers being forced to cover their bets and options dealers unwinding hedges — and said it would be "naïve to believe that a one-day 'Up Shock' absolves the market of underlying concerns." Peter Boockvar, chief investment officer at One Point BFG Wealth Partners, said the "picks and shovels" AI trade will remain dependent on the rate of capital expenditure spending by hyperscalers. "That, at the end of the day, is the story that those companies are going to have to deal with outside of bounces like we're seeing now," he said.

The sell-off that began before Huang's departure was driven by broader concerns: worries about financing the AI buildout, data center approvals after Democratic wins in the midterm elections, and fears that hyperscaler capital expenditure may have peaked. Those issues remain unresolved. Traders at Goldman Sachs summed up the market's dependence on Nvidia's fate with a note: "Not sure yet whether good or bad that the fate of the market is almost equal to the fate of one person. But hey ... We are not picky here."

Elsewhere in markets, Oatly stock surged as much as 108% after Belgian investment company Verlinvest increased its stake to roughly 42.6%, purchasing the interest from China Resources. Jefferies initiated coverage of SpaceX with a Buy rating and a $210 price target, citing AI rather than rockets as the main value driver. The broader tech rally also lifted SpaceX shares more than 7% in sympathy with the sector.
