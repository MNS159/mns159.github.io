Ukraine war spills into NATO territory as attacks reach Libya and Baltic

International & World desk

The war between Russia and Ukraine has again spilled into NATO territory, with a Russian cargo ship struck by a drone in the Baltic Sea over the weekend and Russian cruise missiles landing in Poland last week, while Ukrainian drones also hit Russian naval assets off the coast of Libya, according to reports from NPR and CNN.

On Saturday, a drone struck the Russian cargo ship Sparta II in the Gulf of Finland, setting it ablaze. Russia took control of the vessel and towed it out of Finnish waters a day later, after the crew was rescued by another Russian ship. The attack is presumed to have come from Ukraine, but Kyiv has not claimed responsibility. Finnish President Alexander Stubb called the incident an "unfortunate reminder" that the consequences of Russia's war are visible in the region. Teams from Finland and Estonia helped Russian ships extinguish the fire, and Stubb thanked them for their "responsible and professional actions." The Sparta II transports weapons and other supplies to Russia for use against Ukraine; the vessel is under U.S. and U.K. sanctions, and its owner is under European Union sanctions. Finland chose not to seize the ship, citing the risk of a major environmental hazard if it sank. Jukka-Pekka Lumilahti, director of sea operations for the Finnish Border Guard, said, "If we would let the vessel burn, it would most likely sink. And after that, it would cause a major environmental risk for Finland and Estonia."

Last Friday, Russia launched three cruise missiles that landed between 30 and 60 miles from a military exercise in Poland involving nearly a dozen countries, which was training forces for a potential peace-monitoring mission in post-war Ukraine. Multiple air alerts were issued, and participants were told to take cover. NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte, asked in Warsaw whether the alliance could stop such incursions, said, "I cannot guarantee you that it will not happen. But I can guarantee you is that NATO will defend every inch of Polish territory, that as a collective alliance, we impose immense costs on Russians." NATO ambassadors were scheduled to meet Monday in a special session to discuss further measures to protect Poland.

Ukraine also struck Russian naval assets near the port of Benghazi, Libya, according to Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky, hitting a Russian naval ship and a cargo vessel. The strikes showcase Kyiv's ability to reach Russian assets in the Mediterranean. Meanwhile, Russia launched what Zelensky called the most intense attack of the year on Friday — a six-hour barrage of drones and missiles that struck bridges and power infrastructure across Ukraine, killing dozens and leaving swaths of the country and capital in blackout.

The widening theater of conflict comes amid a stalled peace process. President Donald Trump has called Russian President Vladimir Putin his friend, and the U.S. has reframed talks as being about wider European security infrastructure — a long-standing Russian goal. Putin stated in Shenzhen that Russia has no interest in negotiating a ceasefire, and he added that if Europeans want to join talks, they should travel to Moscow to make their case. The Kremlin has also accused Lithuania of allowing a drone to fly through its airspace — NATO territory — to strike the Sparta II, an allegation that feeds Russia's fiction that it is at war with all of NATO. The two sides' demands are moving further apart, with the Kremlin reportedly wanting NATO to fall back westward and the U.S. reportedly floating Ukraine's NATO membership — incompatible red lines. As CNN noted, "You can't expect nonsense talks to ever stop the real business of killing."
