Gaza Conflict

Israeli strikes kill UNRWA worker in Gaza; Oxfam report calls enclave a toxic wasteland

International & World desk · anchored on Reuters

Israeli air strikes killed at least three people in Gaza on Monday, including Adel al-Helo, a worker for the UN Palestinian refugee agency UNRWA, according to the agency and local health officials. UNRWA said al-Helo was working near a water well in the Jabalia refugee camp in the north of the enclave when he was hit; three others were wounded in the strike. Later on Monday, another Israeli air strike targeted a vehicle in western Gaza City, killing two people and wounding others, medics said. The Israeli military did not immediately comment. The strikes came despite an October 2025 ceasefire deal with Hamas that formally halted around two years of fighting but has not ended Israel's genocidal war on Gaza, Al Jazeera reported. Since the ceasefire, at least 1,679 Palestinian people and five Israeli soldiers have been killed. Israeli forces have also continued to destroy residential buildings beyond the 'Yellow Line' to which they were required to withdraw under the ceasefire; Mohammed Musleh, mayor of Deir el-Balah's Maghazi municipality, told Al Jazeera that more than 500 housing units had been destroyed in less than a month, describing what he called a systematic policy of gradual creeping displacement.

Separately, the charity Oxfam released a report describing Gaza as 'a toxic wasteland,' saying more than 99% of the Palestinian territory shows indicators of environmental contamination from Israeli military attacks. Heavy metals, explosive residues, asbestos from destroyed buildings, and dioxins and biological contaminants pose threats now and potentially for decades, the report said. Oxfam and the UK-based Conflict and Environment Observatory (CEOBS) used satellite imagery, remote sensing, spatial analysis and other open-source data. The report notes that all six wastewater treatment plants in Gaza are out of operation, with untreated sewage flowing into groundwater and the Mediterranean Sea; heavy winter storms have recently caused rainwater to mix with wastewater and flow through streets and tent sites housing displaced people. It cites a February statement by a UN special rapporteur that more than 200,000 tonnes of explosives were dropped on Gaza in almost two years, 'the equivalent of the total energy of approximately 13 Hiroshima-type nuclear bombs.' Last year, the UN Environment Programme estimated that Israel's military operations had generated more than 61 million tonnes of debris, with about 15% potentially carrying a high risk of hazardous contamination.

Oxfam report author Lama Abdul Samad told the BBC that 'there are going to be chronic health impacts that are long term because a lot of these contaminants are persistent in the environment.' She said Gazans are likely to suffer from increased respiratory diseases, cancers, organ damage, and a rise in birth defects and stillbirths. Gazan doctors have already reported increased acute respiratory infections caused by dust and pollution from destroyed buildings and from displaced people burning plastic, rags and cardboard, while gastrointestinal diseases are linked to unsafe water and poor sanitation. Palestinian dermatologist Dr Alaa Abu Hajar told the BBC he had seen a five-fold increase in chronic skin diseases since the war began, attributing it to environmental pollution and overcrowded, unhygienic conditions in tents and displacement camps: 'There are rodents, rubbish and sewage that's spread through the streets. Unfortunately, the age group most affected is children.' Fishermen say untreated sewage and rubble dumped into the sea have harmed marine life; fisherman Abu al-Abed Sultan said, 'Sewage and wastewater are discharged into the sea without treatment and this is a great disaster... When we lower our nets, we catch rubble and garbage.' Testing of contamination levels is often impossible because Gazan laboratories have been destroyed and Israeli restrictions block specialist testing equipment and scientists, the BBC reported.

Oxfam International's executive director, Amitabh Behar, said the impact on Gaza 'will have fundamentally degraded the environmental conditions necessary for human safety and survival.' He accused Israel of 'environmental war crimes' as part of an 'ongoing genocide in Gaza.' Israel has rejected genocide accusations in an ongoing case at the International Court of Justice as baseless and distorted, maintaining that its military campaign is an act of self-defence against Hamas in response to the group's deadly attack on Israel on 7 October 2023, and that it takes steps to mitigate civilian harm. The Israeli embassy in the UK sharply criticised the Oxfam report, saying it 'falls short of professional standards' and relies on satellite imagery 'and unverified assumptions, and its methodology lacks the academic rigour needed to withstand serious scrutiny.' It argued the report drew conclusions about Gaza's soil and groundwater without considering the excavation of hundreds of kilometres of tunnels by Hamas, rendering it fundamentally flawed. The Oxfam report calls for a full, independent environmental assessment to identify contamination risks ahead of a clean-up, with special attention to protecting workers involved.

At least 74,746 Palestinians have been killed since the start of the war in Gaza, according to the Hamas-run health ministry, whose figures are considered reliable by the UN. The UN says humanitarian conditions remain dire. UN Deputy Secretary-General Amina Mohammed, speaking Monday ahead of the International Day of Solidarity with the Palestinian People, said the war has undone 77 years of human development in Gaza, according to a joint UN-World Bank assessment. She condemned 'the abhorrent terror attacks by Hamas on 7 October 2023' but added that 'nothing can justify the collective punishment of the Palestinian people.' She called for unhindered humanitarian access and protection for aid workers, saying 'aid must never be obstructed, diverted, or used as a bargaining chip,' and urged political and financial support for UNRWA. She stressed that recovery must be Palestinian-led, with women and youth playing a meaningful role, and that reconstruction cannot substitute for a political solution: 'The Occupied Palestinian Territory must remain politically, economically, and territorially integrated – and united under a single, legitimate, sovereign, democratic Palestinian Government.' She reaffirmed UN support for two states, Israel and Palestine, living side by side along pre-1967 lines as 'the only vital path forward.' Israel has conditioned large-scale Gaza reconstruction on the full disarmament of Hamas and other armed groups; in July, Hamas accepted a US-led Board of Peace plan to give up its weapons in exchange for an Israeli troop withdrawal, but the Israeli government said the proposal was insufficient.
