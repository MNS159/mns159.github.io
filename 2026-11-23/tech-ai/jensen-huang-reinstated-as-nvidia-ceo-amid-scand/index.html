Jensen Huang reinstated as Nvidia CEO after scandal, pressure from Trump and tech allies

Tech / AI desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

Jensen Huang returned to Nvidia as CEO and president on Monday, less than two weeks after resigning amid reports of affairs including with an alleged Chinese spy. The board reinstated him after receiving input from senior levels of the U.S. government and determining that his return poses no national security risk. Nvidia stock rose about 30 percent in early trading following the announcement, recovering some of the roughly 25 percent decline since Huang's resignation.

Huang resigned on November 12 after admitting he was "unfaithful to my wife" and that he "created problems for the board." Reports had detailed affairs with Alice Huang, an executive at Nvidia customer Megaspeed who was allegedly enlisted by Chinese intelligence to spy on Nvidia, and with Nvidia vice president of healthcare Kimberly Powell. The board initially said it would not reinstate him, but a pressure campaign mounted by tech leaders and President Donald Trump changed the outcome.

Trump said he was "totally involved" in Huang's reinstatement. Vice President JD Vance worked over the weekend with Huang and the board to resolve the situation, and Trump called into a board meeting to advocate for Huang's return. The board formed a National Security and Export Control Committee and is seeking a chief compliance officer. The Securities and Exchange Commission is investigating Nvidia's accounting practices, and the Justice Department is investigating Megaspeed for potentially smuggling AI chips to China.

Tech leaders celebrated Huang's return. Elon Musk called it "the mistake has been corrected," having previously likened Huang's departure to "kicking Michael Jordan off your team when you're on a winning streak." OpenAI CEO Sam Altman, who was himself fired and reinstated nearly three years ago, posted "Jensen!" Epic Games CEO Tim Sweeney called Huang's return "a demonstration of American resiliency." Binance co-founder Changpeng Zhao and former Uber CEO Travis Kalanick also voiced support.

Huang returned to Nvidia's office to a crowd of applauding employees. "Thank you all for the second chances. I'm gonna be a better CEO the second time around," he said. "This is my first day at work. I'm terribly sorry for putting all of you through all of this." The board said it will finish its investigation into Huang, and Sen. Elizabeth Warren called the reinstatement "reckless," urging the administration to explain the input government officials provided.
