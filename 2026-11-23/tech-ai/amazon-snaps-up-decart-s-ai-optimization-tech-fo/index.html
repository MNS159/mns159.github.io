Amazon to acquire Decart's AI optimization tech for $7.5B ahead of Trainium sales push

Tech / AI desk

Amazon has agreed to acquire a significant portion of Israeli startup Decart for $7.5 billion, according to Bloomberg, as the tech giant prepares to sell its Trainium AI chips to customers. The deal, announced in an Amazon blog post, includes the technology behind Decart's Decart Optimization Stack (DOS), software that helps AI models run efficiently on hardware from Nvidia, Amazon, and Google, as well as Decart's founding team and its roster of AI researchers and engineers.

Decart is known for building interactive real-time world models—most recently Oasis 3—and AI video-generation tools. DOS is designed to maximize the efficiency of AI workloads across different hardware platforms.

“Every leap forward in AI starts with getting more out of every chip,” said Peter DeSantis, Amazon's SVP of foundational AI models, custom silicon, and quantum computing. “More efficient compute means better training, better reinforcement learning, faster inference, and better models for customers at a lower cost.”

Amazon has so far used its Trainium chips internally at its own data centers, but is now preparing to sell them to customers as a direct competitor to Nvidia's offerings, which have been the industry standard. Decart's technology could make it easier for customers to run their workloads on Amazon chips instead of Nvidia's, lowering the barrier to adoption.

Neither Decart nor Amazon immediately responded to requests for comment.
