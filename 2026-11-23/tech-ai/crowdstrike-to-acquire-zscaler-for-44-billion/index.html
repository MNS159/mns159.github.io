CrowdStrike to acquire Zscaler for $43.9 billion in all-stock deal

Tech / AI desk

Cybersecurity giant CrowdStrike has agreed to acquire Zscaler in a $43.9 billion all-stock deal, the companies announced Monday. The transaction is expected to create what the companies described as the 'largest single addressable market' for cybersecurity, positioning them for the AI era.

Zscaler, based in San Jose, California, provides cloud cybersecurity products to approximately 8,000 enterprise companies. Its core offering is zero-trust technology, a security feature that requires employees to authenticate each time they access an internal system or resource, designed to prevent hackers from stealing credentials and gaining unfettered network access.

CrowdStrike secures the computers, laptops, phones, and other devices connected to a company's network. The companies said the deal will unite CrowdStrike's endpoint protection software with Zscaler's zero-trust platform to better defend customers against data breaches, offering 'superior protection' by combining the two products.

In remarks on Monday, the companies said the acquisition will help customers safely adopt AI. The deal is subject to regulatory approval and is expected to close around this time next year.
