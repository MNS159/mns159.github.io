Trump claims credit for Nvidia reinstating Jensen Huang as CEO amid backlash

Politics desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum
✓ Facts
  • Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia CEO on Nov. 12 following reports of extramarital affairs, including one with a woman alleged to have ties to Chinese intelligence.
  • Nvidia's board reinstated Huang as CEO on Monday, 11 days after his resignation, saying it believed he was the right person to lead the company.
  • Nvidia's stock closed at $165.05 per share Monday, up roughly 31.5% from Friday's close of $125.53, with BBC reporting about $1 trillion added to the company's market value.
  • President Trump told CNBC he personally spoke to Nvidia's board and 'convinced' them to reinstate Huang, saying the administration gave the board assurances that Huang's affair did not pose a national security issue.
  • Trump said he spoke with Huang by phone on Sunday night, before Nvidia's Monday announcement of his reinstatement.
  • Huang is scheduled to give a closed-door interview to three House committees on Dec. 1 and to testify before the Senate Banking Committee on Dec. 3.
  • Trump's financial disclosures show he purchased $265,000 in Nvidia stock and sold as much as $15,000 worth of Nvidia stock in September.
  • An independent investigation into Nvidia's compliance with U.S. export controls remains ongoing.
○ Analysis

Nvidia, the world's second-most-valuable company, sits at the center of the global AI buildout and the U.S.-China technology rivalry, with its chips powering major AI systems used by Google, Meta, Amazon, Microsoft and others. Huang's abrupt resignation and rapid reinstatement — reversing the board's prior public insistence that he would not return — unfolded alongside a roughly 31.5% stock rebound, underscoring how closely Nvidia's market value, and by extension financing across the AI sector, is tied to Huang personally. The episode intertwines corporate governance, national security concerns over chip exports to China, and direct presidential involvement, setting up congressional scrutiny of both Nvidia and the administration's role in the reversal.

◐ Debate

Democratic

  • Sen. Elizabeth Warren (D), incoming Senate Banking Committee chair, called the reinstatement 'reckless' and said it increases urgency for passing bipartisan export control legislation that Nvidia should not lobby against.
  • Rep. Hakeem Jeffries (D) called the decision to reinstate Huang 'indefensible, irresponsible and incomprehensible' and said Congress will investigate alleged 'business malfeasance' by Nvidia and 'corrupt abuse of power' by the Department of Commerce.

Republican

  • Rep. Michael McCaul (R-Texas) called for a briefing from the Trump administration on Huang's reinstatement, questioning officials' assessment given Huang's relationship with an alleged Chinese intelligence asset and Nvidia's chip sales.
  • Rep. Don Bacon (R-Neb.) said Nvidia should have moved on without Huang and criticized the administration's push to save him, arguing the U.S. cannot pursue the AI race while giving China its best technology.
Steelman

Across party lines, lawmakers are converging on the same underlying worry — not necessarily Huang's personal conduct, but whether U.S. chip export policy and oversight of Nvidia are robust enough given the company's outsized role in AI infrastructure and national security.

Implications

Huang's reinstatement sets up a high-profile clash in Congress, with closed-door House testimony and Senate Banking Committee hearings in early December likely to focus on export controls, the administration's role in his reinstatement, and Nvidia's compliance practices.
