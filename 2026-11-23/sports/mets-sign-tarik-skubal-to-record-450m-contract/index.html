Mets sign Tarik Skubal to record $450M contract, then add Paul Goldschmidt

Sports desk

The New York Mets signed left-handed pitcher Tarik Skubal to a nine-year, $450 million contract and followed it within 24 hours by agreeing to a one-year, $8 million contract with first baseman Paul Goldschmidt, according to multiple reports. The Goldschmidt deal, reported by Robert Murray of FanSided and confirmed by Will Sammon of The Athletic, includes up to $2 million in incentives, per Joel Sherman of the New York Post. Because the Mets are a perennial luxury-tax payor in the highest penalty tier, the $8 million base salary will be taxed at 110 percent, bringing the total cost to approximately $16.8 million.

Goldschmidt, 39, spent the 2026 season with the New York Yankees, appearing in 105 games and totaling 380 plate appearances. He hit .252/.343/.452 with 18 home runs, 13 doubles, a triple, a 7.6 percent walk rate and a 22.6 percent strikeout rate. All of his production came against left-handed pitching: in 183 plate appearances versus southpaws, he slashed .299/.393/.573, while against right-handers he managed just .213/.239/.351 in 197 plate appearances. Defensively, he posted two defensive runs saved and five outs above average in 623 1/3 innings at first base, according to FanGraphs.

The Mets’ need at first base arose after Pete Alonso departed in free agency following the 2025 season, signing a five-year, $155 million contract with the Baltimore Orioles. New York pivoted to sign Jorge Polanco to a two-year, $40 million deal, but Polanco played only 37 games in 2026 and slashed .148/.201/.230/.431. The Mets have a platoon option in Jared Young, who hit .266/.330/.429 in 342 plate appearances last season but struggled against left-handers. Young also has corner outfield experience and could move to a bench role.

Sherman, writing for the New York Post, said the Goldschmidt signing follows a pattern of the Mets acquiring former Yankees. "They needed a right-hander who hits left-hand pitching well to play first base, and Goldschmidt still does that, even in the upper-30s," Sherman said. "He still plays first base defensively at a high level, though not the same level he did in his prime Gold-Glove age. He's New York proven. The Mets are trying to improve their clubhouse culture continuously. Goldschmidt works to that. Just a solid dude."

The Mets have added more than $50 million in salary for 2027 between Skubal and Goldschmidt, plus a $45 million signing bonus for Skubal, with total luxury-tax obligations exceeding $111 million. In the previous 48 hours, the club also signed center fielder A.J. Ewing to an eight-year, $95 million extension. President of baseball operations David Stearns is expected to continue reshaping the roster, with potential trade candidates including Kodai Senga, Jorge Polanco and Mark Vientos. The Mets finished last in their division in 2026 after a late-season collapse in 2025.
