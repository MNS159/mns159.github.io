Pochettino's Man Utd slide continues after Liverpool thrashing

Sports desk

Mauricio Pochettino's reign as Manchester United head coach began in desperate fashion on Sunday as his 10-man side were thrashed 4-0 by Liverpool at Anfield, leaving United second from bottom of the Premier League and in the relegation zone. Liverpool fans sang that Pochettino would be "sacked in the morning" after 15 minutes, by which point his team were already 2-0 down and had been reduced to 10 men. United midfielder Kobbie Mainoo summed up the mood afterward, telling MUTV: "We're at the lowest we've ever been and no-one's coming to save us. There are no helping hands coming, so we've got to work together, lean on each other, keep working and hopefully it will come good. It's all about character now."

The defeat extended a dire run in which United have conceded 19 goals in their past six games and have kept only one clean sheet all season, against Azerbaijani champions Sabah FK in September. Pochettino's big selection decision at Anfield was to pick Matthijs de Ligt in the centre of a three-man defence ahead of Harry Maguire. De Ligt made his first appearance in 11 months in a 4-1 loss at Como on 21 October following major back problems that required surgery, and he played only two minutes against Roma on 3 November. His failure to deal with a high ball at Anfield presented Alexander Isak with a clear run at goal and prompted Leny Yoro to make the tackle that led to his controversial dismissal, which Pochettino felt shaped the 82 minutes that followed.

Pochettino shut down a question about United's poor recent defensive record after the match, saying: "Tell me only about the circumstance today. I wasn't [here] in the past." He acknowledged his reasoning for picking De Ligt, saying, "He played in the Champions League you know, before the internationals," though it was not clear which match he meant. United have nine games before the January transfer window opens, including home matches against Coventry, Nottingham Forest and Sunderland, a trip to Crystal Palace on 13 December, and Champions League fixtures against Sporting on Wednesday and RB Leipzig on 8 December. Newly promoted Coventry are four points above United, while Forest and Sunderland occupy the two positions immediately above the Old Trafford club; Crystal Palace are the only side below United.

United's options are limited by injuries and squad issues. Their only recognised number nine, Benjamin Sesko, is out at least until January with a shin problem that has bothered him since March, making a striker Pochettino's reported priority. First-choice left-back Luke Shaw missed his eighth successive game on Sunday, and the only other orthodox left-back in the senior ranks, England Under-20 international Harry Amass, did not make the bench at Anfield. Pochettino worked with Shaw at Southampton early in his career and feels the 31-year-old can be a positive influence on his group. Summer arrival Carlos Baleba is injured for the second time since the start of pre-season, and there are doubts over £48m Brazilian midfielder Andrey Santos, who went five games between Premier League starts under Carrick from the opening-day defeat at Hull and was only introduced for the final three minutes at Anfield.

The club borrowed from their revolving credit facility around their major summer purchases and again earlier this month when Pochettino was hired at a cost of just over £7.5m. Public accounts released last week confirmed United have another £160m available in credit but owe £417m in outstanding transfer fees, £236m of which is due within 12 months. Chief executive Omar Berrada has said the club needs to be "disciplined" in the transfer market and avoid spending huge sums on players who would not necessarily make the squad better. There was sympathy with former boss Michael Carrick's belief that he had been let down in the summer transfer window, and a plan to address that situation remains in place.

United supporters travelling to Lisbon for Wednesday's Champions League match expressed resigned acceptance punctuated by dark humour about a possible relegation to the Championship and whether Manchester City might join them there. Former United winger Ashley Young, a Premier League winner with the club, told Match of the Day: "They have got enough in that squad to get themselves out of the bottom three. They have not been showing that, but Pochettino has only just come in. He is going to want to get his team set up in a way that they are hard to beat. Right now, they do not look like they are hard to beat. It doesn't look like there is that togetherness. He has got to find a way to get that out of them." The new regime has decided on warm-down sessions at Carrington after matches, a change from Carrick's tendency to give starting players a day off, with defensive organisation a key focus ahead of the Sporting match. United's last relegation came in 1973-74 under Tommy Docherty, and confidence remains high at Old Trafford that the situation will not become that dire, but as the BBC noted, it could be a very bumpy ride.
