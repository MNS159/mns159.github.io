UK to host 2035 Women's World Cup, Fifa confirms

Sports desk

England, Northern Ireland, Scotland and Wales will jointly host the 2035 Women's World Cup, Fifa has confirmed, marking the first time a football World Cup has been held in the United Kingdom since the men's tournament in 1966. The four countries were the only bidders for the tournament and were ratified as co-hosts at a specially arranged virtual meeting from Fifa's Zurich headquarters. The tournament will consist of 104 matches contested by 48 teams over 39 days.

The 'All Together' bid proposed using 19 venues: 14 in England, one in Northern Ireland, two in Scotland and two in Wales. English stadiums listed in the bid are Villa Park, The Amex, Ashton Gate, Elland Road, Hill Dickinson Stadium, Emirates Stadium, Selhurst Park, Tottenham Hotspur Stadium, Wembley, Etihad Stadium, St James' Park, the City Ground, the Stadium of Light and Old Trafford. If Manchester United's proposed new Old Trafford stadium does not go ahead, the current stadium would still be considered. The Welsh venues are Cardiff City Stadium and Principality Stadium, with Easter Road in Edinburgh and Hampden Park in Glasgow in Scotland. A redeveloped Windsor Park is set to stage matches in Northern Ireland. Three more stadiums could be added — Birmingham's new Powerhouse Stadium, Wrexham's redeveloped Racecourse Ground and a potential new Chelsea ground — which would take the tournament to 16 cities and 22 venues.

Fifa's special meeting also approved the joint bid by Mexico, the United States, Costa Rica and Jamaica to host the 2031 finals, which will be the first to feature 48 teams. Fifa has yet to decide whether all four UK co-hosts will qualify automatically. The news came on the same day the UK government announced a vision for one million more girls to play, watch or be in leadership roles in sport by the time the tournament takes place.

England failed to win the right to host the men's World Cups in 2006 and 2018, and a proposed UK and Ireland bid for the 2030 tournament was abandoned. England and Scotland hosted matches at the men's Euros in 2021, and England staged the women's European Championship in 2022, which the Lionesses won. England, Scotland, Wales and the Republic of Ireland will jointly host the men's European Championship in 2028.

Football Association chief executive Mark Bullingham said: "Hosting Euro 2022 allowed us to change the game in our country, by creating a generation of aspirational role models and growing demand from women and girls to play. Hosting a World Cup would give us the chance to build on that across all four nations." Football Association of Wales chief executive Noel Mooney called it "a proud day for Welsh football", while Irish Football Association chief executive Patrick Nelson said the event "would be felt in every club and every age group, in every part of the country". Scottish Football Association chief executive Ian Maxwell said hosting would be "the most significant thing to happen to the women's and girls' game in Scotland".

The participation of women and girls in football has risen by 2.3 million since the Women's European Championship was held in England in 2022, but women remain underrepresented in leadership positions: only a quarter of chief executive, chair or performance director roles in major UK sports are held by women, and only 12% of sports federations are led by a woman. Fifa's evaluation, published earlier this month, rated the bid 4.3 out of five. Its contractual framework was rated 'medium risk', and the document said "certain government guarantees contain material qualifications, particularly regarding tax and safety and security" — a reference to a lack of agreement over Fifa's request for tax-free status for all football-related activities. The evaluation added that "certain contractual matters remain subject to further discussion", but that "the bidder's proactive engagement with Fifa and the substantial lead-in time provide a sound basis for resolving these outstanding issues".
