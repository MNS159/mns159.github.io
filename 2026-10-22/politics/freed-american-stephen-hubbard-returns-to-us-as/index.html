Freed American Stephen Hubbard returns to US as GOP lawmakers slam Witkoff's praise of Putin

Politics desk · anchored on Reuters
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum
✓ Facts
  • Stephen Hubbard, a 74-year-old American from Michigan who had been living in Ukraine, was released by Russia after being detained since 2022 and sentenced in 2024 to nearly seven years in a penal colony on accusations of fighting for a Ukrainian defense unit.
  • Hubbard was released on Wednesday and arrived back in the U.S. at Lackland Air Force Base in San Antonio early Thursday; he was reported to be in poor health and expected to receive treatment in Texas.
  • The release occurred as Trump special envoy Steve Witkoff met with Russian President Vladimir Putin in St. Petersburg; Putin's spokesman Dmitry Peskov said Putin acted for humanitarian reasons.
  • President Trump said he personally asked Putin to free Hubbard during talks on settling the Ukraine war and that no prisoner swap was required.
  • After the release, Witkoff publicly called Putin a 'humanitarian,' prompting criticism from Republican lawmakers including Sen. Kevin Cramer (R-N.D.), Rep. Don Bacon, and Sen. John Cornyn.
  • Vice President JD Vance defended Witkoff, saying his job was to secure the release and that he cared less about Witkoff's wording than about bringing an American home.
  • The State Department said the release was welcome and that there are currently no Americans classified as wrongfully detained in Russia.
  • Separately, Trump has lifted sanctions on Russian diesel exports and waived newly enacted sanctions on Russian officials, banks, and state enterprises, while increasing pressure on Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy to be replaced.
○ Analysis

Hubbard's release came as Trump special envoy Steve Witkoff met with Putin in St. Petersburg, with Trump saying he had personally asked Putin to free Hubbard as part of talks on settling the Ukraine war. The episode unfolded alongside other Russia-related policy moves—including eased sanctions and a new diesel import deal—that have drawn scrutiny in Washington. Witkoff's decision to call Putin a 'humanitarian' after the release sparked criticism from several Republican lawmakers, while the administration defended the envoy's focus on securing the release of a detained American over the specific language he used.

Steelman

The episode highlights a tension between the administration's focus on securing concrete outcomes, such as Hubbard's release, and criticism from lawmakers over the rhetoric used toward Putin during the process.

Implications

The backlash from Republican lawmakers signals potential friction for the Trump administration as it pursues further diplomacy with Russia, particularly as sanctions relief and envoy rhetoric face continued congressional scrutiny ahead of the midterms.
