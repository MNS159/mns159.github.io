Macron blames far-left for unrest, bans protests in Paris and other cities

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

French President Emmanuel Macron has blamed far-left groups for stoking violence during weekend protests, as authorities banned demonstrations in several major cities including Paris, Lyon, Lille and Grenoble. Nearly 150 people have been jailed and more than 800 brought before the courts since the riots, which erupted on the fringe of cost-of-living protests and gripped Paris, Lyon, Marseille and Nice. At least 2,500 people were arrested after shops were looted and property destroyed, with the government admitting police had "lost control" during the violence.

Speaking from the Elysee Palace on Thursday, Macron accused "ultra-left" groups of committing "seditious acts" and trying to "bring down the Republic." He vowed the authorities would be "uncompromising" towards "agitators from ultra-left fringe groups," which he blamed for much of the trouble. The Paris Police Prefecture announced that Thursday's protests would be banned in key parts of the city until 8pm, including areas near the presidential palace, both houses of parliament, main train stations, and parts of downtown where looting occurred. A police source told AFP the ban was necessary due to organisers' "inability to prevent violence and vandalism." Authorities also prohibited protests planned for Thursday in Lyon, Lille and Grenoble, and some Saturday marches, including in Lyon, will be banned.

Macron also accused unnamed foreign leaders of exploiting the riots for electoral purposes, saying they were campaigning "at France's expense" with their comments on the violence. Speaking in Doha, he said: "For me, there is no visible foreign interference in the events that took place. On the other hand, there was foreign manipulation of information afterwards, yes, and that gave rise to unacceptable and stupid comments from many people, including foreign leaders... We had people claiming that we had been invaded by hordes of migrants and Islamists, which is completely false." The comments appeared to be aimed at Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, who faces parliamentary elections next week, and US President Donald Trump, for whom mid-term polls fall next month. On Tuesday, Netanyahu said France had been hit by an "invasion of extremist Islam," while Trump blamed the riots on mass immigration, saying "close your borders and take control before it is too late!"

According to the Financial Times, EU regulators are examining possible foreign online interference and disinformation campaigns around the French riots. Unnamed security officials said pro-Russian and far-right social media accounts linked to previous disinformation campaigns have spread violent videos in an attempt to sow more chaos. Iran and Algeria are also suspected of being behind some of the messaging. David Colon, a researcher at France's Sciences Po university, said "pro-Kremlin influencers" linked to the Russian campaign Storm-1516 had "amplified the events and spread a number of false narratives." BBC Verify has reviewed more than 15 examples of fake news videos on social media which researchers say are the work of a Russian disinformation operation known as Matryoshka. The videos feature near-identical branding to real news outlets including Le Monde, Euronews and France 24. Separately, BBC Verify traced one of the most viral AI-generated images, supposedly showing an elderly couple being robbed in Paris, to an obscure website. The director of Infos-Israel.News, Sabrina Hania, told the BBC her team created the image using ChatGPT without a reference image; it was later shared on X without a disclaimer, attracting more than six million views.

The education demonstrations first erupted last month in a high school in Creteil, southeast of Paris, over a lack of staff, and spread to other Paris suburbs and then nationwide. High school students have raised concerns about underfunded schools, dilapidated facilities and staff shortages, particularly in lower-income neighbourhoods. The school demonstrations and blockades have morphed into larger protests over soaring living costs. More than half a million people gathered across France on Saturday in a protest that brought together students and groups denouncing high living costs. Prime Minister Sebastien Lecornu, who met union representatives on Wednesday, said a "massive" security operation was planned for Saturday to prevent a repeat of the violence. Macron said he would convene the country's defence council on Friday to take further action. Union representatives said their meeting with Lecornu did not result in concrete proposals; they are now focusing on another big day of action scheduled for November 5, calling on private and public sector employees to join.
