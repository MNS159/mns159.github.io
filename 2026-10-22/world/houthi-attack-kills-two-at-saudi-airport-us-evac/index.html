Houthi attack kills two at Saudi airport; US evacuates staff, oil surges amid Iran tensions

International & World desk · anchored on Reuters
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts slightly center-left

Two people were killed on Wednesday evening in a Houthi attack on King Fahd International Airport in Dammam, eastern Saudi Arabia, Saudi authorities said. The General Authority of Civil Aviation identified the victims as a Saudi citizen and an Indian resident. The Iran-backed Houthis claimed the airport was among targets they struck with drones and missiles over the previous 48 hours. The U.S. State Department subsequently ordered non-emergency government employees and their family members to depart Saudi Arabia, citing escalating Houthi hostilities against the kingdom, including attacks on civilian airports and critical infrastructure.

Earlier Wednesday, two more people — residents from the Philippines and Nepal — were killed by debris from a drone intercepted over Eastern Province as it attempted to target an Aramco processing facility at the Shaybah oil field, according to the Saudi energy ministry. A contractor’s vehicle was set ablaze. The Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted four drones over Eastern Province on Wednesday night and four more over Riyadh and Medina provinces on Thursday. At least 21 people have now been killed in Houthi attacks on Saudi airports since October, according to Saudi authorities.

The Houthis said they were retaliating for Saudi air strikes on territory they control in western Yemen. Houthi-run media reported at least six killed and 11 injured in air and artillery strikes over the previous 24 hours. The war in Yemen, which began in 2014 when the Houthis ousted the internationally recognized government, escalated in 2015 with a Saudi-led intervention. An informal ceasefire unraveled in July when the Houthis announced a “maritime embargo” and began targeting Saudi airports, oil facilities and Red Sea tankers. In September, the Houthis seized part of Yemen’s southwestern coast near the Bab al-Mandab Strait, a global shipping chokepoint, contributing to rising oil prices.

The U.S. military has also quietly reduced its footprint at posts in the Middle East due to ongoing threats from the Houthis and Iran, officials told CBS News. The U.S. and Israel are planning for a possible attack on Iran in early November, after the U.S. midterm elections, a person briefed on war planning told NPR. Iran’s foreign minister said, “We are just as prepared for diplomacy as we are for war.” The Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps announced a shift to an “offensive and asymmetrical” defense doctrine, threatening to invade neighboring countries that launch attacks against Iran. IRGC spokesman Hossein Mohebi said Iran’s missile stockpile has increased 1.5 times since the 12-day war with Israel in June 2025, and that it is preparing to unveil the “father of all bombs.”

Brent crude oil climbed above $110 a barrel on Thursday amid fears of further disruptions to regional energy supplies. Iran’s Revolutionary Guard navy said it struck four vessels involved in illegal oil transfers in the northern Gulf, and three other vessels hit mines west of the Strait of Hormuz. The International Monetary Fund warned that if the U.S.-Israeli war with Iran continues and energy supply disruptions persist, oil will average $130 a barrel in 2027. Meanwhile, the United Kingdom has offered to deploy jets to defend Saudi Arabia from Houthi attacks, as the conflict threatens to draw in wider regional powers.

Analysts warned that renewed confrontation between the U.S., Israel and Iran is increasingly possible. Negar Mortazavi, senior fellow at the Center for International Policy, told Al Jazeera that Iran wants to establish a new equation: “Any future attack on Iran will come at a much higher cost for the aggressors.” Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, seeking reelection, has campaigned on a pledge to topple Iran’s government. U.S. President Donald Trump said this week that “we are going to have something one way or the other that’ll happen fairly soon,” while also saying Washington could end the war immediately but wants a lasting outcome.
