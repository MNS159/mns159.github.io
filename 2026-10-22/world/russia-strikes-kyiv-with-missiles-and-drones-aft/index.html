Ukraine War

Russia and Ukraine trade missile and drone strikes after agreeing to resume peace talks

International & World desk · anchored on Reuters

Russia and Ukraine traded missile and drone strikes overnight, hours after Moscow agreed to resume US-mediated peace talks. Ukraine said Russia fired at least 21 ballistic missiles at Kyiv in three waves, hitting several houses; Kyiv Mayor Vitaliy Klitschko said six people were injured, while Al Jazeera reported 15 wounded in the capital. Russia said Ukraine launched 630 drones toward Moscow, with Russian officials reporting tallies of intercepted or downed drones varying from 163 to more than 800, and Mayor Sergei Sobyanin said two people were wounded near the capital.

The agreement to resume the three-way talks came after President Vladimir Putin hosted US envoy Steve Witkoff in St Petersburg on Wednesday, a day after Witkoff met Ukraine’s President Volodymyr Zelenskyy and the leaders of Norway and Finland in Oslo. “The other day I was talking to Vladimir Putin about settling his war with Ukraine, which I think we’re getting closer to, by the way, maybe now the closest we’ve been,” President Donald Trump said. Zelenskyy said the Oslo discussions covered “strong” security guarantees for Ukraine and energy ceasefire proposals. Kremlin aide Yuri Ushakov said a new round — the first since the talks were paused in February — could be held around 10 November in Abu Dhabi and must focus on “building a security system for Russia and Ukraine after the end of the conflict.” He cautioned, however, that the Americans’ ideas were not very “useful” and that “these nuances, so to speak, do not entirely satisfy us.”

Zelenskyy said he hoped negotiations would begin early in November, and told Fox News he would accept a deal freezing the war along the current front line. “We call it ‘ceasefire’ or ‘stop killings’ like President Trump called it. Freeze and stop. And of course this is minimum what we need from Russia today,” he said, adding that security guarantees for Ukraine could be discussed afterward. Russia has said it will not end the fighting unless Ukraine cedes more land, limits the size of its forces and is permanently barred from forming alliances — terms Kyiv dismisses as a demand for capitulation. Putin launched the full-scale invasion in 2022, and Moscow controls about 20 percent of Ukrainian territory; Ushakov said Putin had told Witkoff that Russian troops were advancing everywhere and held the “strategic initiative,” a claim Ukraine and its European allies dispute, pointing to a virtual stalemate on the front.

Despite the diplomatic movement, both sides kept up their long-range attacks. Russia said it carried out a “massive strike” overnight on port infrastructure in Ukraine’s Odesa region and on vessels used by the Ukrainian military; in Russia’s Belgorod border region, one man was killed and another wounded by Ukrainian drones, local officials said. The two sides also conducted an exchange of war dead, with Moscow handing over 252 bodies and Ukraine handing over 25, according to Belarus’s Belta state news agency — the kind of step that has accompanied moves toward talks in the past. The war has now ground on for more than four and a half years, defying Trump’s 2024 campaign pledge that he could end it in a day; no breakthrough emerged from three short meetings in Istanbul last year or from three rounds of US-mediated talks in Abu Dhabi and Geneva at the start of this year.

The renewed push came amid sharp friction between Kyiv and Washington. Ukraine says the US has withheld early-warning data on Russian missile launches, throttled the Starlink satellite system used to guide Ukrainian drones, and withheld missile-defense systems bought on its behalf by Europeans; Washington denies any deliberate policy shift. US officials have denied requesting a Starlink slowdown, and a bipartisan group of senators has called for an urgent investigation. Secretary of State Marco Rubio dismissed the intelligence allegations as “scandalous,” and Zelenskyy said after the Oslo talks that intelligence sharing “works now.” Trump, who has called for Zelenskyy to be replaced and said Russia’s actions in Ukraine have been “fine,” has blamed Ukrainian attacks on Russian oil refineries for fuel prices rising since the start of the US-led war on Iran. He also lifted sanctions on Russia — allowing it to release diesel “to the American and global marketplace” — a move criticized by Democratic and some Republican lawmakers.

Witkoff’s visit also produced the release of Stephen Hubbard, a 74-year-old American who had been sentenced to nearly seven years in prison for allegedly fighting on Ukraine’s behalf. Trump said Hubbard was “on his way back home” and that “there was no ‘swap’ necessary,” while Witkoff thanked Putin, calling him a “humanitarian.” Vice President JD Vance defended that description: “I care much less about what Steve said than about the fact that he is bringing an American hostage home to his family.” Zelenskyy mocked the label on X, writing: “Three waves of ballistic missiles have been launched at Kyiv by Russian ‘humanitarians’ since yesterday evening. They used 21 missiles.” He said two people were killed and 34 injured, including five children, in attacks on a dozen Ukrainian regions. Witkoff called the talks “constructive,” while Trump adviser Jared Kushner, joining by video link, said: “There is still hard work ahead, but we have a real opportunity to move beyond this war and build a framework for lasting peace, security, and prosperity for generations to come.”
