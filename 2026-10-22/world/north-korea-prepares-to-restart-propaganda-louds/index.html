North Korea says it will restart propaganda broadcasts along the entire border with South

International & World desk

North Korea said it would put propaganda loudspeakers along the entire border with South Korea on standby within days and begin broadcasting from some time in October, a move that would mark the first cross-border loudspeaker exchange in 16 months. Kim Yo-jong, the powerful sister of North Korean leader Kim Jong-un, made the announcement in a statement carried by the state news agency KCNA, a day after she first said the loudspeakers would operate along the whole border.

Speaking after a border inspection with Marshal Pak Jong-chon, an adviser to Pyongyang's defence ministry, Kim — her brother's top spokesperson on Washington and Seoul — said North Korea's "tactical hunting teams are taking new positions, and within October, the loudspeakers for psychological warfare against the ROK will be in operation along the whole border," using the initials of South Korea's formal name, the Republic of Korea. She also said "new counter means" would be placed on alert, and described the border as "clearly blocked and turned into a fortress more thoroughly." Calling the fortification work a "sacred and great undertaking" supporting Kim Jong-un's policy of permanently severing ties with the South, she quoted the leader as saying the border "should be a permanent line never to be crossed." KCNA initially reported the inspection as occurring Tuesday; Kim later corrected it to early Wednesday. South Korea's Joint Chiefs of Staff said routine maintenance of the North's loudspeakers had been observed before her statements.

South Korean President Lee Jae Myung suspended broadcasts from the southern side days into his presidency in June 2025 to ease tensions with Pyongyang, and the military dismantled the speakers that August. If the broadcasts resume, they would be the first loudspeaker exchange across the border in 16 months. Seoul's Defense Ministry spokesperson, Chung Binna, said Thursday that if North Korea went ahead, "naturally there would have to be a proportional measure," but made clear no decision had been made. Joint Chiefs of Staff Chairman Jin Yong-Sung called Kim's threats "intolerable" and ordered the military to "sternly respond" to potential provocations.

Tensions between the two Koreas have risen since September, when a landmine blast south of the military demarcation line injured three South Korean soldiers, two of them seriously. Seoul and the U.S.-led United Nations Command, which oversees the 1953 armistice that halted but did not end the Korean War, concluded it was a North Korean anti-personnel mine and an armistice violation. Pyongyang denied responsibility and called the incident a "self-staged farce."

Since Kim Jong-un declared in December 2023 that the two Koreas were "two states hostile to each other," North Korea has laid mines, strung barbed wire and erected anti-tank barriers along the border. Seoul says North Korean soldiers have crossed the military demarcation line 50 times this year during the fortification work, prompting South Korean troops to issue warnings over loudspeakers and fire warning shots; the North has dismissed those claims as a "false pretext" and called the broadcasts and shots provocations by "armed rogues." Kim Yo-jong warned that if South Korean warning shots caused "actual harm to our territorial security and our service persons' safety," North Korean border guards "will show no mercy for the armed gangsters bereft of reason."

The standoff comes amid a yearslong stalemate in nuclear negotiations between Pyongyang and Washington, with Kim Jong-un having cut off all diplomacy and cooperation with the South and declared it a permanent enemy. The two Koreas have historically used giant loudspeakers to broadcast propaganda across the border — the North in recent years playing haunting bestial howls and the South airing K-pop music and news criticizing Pyongyang. During the border inspection, Pak Jong-chon accused the South of making "deliberate, premeditated and absurd" claims to shift blame over the rising tensions, denied that North Korean troops had breached the border, and threatened an "overwhelming response" to provocations.
