Russia releases American Stephen Hubbard as Witkoff and Putin discuss Ukraine peace plan

International & World desk

Russian President Vladimir Putin announced the release of jailed American Stephen Hubbard during talks Wednesday with U.S. special envoy Steve Witkoff in St. Petersburg. Putin said the 74-year-old Michigan native was already at the airport, and Witkoff said Hubbard was now "going home to his family." President Donald Trump later said he had made the request directly to Putin during a phone call last week and insisted the U.S. gave nothing in return, writing on Truth Social that "there was no 'swap' necessary for this release."

Hubbard, a former schoolteacher, was taken captive by Russian forces in eastern Ukraine in 2022 after moving there following his marriage to a Ukrainian woman. He was sentenced to seven years in prison at a closed trial in 2024 on mercenary charges he denies, and the U.S. formally designated him wrongfully detained under the Biden administration. State media footage showed a smiling Hubbard shaking hands with Witkoff, and his son, Orthodox priest Father Joseph Coleman, thanked both Trump and Putin. Trump also acknowledged that suspending a new round of sanctions had been a necessary concession, saying, "If sanctions would have been placed, certainly those negotiations would have come to a complete and total halt."

The release came during the ninth meeting between Witkoff and Putin, which centered on a potential settlement of the nearly 4 1/2-year war in Ukraine. Witkoff said he had brought "some ideas, some sort of peace deal between Russia and Ukraine" for Putin to review. The Kremlin said the proposals "lacked substance" and would not be of real use, but Putin agreed "in principle" to resume trilateral peace talks with Russian, Ukrainian and U.S. delegates after November 10, with his foreign affairs adviser Yuri Ushakov saying such talks should focus on "building a security system for Russia and Ukraine after the conflict ends." Ushakov added that a possible first-ever meeting of the leaders of China, Russia and the United States in Beijing "is being prepared."

Hubbard's release followed a series of recent U.S. gestures toward Moscow against the backdrop of the negotiations. Trump has agreed to buy previously sanctioned Russian diesel fuel, placed a waiver on a congressional sanctions package he had signed into law, pressed Ukraine to stop striking Russian oil facilities and called for Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy's ouster. Putin, meanwhile, announced that the first shipments of Russian diesel, 60,000 tons, would head to the U.S. in the coming days. The White House has framed the engagement with Russia as part of a wider plan to keep Moscow involved in peace negotiations and in the interest of American consumers.

Zelenskyy, who met Witkoff in Norway a day before the St. Petersburg talks, called Wednesday for freezing the front line as a first step toward ceasefire negotiations. "Right now we have to freeze, and I think this is (the) real way to end this war. We call it ceasefire... freeze and stop," he said. German Chancellor Friedrich Merz said Berlin was supporting Ukraine to end the war as quickly as possible, adding that any capitulation by Ukraine would be "a capitulation by all of us." The European Union, which had delegates from several member states at the Oslo talks on Tuesday, said it expected to be debriefed by Washington after Wednesday's meeting.

Witkoff planned to return to Oslo for further consultations, Norwegian Prime Minister Jonas Gahr Store said, adding, "I think we may have entered a phase now where the Russians are also looking for opportunities for diplomacy." While the Kremlin's agreement to revive trilateral talks marked a shift after Putin had refused such discussions, its characterization of Witkoff's proposals as lacking substance left open the question of whether the renewed process would yield concrete progress toward ending the war.
