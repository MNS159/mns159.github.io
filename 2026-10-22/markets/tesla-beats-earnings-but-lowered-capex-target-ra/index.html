Tesla beats earnings estimates but lowered capex target raises AI and robotics doubts

Financial & Markets desk

Tesla reported better-than-expected third-quarter results after the bell on Wednesday, even as automotive revenue declined. The company posted adjusted earnings per share of 48 cents, beating the 43-cent estimate from analysts polled by LSEG, while revenue came in at $28.05 billion against a $27.9 billion forecast. The stock slid more than 3% in extended trading.

Automotive revenue fell 3% from a year earlier to $20.56 billion, and energy revenue dropped 16% to $2.88 billion. Offsetting those declines, services and other revenue climbed 33% to $4.61 billion. Net income attributable to common shareholders plunged 29% to $787 million, or 22 cents a share, from $1.11 billion, or 32 cents a share, a year earlier. Tesla attributed the weaker automotive results to lower average sales prices and trouble securing battery supplies to fulfill a backlog of orders.

The drop in profitability reflects a surge in spending on Optimus humanoid robots and other artificial intelligence initiatives. Capital expenditures more than doubled from a year earlier to $3.73 billion, and free cash flow fell 62% year over year to $1.53 billion after turning negative in the second quarter.

Tesla said uptake of its FSD (Supervised) partially automated driving system, sold for $99 per month in the U.S., grew by 20 percentage points from the second quarter. Active FSD subscriptions increased 67% from a year earlier to 1.74 million. Ashok Elluswamy, Tesla's vice president of Autopilot, said the company plans to give some customer cars access to its forthcoming FSD (Unsupervised) system around the second quarter of next year. On the robotaxi front, CEO Elon Musk said the driverless fleet, which now includes 362 Model Y and 449 Cybercab vehicles authorized for driverless operations in Texas, will start to contribute to financials in 2027, though he added it could become noticeable by the second quarter of next year and a big deal by the third or fourth quarter.

The earnings report came a day after Musk told investors on SpaceX's quarterly call that it would be a 'good idea' for his two companies to merge and said the boards at both have formed special committees to discuss a possible tie-up. 'I feel like I'm tap dancing through a minefield,' Musk said on that call, responding to a question about a potential merger. On the Tesla call, he said only, 'I think I said everything that should be said yesterday.' Tesla is also building a semiconductor factory with SpaceX and Intel in Grimes County, Texas, and the companies have other areas of overlap, including work on Tesla's recently announced Roadster refresh, intensifying speculation about a combination.

On Optimus, Musk said the company is 'making good progress' on the AI systems that control the robots but that there is 'still quite a lot of work to do with regard to the AI and the robot for it to be truly useful for open-ended tasks.' He expects current issues around electromechanical design, reliability, usage duration, and AI to be 'solved around the end of next year.' Tesla reported vehicle deliveries of 486,532 for the quarter, about 2% below the year-ago number. The company said auto sales in Canada, Japan, Taiwan and Australia hit new records, though it has seen meager growth in its core market as Chinese EV makers such as BYD and Xiaomi gain popularity in Asia and Europe.
