Meta earnings beat overshadowed by rising AI spending and nonvoting share overhang

Financial & Markets desk

Meta reported better-than-expected third-quarter earnings on Wednesday, with revenue of $64.6 billion—a 26% increase from a year ago and above the $63.37 billion analysts had expected—and GAAP earnings per share of $3.83, beating the $3.56 consensus estimate. The company also issued strong guidance for the current period, forecasting fourth-quarter revenue of $73 billion to $76 billion, with a midpoint of $74.5 billion that topped the $73.73 billion analysts projected. The stock initially rose about 2.5% in extended trading after the release, but fell 2% in premarket trading Thursday as other concerns weighed on investors.

The strong quarter was powered by Meta's core advertising business, which benefited from AI-driven recommendations, higher ad volume, and rising ad prices. CEO Mark Zuckerberg highlighted the launch of Muse and Muse Wonder, Meta's first frontier model, as major milestones toward delivering 'personal superintelligence for everyone.' The Muse AI personal assistant, which debuted in September, helped Meta end the month with its best performance on Wall Street since 2022. Meta and its AI chief Alexandr Wang have continued rolling out new foundation models and updates at a rapid pace, including Muse Wonder, released last Monday.

However, the positive earnings news was overshadowed by Meta's announcement that it is creating a new class of nonvoting Class C common stock, effectively a 2-for-1 stock split. Under the plan, existing shareholders will receive one nonvoting Class C share for every Class A or Class B share they hold, with trading slated to begin in January 2027. The move is designed in part to keep founder Mark Zuckerberg working in California as the 5% billionaire's tax looms, and it allows him to raise external capital for Meta's AI build-out without diluting his personal voting control over the company.

Wall Street reacted negatively to the unexpected overhang. JPMorgan analyst Doug Anmuth advised Meta to issue the stock quickly so that focus can return to the company's product momentum. 'The sooner the better in our view as it would remove the overhang and the Street now knows it will have non-voting shares in Jan'27, and Meta can still do a substantial equity raise before then without even coming close to impacting Mark's voting control,' Anmuth said. He added that despite 'some near-term noise in Meta shares,' he is encouraged by the core advertising business, the outlook for strong overall revenue growth in 2027, and the increasingly clear path to superintelligence with the Muse series of models and products.

The combination of rising AI spending anxiety—exacerbated by similar concerns after Tesla's earnings—and the new nonvoting share overhang contributed to a slide in AI stocks following the reports. Meta's stock decline in premarket trading reflected investor unease even as the company posted a revenue beat and strong guidance. The tension between Meta's promising new AI businesses and the financial mechanics of funding them without ceding control underscores the challenges facing the company as it pursues its ambitious AI agenda.
