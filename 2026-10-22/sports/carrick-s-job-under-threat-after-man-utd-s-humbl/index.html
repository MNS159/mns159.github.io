Carrick's job under threat after Man Utd's humbling by Como; Fernandes criticises tactics

Sports desk

Michael Carrick is set to remain in charge of Manchester United for Sunday's Premier League match against Bournemouth, but senior figures at the club are increasingly concerned by performances and results after Wednesday's humiliating 4-1 Champions League defeat by Italian side Como. The loss extended United's winless run to seven matches in all competitions, their worst such streak since 2015-16. United have won just two of their 11 games this season, with only one clean sheet kept across all fixtures.

After the defeat, captain Bruno Fernandes publicly questioned Carrick's tactics, telling TNT Sports: "It's a decision from the staff to not press man-to-man and sometimes leave space behind and try to get the ball, but sometimes when you give too much the ball to these teams they get confidence." Asked if Carrick got his tactics wrong, Fernandes replied: "That's on the manager and staff to see it, not me." Carrick responded by saying: "There's a little bit of whatever system you play, whatever tactics you play, if you don't run, and you don't run forward, and you don't run back, you aren't going to win."

Fernandes' comments have drawn widespread criticism. Former Liverpool vice-captain Jamie Carragher called his attitude "an absolute disgrace" and "not what a Manchester United captain should be", while former Chelsea midfielder Joe Cole described the public questioning as "sacrilege". Former Leicester and Celtic goalkeeper Kasper Schmeichel said Fernandes "probably should not be saying these things because it's only going to add to the problems". The outburst follows similar remarks from defender Lisandro Martinez after a previous defeat, for which he later apologised.

Despite the mounting pressure, Carrick is expected to remain in charge for Sunday's match, with United's hierarchy divided over whether a change would help. Some believe the squad, which one source described as a "Champions League squad", should be performing far better, while others note that chopping and changing managers in recent years has failed to arrest the club's decline. United have already begun identifying possible replacements, with Eddie Howe, Antonio Conte and Julian Nagelsmann all understood to be interested in the role. Aston Villa's Unai Emery and Brighton's Fabian Hurzeler are also highly regarded but would be difficult to extract from their current clubs.

The team have been hampered by injuries and a lack of form, with players failing to reach expected levels. Accusations of a lack of passion, desire and intensity have dogged United all season. The proximity of Sunday's Premier League game makes an immediate change difficult, but a clear midweek following their EFL Cup exit could provide time for the hierarchy to take stock. United have yet to confirm arrangements for Friday's planned news conference to preview the Bournemouth match, adding to the sense of uncertainty that continues to surround the club.
