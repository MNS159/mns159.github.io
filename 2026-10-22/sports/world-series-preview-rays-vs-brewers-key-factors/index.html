World Series preview: Rays vs. Brewers – key factors and predictions

Sports desk

The top-seeded Tampa Bay Rays (98 wins) will face the top-seeded Milwaukee Brewers (103 wins) in the World Series, beginning Friday at 8 p.m. ET in Milwaukee. Neither franchise has ever won a World Series title. The Rays swept the Yankees in the ALDS and rallied from a 2-1 deficit to beat the Guardians in the ALCS, while the Brewers lost just one game this postseason, sweeping the back-to-back champion Dodgers in the NLCS after dispatching the Padres.

Though the Brewers are favored – they won more regular-season games, posted a better run differential, and faced a tougher schedule – the Rays may have an edge at the front of the rotation. Tampa Bay's Drew Rasmussen and ALCS MVP Freddy Peralta have a 1.19 ERA and 3.50 K/BB ratio in 37⅔ postseason innings, compared to Milwaukee's Jacob Misiorowski (NL Cy Young favorite) and Logan Henderson, who have a combined 3.12 ERA and 1.89 K/BB in 17⅓ innings. There are concerns that the younger Brewers arms are hitting a workload wall: Misiorowski's combined innings jumped from 141⅓ in 2025 to 183 this season, and Henderson's from 103 to 131⅓. Rasmussen and Peralta are older and more experienced with high workloads.

The Rays have been a different team since the trade deadline, going 40-20 (including 7-2 in the playoffs). Key additions include Peralta, who posted a 131 ERA+ in 10 starts after being acquired from the Mets; catcher Liam Hicks, who batted .288/.379/.440 for Tampa Bay and became Kevin Cash's No. 3 hitter; and right-handed reliever Tyler Wells, who improved the setup depth.

Junior Caminero is the best hitter in the series, according to sources. The 22-year-old third baseman played in every game this season, posting a 144 OPS+ with 42 homers after hitting 45 in 2025. He has top-tier quality-of-contact indicators and is the most likely player to take over a game or series with his bat. However, he has not yet erupted in the postseason, entering the ALCS clincher 7-for-30 with two RBI and three extra-base hits before his Game 6 homer.

In position-by-position matchups, the Brewers hold edges at catcher (William Contreras, who leads all LCS players with a 1.157 OPS this postseason) and first base (Jake Bauers, NLCS MVP and the best hitter on baseball's best team during the regular season). At second base, Brice Turang is a sensational defender with a well-rounded offensive game. At shortstop, the Rays' Taylor Walls is the best defensive shortstop in the sport, while Milwaukee's rookie Cooper Pratt has impressed with his arm and contact hitting. In left field, Chandler Simpson's slash-and-dash style is unique, but Jackson Chourio remains dangerous despite a .171/.211/.343 line in October.

The World Series pits two teams that have never won a title, with the Rays as underdogs despite a 98-win season. As one source notes, 'underdog does not mean fated to lose,' and a Rays series win would be barely a mild upset. The outcome may hinge on the front-of-rotation duel, Caminero's bat, and whether Milwaukee's young arms can sustain their performance through a potentially long series.
