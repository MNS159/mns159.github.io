Oura files for IPO; Nvidia's Huang pushes back on AI doomsday talk

Financial & Markets desk

Health tracking ring maker Oura on Monday publicly filed paperwork for its initial public offering, seeking to raise upward of $2.2 billion. The company plans to sell 50 million shares priced between $40 and $44 apiece, according to the filing.

Oura's revenue in the nine months ending June 30 reached $1.2 billion, up from $697 million in the same period last year. The company posted a net loss of $924 million for that span. Oura said it sold 4.1 million Oura Rings during the period, up from 1.8 million in the same period last year.

In a separate development, Nvidia (NVDA) CEO Jensen Huang pushed back against AI doomsday talk during an interview with CBS News on Sunday. The interview follows an essay by Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei calling for a slowdown in the development of frontier AI systems.

Huang called fears that AI will kill all humans "completely false," saying "it is not grounded on facts; it's not grounded on science." He also acknowledged that AI researchers' concerns about building safe products are not wrong.
