AMD hits $1 trillion market cap for the first time

Financial & Markets desk

Advanced Micro Devices shares surged 9% on Monday, pushing the stock to a record high and topping a $1 trillion market capitalization for the first time. The chipmaker's shares hit an intraday high of $613.92, boosting the company's value just above the trillion-dollar mark and making AMD the 14th U.S. company to reach that milestone.

The move extends a five-day winning streak during which AMD shares have gained about 24%. The recent rally has been a welcome sight for investors, who saw the stock fall last month even after the company reported second-quarter earnings that beat expectations. AMD reported revenue of $11.54 billion in Q2, up 50% from $7.69 billion a year earlier, with its artificial intelligence chips at the heart of that growth. The company's Data Center unit posted sales of $6.7 billion, up 107% year over year.

AMD is having a blockbuster year, with shares up more than 180%. Despite that gain, it remains well behind AI chip leader Nvidia, which boasts a market cap of about $5.4 trillion.

The AI buildout that has fueled the surge in chip names this year shows few signs of slowing, despite public backlash against data centers and concerns about the underlying safety of large language models. AMD CEO Lisa Su said last month on the company's earnings call that it expected to double data center sales in 2027. Last week, Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang offered another look at silicon demand, saying he expected his company to double the number of chips it sells next year.
