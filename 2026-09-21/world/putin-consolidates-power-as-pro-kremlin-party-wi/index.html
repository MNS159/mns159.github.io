Russia-Ukraine War

Putin consolidates power as pro-Kremlin party wins election amid Ukraine war

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts slightly center-left

Russia's ruling United Russia party won a record 355 seats in the 450-seat State Duma in parliamentary elections, according to preliminary figures, consolidating President Vladimir Putin's grip on power in the first such vote since the 2022 full-scale invasion of Ukraine. The result gives United Russia a super-majority larger than its previous record of 343 seats set in 2016.

The new parliament will include 49 deputies who took part in the war against Ukraine, according to United Russia's general secretary Vladimir Yakushev, who initially said 86 veterans were likely elected before revising the number. Putin's spokesman Dmitry Peskov said the vote demonstrated "the highest level of consolidation within Russian society around the head of state." Other parties that support the conflict also won seats: the Communists came second with 40 seats, the ultra-nationalist LDPR won 25, and the New People party won 20.

The elections were widely criticized. Russia's supreme court last month banned the only party opposing the war, Yabloko, from participating, leaving liberal voters with no option to express discontent. Internal criticism of the conflict is banned, and anti-war bloggers have been arrested and jailed. Local politician Boris Nadezhdin, who previously ran on a peace ticket, was barred from September's elections and accused of being a "foreign agent." In Chechnya, leader Ramzan Kadyrov, a Putin ally, was re-elected and said to have won more than 99% of the votes.

Western governments denounced the polls, held in Russian-occupied areas of Ukraine, as a "sham." The European Union issued a statement declaring the elections "non-applicable on Ukrainian territory." EU top diplomat Kaja Kallas called the vote "another major setback for Russian democracy," writing on X: "The Kremlin silenced dissent and shut out international election monitors. The veneer of democracy is all that remains."

Russia's elections chief, Ella Pamfilova, said United Russia won a resounding victory with 57.83%, with 95% of ballots counted, up from the 49.8% it won in the last parliamentary election, in 2021. She praised voters for resisting "psychological pressure."

The elections came amid speculation that Putin may announce a large mobilization campaign this autumn to inject fresh momentum into Russia's bogged-down military campaign. Ukraine's President Volodymyr Zelenskyy has suggested the Kremlin wants to recruit another 300,000 troops. Russia has escalated its bombardment, striking Kyiv and other cities with attack drones; overnight, Zelenskyy described a "brutal, targeted attack" on Zaporizhzhia that damaged a nine-storey residential building, a university building, a shopping centre, and a boiler house, injuring five people, while a woman was killed in a drone strike in the Kharkiv region. Russia has also intensified its offensive in Ukraine's eastern Donbas region, targeting Kramatorsk and Sloviansk, without breaking through, while Ukrainian commanders reported a counteroffensive near Lyman. Zelenskyy said: "Russia is not scaling back its escalatory actions and is signalling a readiness to expand the war. All this is happening against the backdrop of global stability already having been shattered by this war. We must continue to pressure the aggressor to bring this war to an end."
