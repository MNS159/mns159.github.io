Russia-Ukraine War

Ukraine launches largest drone attack on Moscow as Russia concludes elections

International & World desk · anchored on AP
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

Ukraine fired what Moscow described as its largest ever drone attack on the Russian capital and surrounding region on Sunday, the third and final day of tightly controlled parliamentary elections in Russia. Moscow Mayor Sergei Sobyanin said 450 drones heading for the city had been shot down in what he called “the most massive” attack. At least two people were killed and 20 injured, according to the region's governor, Andrei Vorobyev.

The attack hit the Moscow oil refinery, which supplies more than a third of the region’s fuel, and a residential building where a 44-year-old woman was killed. An elderly man died after a private house was hit in the Orekhovo-Zuevo district. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky confirmed Kyiv had struck a “key” Russian oil facility and a logistics site, saying the strikes represented “billions of dollars that sustain the war machine.” Ukrainian defense officials said three key units at the refinery were damaged. Moscow’s airports Sheremetyevo and Vnukovo imposed temporary restrictions on flights, though both resumed operations by Sunday morning.

The attack came as Russia held a parliamentary election for the 450-seat State Duma, the first since its full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022. With more than 71 percent of ballots counted, President Vladimir Putin’s United Russia party was set to win 57.5 percent of the vote, data from the Central Election Commission showed. All parties on the ballot have broadly pledged loyalty to Putin; the liberal Yabloko party, the only registered party opposed to the war, was barred from standing. Sobyanin said the drone attack was “an attempt to disrupt” the vote. Russian state media reported that an election official was among two people killed by a drone in a Moscow-held part of Kherson.

Ukraine also reported deadly Russian strikes over the weekend. In the Kyiv region, a mother and her two young children were killed in an attack on the city of Fastiv, and a child injured in another attack near Kyiv died in hospital. In the Sumy region, four people were killed when a drone hit their car. Zelensky said Russia struck a monastery in the Kherson region, killing one clergy member and injuring four others. On Monday, a Russian drone struck a home in Ukraine’s Kharkiv region, killing a 45-year-old woman and seriously injuring her 13-year-old son. Zelensky said Russia struck 10 regions overnight, calling the attacks “vicious” and accusing Moscow of signaling it is “ready to expand the war.”

President Donald Trump called Monday for an end to the war, posting on Truth Social that “Russia has unfortunately lost control of its Diesel Oil Industry” due to Ukraine’s attacks on its refineries. “This ridiculous and never ending War with Ukraine must be ended,” he wrote. He is scheduled to meet Zelensky in New York on the sidelines of the United Nations General Assembly. Zelensky said the meeting “could change a lot” and that “there is diplomatic momentum.” The two leaders agreed on the meeting after a phone conversation, Zelensky said. The Trump administration signed a sanctions bill on Friday that allows the president to impose 100 percent tariffs on major purchasers of Russian oil and gas, including China and India.
