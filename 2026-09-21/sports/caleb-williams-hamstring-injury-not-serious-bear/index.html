Caleb Williams hamstring injury not serious; Bears QB status updated

Sports desk

Chicago Bears quarterback Caleb Williams suffered a right hamstring injury during a first-down scramble in the fourth quarter of the team's 9-3 loss to the Minnesota Vikings on Sunday. The 24-year-old reached for the back of his leg before sliding to the ground in pain, received medical attention on the field, and was eventually carted to the locker room while flashing a thumbs-up to the crowd. He was ruled out for the remainder of the game.

Initial reports indicate optimism that the injury is not serious. According to Mike Florio of Pro Football Talk, the early diagnosis is a Grade 1 hamstring strain, the mildest grade. Williams is scheduled to undergo an MRI on Monday to determine the full extent of the injury. Bears coach Ben Johnson told reporters after the game that Williams was in "good spirits" and expressed relief that the knee was not involved. "I'm actually happy it's nothing with the knee," Johnson said. "That was my first inclination. But yeah, the hamstring could be pretty damaging as well. So we'll see how bad it is tomorrow and we'll go from there."

Even with a Grade 1 strain, Williams could miss at least one game. The Bears are scheduled to play the Philadelphia Eagles on Monday Night Football in Week 3. Hamstring injuries can be tricky for mobile quarterbacks, and the team may opt for caution to avoid re-aggravation. If Williams is unable to play, backup Tyson Bagent will start. Bagent, 26, replaced Williams on Sunday and completed 5 of 9 passes for 54 yards. He joined the Bears as an undrafted free agent in 2023 and has started four games in his career, completing 66.4% of his passes for 917 yards, three touchdowns and six interceptions. The Bears signed him to a two-year, $10 million extension in 2025 to be the long-term backup.

Behind Bagent on the depth chart is veteran Case Keenum, 38, who has 66 career starts and is best known for leading the Vikings to an NFC championship game appearance in 2017. The Bears also have practice-squad quarterback John Moss, an undrafted free agent out of Louisville, who could be called up for additional depth. Williams has started all 36 games (including playoffs) since being drafted No. 1 overall in 2024. This is his first significant NFL injury.

The injury occurred on a non-contact play as Williams took a bootleg dropback and outran Vikings linebacker Andrew Van Ginkel to the edge. Several reporters noted that Williams walked through the locker room after the game without crutches and with only a slight limp, a positive sign. The MRI results Monday will provide a more concrete timeline for his return.
