Brewers' Chourio makes MLB history before turning 23

Sports desk

Milwaukee Brewers outfielder Jackson Chourio has become the first player in Major League Baseball history to record three 20-home run, 20-stolen base seasons before turning 23 years old. The 22-year-old achieved the milestone in his third big-league season, a feat confirmed by MLB's Sarah Langs on social media. The Brewers entered Sunday with the best record in baseball, and Chourio has been a central figure in that success.

Chourio debuted at age 20 in 2024 as the No. 2 prospect in baseball and signed an eight-year, $82 million extension with the Brewers before ever playing a major league game. He posted 21 home runs, 79 RBIs and 22 steals in 148 games as a rookie, followed by 21 homers and 21 steals in 2025. This season, despite missing the first month after fracturing his hand during the World Baseball Classic, he has 24 home runs, 70 RBIs and an .825 OPS over 121 games, along with 21 stolen bases, 27 doubles and 89 runs scored. His 125 OPS+ and 4.4 wins above replacement are also career bests.

Chourio has also played Gold Glove-caliber defense in left field. Among MLB left fielders this season, he ranks third in home runs and runs, seventh in RBIs, and fourth in both OPS and batting average. He joins Julio Rodríguez and Bobby Witt Jr. as the only players to produce 20-20 seasons in each of their first three big-league campaigns, but Chourio is the only one to accomplish that before his 23rd birthday.

In 12 career postseason games, Chourio has been elite, slashing .341/.362/.682 with four home runs, tying him for the most postseason home runs in Brewers franchise history. His October production underscores his value as a cornerstone player for Milwaukee.

"Jackson Chourio is the first player in MLB history with three 20-HR/20-SB seasons before turning 23 years old," Langs wrote. Now 22, Chourio is younger than most top National League Rookie of the Year candidates yet is already in his third season and making history. With his continued improvement—including a 25% above-average offensive output (125 OPS+)—he has the potential to win a Most Valuable Player Award later in his career, according to Sports Illustrated.

Sources: Yahoo Sports·Miami Herald·Sports Illustrated
