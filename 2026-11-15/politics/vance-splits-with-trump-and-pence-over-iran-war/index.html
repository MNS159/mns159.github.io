Vance splits with Trump and Pence over Iran war blame for GOP midterm losses

Politics desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum
✓ Facts
  • In a Nov. 4 post on X, VP JD Vance said 'the reason we lost is the war' and cited inflation, America First priorities, and the U.S.-Israel relationship as other voter concerns driving GOP midterm defections.
  • Republicans lost majorities in both chambers of Congress in the midterms, with at least 30 GOP House members losing their races.
  • Asked by reporters Sunday whether Vance's comments strained their relationship, Trump said 'No, not at all,' and has said he does not disagree that the Iran war fueled GOP losses.
  • Appearing on NBC's 'Meet the Press,' former VP Mike Pence said Vance was 'wrong,' calling him a 'good man' but arguing the war itself wasn't the problem — the administration's failure to finish it was.
  • Rep. Randy Fine (R-Fla.) told Fox's LiveNOW on Nov. 8 he will not support Vance in a 2028 primary, comparing Vance's remarks to 1932 Germany-era rhetoric; Vance's spokesman said he 'did not blame Jews' for the losses.
  • On Thursday, Trump posted on X directing Vance to focus on alleged corruption in Democratic-led states rather than Iran negotiations, writing 'We don't need a deal with Iran.'
  • The Wall Street Journal reported Nov. 7 that some Vance allies urged him to consider sitting out 2028, while The Washington Post reported Friday that others close to him still expect him to run.
  • Rep. Marlin Stutzman (R-Ind.) said he is 'all in' for Secretary of State Marco Rubio as a potential 2028 contender; Gov. Ron DeSantis and Sen. Ted Cruz were also named as possible alternatives to Vance.
○ Analysis

The dispute centers on how Republicans should explain their loss of House and Senate majorities in the midterms, with Vance pinning blame on the Iran war while other prominent Republicans, including Pence, offer competing diagnoses. Because Vance is widely viewed as Trump's heir apparent for 2028, the disagreement has taken on added significance as a proxy fight over the party's direction and over who might lead it next. Trump's own position — publicly backing Vance's assessment while also redirecting Vance's attention away from Iran diplomacy toward domestic political messaging — adds ambiguity about the depth of any rift between the two.

Steelman

Beneath the Iran-war blame game lies a larger, more consequential fight over who controls the GOP's post-midterm narrative and, by extension, its 2028 direction — making this less a policy dispute than an early proxy battle for the party's future leadership.

Implications

How this intra-party disagreement is resolved — whether Trump publicly sides more firmly with Vance or lets rivals like Pence, Rubio, DeSantis, or Cruz gain traction — will likely shape the emerging 2028 Republican primary field and influence how the party frames its foreign policy and midterm losses heading into the next election cycle.
