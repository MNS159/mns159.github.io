Australia thrash New Zealand to win fourth consecutive Rugby League World Cup

Sports desk

Australia defeated New Zealand 32-12 in the men's Rugby League World Cup final on Sunday, securing a fourth consecutive title and a 10th victory in the past 11 tournaments dating back to 1975. The Kangaroos ran in six tries to overpower the Kiwis in a one-sided final that never materialised into the gripping contest many had anticipated. The victory gave Australia a record-extending 13th Paul Barriere Trophy overall.

The result capped a tournament in which Australia faced persistent questions about the strength of their pack, following narrow wins against New Zealand (24-22) and Fiji (36-22) earlier in the competition. Superstar full-back Reece Walsh, who had been visibly unwell after the opening match following an eight-week layoff due to an ankle injury, delivered an impassioned response to the criticism. "I feel we got disrespected," Walsh said. "The chat from everybody coming into this tournament, everyone wrote us off, talking about how good all the other teams are and how good their coaches are."

Walsh, who acknowledged he was unfit for the first game after his rehab, praised head coach Kevin Walters for his belief. "It was hurting that first game. Coming off eight weeks of rehab, I was unfit, I didn't touch a ball but the belief Kev had in me, all the boys made my job so easy. To be out here now and win for Australia, I am so proud." Walters later paid tribute to veteran full-back James Tedesco, who captained Australia to victory four years ago and announced his international retirement immediately after the match. Tedesco, a three-time NRL Premiership winner and two-time World Cup champion, was brought on as a mark of respect and awarded the Clive Churchill medal.

New Zealand head coach Stacey Jones was blunt about his team's performance. "It was our worst performance. We got off to a bad start and could not fight our way out of it. When you make errors and your discipline is poor and give them all that ball, no matter what team you are it is just too hard." The Kiwis, appearing in their first final since 2013, were unable to deliver a twist in a tournament that also saw Australia's women's team win a record fourth World Cup title.

The 2026 World Cup, co-hosted by Australia, New Zealand and Papua New Guinea, has drawn mixed reviews. The men's tournament was cut from 16 teams to 10, a decision that drew criticism from former England international Ryan Hall, who said the reduced format lost the "festival" feel of a World Cup. The group stage format, which featured one group of four and another of six with no quarter-finals, was widely criticised. England's men were the major losers: they won two of three group games but still failed to reach the semi-finals. Average attendance rose to 21,360 from 9,811 in 2022, helped by lower ticket prices (A$19, about £9.74) and strong support for Pacific nations. However, viewer access was uneven: Australia had free-to-air coverage, while UK fans could only watch the wheelchair final on BBC iPlayer and needed a Premier Sports subscription for other matches.
