Ebola outbreak reaches 11,145 cases, 5,437 deaths; Africa CDC says situation still out of control

Health & Science desk · anchored on Reuters

The ongoing Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC) and neighboring countries has reached 11,145 cases and 5,437 deaths, according to the latest update from the U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) on November 13, 2026. The Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention (Africa CDC) declared the outbreak six months ago and now says it remains out of control. The virus driving the outbreak is Bundibugyo virus, a type of Ebola virus, which has been reported in DRC, Kenya, and Nigeria.

Outside DRC, Kenya's Ministry of Health has confirmed nine cases and two deaths as of October 23. The initial case, reported October 6, involved a man who traveled from DRC through Uganda to Nairobi, where he was hospitalized and died in an isolation unit. Subsequent cases, including the second death, occurred among relatives and health care providers in close contact with him, all of whom were already in quarantine when diagnosed. Kenya has reported no community spread, and all cases were in Nairobi. Nigeria reported its first case in the outbreak on October 20, with testing confirming Bundibugyo virus. Uganda reported 21 confirmed and probable cases and three deaths overall; the last confirmed case in Uganda recovered and was released from care on July 16, 2026. France's single patient was released on July 4, and neither country has reported active cases.

The CDC reports that no Ebola cases associated with this outbreak have been reported in the United States and considers the risk to the American public low. However, travel restrictions remain in place: travelers who have been in DRC within 21 days of their flight cannot board commercial flights to the U.S. Travelers from Uganda or South Sudan must enter through designated airports for enhanced screening. Since 11:59 PM Eastern Standard Time on November 3, all passengers boarding an international flight to the U.S. must attest they have not been in DRC during the 21 days before departure. The CDC recommends avoiding all travel to Ituri and Nord-Kivu provinces and avoiding nonessential travel to Haut-Uele and Tshopo provinces. Approximately 500 CDC staff are involved in the response, including more than 120 people deployed to affected countries.

The CDC cites limited health infrastructure, ongoing conflict, and frequent cross-border population movement as barriers to containment. Violence against health care workers and shortages of personal protective equipment are leading to infections among health care workers treating patients. Mistrust of government and misinformation further complicate outreach. For U.S. clinicians, the CDC directs providers to its clinical guidance for Ebola disease, screening and diagnosis resources for viral hemorrhagic fevers, and infection prevention and control recommendations for U.S. hospitals. A travel history covering the prior 21 days remains central to identifying patients at risk.
