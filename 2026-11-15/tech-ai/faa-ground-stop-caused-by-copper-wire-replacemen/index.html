FAA ground stop caused by copper wire replacement, officials say

Tech / AI desk

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said Saturday that a nationwide ground stop ordered by the Federal Aviation Administration on Friday was caused by routine maintenance work to replace outdated copper wire with a fiber optic line at a remote FAA facility. The upgrade, performed by longtime contractor L3Harris, led to a newly installed router flooding other routers in the Air Traffic Control system with more than 1,000 connection attempts every four to five seconds, disrupting radar feeds and communication with planes. The ground stop was only the third such nationwide halt of the 21st century; aviation data firm Cirium reported more than 8,200 flights were canceled Friday evening, with at least another 3,000 canceled Saturday as effects continued after the stop was lifted.

Duffy told reporters the work was "routine maintenance" rather than part of a major modernization effort, noting the copper wire was so old it was being discontinued. He said the root cause remains unclear, citing possible faulty hardware, a misconfigured router, or "incompetence" on the part of L3Harris. FAA acting CTO Rebecca Guy said the agency saw two network locations pinging at a very high rate and disabled them to stabilize the network; a root cause analysis is now underway. Asked why the upgrade was performed at 3 p.m. on a Friday rather than late at night, Duffy said remote switchovers occur during the day, though he acknowledged there will be a reevaluation of when such work is done.

L3Harris said in a statement that it has completed more than 70% of the planned airspace communications conversion from legacy copper wires to a fiber optic system in coordination with the FAA and its telco partners, with safety and reliability as top priorities. The company said it is supporting the FAA's investigation and takes the ground stop and its impact on travelers, airlines, and air traffic operations seriously.

Duffy used the press conference to call on Congress for additional funding to modernize the Air Traffic Control system, saying lawmakers have given him only a "partial payment" to complete necessary work. The ground stop came a day after the FAA announced the rollout of an AI tool for air traffic controllers, but FAA administrator Bryan Bedford said there is "zero evidence of anything nefarious and zero evidence of anything AI-related," emphasizing the issue is a defective piece of equipment. Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin, asked earlier whether AI caused the ground stop, replied, "We call it SI [super intelligence] around here, remember? SI."
