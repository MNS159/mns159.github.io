Nvidia leadership crisis deepens after Jensen Huang's resignation; employees call for his return

Tech / AI desk

Nvidia co-founder and former CEO Jensen Huang resigned on November 12th after a series of scandals involving extramarital affairs and a Chinese spying plot, triggering a leadership crisis at the AI chip giant. Nvidia’s stock, which closed at an all-time high of $239.24 on October 6th, fell to $162.85 on November 13th. CFO Colette Kress was named interim CEO, and Harvey Jones became the first board chair in the company’s history, signaling a possible restructuring of the unusual corporate structure where Huang had around 60 direct reports.

The crisis began on October 29th when The New York Times reported that Huang was the target of a Chinese spy plot involving Alice Huang (no relation), an executive at Singapore-based data center company Megaspeed. US officials believed Alice Huang may have been passing information to Beijing and that her company was illegally smuggling Nvidia’s AI chips into China. On November 6th, the Times reported that Alice Huang was specifically recruited by China’s Ministry of State Security to spy on Nvidia’s CEO. Huang admitted to an extramarital affair on November 8th without naming her, prompting the board to launch an investigation. On November 11th, a second affair was reported with Nvidia VP of health care Kimberly Powell, one of his direct reports. By Thursday night, Reuters reported a third affair involving another Nvidia employee.

Huang’s relationship with Alice Huang raised national security concerns because Nvidia’s chips are considered critical to America’s AI superiority and are subject to export restrictions to China. Huang had reportedly misled Nvidia leaders, including general counsel Tim Teter and chief marketing officer Alison Wagonfeld, about the nature of his relationship with Alice Huang. Before resigning, Huang told people he felt “broken and shattered,” calling himself “a piece of shit,” according to Bloomberg.

Many in the tech industry and Nvidia’s own employees rallied behind Huang. Epic Games CEO Tim Sweeney called it “a sad day for NVIDIA, developers, America, and all humanity.” Elon Musk said on X it would “be an honor” to hire him. Internally, Nvidia employees voiced support to “bring back THE boss.” Some speculated Huang was the victim of a coup or a conspiracy by China to chase him from the CEO chair.

The board’s investigation, which began November 8th, is ongoing. Nvidia faces a shareholder lawsuit claiming it deceived investors over the Huang relationship. Huang could face criminal charges if evidence emerges that he knowingly helped Megaspeed or other intermediaries illegally divert restricted chips to China, where a Culper Research report claimed over 20% of Nvidia’s 2026 compute revenue came from—a number not independently verified. The incoming Democratic majority in Congress is expected to tighten scrutiny on Silicon Valley and may impose stricter trade restrictions on Nvidia’s chips. The company has invested $99 billion in AI labs and plans to mobilize over $500 billion for data centers, making the scandal’s impact on the broader AI industry and US economy significant.
