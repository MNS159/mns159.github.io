SpaceX successfully catches Starship upper stage with 'chopstick' arms for first time

Tech / AI desk

On Saturday, SpaceX successfully caught its Starship upper stage using the "chopstick" arms of its Mechazilla launch tower at Starbase, Texas, marking the first time the maneuver has been executed on the Starship vehicle itself. The catch occurred at around 4:20 PM ET after the spacecraft reentered from orbit, flipped vertically, and gently nestled between the tower's arms.

The Starship upper stage descended horizontally before three Raptor engines ignited to swing it vertical. Two small nubs on either side of the rocket, each protruding about one foot, were the only purchase points for the tower's arms. The arms, known as "chopsticks," had previously been used to catch boosters, but this was the first successful capture of the Starship spacecraft.

The flight was not a complete success: the Super Heavy booster aborted its planned catch and detonated over the Gulf of Mexico. SpaceX is investigating the cause. During the company's third-quarter earnings call, CEO Elon Musk said, "Success is not guaranteed on these first flights, but I’d say it’s more likely than not that this succeeds next flight."

The achievement is a major step toward rapid reusability of the massive Starship system. Ars Technica noted that "this was a breathtaking feat" and that it felt "like stepping into the future." Unlike the Space Shuttle, which recovered solid rocket boosters from the ocean and did not recover the external tank, Starship is designed for full and rapid reuse—a goal that this catch brings closer to reality.
