Nvidia gears for pivotal earnings call after leadership crisis

Financial & Markets desk

Nvidia is preparing to report its third fiscal quarter earnings on Nov. 17, a day that has become one of the most anticipated on Wall Street after the stunning ouster of founder and longtime CEO Jensen Huang. The company’s stock has tumbled 24% since Oct. 29, when The New York Times reported Huang’s involvement with a woman believed by intelligence officials to be a Chinese spy, and subsequent reports revealed he had affairs with two company employees. Huang resigned on Nov. 12, leaving interim CEO Colette Kress, previously executive vice president and chief financial officer, to lead the earnings call.

Despite the turmoil, the underlying business remains formidable. Analysts expect Nvidia to report revenue of roughly $109 billion, an increase of nearly 50% from a year ago and about 13% from the second quarter. Earnings per share are forecast at $2.48, up from $1.30 a year ago. The company also recently announced a historic $150 billion stock buyback authorization. However, nine investment houses have downgraded their opinions on Nvidia’s stock, and two have cut their price targets. Shares closed at $162.85 on Nov. 13, down 12.7% for the year.

The leadership vacuum has raised urgent questions about Nvidia’s future. Rumors indicate that the board’s search for a permanent CEO has narrowed to three or fewer candidates, reportedly all external hires. Yahoo Finance Executive Editor Brian Sozzi suggested that the company should offer former Apple CEO Tim Cook whatever he wants to take the job for two to three years. Meanwhile, the board is conducting an investigation into whether Huang had additional affairs with direct reports and whether he divulged company secrets to the alleged spy. Investors are watching for clarity on both fronts, as well as the company’s long-term product roadmap and the viability of its annual GTC conference, which had been a showcase for Huang’s personal charisma.

The earnings call will be closely scrutinized not just for financial results but for signs of stability. Analysts are expected to press Kress on the China spying scandal, the company’s guidance under the new buyback plan, and any updates on the CEO search. “Nvidia is a very important company,” said Reddit co-founder and CEO Steve Huffman at Yahoo Finance Invest. “I am watching along with everybody else where this goes.” The broader implications extend beyond Nvidia: customers and neocloud providers such as CoreWeave have relied on Huang’s leadership, and a shift away from Nvidia chips could reshape the supply chain powering the AI infrastructure boom.

For now, investors are weighing whether to buy the bruised stock. Sozzi outlined several guideposts, including a definitive conclusion from the board’s investigation, aggressive share buybacks, a strengthened board with semiconductor expertise, and detailed product roadmap disclosures. “The Jensen premium is evaporating,” he wrote. “It’s hard to envision where the company will be 18 to 24 months from now.” The earnings call on Nov. 17 will be the first test of whether Nvidia can steady itself without the founder who led it for 33 years.
