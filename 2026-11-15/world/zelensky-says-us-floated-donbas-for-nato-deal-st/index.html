Ukraine War Diplomacy

Zelensky says US floated Donbas-for-NATO deal; Starlink restrictions also criticized

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts slightly center-left

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky said Saturday that US peace envoys raised the possibility of Ukraine ceding the Donbas region in exchange for NATO membership, a proposal he said he would put to a referendum if it included robust security guarantees, no restrictions on Ukraine's military, and funds for reconstruction. The United States has not confirmed Zelensky's account; a US official told the BBC that “many ideas continue to be discussed but any final deal will have to be agreed to by both parties.”

Zelensky’s remarks, released by his office, followed meetings this week between US envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff and both Zelensky in Dublin and Russian President Vladimir Putin in Moscow. “The Americans raised a question: ‘What if Ukraine joins NATO? Would Ukraine withdraw from Donbas?’ This was the first time this had been raised,” Zelensky said. Hours later, Kushner urged “confidentiality,” described reported proposals as “maximalist,” and said private discussions were aimed at exploring ideas that might lead to “new proposals or breakthroughs.”

Russia currently occupies roughly a fifth of Ukrainian territory, including most of the Donetsk and Luhansk regions that make up the Donbas. Moscow has insisted it will not agree to a ceasefire until a final peace deal includes Ukraine ceding control of those territories—including parts not yet captured—and the Crimean peninsula annexed in 2014. The Kremlin has also demanded limits on the size of Ukraine’s military, territorial concessions, and guaranteed neutrality, conditions Kyiv has rejected as leaving it vulnerable to future invasion.

Zelensky has long sought NATO membership, but the US and other allies have been wary, and Russia has cited NATO enlargement as a cause of the war. The Ukrainian leader said the idea was now “back on the agenda” as part of an “extremely difficult conversation” about European security. He warned that Moscow’s talk of a “new European security architecture” is a code for a weaker NATO and could lead to more conflict. “Real security in Europe means the EU and NATO. That’s it,” he said.

The comments add to recent tensions between Kyiv and Washington. Zelensky last week accused US officials of limiting Ukraine’s use of Starlink internet service, which it relies on for coordinating military operations. President Donald Trump responded by saying it was “time for Ukraine to get a new president” and called for fresh elections, which are suspended under martial law. Meanwhile, the European Union said it would include diplomats from Germany, France, the UK, and Poland in future peace talks, despite Moscow’s objection to European participation.

On the battlefield, Russia’s Defense Ministry claimed its forces captured the settlement of Tersianka in Ukraine’s southeastern Zaporizhzhia region and struck energy and transport infrastructure across Ukraine. Zelensky warned that Putin is “betting everything” on the winter campaign to damage civilian morale. The latest diplomatic exchanges were preceded by trilateral talks in Abu Dhabi, where the Donbas-NATO proposal appeared to be a central topic.
