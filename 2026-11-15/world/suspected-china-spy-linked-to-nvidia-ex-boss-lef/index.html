Nvidia Scandal

Suspected China spy linked to Nvidia's ex-boss left in 2025, Megaspeed says

International & World desk

Alice Huang, a suspected China spy linked to former Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang, left her executive role at Singapore-based technology firm Megaspeed in August last year, the company said on Sunday. Megaspeed, the largest buyer of Nvidia chips in Southeast Asia, said it had launched an internal investigation following reports that questioned whether high-end AI chips may have reached China in violation of US export restrictions.

The scandal erupted after Jensen Huang resigned as CEO of Nvidia over alleged affairs with Alice Huang and an employee. Megaspeed is suspected of providing Chinese companies with backdoor access to Nvidia chips via its data centers in Malaysia, Indonesia, and Thailand. The United States has banned the sale of advanced Nvidia chips to China. While Singapore does not enforce unilateral export controls imposed by other countries, it expects companies in its jurisdiction to respect such regulations.

Megaspeed said it was aware of reports containing "assertions about our business, supply chain and global operations that are not supported by verifiable evidence." It added that it "conducts legitimate and lawful business" and has "retained external legal counsel to conduct a thorough internal investigation." The company stated that it designs and maintains its global operational and supply-chain control frameworks to comply with all applicable laws, including US export control requirements.

On Friday, US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent criticized the Commerce Department, led by fellow Trump cabinet colleague Howard Lutnick, for failing to properly oversee the export of high-end chips. Bessent told CNBC that several agencies "were in favour of putting Megaspeed on the entity list for months... but Commerce has the pen... and you will have to ask them about their internal process." The entity list restricts US trade with overseas firms and individuals.

Howard Lutnick has been called to testify before the Senate Banking Committee next month over his department's role in allowing Nvidia to export advanced chips. Other congressional committees have asked Lutnick and his department to retain all records related to Nvidia and Jensen Huang. China has denied that Alice Huang is a spy and warned the US not to use the scandal as a "pretext for imposing a technological blockade" on AI technology.
