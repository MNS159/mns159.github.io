Trump, Putin and Xi plan historic joint meeting at APEC summit in China

International & World desk

The White House has announced that US President Donald Trump, Russian President Vladimir Putin and Chinese President Xi Jinping are planning a historic trilateral meeting on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) summit in the southern Chinese city of Shenzhen next week. White House spokeswoman Olivia Wales told reporters the meeting would take place as Trump attends the two-day summit of 21 member states, and that the president intended to discuss the reduction of nuclear weapons, whose proliferation he believes is very dangerous. It is understood Iran may also be discussed, but not Ukraine. Russia has confirmed the meeting; China has yet to react. No agreement was expected to follow the talks, an unnamed senior US official said, according to news agencies AP and Reuters.

Trump will arrive in Shenzhen late on Wednesday and will also hold bilateral talks with Xi on Thursday, their fourth meeting since the US president returned to office in 2025. Wales said Trump's trip, which includes an Association of South-east Asian Nations (Asean) summit in the Philippines on Tuesday, would "secure historic wins to the American people and advance America's interests in the Indo Pacific." The potential summit has already been dubbed a "new Yalta" by media, invoking the optics of the US, UK and Soviet Union convening at the end of World War II to decide the new geopolitical power balance. However, analysts note that Beijing is uneasy about this framing and that China has deliberately remained vague in its public statements, with a Chinese Foreign Ministry official saying only that Beijing was "in communication" with relevant parties.

The meeting would come at a time when the world's biggest nuclear arsenals lack major guardrails, following the expiration of a US-Russia nuclear weapons control treaty in February. The UN has urged both sides to agree a new framework without delay, while Trump has said he could replace the deal with a better agreement that includes China, which has been building up its arsenal. Former senior US officials and analysts have said that seeking Beijing and Moscow's co-operation could create major hurdles and take years. Putin first pitched the idea of a trilateral summit last month during a call with Trump, with the Wall Street Journal reporting that Putin wanted the meeting to discuss an end to his war in Ukraine.

China is in a delicate position as host. While Beijing and Moscow are broadly aligned on many issues, China does not want to be explicitly tied to Russia's war in Ukraine and harm its efforts to present itself as a force for global stability. The European Union is wary of being left out of any Ukraine talks, and analysts say the trilateral meeting could trigger further escalation in already worsening relations between China and the EU if Ukraine is discussed without Europeans present. China has also been working to rebalance economic ties with the EU. Beijing has quietly provided Russia with a crucial market for energy exports and supplied technology and dual-use goods throughout the war, but officially maintains neutrality and has refused to take responsibility for the conflict's outcome.

Xi and Putin have met some 40 times since Xi came to power in 2012, and the two countries declared a "no limits" partnership in early 2022, weeks before Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine. The current Russia-China ties are deeper than they have been in decades, with a long-term strategy to contain US power and foster an alternative global order. However, analysts note limits to the alignment: Russian defense firms have accused Chinese companies of stealing intellectual property, Beijing is concerned about Russia's growing strategic relationship with North Korea, and the two countries differ on nuclear doctrine, with China maintaining a "no first use" policy while Moscow uses nuclear threats as part of its strategy. A US commission has described China as a "decisive enabler" of Russia's war, but also noted that intelligence and security concerns remain a constraint on deeper co-operation.

Chinese state media reported on Friday that Xi would take part in bilateral talks on the sidelines of the APEC summit, but has not reported on the trilateral meeting. Earlier this month, Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov told Russian state media that there were "signals confirming readiness" for such talks at the summit. For Russia, a three-way meeting with a photo op would be a big diplomatic win for Putin, who has faced years of diplomatic boycott by Kyiv's allies since the invasion. For the US, standing alongside the leaders of America's foremost rivals could be a difficult image, though Trump has already made a show of personally greeting both leaders with a red carpet at different points during his second term. For China, analysts say, even a meeting that yields only a photo opportunity could severely erode its image as a champion of the Global South and developing countries.
