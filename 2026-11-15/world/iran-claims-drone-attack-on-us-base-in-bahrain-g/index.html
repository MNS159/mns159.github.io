Iran-US Conflict

Iran claims drone attack on US base in Bahrain; Gulf states condemn strike

International & World desk

Iran launched a drone attack against a US military base in Bahrain on Sunday, its second such attempt in three days, the country's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) said. The IRGC said it targeted a US air defence radar facility at al-Riffa air base in Bahrain using six drones. Bahrain's military said its air defence systems intercepted and destroyed the Iranian attacks "before they could achieve their objectives". The interior ministry called on citizens to head to their nearest safe location as sirens sounded across the kingdom.

The attack came after Iran fired drones at Bahrain on Friday, its first attack against Gulf countries since September. The US military denied that the Friday strike caused any damage or injuries, stating that the IRGC launched two one-way attack drones towards Bahrain and both were shot down by US forces. Sunday's strike prompted a wave of condemnation from Gulf and regional states. Saudi Arabia said the attacks "constitute a violation of international law and the principles of good neighborliness", while Qatar said the "continuation of these attacks represents a dangerous escalation that will complicate efforts to contain the tension". Jordan and Egypt also strongly condemned the attack and expressed solidarity with Bahrain.

The US and Iran are locked in a stand-off over the Strait of Hormuz, through which about a fifth of the world's gas and oil supply usually flows. Shipping has been restricted since the outbreak of war in February. Iran insists it should control the strait and has been attacking what it calls "unauthorised" traffic, while the US military is providing protection for tankers shuttling oil through the strait to keep exports flowing from Gulf Arab states. Earlier this week, the US carried out strikes on Iran's Qeshm Island in the Strait of Hormuz, which US officials described as limited and defensive, in response to the IRGC firing on a US aircraft.

In a statement on the strikes shared by Iran's state broadcaster on Telegram, the IRGC said: "The Strait of Hormuz is closed, and any aggression by the American enemy will be met with an even harsher and more forceful response." Iran's Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei said at a press conference in Tehran on Sunday that Iran would continue to respond to any military aggression by the US or other states. Asked whether the strikes showed a change in Iranian direction as negotiations with the US have stalled, he said Iran was "not waiting for anyone" and was focused on its security.

The US and Iran reached a preliminary agreement to end the war and reopen the strait in June, but it collapsed within weeks after Iranian attacks on shipping resumed and the US reinstated a naval blockade of Iranian ports. This week Donald Trump said that the US was no longer interested in a deal to end the war with Iran and ordered Vice-President JD Vance, who had been involved in efforts to restart negotiations, to focus on domestic US politics. The IRGC also claimed on Sunday the sinking of an oil supertanker which hit a mine in the Strait of Hormuz, saying two ships had hit its mines while travelling through the strait, resulting in "massive explosions and fire". There was no independent verification of the claim.
