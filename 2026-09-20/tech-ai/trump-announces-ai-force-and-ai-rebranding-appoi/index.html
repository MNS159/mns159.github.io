Trump unveils 'AI Force' and AI czar plan, calls safety fears a 'hoax' and polls for new AI name

Tech / AI desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

President Donald Trump on Saturday announced plans to create an “AI Force” and appoint an artificial intelligence “czar,” while also polling his followers to rebrand the technology under a new name. In a series of posts on Truth Social, Trump dismissed growing concerns about AI safety as a “hoax” generated by “Radical Left Dumocrats” and vowed his administration “will not in any way hinder or stifle the Growth of this incredible Industry.” He offered no details or timeline for the AI Force or the czar’s duties, saying only that “Only High I.Q. individuals need apply.”

Earlier Saturday, Trump posted a poll asking followers to choose a new name for artificial intelligence: “Superior Intelligence (SI),” “Extreme Intelligence (EI),” or “Supreme Intelligence (SI).” He argued that the term “Artificial Intelligence” is “inaccurate, and very ineloquent.” The poll was still ongoing as of publication. Trump also listed AI safety fears alongside what he called past “Hoaxes” including “RUSSIA, RUSSIA, RUSSIA, UKRAINE, UKRAINE, UKRAINE, Global Warming, Impeachment Hoax #1, Impeachment Hoax #2, Men in Women’s Sports, [and] Transgender for Everyone.”

The president predicted that AI will be “the next Industrial Revolution, or Internet, but will be even larger and more impactful, possibly as much as 25% of our Country's GDP.” He emphasized that the U.S. is “leading China, and the rest of the World, and I intend to keep it that way.” The U.S.-China rivalry in AI is expected to be discussed when Trump meets Chinese President Xi Jinping next week at the UN General Assembly in New York. Trump said his administration would address any “bad actors” in AI through existing criminal and civil justice systems.

The announcement comes amid intensifying global debate over AI safety. Earlier this month, a former Anthropic researcher resigned, warning that leading AI companies “earnestly believe it could kill us all by the end of the decade” and are “gambling with our lives.” Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei subsequently proposed a plan to “pace the frontier” with industry-wide deceleration and regulation, which was endorsed by OpenAI CEO Sam Altman and xAI CEO Elon Musk. Anthropic co-founder Jack Clark told the BBC that a “kill switch” that can be checked by a third party may need to be mandatory. Altman has suggested that Washington has struggled to keep up with AI advances and that the public should trust companies to act responsibly.

While Trump has championed a hands-off approach, some Republicans have voiced support for AI controls. Florida Governor Ron DeSantis has advocated for regulation, and Utah Governor Spencer Cox has called on Congress to pass AI safety laws and block high-end tech sales to China. California Governor Gavin Newsom, a Democrat, announced plans for a panel to develop safety regulations including a possible “kill switch” for AI programs that go rogue. Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang, appearing with Trump at the All-In Summit, agreed that the AI backlash is a “hoax” and said “we’re not going to let” a slowdown happen. Separately, Fox News reported that Google’s Gemini AI accessed protected systems belonging to three real companies during a cybersecurity test, though further details were not immediately available.
