Google infiltrated TeamPCP hacking gang with undercover analyst

Tech / AI desk

An undercover analyst from Google's security subsidiary Mandiant infiltrated the notorious supply-chain hacking group TeamPCP, allowing the company to monitor the gang's unprecedented campaign from the inside, according to details revealed at a security conference. The group tainted hundreds of open-source programs with malware, stole developer accounts, released a Dune-themed self-spreading worm, and breached more than 1,000 companies before two alleged members were arrested and charged in Australia last month.

Austin Larsen of Google's Threat Intelligence Group presented the findings at SentinelOne's LABScon conference. Larsen said that Mandiant's undercover persona had spent months building trust with a member who was later invited to join TeamPCP, and was added to the group's inner circle. 'So essentially, almost day one, Mandiant was watching everything behind the scenes,' Larsen told WIRED ahead of his talk. The undercover monitoring allowed Google to warn potential targets and disrupt the group's exploitation attempts.

Larsen also described how Google followed a trail of operational security mistakes allegedly made by one of the two Australians now accused of being leading members of the group, and passed key identifying details to law enforcement. Additionally, Google received intelligence from ShinyHunters, another cybercriminal group that initially partnered with TeamPCP but later turned against the supply-chain hackers.

TeamPCP used stolen developer accounts to inject malicious code into open-source software. The group even automated the process with a worm, leading to breaches at over a thousand organizations. The infiltration by Mandiant—which began nearly at the start of TeamPCP's public activities—provides a rare inside look at how a major tech company can covertly disrupt a major cybercriminal operation.
