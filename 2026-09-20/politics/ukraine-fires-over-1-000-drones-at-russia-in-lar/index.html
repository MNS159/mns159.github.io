Ukraine fires over 1,000 drones at Russia in largest attack on Moscow

Politics desk
✓ Facts
  • Ukrainian forces fired more than 1,000 drones at Russia overnight, including hundreds aimed at Moscow, officials said Sunday.
  • Russia's Ministry of Defense said it shot down 1,100 Ukrainian drones across the country, Crimea and the Black Sea.
  • Moscow Mayor Sergei Sobyanin called it the "largest ever" drone attack on the capital, saying 450 drones were destroyed en route and that the Moscow Oil Refinery and a residential building were damaged.
  • Local Gov. Andrei Vorobyov said the attack across the wider Moscow region killed two people — a 74-year-old man and a 44-year-old woman — and wounded 20.
  • President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Ukraine used a mix of drones and missiles, including domestically produced Flamingo and Pelican missiles, to strike oil and logistics facilities.
  • The attack occurred as Russia concluded the third and final day of its first wartime parliamentary election, a vote with no substantive opposition to President Vladimir Putin.
  • Russia also held the parliamentary vote in Crimea and four Ukrainian regions it illegally annexed but does not fully control; Kyiv has denounced this as illegal.
  • Sobyanin said the strike appeared aimed at disrupting the elections and stated that the attempt "failed."
○ Analysis

Ukraine has built one of the world's most active military drone programs during the war, using domestically produced long-range systems alongside missiles to strike energy and logistics infrastructure deep inside Russia in an effort to raise the economic and political costs of the war for the Kremlin, as ground fighting along the roughly 780-mile front line has slowed amid large numbers of drones and ground robots threatening troop movements. The timing of the strike — coinciding with the third and final day of Russia's first wartime parliamentary election, a vote with no substantive opposition to Putin and held partly in illegally annexed Ukrainian territory — added symbolic weight to the attack, though officials on both sides offered competing characterizations of its military and political significance.

Implications

Ukraine's continued strikes on Russian energy and logistics infrastructure reflect an effort to increase economic pressure on Moscow, with Zelenskyy describing the targeted facilities as sustaining Russia's war effort. Russia's completion of its parliamentary election, held partly in occupied Ukrainian territory and without meaningful opposition to Putin, is likely to reinforce the Kremlin's domestic political dominance.
