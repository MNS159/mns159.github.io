Trump administration bars CNN, Politico, MS NOW journalists from White House

Politics desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts slightly center-left
✓ Facts
  • President Trump announced in a Friday Truth Social post that he was banning CNN, MS NOW (formerly MSNBC), and Politico from the White House, citing what he called negative and 'FICTION and LIES' coverage of his administration.
  • On Saturday morning, reporters from CNN (Betsy Klein), MS NOW (Akayla Gardner and a photographer), and Politico (Cheyenne Haslett) were denied access to White House grounds; Secret Service agents told them their press badges had been deactivated or confiscated.
  • An MS NOW producer was still able to access the grounds using her badge, despite Gardner's and a photographer's badges being disabled; no explanation was given for the discrepancy.
  • Trump said the ban was not tied to any single story but reflected cumulative frustration over roughly two years of coverage, and stated other outlets 'may' be added, naming the New York Times and Washington Post as 'fake news' when asked.
  • CNN, MS NOW, and Politico each issued statements saying they would defend their First Amendment rights and continue reporting on the administration.
  • White House Correspondents' Association President Jacqui Heinrich said denying access to journalists violates the First Amendment and called on the administration to immediately restore access.
  • Reporters Committee for Freedom of the Press President Bruce Brown said the ban was 'flatly unconstitutional' and predicted it would be struck down quickly by the courts, arguing that once the White House grants some journalists access, it cannot exclude others because it dislikes their reporting.
  • First Amendment lawyer Theodore Boutrous told NPR the ban is unconstitutional on its face.
  • Trump previously barred an Associated Press reporter in 2024 over Gulf of Mexico/America naming and barred a CNN reporter during his first term; courts sided with the media in both cases, with the AP case still under appeal.
○ Analysis

The move extends a broader pattern of the Trump administration restricting or punishing news outlets it views as unfavorable, following prior actions against the AP, lawsuits against the New York Times, Wall Street Journal, and BBC, and settlements with some media organizations. Legal experts and press-freedom groups argue the ban conflicts with established case law holding that once the White House grants press access, it cannot revoke it based on the content of an outlet's reporting — a precedent reinforced by court rulings in the AP and prior CNN disputes. The action also comes amid a difficult political stretch for Trump, including a Supreme Court rejection of his mail-in voting restrictions, a Federal Reserve rate hike he had opposed, a court order limiting changes to the Kennedy Center, and historically low approval ratings for his second term — context that outlets note without asserting causation.

Steelman

The dispute centers on a well-established legal principle — that White House press access, once granted, cannot be revoked based on the content of coverage — versus a president's stated view that outlets have produced sustained, unfair negative coverage; both the constitutional question and Trump's stated grievance are matters of public record, even though only one has clear judicial precedent behind it.

Implications

The banned outlets have signaled they intend to challenge the restriction, and given how courts ruled in the earlier AP and CNN access disputes, this case is likely to face swift legal scrutiny. Trump's suggestion that further outlets 'may' be added leaves open the possibility of an expanded confrontation with the media.
