Ukraine launches largest drone attack on Moscow, killing two

International & World desk

Ukraine launched its largest drone attack on the Moscow region overnight, with Mayor Sergei Sobyanin reporting that 450 drones heading for the capital were shot down in what he called 'the most massive' attack. At least two people were killed and 20 injured, according to the region's governor, who said a 44-year-old woman died after a drone hit a three-storey residential building in Sofyino and an elderly man was killed when a private house was struck in the Orekhovo-Zuevo district. The attack also targeted the Moscow Oil Refinery, which supplies more than a third of the Moscow region's fuel, and Ukrainian officials said three key units were damaged.

Videos on social media, not yet verified, showed explosions over Moscow and a burning warehouse. The refinery, operated by state-run Gazprom and with a capacity of about 11 million tons of oil per year, supplies petrol, diesel and aviation fuel to Russia's military, according to Ukraine's defence ministry. Kyiv's Defence Minister said hitting such facilities 'directly undermines the enemy's ability to continue its war of aggression.' Moscow's largest airports, Sheremetyevo and Vnukovo, imposed temporary restrictions on arrivals and departures, though they were lifted by Sunday morning.

The attack came on the final day of Russia's tightly controlled three-day parliamentary election, which critics say is neither free nor fair. Mayor Sobyanin accused Ukraine of attempting to disrupt the vote, writing on Telegram: 'The unprecedented attack was clearly planned with the aim of disrupting the elections. The enemy did not succeed.' Meanwhile, Russian state media reported that a drone strike on a bus in a Moscow-controlled part of Kherson killed two people, including an election official.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky confirmed the strikes, saying that Kyiv used long-range munitions including the Ukrainian-made Flamingo cruise missile and the Pelican ballistic missile—the latter reportedly used for the first time to attack Russia. 'Our long-range responses had a very significant impact in the Moscow region last night. One of Russia's key oil industry facilities and the aggressor's logistics facility were hit. These are billions of dollars that sustain the war machine,' he posted on X. The Ukrainian company Fire Point said its FP-1 drones were used in the refinery strike.

Separately, Ukrainian authorities reported that nine people, including three children, were killed in Russian attacks in the Kyiv and Sumy regions on Saturday. In Fastiv, a mother and her two three-year-old children died when a Russian drone hit their home. The escalation comes despite US President Donald Trump's claim that both sides had agreed not to strike energy infrastructure. On Friday, Trump signed a sanctions bill targeting Russia's economy by imposing tariffs on major buyers of Russian oil and gas, including China and India. Al Jazeera's correspondent in Moscow noted this was among the largest single-day attacks on Russia since the war began in February 2022.
