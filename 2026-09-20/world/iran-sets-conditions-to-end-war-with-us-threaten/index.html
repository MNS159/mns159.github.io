Iran-US Conflict

Iran sets conditions to end war with US, threatens ‘painful’ strikes if attacked

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

Iran’s security chief Mohsen Rezaei laid out Tehran’s latest conditions to end its war with the United States in an exclusive interview with Al Jazeera on Saturday, while the country’s military warned of sustained, painful strikes across the Middle East if Washington launches new attacks. Rezaei said the conditions had been conveyed to the US via Qatari mediators, adding that Tehran was more battle-hardened than in the first 40 days of the conflict, which began in late February after joint US-Israeli strikes on Iran. “We will confront America with greater force, and with more numerous and more painful strikes,” he said.

The conditions Rezaei listed include: an end to the war on all fronts, particularly in Lebanon; the release of Iran’s frozen funds, which run into billions of US dollars; an end to the naval blockade on Iranian ports; an end to US interference in Iran’s internal affairs; an end to US cooperation with “the opponents of our revolution”; and an end to all US attacks on Iranian territory. “It is in Washington’s interest to accept these conditions. Trump’s threats will not achieve any results. We are prepared for a decisive war,” Rezaei said. On Sunday, Iran’s parliament speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf reiterated that the Strait of Hormuz will remain closed until the US fulfils its commitments, saying there would be no reopening of the waterway or return to previous negotiations until conditions are met.

The demands bear similarities to a 60-day memorandum of understanding (MoU) signed in June, which expired last month. That agreement also called for an end to the war on all fronts, respect for sovereignty, an end to the naval blockade, and release of frozen funds, as well as a $300bn US-backed reconstruction plan, a commitment by Iran not to develop nuclear weapons, and the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz. Rezaei did not mention war reparations in the latest conditions, a change noted by analysts. Since the MoU expired, attacks have risen, especially around the Strait of Hormuz, which Iran has effectively closed, while the US maintains a blockade of Iran’s southern ports. Escalating attacks on oil tankers and Houthi control of the Red Sea route have pushed oil prices above $100 per barrel in recent weeks.

The US has not yet responded to Iran’s new conditions. On Saturday, Washington warned its citizens in nine countries—Iraq, Bahrain, Oman, Saudi Arabia, Iran, Qatar, Lebanon, Kuwait, and Jordan—to exercise “heightened vigilance” due to the potential for “unforeseen escalation”. President Donald Trump cut short his weekend at Camp David and returned to Washington on Saturday; no explanation was given. Analysts offered contrasting views: Rob Geist Pinfold of King’s College London said Trump may take Iran’s memo seriously to end the war before the November midterms, while former Pentagon official David Des Roches said the terms would likely be viewed as “surrender” but noted Iran’s apparent abandonment of war reparations as a sign of movement.

Separately, Iran’s Khatam al-Anbiya military headquarters warned on Sunday that if the US makes a “mistake” and launches new attacks, “all of that country’s deployment centres and interests in the region will be targeted with continuous, effective, and painful attacks, without any restrictions or considerations,” according to the Fars news agency. The warning also cited unconfirmed reports of a meeting between US representatives in a European country at which agreements were allegedly reached on renewed attacks against Iran, with some Arab countries reportedly giving approval. Any country supporting such an attack would be regarded as “complicit” and could expect no leniency, the headquarters said.

On the nuclear programme, Rezaei said Iran could have withdrawn from the Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty after US and Israeli attacks but has not done so, and continues to abide by a fatwa from the late Supreme Leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei banning nuclear weapons. However, he said future sanctions or a UN Security Council referral could change that. Regarding Yemen, Rezaei denied Iranian involvement and offered to mediate between the warring sides, saying Tehran is “fully prepared to help achieve peace between Yemen and Saudi Arabia”. He also dismissed speculation that Iran could close the Bab al-Mandeb strait, calling it a Yemeni matter relating to the Houthi movement.
