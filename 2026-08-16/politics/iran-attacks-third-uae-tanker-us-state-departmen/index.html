Iran attacks third UAE tanker; US State Department comments on war and visa revocations

Politics desk
✓ Facts
  • The UAE accused Iran of attacking a third Abu Dhabi National Oil Company (ADNOC) tanker in less than a week, with the vessel struck on Friday while transiting the Strait of Hormuz.
  • President Trump said in a public remark that after 'defeating' Iran he would 'pretty soon' be declaring the Strait of Hormuz 'a territory of the United States.'
  • State Department spokesperson Tommy Pigott told NPR's Rob Schmitz that Iran is losing money daily due to U.S. economic pressure and a blockade of Iranian ships and ports, and cited continuing sanctions from the State and Treasury Departments.
  • After the interview, a State Department spokesperson clarified that Pigott's on-air claim that Iran loses 'hundreds of billions of dollars every single day' actually referred to 'hundreds of millions' per day from the blockade, while third-party estimates put the cumulative economic effect of the pressure campaign in the hundreds of billions.
  • A State Department fact sheet released this week said the Trump administration has revoked more than 175,000 visas from foreign nationals for alleged crimes or visa violations, with DUIs, assaults, and thefts cited as the most common offenses.
  • The fact sheet also listed visa revocations for foreign nationals who celebrated the assassination of Charlie Kirk, quoting statements including 'when fascists die, Democrats don't complain' and that Kirk 'died too late.'
  • Pigott said a visa is 'a privilege, not a right' and that visa holders who 'wish death' to Americans or celebrate violence against Americans can have their visas revoked as a national security matter; when asked what law the individuals broke, Pigott did not cite a specific statute.
○ Analysis

This story combines two threads of the ongoing U.S.-Iran conflict: military and economic escalation in the Strait of Hormuz, and domestic immigration enforcement tied to the war's political fallout. The Strait of Hormuz is a critical global oil-shipping chokepoint, and repeated attacks on UAE tankers there signal an intensifying maritime conflict alongside the broader U.S.-Iran military standoff. Separately, the State Department's visa revocation figures and its inclusion of foreign nationals who made statements about Charlie Kirk's assassination raise questions about how the administration is using visa policy as an extension of national security and immigration enforcement, and how it distinguishes between protected speech and grounds for revocation. The discrepancy between Pigott's on-air economic claim and the department's later clarification also highlights the challenge of verifying wartime economic statistics in real time.

Steelman

Across the political spectrum, the story raises a shared question of accountability in wartime messaging—whether it's the precise scale of economic pressure on Iran or the legal basis for revoking visas over political speech—since both cases show administration officials asserting broad claims that require clarification or lack clear legal grounding when pressed.

Implications

Continued tanker attacks in the Strait of Hormuz could further strain U.S.-Iran relations and global oil shipping, while the administration's visa revocation policy—particularly its application to political speech—may face legal or congressional scrutiny over First Amendment protections for noncitizens and the standards used to define national security threats.
