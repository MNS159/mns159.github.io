Douglass breaks 50m freestyle world record twice in one day

Sports desk

American Kate Douglass broke the 50m freestyle world record twice in one day, dominating the Pan Pacific Swimming Championships in Irvine, California. The 24-year-old double Olympic champion won the final in 23.19 seconds, shattering the 23.49 mark she had set hours earlier in the heats. That final time was her third world record in the event, following a 23.59 swim in June and a 23.55 effort by compatriot Gretchen Walsh nine days later.

Walsh finished second in the final with a time of 23.74, while Australia's Meg Harris took third. Douglass's 23.19 final swim lowered the record by 0.30 seconds from her own morning performance, capping a remarkable single-day improvement.

At the European Aquatics Championships in Paris, Germany's Johannes Liebmann knocked nearly four seconds off the world record in the men's 1500m freestyle. The 19-year-old completed the distance in 14 minutes 26.79 seconds, finishing 3.88 seconds faster than the previous record set by American Bobby Finke at the 2024 Olympics, also held in the French capital.

Liebmann became the first swimmer to go under 14:30 for the distance, slashing the most time off the record since Australia's Grant Hackett in 2001. Hackett, a three-time Olympic champion, had taken more than seven seconds off the record when he swam 14:34.56, a mark that stood for 10 years.

The two record-breaking performances highlight a rapid shift in swimming's sprint and distance standards, with Douglass's 50m freestyle mark and Liebmann's 1500m record both coming within the same day at separate major competitions.
