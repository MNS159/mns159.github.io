Basketball Hall of Fame class of 2026 enshrines nine, including Stoudemire, Rivers, Della Donne, Parker, Holdsclaw

Sports desk

The Naismith Memorial Basketball Hall of Fame formally welcomed nine new honorees on Saturday night in Springfield, Massachusetts, with a class headlined by former NBA star Amar’e Stoudemire, coaches Doc Rivers and Mike D’Antoni, and three WNBA legends: Elena Della Donne, Candace Parker and Chamique Holdsclaw. Also enshrined were longtime NBA official Joey Crawford, Gonzaga head coach Mark Few, and the 1996 U.S. Olympic women’s basketball team, which included Hall of Famers Lisa Leslie, Dawn Staley, Sheryl Swoopes and Rebecca Lobo and set the stage for the WNBA’s launch later that year.

Stoudemire, the 2003 Rookie of the Year and a six-time NBA All-Star, was the only former NBA player inducted this year. He never won a championship over his 14-year career and waited seven years after first becoming eligible, last playing in the NBA in the 2015–16 season. Doc Rivers and Mike D’Antoni were enshrined for their coaching contributions; Rivers, who spent 13 seasons as a player before transitioning to the sidelines, drew laughter during his acceptance speech when he quipped, “This isn’t my real voice. Coaching stole it from me. I swear I used to have a high voice and used to be able to really sing, can’t do that anymore.”

With the 2026 class celebrated, attention turns to former NBA players on the doorstep of induction. Blake Griffin, who became eligible this year and was a finalist but did not make the class, posted career averages of 19.0 points, 8.0 rebounds and 4.0 assists over 13 seasons, was a six-time All-Star and the 2011 Rookie of the Year. Andre Iguodala, a four-time NBA champion and 2015 Finals MVP, is also eligible now; the Hall confirmed to Sports Illustrated that players must be retired for two seasons (three calendar years) to qualify. Iguodala was not included as a first-time nominee this year.

Derrick Rose, the 2011 NBA MVP and three-time All-Star, will become eligible in 2027 after retiring in 2023. He remains the youngest MVP in league history at 22, but a torn ACL in the 2012 playoffs limited his explosiveness. Rose would be the first NBA MVP to miss the Hall of Fame if he does not eventually gain induction. Chris Paul, a 12-time All-Star and four-time All-NBA first team selection, will be eligible in 2029; he ranks second all-time in assists and steals and is considered a lock despite never winning a title. Russell Westbrook, also eligible in 2029, is the NBA’s all-time leader in triple-doubles and averaged a 30-point triple-double during his 2017 MVP season.

The nonexclusive nature of the Hall of Fame—which considers a player’s entire basketball résumé, including college and international achievements—could bolster borderline cases. Stoudemire’s enshrinement this year, without a championship, may help similar candidates such as Griffin, whose résumé is comparable if not stronger. Iguodala’s four championship rings and Finals MVP award provide a different path, while Paul and Westbrook’s sustained dominance and Olympic gold medals reinforce their candidacies. The next few years will determine which of these players join the fraternity of Hall of Famers in Springfield.
