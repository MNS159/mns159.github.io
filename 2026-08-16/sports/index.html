Cricket

Bangladesh thrash Australia in historic Test upset

Sports desk

Bangladesh completed a stunning nine-wicket victory over Australia on the fourth day of the first Test in Darwin, securing what many regard as one of the greatest upsets in Test cricket history. Set a target of 57 runs, Mominul Haque cut Beau Webster to the boundary to seal the win, sparking jubilant celebrations among the visitors. Australia had been bowled out for 284 in their second innings, with Mehidy Hasan Miraz taking 5-66, leaving Bangladesh needing just 57 to win. Despite losing opener Tanzid Hasan Tamim for a duck in the second over, Mominul's unbeaten 30 and Shadman Islam's 25 guided the team home.

UFC

Makhachev defeats Garry at UFC 330, breaks UFC win record

Sports desk

Islam Makhachev cemented his status as the pound-for-pound best fighter in the world by defeating Ian Machado Garry via unanimous decision at UFC 330 in Philadelphia on Saturday, defending his welterweight title and breaking Anderson Silva's 13-year record for consecutive UFC wins with his 17th straight victory. The 34-year-old Russian, a former lightweight champion, improved to 29-1 while Machado Garry (17-2) suffered his second career loss. The judges scored the bout 49-46 twice and 48-47.

Swimming

Douglass breaks 50m freestyle world record twice in one day

Sports desk

American Kate Douglass broke the 50m freestyle world record twice in one day, dominating the Pan Pacific Swimming Championships in Irvine, California. The 24-year-old double Olympic champion won the final in 23.19 seconds, shattering the 23.49 mark she had set hours earlier in the heats. That final time was her third world record in the event, following a 23.59 swim in June and a 23.55 effort by compatriot Gretchen Walsh nine days later.

NBA

Basketball Hall of Fame class of 2026 enshrines nine, including Stoudemire, Rivers, Della Donne, Parker, Holdsclaw

Sports desk

The Naismith Memorial Basketball Hall of Fame formally welcomed nine new honorees on Saturday night in Springfield, Massachusetts, with a class headlined by former NBA star Amar’e Stoudemire, coaches Doc Rivers and Mike D’Antoni, and three WNBA legends: Elena Della Donne, Candace Parker and Chamique Holdsclaw. Also enshrined were longtime NBA official Joey Crawford, Gonzaga head coach Mark Few, and the 1996 U.S. Olympic women’s basketball team, which included Hall of Famers Lisa Leslie, Dawn Staley, Sheryl Swoopes and Rebecca Lobo and set the stage for the WNBA’s launch later that year.
