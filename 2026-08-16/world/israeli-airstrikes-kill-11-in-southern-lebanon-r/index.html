Israel-Lebanon Conflict

Israeli airstrikes kill 11 in southern Lebanon; deadliest since June ceasefire

International & World desk

At least 11 people were killed in Israeli airstrikes in southern Lebanon on Saturday, in the deadliest day of attacks since a US-brokered ceasefire framework was announced in June, according to Lebanese authorities. The strikes hit two villages: a dawn raid on the outskirts of Ansar killed seven people, including three children and two women, and a later strike on Deir al-Zahrani killed four others and wounded others, the Lebanese Health Ministry reported.

The Israeli military said it killed a Hezbollah battalion commander, Ali Samir al-Haj Hassan, of the elite Radwan Force, whom it accused of using his family as human shields. The IDF stated that the strike targeted a military headquarters where the commander was hiding alongside civilians, and that alongside him, several other terrorists were eliminated. The office of Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu defended the strike, saying it responded to a Hezbollah attack that seriously injured three Israeli soldiers, and that Hezbollah deliberately placed civilians at the site.

Lebanese Prime Minister Nawaf Salam condemned the attacks, stating: “The seven martyrs of the Israeli airstrike on the town of Ansar are not military infrastructure and the children and women killed were not military targets.” He called on Israel to halt the escalation. Hezbollah dismissed Israel’s claims as “false,” asserting that civilians were deliberately targeted in their homes, and later vowed an “appropriate” response, warning Lebanese authorities against continuing what it called “humiliating” negotiations with Israel.

The strikes sent hundreds of families fleeing north, clogging roads out of the Nabatieh district, according to reports. The violence marks the latest breakdown of a ceasefire framework reached in late June, which calls for Hezbollah to disarm, Israeli forces to gradually withdraw from southern Lebanon, and the Lebanese army to deploy across the area. Hezbollah was not party to the agreement and has refused to disarm. Israel continues to occupy roughly 5–6% of Lebanese territory, which it calls a security zone.

The broader war reignited in March when Hezbollah launched rockets into Israel in retaliation for a strike that killed Iran’s supreme leader, leading to a full-scale Israeli bombing campaign and ground invasion that has killed over 4,300 people. Lebanese President Joseph Aoun condemned the strikes as “repeated violations” of the ceasefire framework, saying they sent a clear message to US-led negotiations. The two sides have held seven rounds of talks, with an eighth round expected in September.
