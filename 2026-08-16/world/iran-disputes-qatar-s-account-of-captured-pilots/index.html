Iran-Qatar Tensions

Iran demands Qatar release captured pilots, rejects Doha's denial

International & World desk · anchored on AP
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

Iran has demanded that Qatar release three pilots it says were captured after their fighter jets were shot down during a combat mission targeting a Qatari military base on March 2, 2026, the third day of the US-Iran war. In a letter to the head of the International Committee of the Red Cross (ICRC), Brig Gen Mohammad Bagherzadeh of Iran's Armed Forces General Staff identified the pilots as Javad Salehi, Abdul Majid Dashtian and Imran Behroshian, and said a fourth pilot, Majid Kazemi, was killed and his remains returned. Qatar "categorically denied" the claims, saying it was "surprised by these misleading statements."

Bagherzadeh accused Qatar of violating the pilots' rights under international law and said they had not been allowed to meet or contact their families. He later told the semi-official Tasnim news agency that an Iranian fact-finding team had been denied entry to Qatar for months and called on the ICRC to urgently follow up. Qatar's foreign affairs spokesperson, Majed Mohammed Al Ansari, said on X that Qatari search and rescue teams had found the remains of one pilot after the Iranian aircraft violated Qatari airspace and failed to respond to contact attempts. He said Qatar had invited Iran in April to review the search-and-rescue findings but had received no response.

The incident occurred after Qatar said its air force shot down two Iranian Su-24 bombers on March 2 as Iran struck countries hosting US military bases in the opening days of the war. Until now, Tehran had said it had no information about the pilots' fate. This is the first known case in which Iran has accused a regional country of holding its fighters. In a separate development, Bagherzadeh also accused Kuwait of withholding information about four Iranians affiliated with the Revolutionary Guards who entered Kuwaiti territorial waters in May due to a navigation disruption; Kuwait has said the four were suspected of planning hostile actions.

The dispute comes amid wider regional tensions. The United Arab Emirates reported that a tanker owned by state oil company ADNOC was attacked while transiting the Strait of Hormuz on Friday evening, the third such attack on an ADNOC vessel in a week. The UAE called on Iran to stop the unprovoked strikes. In southern Lebanon, Israeli airstrikes killed at least 11 people on Saturday, including three children, in one of the deadliest days since a June 20 truce between Israel and Hezbollah. Israel said it killed a Hezbollah battalion commander and accused him of using family members as human shields. In Yemen, Houthi forces attacked the Red Sea port of Mokha with six ballistic missiles, killing at least three people and suspending port operations, in an escalation analysts say reflects Iran's efforts to pressure the US and its allies.
