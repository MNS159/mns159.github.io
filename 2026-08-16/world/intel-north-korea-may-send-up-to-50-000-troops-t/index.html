North Korea Plans to Send Up to 50,000 More Troops to Russia, Intelligence Says

International & World desk

Intelligence agencies report that North Korea is preparing to send an additional 30,000 to 50,000 troops to Russia, according to Ukrainian and international officials. The deployment would supplement an estimated 14,000 to 15,000 North Korean troops already sent to help defend Russia's Kursk region since late 2024. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy has warned that North Korea's improved combat and weapons experience could pose a security threat to both Ukraine and the Asia-Pacific region.

North Korea has also supplied Russia with ballistic missiles. Ukraine's Main Directorate of Intelligence (HUR) states that 148 KN-23 and KN-24 missiles have been delivered since 2025. The HUR recently reported that a North Korean missile unit is deploying to Russia's Voronezh region, potentially equipped with 120 ballistic missiles. Military experts describe North Korean missiles as similar to Russia's Iskander systems but with a greater payload, longer range, and lower accuracy. Yuri Fyodorov, a military expert, called them 'instruments of terror,' noting that they are difficult to aim at small targets and have been used to strike residential areas.

Military analysts question whether the additional troops will significantly alter the war's course. Oleksandr Kovalenko of the Ukrainian Center for Security Studies told DW that North Korean forces would likely be used to secure Russian border territories, such as Kursk and southwestern Voronezh, freeing up Russian troops for front-line battles in Vovchansk, Kharkiv, Sumy, or Chernihiv. He does not expect North Korean troops to fight on Ukrainian territory. Russian military analyst Ruslan Leviev agreed, saying that 'it's one thing to recapture Russian territory, and an entirely different thing to be present on internationally recognized Ukrainian territory, taking part in a war of aggression.' Leviev added that he has seen no concrete evidence of a noteworthy North Korean presence and that Russia still has enough troops, recruiting around 1,000 contract soldiers daily according to economist Janis Kluge.

None of the experts consulted believe North Korea's involvement will be a turning point. Jenny Town of the Foreign Policy Research Institute said the time for such a difference has passed, and the partnership between Russia and North Korea is likely to deepen. However, Kovalenko expressed concern about the coming winter: 'October, November and December could become very trying for Ukraine.' He noted that Russia could benefit from a new military call-up, replacing troops with North Korean forces, and from a steady supply of ballistic missiles. 'That would be no good for the course of the war, and for Ukraine's war interests,' said Fyodorov.
