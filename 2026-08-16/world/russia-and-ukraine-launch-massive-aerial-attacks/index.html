Ukraine War

Russia and Ukraine exchange massive aerial attacks, killing at least 9

International & World desk · anchored on Reuters

Russia and Ukraine launched large-scale aerial attacks against each other overnight, killing at least nine people across both countries, officials said Sunday. In one of Ukraine's largest drone offensives of the war, Russia's Defense Ministry reported destroying 822 Ukrainian drones nationwide. Moscow Mayor Sergei Sobyanin said about 600 drones were detected heading toward the capital, with roughly a third destroyed over the Moscow region. An 83-year-old man was killed when a drone hit a private home in the Ramensky district, local Gov. Andrey Vorobyov said. In Russia's southwestern Rostov region, a drone attack killed five people, damaged homes and a railway station, and sparked a forest fire, Gov. Yury Slyusar said. A woman was also killed in the Belgorod region after a bus was struck, Gov. Vyacheslav Gladkov said.

In Ukraine, Russian strikes killed at least five people and wounded more than 10, officials said. In Kryvyi Rih, President Volodymyr Zelensky's hometown, a 44-year-old man was found dead in rubble and 14 steel plant workers were wounded. Two people in their 60s were killed when a house was destroyed in Zaporizhzhia, and one person was killed in Sumy, Zelensky said. In Kyiv, air-raid sirens sounded four times since Saturday night; at least six people, including a child, were injured, Mayor Vitali Klitschko said. A book market in the city's Pochayna district was damaged, with burnt books scattered on the ground. Vinyl store owner Luba, 42, said she lost 500,000 hryvnias (£8,262) worth of stock, telling the BBC: "This business was my baby — many of the vinyls destroyed were rare and irreplaceable."

Russia said its attacks on Kyiv targeted a FirePoint facility producing parts for Ukrainian Flamingo missiles and a factory making drone components. Moscow also said it struck the Kremenchuk oil refinery, a steelworks in Kryvyi Rih, and a dry cargo vessel near Odesa, claiming each was used for military purposes. Zelensky accused Russia of targeting civilian infrastructure, saying: "Wherever the Russians can reach with their ballistic missiles, they strike civilian infrastructure." He said 13 regions had come under attack this week, with Russia launching more than 1,550 attack drones, nearly 1,560 guided aerial bombs, and 62 missiles.

In Russia, a Ukrainian drone strike hit a Wildberries warehouse in Koledino, about 25 km (15.5 miles) from central Moscow, sparking a fire and a large plume of smoke. Wildberries, Russia's largest online retailer, has been repeatedly targeted since July; Kyiv said it was aiming to disrupt logistics hubs used by the Russian military. Three people were injured in the overnight Ukrainian strike on Moscow, Sobyanin said.

Separately, Romania's Defense Ministry said a Spanish F-18 fighter jet on NATO air policing duties shot down a drone that breached Romanian airspace from Moldova early Sunday. Debris fell in an unpopulated area of the Galati region near the Ukrainian border. It was the fourth drone shot down in Romania this year. Martin O'Donnell, a spokesman for NATO's military headquarters, said the drone "appears to be Russian."

Russia launched its full-scale invasion of Ukraine in February 2022 and currently controls about a fifth of Ukrainian territory. With marginal gains along the front line, the war has become predominantly an aerial conflict, with both sides using missiles and drones to degrade each other's warfighting ability. Ukraine has stepped up attacks on Russian military, industrial, and logistics targets this year, while Russia maintains near-daily strikes on Ukrainian civilian and military infrastructure.
