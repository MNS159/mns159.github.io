Saudi Arabia warns of 'red line' after Houthi drone near Mecca; conflict escalates

International & World desk · anchored on Reuters, AP

Saudi Arabia said it shot down a Houthi drone south of the Islamic holy city of Mecca on Tuesday evening, prompting warnings from the Saudi-led coalition and the 57-nation Organisation of Islamic Cooperation, which called attacks on Mecca and Medina province an 'unacceptable and intolerable transgression.' The Houthis denied targeting Mecca, with military spokesman Yahya Sarea accusing Saudi Arabia of 'fabrications and lies' and stating that Houthi operations target 'oil facilities and military bases, and are far removed from the holy places.' Sarea also said the Houthis had launched drones and missiles in retaliation for more than 450 Saudi air strikes over the past week, and claimed they shot down a Saudi fighter jet over Marib province.

The incident came as the Houthis seized control of large swaths of Yemen's Red Sea coast, including the strategic port city of Mokha and Perim Island in the Bab al-Mandab Strait, the southern gateway to a key shipping route linking Asia and Europe via the Red Sea and Suez Canal. The Houthis said they pose no threat to international shipping but would target vessels from Saudi Arabia, which has relied on the Red Sea for oil exports since the Strait of Hormuz was effectively closed due to the US and Israel's war with Iran. Meanwhile, Saudi Arabia's key East-West Pipeline, used to transfer crude oil from the Gulf to the Red Sea port of Yanbu, was closed after being damaged in a strike the kingdom blamed on Iran-backed militias in Iraq. US Energy Secretary Chris Wright predicted oil would flow within days, though some experts said repairs could take more than a month.

Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman has personally pressed US President Donald Trump to take military action against the Houthis, but Trump has so far declined, offering only intelligence and targeting support, according to sources familiar with the matter. A senior Trump administration official said, 'We are in continuous dialogue with Saudi Arabia and the Republic of Yemen government regarding regional stability.' The United Nations Security Council held an emergency meeting where Yemen's government and Saudi Arabia called for more decisive action against the Houthis, who they accused of choking the Bab al-Mandab Strait to apply economic pressure. Council members agreed that freedom of navigation in the Red Sea must be maintained.

The escalation has deepened Yemen's humanitarian crisis. The International Organization for Migration reported that more than 90,000 people have been displaced by recent fighting, with director general Amy Pope warning that 'families are being forced to flee for the second or third time in this conflict, with almost nothing left.' The World Health Organization said at least 500 people had been killed since the start of August, including 297 in the previous week. Yemen's civil war, which began in 2014 when the Houthis ousted the government from Sanaa, has left more than 150,000 dead and triggered one of the world's worst humanitarian crises, with over 22 million people in need of aid, according to the UN.

The conflict is also putting pressure on Egypt, which depends on Suez Canal revenues. Houthi attacks in late 2023 had already reduced canal earnings by around $6 billion in 2024, according to the IMF, though revenues had recently recovered, rising 23% to $4.67 billion in fiscal 2025–2026. Stephan Roll of the German Institute for International and Security Affairs noted that the Houthis 'could actually interrupt, or at least significantly disturb, the connection between Asia and Europe.' Egypt has limited military options; its participation in the Saudi-led coalition is largely symbolic, and it has rejected a reported Houthi offer to discuss shipping security for fear of legitimizing the group. Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman traveled to Cairo on Tuesday seeking Egypt's support, but analysts say Egypt is likely to wait and see as it balances its alliance with Saudi Arabia against its own economic vulnerabilities.
