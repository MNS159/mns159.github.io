Gaza building collapse kills 21, dozens missing under rubble

International & World desk

At least 21 people, including eight children, have been killed and dozens are missing after a war-damaged six-storey building collapsed overnight in the Tal al-Hawa area of Gaza City, local rescuers and medics said. The building, which had been left tilting dangerously after an Israeli strike more than a year ago, housed displaced families who had nowhere else to go, witnesses told the BBC. The Hamas-run Civil Defence agency said about 100 people were still under the rubble, and UN officials estimated between 70 and 100 were trapped.

Rescue efforts are proceeding slowly due to a severe lack of equipment. Civil Defence workers initially used their bare hands to dig, and UN agencies and an Egyptian team have joined the search. “We are still hearing voices from under the rubble, so there is still hope to find people,” said Raed al-Dahshan, director of the Civil Defence in Gaza City. Alessandro Mrakic, head of the UNDP Gaza office, said at the scene: “The situation, as you see, is tragic because we don't have the equipment and machinery that is needed. There are actually people digging with their bare hands.” He added that approximately 400 other war-damaged buildings across Gaza are at imminent risk of collapse, particularly as winter approaches.

Witnesses described the sudden collapse. “It felt like an earthquake. There was a loud sound and I couldn't stand up. Stones rained down on us and when I looked outside I saw a lot of dust,” Umm Mohammed Bakrun, a woman living in a nearby tent, told the BBC. Mansour Abu Mohammed said the building collapsed “in a few seconds,” adding: “Every day, a stone or a few stones would fall from it, but the people inside couldn't find anywhere else to live. I used to live next door to it and you could see that it was tilting to one side.” Locals said some 10 families had been living in the building's apartments.

The UN's humanitarian relief co-ordinator, Ramiz Alakbarov, said he was “devastated” by the reports and urged Israeli authorities to approve deliveries of heavy machinery for rescue operations, as well as tents and tarpaulins ahead of winter rains. Israel says its restrictions are meant to keep equipment out of the hands of Palestinian armed groups. The war, triggered by the Hamas-led attack on southern Israel on 7 October 2023, has destroyed or damaged an estimated 80% of all structures in Gaza, according to the UN. More than 1.9 million people — about 90% of the population — have been displaced, with 1.2 million living in tents or makeshift shelters. The Gaza health ministry, whose figures are considered reliable by the UN, reports more than 73,790 people killed since the war began.
