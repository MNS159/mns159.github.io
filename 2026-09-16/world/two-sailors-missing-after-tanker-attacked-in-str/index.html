Two sailors missing after tanker attacked in Strait of Hormuz; oil prices rise

International & World desk

Two sailors are missing and 23 others have been rescued following an attack on an oil tanker in the Strait of Hormuz, Oman's Maritime Security Centre said. The Panama-flagged El Gaia was being towed to an Omani port after a fire broke out in its engine room, with the vessel targeted 1.3 nautical miles (2.4km) north of the Musandam peninsula.

Conflicting accounts of the attack have emerged. Iran's Revolutionary Guards said Monday that the tanker hit naval mines and caught fire while passing through what they described as a restricted and unsafe route. The US military insisted the Iranian report was false, saying the tanker was struck by an Iranian missile last month and again by a drone over the weekend. The UK Maritime Trade Operations centre said it had received a report of a fire breaking out on board a vessel struck by an unknown projectile while transiting the Strait of Hormuz in the early hours of Sunday.

The attack follows a similar incident earlier this month that killed two Filipino sailors on the Saudi-owned oil supertanker Sidr. The Revolutionary Guards said that vessel caught fire after hitting a naval mine, but Saudi authorities said it was targeted by Iran, and maritime security firms reported it was hit by projectiles north of Musandam. The International Maritime Organization says at least 22 seafarers have been killed in 79 confirmed incidents in the Strait of Hormuz and the rest of the Middle East since the start of the US and Israel's war with Iran at the end of February.

In a related development, Iran's semi-official Fars news agency, linked to the Revolutionary Guards, reported that "enemy drones" had targeted two fishing boats in the strait overnight near the Iranian port of Kargan, about 80km (50 miles) north-east of Musandam. The agency cited the deputy governor of Hormozgan province as saying several fishermen were reported missing and a search-and-rescue operation was under way. Later, Capt Tim Hawkins, a spokesman for the US military's Central Command (Centcom), told the news website Axios that its forces had destroyed two boats which tried to steal a sea drone. "Iranian small boats recently attempted to take possession of a US unmanned surface vessel, but they were unsuccessful after Centcom forcefully responded," he said.

The Strait of Hormuz, through which a fifth of global oil and gas shipments usually pass, has effectively been closed due to Iranian missile and drone attacks on commercial vessels and a US naval blockade of Iranian ports. The US and Iran reached a preliminary agreement to end the war and reopen the strait in June, but it collapsed within weeks after Iranian attacks on shipping resumed and the US reinstated its blockade. Iran has insisted it will not give up some measure of control over the waterway and has targeted vessels using a southern route through Omani waters that avoids its preferred route through Iranian waters.

Oil prices have ticked up as the conflict widens and disruptions grow in the Middle East, though prices slipped in some trading as Saudi Arabia offered more crude via Oman, with diesel near record highs.
