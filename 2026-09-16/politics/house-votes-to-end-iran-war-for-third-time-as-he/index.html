House votes to end Iran war for third time as Hegseth faces impeachment

Politics desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts slightly center-left
✓ Facts
  • Rep. Thomas Massie (R-Ky.) introduced articles of impeachment against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth on Tuesday, reading them aloud from the House floor, alleging Hegseth waged the Iran war illegally, defied a congressional order to withdraw, and caused unnecessary civilian casualties.
  • Under House rules, the chamber must act on Massie's impeachment resolution by Thursday, just before lawmakers leave Washington for an extended recess lasting until after the November midterms.
  • Republicans currently hold enough votes to defeat the impeachment measure, according to NPR, though the vote could be difficult for GOP members in competitive districts.
  • A Pentagon inspector general report found the Iran conflict, formerly called Operation Epic Fury, cost the U.S. $33.4 billion through mid-June; the nonpartisan Congressional Budget Office estimates it could cost roughly $2 billion more per month if it continues.
  • Attorney General Todd Blanche told reporters Tuesday that Hegseth was "doing a phenomenal job," and Pentagon press secretary Kingsley Wilson said Hegseth has been a "transformative leader" who is unified with the department "behind the Secretary's vision."
  • A recent poll cited by Al Jazeera found fewer than a third of Americans support the Iran war.
  • Massie is not seeking reelection after losing his primary to a Trump-backed challenger; he previously worked with Democratic Rep. Ro Khanna of California on an unrelated effort to force release of files related to Jeffrey Epstein.
  • Iran's Revolutionary Guard Corps said Wednesday it destroyed a U.S. MQ-9 Reaper drone, the 52nd it claims to have downed during the conflict; the Pentagon watchdog report said up to 30 Reaper drones were damaged or destroyed between Feb. 28 and June 30.
○ Analysis

This story sits at the intersection of an unpopular six-month-old U.S.-Iran war and an intraparty Republican clash over how that war has been conducted. Massie is using impeachment as a mechanism to force a public reckoning over the legality and cost of military action, alleging Hegseth waged the war for more than 90 days without congressional authorization. The administration has defended Hegseth's conduct, and Republican leaders are expected to have the votes to block the impeachment attempt, with the House required to act by Thursday, just before lawmakers leave for an extended recess. The backdrop is a costly and expanding regional conflict: independent Pentagon and CBO estimates put the price tag at over $33 billion and rising, while fighting has spilled into Saudi Arabia and Yemen through Iran-backed Houthi attacks, and Iran has publicly rejected U.S. overtures toward talks. Public opinion polling shows broad American opposition to the war, giving Massie's effort political salience even though it is unlikely to succeed procedurally.

Steelman

Beyond the partisan mechanics of an impeachment vote widely expected to fail, Congress has voted three times to limit the president's war powers regarding Iran, including a resolution passed the same week as Massie's impeachment effort, though the White House has argued against the validity of these resolutions.

Implications

The war has already cost tens of billions of dollars according to Pentagon and CBO estimates, and has coincided with escalating conflict involving Saudi Arabia and Yemen's Houthi rebels, alongside sustained public opposition to the war reflected in recent polling.
