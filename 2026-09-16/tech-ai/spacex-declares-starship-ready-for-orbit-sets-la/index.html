SpaceX declares Starship ready for orbital attempt, sets launch date of September 22

Tech / AI desk

SpaceX announced Tuesday that it intends to launch the 14th test flight of its Starship rocket as soon as September 22, pending regulatory approval. The mission will be the first time the company attempts to put the Starship upper stage into orbit around Earth. Liftoff from SpaceX’s facility in Brownsville, Texas, is targeted for 7:15 a.m. CT (12:15 UTC) within a 75-minute launch window.

For this flight, Starship will carry 26 of SpaceX’s third-generation V3 Starlink satellites to an orbit 275 kilometers above Earth. The upper stage is expected to complete six orbits over roughly 10 hours before ending its mission. If successful, the deployment would mark the first Starship launch to generate revenue for SpaceX’s launch division, even if the customer is another part of the company.

SpaceX detailed design changes to the Super Heavy booster following issues on the previous flight, which occurred on July 24. During that test, the booster’s three center engines showed signs of ice clogging during the return through the atmosphere, triggering an early end to the maneuver. The booster attempted a landing burn with 8 of 13 planned engines reigniting before making a hard splashdown in the Gulf of Mexico. SpaceX said the Super Heavy on the upcoming flight has hardware modifications to improve engine filtering and software changes to enhance relight reliability.

The company also said it made additional changes to the Starship heat shield based on what it learned from studying the recovered upper stage from the July test. That flight was the first to successfully deploy V3 Starlink satellites, though those burned up in the atmosphere after roughly 20 minutes. The upper stage had a smooth simulated landing in the Indian Ocean and did not explode when it tipped over, allowing SpaceX to recover and tow it back to Texas for analysis.

SpaceX will not attempt to catch either the Super Heavy booster or the Starship upper stage with the launch tower on this flight, following founder and CEO Elon Musk’s statement that an explosion would be too big a setback for the company. Reaching orbit would represent a major milestone in Starship’s development, which began with its first test flight on April 20, 2023. SpaceX aims to eventually retire its Falcon 9 and Falcon Heavy rockets in favor of Starship once the vehicle is flying reliably multiple times per week, a cadence the company promised during its initial public offering process in June.
