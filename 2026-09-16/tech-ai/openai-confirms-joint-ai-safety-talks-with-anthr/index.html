OpenAI confirms joint AI safety talks with Anthropic and Google DeepMind

Tech / AI desk · anchored on Reuters

OpenAI has confirmed that it has been in talks with rivals Anthropic and Google DeepMind for several weeks regarding AI safety, according to a report by Bloomberg. The company's global policy chief, Chris Lehane, disclosed the discussions during a Tuesday briefing in Washington, D.C., saying, “It’s better to try to work together to prioritize safety.” The confirmation follows earlier reporting from The Information and an interview in which OpenAI CEO Sam Altman hinted at private discussions with other AI leaders.

The collaborative talks have roots in a July proposal from Google DeepMind chair Demis Hassabis, who called for a U.S.-led “Standards Body” — structured as a public-private partnership or a self-regulatory organization with federal oversight, similar to the Financial Industry Regulatory Authority — that could screen advanced AI models and coordinate industrywide slowdowns if dangers emerged. An OpenAI spokesperson confirmed to CNBC that the companies have been discussing the proposal since its release. Lehane argued that no antitrust waiver is necessary for the coordination, comparing it to safety cooperation in the commercial airline industry.

The announcement follows a 3,800-word essay published Saturday by Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei, who urged AI companies to slow the pace of frontier model development so researchers can better understand potential catastrophic risks. Amodei’s call received backing from OpenAI’s Sam Altman, Google DeepMind’s Demis Hassabis, and xAI founder Elon Musk. Altman posted on X that a slowdown has been a “primary topic” of discussion at OpenAI in recent weeks and that the company will have more to share “soon.” Hassabis wrote that Amodei’s essay “points towards the right path forward.”

The prospect of direct competitors coordinating on safety has drawn scrutiny from antitrust regulators. FTC Chair Andrew Ferguson, speaking Tuesday, said he would be “deeply suspicious” of requests for antitrust exemptions, viewing the coordination as an attempt to create barriers to entry that insulate incumbent firms from challengers. “That sure sounds like moat digging,” Ferguson said, according to Bloomberg.

Lehane, who is in Washington working with lawmakers, said OpenAI is prepared to back bipartisan legislation targeting catastrophic AI risks. The company supports a federal governance framework proposed by Representative Jay Obernolte (R-CA) and Representative Lori Trahan (D-MA), as well as a Senate effort led by Majority Leader John Thune (R-SD), along with Senators Ted Cruz (R-TX) and Amy Klobuchar (D-MN). OpenAI also supports a provision in the FRONTIER Act that would require top frontier labs to allow independent verification organizations into their companies to ensure safe model development, according to TechCrunch. However, any AI bill becoming law before the end of 2026 is considered a long shot, as the House departs Washington on Thursday and has no scheduled sessions before the November election.
