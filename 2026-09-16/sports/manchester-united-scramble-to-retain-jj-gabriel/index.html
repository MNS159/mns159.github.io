Manchester United scramble to keep JJ Gabriel after planned record debut collapses

Sports desk

Manchester United are scrambling to keep 15-year-old forward JJ Gabriel after the teenager decided he wants to leave the club, reversing plans that would have seen him become the club's youngest ever player. The decision comes a week after Gabriel scored a hat-trick on his Uefa Youth League debut, which had been seen as a prelude to a senior debut against Brighton in the Carabao Cup third round on Wednesday. Chief executive Omar Berrada and director of football Jason Wilcox are now involved in talks aimed at persuading Gabriel to stay, though sources describe the effort as a 'forlorn task'.

Gabriel was named Under-18 Premier League player of the year last season despite being only 14 when the campaign began, scoring 23 goals in the league and three in the FA Youth Cup. He was also United's Under-18 Player of the Year. One United source recently described him as 'the best 15-year-old in Europe'. His talent has attracted attention from Real Madrid, Barcelona and Paris St-Germain, who all focus on elite youth players. Gabriel's potential is not confined to England; he holds a European Union passport through his mother's Greek-Cypriot heritage and his father's two caps for the Republic of Ireland.

The deterioration in relations stems from a belief that promises made to Gabriel about his career path have not been kept. After committing his short-term future to United last summer, he did begin training with the senior squad under Ruben Amorim and then Michael Carrick. However, a senior debut last season was ruled out by then-interim manager Darren Fletcher, who felt it was too early. During pre-season, Gabriel played only eight minutes in a friendly against Atletico Madrid and was an unused substitute in a match against Rosenborg. It was noted that 18-year-old Tynan Thompson, who joined from Tottenham three weeks earlier, made a 45-minute senior debut against Leeds United in a match Gabriel also travelled to but did not feature in.

Gabriel had been expected to make his senior debut this autumn, likely in the EFL Cup. But when United faced Sabah FK in the Uefa Youth League, Gabriel could not be used as a substitute because of Uefa registration rules: as a 15-year-old, he needed to be on the club's A list of players for the senior squad, yet United did not name him on that list. The club's coaches have suggested Gabriel is not yet at the required physical level, but sources close to the player reject that, pointing to Arsenal giving 15-year-old Max Dowman a chance and Bayern Munich giving 17-year-old Lennart Karl a debut.

Gabriel's registration with United runs until the end of the season, which may buy time regarding moves to Premier League rivals. But European clubs could exploit his EU passport. Legal precedents exist: in 2019, 16-year-old Louie Barry left West Brom for Barcelona with West Brom receiving no compensation after failing to formally offer him a scholarship. A worst-case scenario for United would be Gabriel walking away when he turns 16 on 6 October, potentially leaving the club with no compensation for a player whose market value, even at this age, is measured in millions.

The situation echoes other youth transfers. Jadon Sancho was 17 when he stopped training at Manchester City to force a £10m move to Borussia Dortmund. Morgan Rogers left West Brom for Manchester City on his 17th birthday. United Under-21s coach Adam Lawrence, after Gabriel's Youth League hat-trick, said: 'The first thing in my job is not to ruin him... you obviously don't want to stifle their freedom and their creativity.' For now, Berrada and Wilcox are trying to prevent a second thing: losing him entirely.
