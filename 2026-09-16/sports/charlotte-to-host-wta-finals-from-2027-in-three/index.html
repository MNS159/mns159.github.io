Charlotte to Host WTA Finals From 2027; Tour Takes Over Operations

Sports desk

The WTA Finals, the season-ending championship for women's tennis, will be held in Charlotte, North Carolina, for a minimum of three years beginning in 2027, the WTA and the Charlotte Sports Foundation jointly announced Wednesday. The event will take place at the Spectrum Center, home of the NBA's Charlotte Hornets. Under the agreement, the WTA will also relocate its global headquarters from St. Petersburg, Florida, to Charlotte and has pledged to create a path toward bringing a WTA 500 tournament to the city after 2029.

WTA chair Valerie Camillo said the tour is taking operational control of the Finals for the first time, moving away from the previous model of receiving licensing fees from host cities. "The WTA has always owned the WTA Finals, but now going forward, we're going to operate it too," Camillo said. "We're taking control of the creation of the revenues and the controlling of the expenses and the delivery of the entire thing. And that allows us to control our own destiny." Camillo's ambition, she stated, is for the Finals to become "the Super Bowl for women's sports," adding that audiences and investment in women's athletics are growing.

The Finals, which feature the year's top eight singles players and doubles teams, have had six different hosts in seven years after a planned 10-year stay in Shenzhen, China, ended after a single edition. Subsequent stops included Guadalajara, Fort Worth, Cancun, and Riyadh, Saudi Arabia, where crowds were frequently sparse. This year's tournament will be held in Indian Wells, California, after the WTA opted out of the final year of a three-year hosting deal with Saudi Arabia. Camillo cited the unexpected cost of that relocation and ongoing belt-tightening as part of a transition year for the tour in 2026.

The move to the Spectrum Center — an NBA arena that will be in regular-season use during the Finals — will compress the tournament schedule from eight days to five. Camillo said the WTA is exploring alternate formats with players; if the round-robin component remains, finalists would need to play every day. Prize money for the Charlotte edition has not been announced. The prize pool for this year's Finals in Indian Wells is $10 million, $5.5 million less than the sum put up by Saudi Arabia last year. In 2025, winner Elena Rybakina earned a women's-sports-record payout of approximately $5.3 million in Riyadh.

The WTA described the Charlotte arrangement as a minimum three-year deal. Financial backing comes from regional economic enterprises, local government funding, and investment from Charlotte's corporate community, according to the Charlotte Sports Foundation. Spectrum Center has previously hosted high-profile tennis exhibitions.

Camillo indicated the tour will continue seeking control over its own media rights and calendar. Asked about a potential merger with the men's ATP Finals, she said there had not been an in-depth conversation on that topic, adding: "I think that when we create our ambition, people are going to want to merge with us on this event and many more." This year's WTA Finals will run Nov. 7-14 in Indian Wells.
