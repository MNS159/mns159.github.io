Silver Defends Clippers Penalties in Kawhi Case; Leonard Not Suspended

Sports desk

NBA Commissioner Adam Silver defended the league's punishment of the Los Angeles Clippers in the Kawhi Leonard salary cap scandal, calling the sanctions 'appropriate consequences' for the organization. The NBA imposed a record $30 million fine on the Clippers, stripped them of five first-round draft picks, and suspended owner Steve Ballmer for one year after an investigation uncovered a scheme in which the team used sponsorship deals to funnel millions in under-the-table payments to Leonard and his uncle, Dennis Robertson.

Leonard, who was traded back to the Toronto Raptors, received a $700,000 restitution order but was not suspended by the NBA, and his contract was not voided. Robertson was banned from all future business dealings with NBA teams for five years. The scandal has also drawn federal and Securities and Exchange Commission probes into the illicit side deals.

ESPN's Shams Charania explained why the league did not take harsher action against Leonard, citing collective bargaining agreement restrictions and the role of the National Basketball Players Association. 'It's hard for the league because of how the collective bargaining agreement is, and there is a players union,' Charania said. He added that voiding Leonard's contract or administering a suspension would have required 'a stone cold case' to withstand litigation and arbitration from the players' union.

The investigation, which spanned a year, found that the Clippers acted like 'a power agent, brokering deals' for Robertson on Leonard's behalf, according to Charania. The team funneled endorsement income to Leonard during the 2020 and 2021 seasons through sponsors such as Aspiration and Daktronics, with Leonard not required to fulfill any real obligations in return. Reporting also detailed Robertson's alleged requests to teams over the years, including partial ownership stakes, a private plane, and a guaranteed house, all outside the bounds of the collective bargaining agreement.

The punishment drew sharp criticism from ESPN commentator Stephen A. Smith, who called Leonard 'a gangster' for avoiding significant penalty. 'He broke the rules, got caught, pointed the finger at somebody else, threw the organization under the bus, got his money and left the country,' Smith said on 'First Take.' Leonard, who waived a $7.45 million trade kicker to facilitate his return to Toronto, 'let management take the bullet while he skated away untouched,' Smith added.

With the investigation closed and Leonard's trade finalized, the league's sanctions are seen as a message to the NBA's owner class about compliance with salary cap rules. The Clippers' penalties represent the most severe in league history, while Leonard's light punishment underscores the legal constraints the NBA faced in disciplining a player under the collective bargaining agreement and union protections.
