2030 Winter Olympics venues confirmed; speed skating in Netherlands

Sports desk

The International Olympic Committee (IOC) on Wednesday confirmed the 11 competition venues for the 2030 Winter Games, with events split across southern France and, for the first time in Olympic history, an independent nation outside the host region: the Netherlands will host the long track speed skating events.

Lyon will serve as the ice sports hub, hosting curling, figure skating, and short track speed skating at three sites including Parc Olympique Lyonnais, home of Ligue 1 side Lyon. Alpine skiing speed events — downhill, super-G, and team events — will be held in Courchevel, while Val d'Isere stages the slalom events. The long track speed skating competitions will take place at the Thialf arena in Heerenveen, Netherlands, a pre-existing facility chosen to avoid building new venues without a clear legacy in the host region.

The announcement caps a turbulent period for the 2030 Games organisers. In May, the ice sports hub was moved from Nice to Lyon after Nice's mayor, Eric Ciotti, refused to allow the city's football stadium to be converted into a temporary ice rink. Last month, organising committee president Edgar Grospiron resigned after just 18 months in the role, amid budget cuts for the Games.

The 2030 Winter Olympics will feature 16 sporting disciplines, including two new additions: freeride skiing and snowboarding, and synchro9 — synchronised figure skating. However, the Games will be the first not to include nordic combined, which has been removed from the programme.

Holding the speed skating in the Netherlands was a condition agreed with the IOC when the French Alps were awarded the Games, as organisers sought to reduce costs by using an existing arena rather than constructing a new one. This marks the first time an Olympic event — summer or winter — will be held in a different independent nation from the host city or region.
