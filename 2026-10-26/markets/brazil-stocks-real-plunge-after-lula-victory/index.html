Brazil Stocks, Real Plunge After Lula Victory

Financial & Markets desk · anchored on Reuters

Brazilian stocks and the real plunged on Monday after Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva won an unprecedented fourth term as Brazil's president, a dramatic comeback from a first-round vote in which he had trailed rival Bolsonaro. The iShares MSCI Brazil ETF (EWZ) fell more than 20% overnight on the result, a move that could mark the biggest one-day drop in the ETF's history, while November futures on the Brazilian real slid 10% in Asian trading.

U.S.-listed shares of Brazilian companies also fell in premarket trading, with state-controlled oil giant Petrobras down more than 20%, banking group Itaú Unibanco down almost 30%, and brewer Ambev off 19%.

Bolsonaro has refused to concede defeat, saying he would seek the annulment of the outcome, Reuters reported. He also called a ruling by Supreme Court Justice Cristiano Zanin to extend voting by two hours "unacceptable."

Bolsonaro had been seen as the more market-friendly candidate, with expectations that he would push for fiscal austerity and deregulation, while Lula will now have to convince markets he can stabilize Brazil's debt trajectory. Reuters said the win ended a bullish run in Brazilian assets, with analysts having forecast a market plunge after Lula's turnaround victory.

The result also carries geopolitical stakes: U.S. President Donald Trump had celebrated Bolsonaro's lead in the first round, hoping that a fellow right-wing leader could bring closer alignment with U.S. foreign policy.
