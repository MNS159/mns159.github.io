Formula 1

Antonelli's US Grand Prix win strengthens title bid

Sports desk

Kimi Antonelli moved to the brink of a record-breaking Formula 1 world championship with a commanding victory in the United States Grand Prix in Austin. The 20-year-old Mercedes driver's win leaves him in an impregnable position: he will clinch the title next weekend in Mexico City if he finishes ninth or, if Ferrari's Lewis Hamilton wins the race, 10th.

Mercedes team principal Toto Wolff described Antonelli's performance as 'the best he has delivered so far' and 'the race of a champion who isn't the champion yet.' Antonelli started from pole and dominated, while Russell struggled to eighth after a five-second penalty. Wolff said Antonelli 'outperformed the car,' noting that the upgraded Mercedes is 'difficult to operate' and that Antonelli's driving style 'just glues with the car.' Antonelli agreed that the execution was better than his spectacular comeback win at Monza, saying, 'This one probably, in terms of execution, was better, because the way we built up and the way we built the performance throughout the weekend was very good.'

Antonelli arrived in Austin with a strong championship lead, knowing he would secure the title if he won with Russell lower than eighth and Hamilton lower than fourth. He won, but Russell (eighth) and Hamilton (fourth) each did just enough to keep the title fight alive until Mexico. Wolff noted that Antonelli showed no signs of pressure, sitting in his chair after the race, saying, 'This is so funny. I'm not thinking about the championship at all.' Wolff added, 'The advantage helps. But it's also innate in him and I don't think that it's something to be trained for. He's not over-thinking it.'

When Antonelli clinches the title, he will become the first Italian champion since Alberto Ascari in 1953. McLaren team principal Andrea Stella, an Italian, said, 'As an Italian, wow, we need to go back to Ascari... To see an Italian achieving this level of performance and results in Formula 1, that's particularly thrilling.' Stella also noted Antonelli's aggressive yet effective driving style, saying he 'pretty much attacks all the times' but can also 'bring home the result.' Antonelli will also become the youngest champion in F1 history, beating Sebastian Vettel's 2010 record by more than three years.

Antonelli said he will approach the Mexico weekend with the same mindset: 'Just trying to have as much fun as possible, trying to hopefully fight for another win. It would be best to finish on a high, and that's the goal. But I'm just going to try to enjoy the rest of the season from now on.'
