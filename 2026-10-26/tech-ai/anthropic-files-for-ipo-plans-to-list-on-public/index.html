Anthropic files for IPO, plans to list on public market

Tech / AI desk

Anthropic, the artificial intelligence company behind the chatbot Claude, filed its S-1 prospectus with the Securities and Exchange Commission on Monday, setting the stage for an initial public offering that could be the largest in U.S. history. The San Francisco-based company plans to list on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under the ticker symbol "ANT," according to the filing. The IPO, expected next month, may raise as much as $100 billion, surpassing the record $86 billion raised by SpaceX in June, and could value Anthropic at between $1.5 trillion and $2 trillion, up from a private valuation of $965 billion earlier this year.

The 335-page filing reveals rapidly growing revenue but continued losses. Anthropic reported revenue of $16.3 billion for the first half of 2026, up from $1.2 billion in the same period last year. The company recorded an operating loss of $693 million, an improvement from $2.1 billion in the first half of 2025. However, its net loss ballooned to $80.7 billion, driven largely by noncash accounting adjustments related to convertible debt. Anthropic cautioned that it "may not achieve or sustain profitability in the future." The company also disclosed detailed customer metrics: as of September, it had more than 11,900 customers spending $100,000 or more annually, 1,900 spending $1 million or more, and about 250 spending $10 million or more.

The filing outlines massive infrastructure commitments to power its AI systems. Anthropic has deals worth billions of dollars for compute access, including about $3.1 billion per month to SpaceX for Nvidia GPUs, totaling as much as $120.5 billion by 2029. Other commitments include $44.9 billion with Microsoft through 2033, at least $114.5 billion with Google through 2033, and $110 billion with Amazon over the next ten years.

Anthropic's governance structure will allow its seven co-founders—Dario Amodei, Daniela Amodei, Tom Brown, Jack Clark, Jared Kaplan, Sam McCandlish, and Christopher Olah—to maintain control after going public. They will form a "Founder LLC" that collectively controls 50.1% of shareholder voting power, a move the company says is designed to preserve its AI safety mission. The Long-Term Benefit Trust, established in 2023, will have the authority to appoint most of the board. The governance structure is controversial because public shareholders will have little practical control. Executive compensation for 2025 totaled nearly $18 million for CEO Dario Amodei and $16.4 million for his sister, co-founder and president Daniela Amodei.

The filing lists extensive risk factors, including the possibility that Anthropic's AI models could "develop forms of sentience," face "AI-specific taxes," and be subject to unfavorable resolutions in ongoing copyright disputes over training data. The company also warns of "catastrophic or existential risks to humanity" from its AI and states that no single insurer—and potentially the insurance industry in the aggregate—could underwrite such risk. The IPO comes weeks after CEO Dario Amodei published an essay calling for a slowdown in the development of the most advanced AI models to allow safety measures to catch up, warning that without such a pause, AI could be capable of taking over the entire internet within six to 12 months.

Anthropic, formed in 2021 by former OpenAI leaders, is locked in a fierce rivalry with OpenAI and other competitors including Google and SpaceX. The company has not yet disclosed how much it hopes to raise nor its proposed share price. The company said it could use proceeds for acquisitions, technology investments, and infrastructure expansion.
