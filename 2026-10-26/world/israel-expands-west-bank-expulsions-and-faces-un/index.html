Israel displaces over 3,000 Palestinians in Hebron; UN warns of ethnic cleansing in earlier West Bank operations

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

Israel has forcibly displaced more than 3,100 Palestinians from the occupied West Bank city of Hebron since Friday, as part of a sweeping military operation that has included home demolitions, mass arrests and the conversion of houses into military barracks. The operation, code-named "Roar of Judah", was launched after two Palestinians killed two Israeli soldiers in a shooting. Hebron Governor Khaled Dudin said 488 families comprising 3,171 people had been displaced across the wider governorate, while Mayor Yousef al-Jabari reported that 3,043 of those forced from their homes are from within Hebron city limits alone. The Palestinian Prisoner’s Society says at least 71 people have been detained since the operation began.

The majority of the displaced are women, children and elderly individuals, according to municipal authorities. Israeli forces have systematically converted dozens of homes into military barracks and field interrogation centres, and demolished others. Mayar Amrish, a 23-year-old whose family home was razed, told Middle East Eye: "My father worked incredibly hard to build our home so we could finally settle down in our own city." Hamza Shabana, displaced from an eight-storey building housing 160 people, said: "We are all living in fear of an unknown future." Israeli Defence Minister Israel Katz said those displaced would not be allowed to return until what he called "Hamas infrastructure" in the city had been dismantled.

Israeli officials have explicitly linked the Hebron operation to previous campaigns, saying they intend to apply the "Jenin camp model". That earlier operation, named "Iron Wall", killed at least 102 people, displaced more than 33,000 and destroyed over half of the residential buildings in the Jenin, Nur Shams and Tulkarem refugee camps. A United Nations Human Rights Office report last month warned that the Israeli military’s actions at those camps "appear to amount to collective punishment and raise concerns of ethnic cleansing". Displaced Palestinians reported being told by Israeli officers that there would be no more refugee camps and that they should "all go to Jordan".

Analysts describe the Hebron crackdown as a calculated effort to permanently displace the Palestinian population. Noman Amr, a professor at al-Quds Open University, said the goal is to create a state of panic and fear and ultimately "to impose a new reality… 'more land, fewer Arabs'." He noted deep collaboration between the army and armed settler groups, with settlers sometimes wearing military uniforms. Mayor al-Jabari accused Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu of exploiting the operation to secure votes in Israel’s upcoming general election. The Israeli army has also imposed a full closure of crossings into and out of the West Bank on election day, and has barred Palestinian workers from entering most areas until Monday.
