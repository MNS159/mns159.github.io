Serbia's Vucic concedes defeat as student-led movement wins snap election

International & World desk

Serbia's long-standing leader Aleksandar Vučić has admitted defeat to a student-led protest movement in snap parliamentary elections, ending more than a decade of his political dominance. With 97 percent of polling stations counted, the election commission said the Student List had won 52.7 percent of the vote (2.25 million votes) against 39.2 percent (1.67 million) for Vučić's Serbian Progressive Party (SNS). Voter turnout reached 68.78 percent, a record high in more than 30 years.

The election was called by Vučić after two years of protests triggered by the collapse of a canopy at Novi Sad railway station in 2024, which killed 16 people and was widely blamed on corruption and negligence. The Student List, formed less than two years ago, fielded candidates from across Serbian society — including doctors, actors, academics and sports personalities — and campaigned on a platform of fighting corruption, purging officials who cracked down on protesters, ending party loyalist control of public enterprises, and freeing the public broadcaster from political control.

Celebrations erupted in Belgrade, Novi Sad and other cities as the result became clear. “Serbia is free,” one student told the BBC. “It's a miracle.” Student List candidate Radomir Saicic said Serbians had shown “no obstacle was greater than their will”. Lead candidate Ilija Srdanović, a cardiologist and professor at the University of Novi Sad, told BBC Serbian: “This will be a free state.” Political scientist Nebojša Vladisavljević of the University of Belgrade described the outcome as “an avalanche that has wiped out an authoritarian regime”.

The Student List is projected to win 140 seats in the 250-seat parliament, well above the 126 needed for a majority, while Vučić's United Serbia list is expected to take 103. The remaining seats go to minority parties. Because the Student List has a clear majority, lengthy coalition talks are not required. The new National Assembly must convene within 30 days of the final results, and a government must be formed within 90 days thereafter. The Student List did not name a prime ministerial candidate during the campaign; Ilija Srdanović is seen as the most likely choice.

Vučić, who had served two terms as president and was constitutionally barred from a third, had hoped to return as prime minister after resigning the presidency on September 27. Parliament speaker Ana Brnabić is now acting president and must call a presidential election, which must be held no later than December 27. Vučić had backed Serbia's EU candidacy while refusing to impose sanctions on Russia. Kremlin spokesperson Dmitry Peskov praised Vučić's contribution to relations with Moscow and expressed hope the new force would continue cooperation. European Commissioner for Enlargement Marta Kos said “a new chapter can begin for Serbia” and that the EU door is open. Hungary's Prime Minister Péter Magyar, whose own party ended 16 years of Viktor Orbán rule in April, welcomed the victory as an inspiration.
