Lula wins fourth term as Brazil's president in runoff victory

International & World desk

Brazil's President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva has won a historic fourth term in office, defeating far-right challenger Senator Flávio Bolsonaro in a dramatic runoff election. With 51.79% of the vote against Bolsonaro's 48.21%, Lula secured a margin of more than 4 million votes, becoming the first candidate since Brazil's return to democracy in 1985 to win a presidential runoff after finishing second in the first round. The result marked a stunning reversal from the October 4 first round, when Bolsonaro led with 47% against Lula's 45%.

Lula's comeback was driven by a revitalised grassroots campaign, youth mobilisation and a shift towards digital campaigning. A WhatsApp channel called 'Lula's Army', led by Congressman André Janones, grew to 10.8 million followers and helped turn online enthusiasm into real-world political engagement. Higher voter turnout also contributed: abstention fell from about 21% in the first round to approximately 19% in the runoff, with previously absent voters appearing to participate. Lula's campaign intensified local efforts and adapted its messaging, linking a Bolsonaro victory with concerns about potential changes to social programmes and the loss of rights.

Bolsonaro refused to concede and called for the result to be annulled, citing a Supreme Court decision to extend voting by two hours after both campaigns requested extensions due to transport disruptions and severe weather. However, electoral law experts said the chances of annulment were remote, noting that Bolsonaro's own campaign had requested the extension and that presidential election dates are fixed by the constitution. The controversy echoed the aftermath of the 2022 election, when Bolsonaro's father, former President Jair Bolsonaro, refused to accept his defeat and was later convicted of plotting a coup, serving a 27-year prison sentence.

In his victory speech to tens of thousands of supporters in São Paulo, Lula declared: 'Democracy always, dictatorship never again. Democracy always, fascism never again.' He pledged to give people a greater say in decision-making and said digital communication would play a much bigger role in his government. Lula also insisted that Brazil 'will not accept being anyone's backyard', a reference to US President Donald Trump's 'Donroe Doctrine'. Trump, who had shown support for Jair Bolsonaro and met Flávio Bolsonaro at the White House earlier this year, did not immediately react to the result.

World leaders from across the spectrum congratulated Lula. European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said 'democracy and multilateralism' had won. Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez called it a 'great victory', saying 'the comeback of global progressivism has just begun'. French President Emmanuel Macron said the two nations should work 'in service of a more just world'. South African President Cyril Ramaphosa called Lula's victory one for the 'global south and progressive forces throughout the world'.

Despite his victory, Lula faces significant challenges. Bolsonaro's Liberal Party and its allies are projected to control majorities in both houses of Congress, potentially restricting Lula's legislative agenda. Economic pressures include public debt exceeding 82% of GDP and forecast growth of just 2.3% next year. Lula, who turns 81 on Tuesday, has already served three non-consecutive terms and will begin his fourth term on January 5. His win also has profound implications for the Amazon rainforest, with former environment minister Marina Silva expressing relief that Indigenous territories would be preserved and that there is no risk of illegal miners returning to destroy these populations.
