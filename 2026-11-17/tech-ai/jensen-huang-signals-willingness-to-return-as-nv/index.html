Jensen Huang signals willingness to return as Nvidia CEO as allies push for reinstatement

Tech / AI desk

Jensen Huang, who resigned as Nvidia’s CEO last week after reports of affairs with a suspected Chinese spy and a subordinate, said he would be willing to return if the board decides it is in the company’s best interest. In a statement posted on X, Huang said he has seen an outpouring of calls from employees, investors, business partners, and customers for his return. “If the Board of Directors decides that my return is in the best interest of our shareholders, our employees, the company, and America, I would be honored to answer these calls and return as CEO,” he wrote. Huang acknowledged “serious mistakes in my personal life” and said he is cooperating with the board’s independent investigation.

A public campaign to reinstate Huang has gathered momentum. Allies in Silicon Valley are building a “war room” to lobby for his return, according to a person familiar with the matter, and are seeking support from the White House. An open letter demanding the board “Bring Jensen Back” has attracted more than 193,000 signatures, including those of Tesla and SpaceX CEO Elon Musk and Epic Games CEO Tim Sweeney. More than 10,000 current and former Nvidia employees have also signed. The letter expresses concern that Huang was removed “without meaningful due process” and asks the board to provide a specific, evidence-based explanation if it believes his continued absence is necessary.

Despite the campaign, Nvidia’s board is reportedly not considering reinstating Huang. CNN reported that the board is not considering his return, and an unnamed source told The Washington Post there were no plans to reinstate him. There is also significant resistance in Congress, where Huang faces questions about his role in chip exports to China-linked firms. One senator called his reinstatement “one of the worst ideas I’ve heard today.” Huang is scheduled to testify before Congress on December 1. In his absence, CFO Colette Kress has been appointed interim CEO, and Nvidia has formed a new executive committee to oversee export control compliance.

Huang resigned following reports of two affairs: one with a direct report and another with former Megaspeed executive Alice Huang, who is suspected of working with Chinese intelligence services. The revelations have intensified scrutiny of Nvidia’s chip exports to China. Huang’s statement expressed love for Nvidia and the United States, saying he came to the U.S. 53 years ago and that becoming an American citizen was one of the proudest moments of his life. He said he owns his mistakes completely and is committed to cooperating with the board’s investigation. The board’s independent probe is ongoing.
