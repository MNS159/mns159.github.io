Tech outages and NSA network disruption fuel broad market sell-off

Financial & Markets desk

Stocks fell sharply Tuesday, with the Dow Jones Industrial Average dropping 766 points, or 1.5%, as a series of technology outages and a reported disruption to a classified U.S. national security communications network added to investor jitters ahead of Nvidia's earnings report. The S&P 500 slipped 1.2%, and the Nasdaq Composite lost 1%. The Dow was down more than 920 points at its session low.

The sell-off deepened after CNN reported that a classified network used by the National Security Agency had gone down, citing six people familiar with the matter. The New York Times confirmed the report, noting the network is used to transmit top secret information. Sources said there was no evidence of a cyberattack. Traders said the report helped push equities lower, though losses were contained. The NSA outage is the latest in a string of technology disruptions that have left markets on edge, including a Federal Aviation Administration ground stop over the weekend, a Microsoft Outlook outage last week, and recent issues with banking apps.

JPMorgan Chase reported a temporary software bug affecting some consumer banking services Monday. The bank said a 'technical issue' related to 'a software bug in on-premise hardware systems' was causing some customer transactions to take longer than usual, though it said customers can still access accounts and funds. A spokesperson said there is no evidence it is cyber-related and that the issue was not systemic, though transactions between Chase and other institutions appeared to be delayed. A U.S. Bank representative said its clients attempting to send or receive Zelle payments with another financial institution may experience delays.

Technology shares led the decline. Nvidia shares traded off their session lows after former CEO Jensen Huang said he would be open to returning as CEO if the board thought it was in the best interest of shareholders, but the stock remained under pressure ahead of its fiscal third-quarter earnings report after the market close. Micron Technology slid roughly 6%, Advanced Micro Devices fell more than 1.5%, Intel dropped 4%, and Marvell Technology shed more than 2%. Microsoft shares were down 3%. Software stocks also slipped, with Salesforce, Palo Alto Networks, and Oracle declining. Neocloud stocks CoreWeave and Iren shed 5% and 6%, respectively; Nebius lost 4.2%.

MarketWatch reported that the technology outages have raised investor concerns that software companies could prove 'particularly vulnerable' to AI misuse. The broader market decline also reflected a shift in investor sentiment toward technology. According to Bank of America's November Global Fund Manager Survey, the net share of investors overweight technology fell to 12% from 37% in October, the biggest one-month cut since June 2006. A majority of 52% now expect an AI hyperscaler to announce a capital expenditure cut in the next six months, up from 30% in October, and a record net 38% say companies are overinvesting. An AI bubble is fund managers' top tail risk (34%), followed by an AI hyperscaler credit event (31%).

Nvidia's stock has fallen 33.3% from its October 2 close, putting it on track for a seventh straight weekly loss, which would tie the longest weekly losing streak in its history from June to July 2008. Traders are focused on Nvidia's earnings report and whether interim CEO Colette Kress can provide clarity on leadership and export controls. Ross Mayfield, investment strategist at Baird, told CNBC that Nvidia 'should report strong results and guidance, but is unlikely to be able to give investors solid answers to the questions heavily weighing on the stock.' The S&P 500 tumbled 1.9% on the day, according to Barron's.
