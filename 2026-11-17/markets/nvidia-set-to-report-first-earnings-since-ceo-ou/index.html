Nvidia set to report first earnings since CEO ouster as stock plunges

Financial & Markets desk · anchored on AFP, Reuters

Nvidia is set to report quarterly results after the close Tuesday in what may be the most anticipated earnings announcement in the chipmaker's 33-year history, landing less than a week after co-founder Jensen Huang's abrupt resignation as CEO. The stock has fallen 26% over the past month and 35% from its all-time high in October, wiping out $2 trillion in market value. Pre-market trading Tuesday saw the Nasdaq-100 down 2.7%, the S&P 500 down 1.9%, and the Dow down 1.4%, with technology the weakest sector, down 3%.

Analysts expect Nvidia to post earnings per share of roughly $2.47 on revenue of about $109 billion, according to LSEG, just above the company's own guidance of $108 billion. Revenue growth remains explosive, with expansion expected to reach 91% from $59 billion in the same period a year ago. Nvidia said in August that sales next fiscal year would grow roughly 70% in what finance chief Colette Kress, now functioning as interim CEO, described as a 'supply constrained outlook.' Kress will deliver her first public remarks since last week's shake-up on the earnings call scheduled to begin at 5 p.m. ET.

The leadership turmoil stems from Huang's reported affair with a businesswoman who is allegedly a Chinese spy, which also raised national security concerns. The Commerce Department on Monday announced a change that would prevent Nvidia's chips from being rented to Chinese companies entirely, putting revenue at risk. Many Huang allies in Silicon Valley have called for his reinstatement, but Nvidia's board is not considering bringing him back, a person familiar with the matter told CNBC. Huang wants his job back, according to AFP.

Options traders are pricing a 7% reaction in the stock after the report, the biggest implied move going into an Nvidia report since February 2025 and well above the median move of 3.2% over the last eight quarters. Implied volatility reached 68 Monday, compared to under 40 in early October. The ratio of open puts to call contracts has widened toward calls, currently at 0.87, down from a one-year high of 1.02 two weeks ago. However, most call contracts traded Monday looked like neutral to bearish positions opened by traders selling calls, and of the $2.3 billion in options premium traded, $1.2 billion was tied to puts. There are very few open put contracts at or below the $157 level at which the stock closed Monday, suggesting any bad surprise would find many traders with little downside protection. By the same token, notable open interest in call contracts from the 175-strike and above suggests profit-taking on a bounce could limit recovery.

Nvidia is the largest weight in both the S&P 500 and Nasdaq-100, so its reaction will move both headline numbers. The company that for much of the AI boom was the most reliable winner in the market is now more volatile than all but 35 companies in the S&P 500. 'Longer term, I see significant downside risk unless Nvidia can attract an exceptional leader, someone like Tim Cook or AMD's Lisa Su,' Nigam Arora, founder of the Arora report, said in a text ahead of the report.
