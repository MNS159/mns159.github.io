Fed's Warsh stresses AI's potential but warns of risks

Financial & Markets desk · anchored on Reuters

Federal Reserve Chairman Kevin Warsh on Monday highlighted the potential economic benefits of artificial intelligence while warning that the technology could lead to widespread job losses or fail to deliver on its lofty expectations, speaking in San Francisco before a task force he assembled to examine productivity and employment.

Warsh used the term 'super intelligence' instead of artificial intelligence, following an executive order from President Donald Trump, and described it as 'an entirely new force in economic life and society.' He outlined three possible outcomes: productivity gains that accelerate growth while keeping employment near full capacity; job displacement through automation raising unemployment; or a scenario where AI 'disappoint[s]' after significant capital investment, leading to falling equity valuations, an economic slowdown, and a more notable rise in unemployment. 'As policymakers, we have to be mindful that great opportunities rarely come risk free,' Warsh said.

The conference was part of a task force Warsh created to evaluate AI's impact on productivity and jobs, one of five such task forces he has established since taking the Fed chair. The task force includes venture capitalist Marc Andreessen, Stanford economist Chad Jones (on leave at AI firm Anthropic and unable to speak due to an IPO blackout), and Xbox CEO Asha Sharma, who did not attend. Warsh moderated three of four sessions and selected speakers, including former Commerce Secretary Gina Raimondo, who called anti-data center measures passed in the midterm elections 'a very troubling sign.'

Warsh drew a parallel between the potential impact of AI and the 'China shock' that followed China's entry into the World Trade Organization in 2001, which lowered goods prices but cost the U.S. an estimated 2.4 million jobs. He suggested AI could produce a similar effect focused on services, lowering prices for legal or administrative work while displacing workers. He asked panelists whether 'we should expect disinflation over the medium term, from what AI could do to services,' and they largely indicated it was possible, which could eventually lead the Fed to lower interest rates.

Notably, Warsh did not address inflation or the Fed's next steps on interest rates, even as recent government data showed inflation accelerating and several of his colleagues signaled support for additional rate hikes at the Fed's December meeting. Fed Vice Chair Philip Jefferson said Friday that if AI accelerates economic growth, it could have implications for policy. The task force is expected to produce a report for Warsh in the coming months, after which the Fed will debate how to incorporate its findings into monetary policy. Meanwhile, AI firm Anthropic plans to price its IPO on Wednesday, potentially raising up to $64 billion at a $1.4 trillion valuation, underscoring the rapid pace of AI investment.
