DeChambeau joins DP World Tour after LIV Golf collapse

Sports desk

Two-time US Open champion Bryson DeChambeau has agreed to join the DP World Tour for next season, following the collapse of LIV Golf. DeChambeau was among a host of star names who joined the Saudi-backed breakaway tour four years ago and maintained his commitment even after Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund withdrew its financial backing in April. Earlier this month the 33-year-old American said he 'gave everything' to keep LIV Golf alive as the tour confirmed its intention to wind down operations. As first reported in the Telegraph, DeChambeau successfully submitted his application to join the European Tour before Sunday's deadline. He has already confirmed he will compete in Gary Player's Nedbank Golf Challenge — a DP World Tour event — in South Africa in December.

As a recent major champion, DeChambeau has been given 'category one' membership by the DP World Tour, which guarantees him entry to any event across the season. Other former major champions who defected to LIV — Dustin Johnson, Cameron Smith and Bubba Watson — have signed agreements to receive invitations to a specific number of tournaments. Veteran English trio Ian Poulter, Lee Westwood and Paul Casey, plus Northern Ireland's Graeme McDowell, are among a number of ex-LIV players who are eligible to play on the DP World Tour in 2027. The full-time return of Spain's Jon Rahm and England's Tyrrell Hatton, who did not resign their memberships, has also been confirmed.

DeChambeau reportedly received £95m to join LIV in 2022 and earned almost £50m in prize money over four seasons. Joining the DP World Tour offers a pathway back to the PGA Tour: the leading 10 players on the DP World Tour in a season are guaranteed PGA Tour cards for the following season, a route taken by Patrick Reed — DeChambeau's former US Ryder Cup team-mate — when he left LIV before the start of the 2026 season. 'I think a name like Bryson DeChambeau and everything he brings with him, good and bad, he brings eyeballs, which is a good thing for tournaments,' said six-time major champion Rory McIlroy. McIlroy also claimed those connected to LIV had expressed an ambition to 'destroy the European Tour' during this year's Masters.

The Saudi-backed venture is to be liquidated, leading to an influx of big names joining the DP World Tour. As well as DeChambeau being given membership for the first time, it was announced that Johnson, Smith and Watson are among a group of nine former LIV players allowed to play a limited number of events. They will be picked at the discretion of the DP World Tour to 'add value to those tournaments', designed to prevent full-time members from missing playing opportunities. 'There has been significant change in the landscape of men's professional golf over the past 12 months,' said DP World Tour chief executive Guy Kinnings. 'As golf's global tour, we have taken a pragmatic approach to this unique and unprecedented situation as we go into next year, always with the best interests of our entire membership at the forefront of our minds.' Kinnings added: 'We realise there are different viewpoints. But this approach, discussed and agreed with our tournament committee and allied to this responsibly managed programme of invites, will add value which, in turn, will benefit the tour as a whole.'
