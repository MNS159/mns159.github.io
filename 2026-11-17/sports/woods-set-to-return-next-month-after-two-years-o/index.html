Woods set to return next month after two years out

Sports desk

Tiger Woods is set to return to competitive golf in December for the first time in more than two years. The 50-year-old, a 15-time major champion, has not played in a traditional ranking event since the 2024 Open Championship at Royal Troon amid a series of off-course issues.

Woods will host next month's Hero World Challenge, an event on the PGA Tour schedule that does not carry FedEx Cup points, and has been announced as part of the 20-player field. He is one of three exemptions for the tournament in the Bahamas, along with fellow Americans Rickie Fowler and Gary Woodland.

Speaking to SiriusXM PGA Tour Radio in a video posted last week, Woods said: "I'm feeling better, no doubt about that. I've been training quite a bit. I will be at Hero either way, so I'll see you there."

Woods' long-awaited return comes after he accepted a five-year driving ban in September following a plea agreement in his reckless driving case. In March, he was arrested and charged with driving under the influence after clipping a truck and rolling his car in Florida. Woods was also found with two white pills in his pocket, identified as hydrocodone, an opioid used to treat pain. He subsequently stepped away from golf to seek treatment in what his attorney described as an "intensive, highly individualised and medically integrated programme".

Woods has endured a succession of debilitating injuries, including multiple back surgeries, serious fractures to his right leg following a car crash in 2021, and a ruptured Achilles tendon in 2025. He also suffered the death of his mother, Kultida, in February 2025, while his girlfriend Vanessa Trump was diagnosed with breast cancer earlier this year. Trump, the former daughter-in-law of US President Donald Trump, said last month that she is now cancer free.
