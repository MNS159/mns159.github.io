FDA approves Roche's breast cancer pill Wilserdly (giredestrant) for advanced ESR1-mutated disease

Health & Science desk · anchored on Reuters

The U.S. Food and Drug Administration on Monday approved Roche's oral breast cancer drug giredestrant, to be marketed as Wilserdly, for a specific subset of patients with advanced disease, teeing up the launch of a medicine the Swiss company has said could become its top-selling product ever. The approval, which came four weeks ahead of the FDA's goal date, is for adults with ER-positive, HER2-negative, locally advanced or metastatic breast cancer that has acquired a mutation in the ESR1 gene and has progressed after prior endocrine-based therapy. The drug is approved in combination with Novartis's everolimus.

The decision was based on the phase 3 evERA trial, which enrolled 373 patients, including 207 with ESR1-mutated tumors. Patients were randomly assigned to receive either giredestrant 30 mg daily or physician's choice of endocrine therapy (exemestane, fulvestrant, or tamoxifen), with both arms also receiving everolimus 10 mg daily. In the ESR1-mutated population, the giredestrant-everolimus combination reduced the risk of disease progression or death by approximately 62% compared with standard endocrine therapy plus everolimus. Median progression-free survival was about 10 months for the giredestrant arm versus 5.5 months for the comparator group. The objective response rate nearly doubled, at approximately 27% versus 14%. Overall survival data were immature at the time of the analysis.

Wilserdly is an oral selective estrogen receptor degrader (SERD), a class of drugs that target the estrogen receptor. The FDA had previously approved two other oral SERDs for the same second-line ESR1-mutated indication: Menarini's Orserdu and Eli Lilly's Inluriyo. In September, Lilly expanded Inluriyo's label for use alongside its CDK4/6 inhibitor Verzenio, showing a 47% progression-free survival benefit versus Inluriyo alone. Additionally, the FDA recently approved AstraZeneca's Etcamah in a novel first-line-switch setting for patients who develop an ESR1 mutation during initial treatment, a decision that was controversial because it went against a negative advisory committee recommendation.

Roche Pharmaceuticals CEO Teresa Graham has said Wilserdly "could be our largest-selling product." The company has placed its biggest bet on the adjuvant treatment of early-stage breast cancer, which Graham noted accounts for about 70% of the overall giredestrant opportunity, compared with about 10% for the first-line metastatic setting. In the adjuvant setting, Roche's lidERA trial showed a statistically significant invasive disease-free survival benefit for Wilserdly versus standard endocrine therapy. The FDA is scheduled to rule on that potential first-in-class adjuvant use by Nov. 30. Roche has projected peak sales above 3 billion Swiss francs; Jefferies analysts have modeled risk-adjusted revenues of 4.8 billion Swiss francs by 2034.

The most common adverse events in the evERA trial, occurring in at least 20% of patients, included stomatitis, rash, fatigue, diarrhea, musculoskeletal pain, pneumonitis, and nausea. Laboratory abnormalities seen in at least 20% of patients included decreases in hemoglobin, lymphocytes, white blood cells, neutrophils, platelets, calcium, potassium, and sodium, along with increases in AST, ALT, and creatinine. The label carries warnings for bradycardia and embryo-fetal toxicity. Because ESR1 mutations often emerge during prior endocrine therapy, the approval requires contemporary molecular testing, with a liquid biopsy companion diagnostic available to identify the mutation at progression.
