Russia-Ukraine talks in Abu Dhabi end with prisoner exchange deal but no major breakthrough

International & World desk · anchored on Reuters, AP

Two days of U.S.-mediated talks between Russia and Ukraine in Abu Dhabi concluded Wednesday with an agreement in principle on a large-scale prisoner of war exchange, but no breakthrough on ending the nearly five-year war sparked by Russia's full-scale invasion. The talks, the first between the delegations since February, were described by Ukrainian negotiator Davyd Arakhamia as 'one of the hardest,' adding that 'the Russians came with very strict directives, so it was extremely difficult to soften the discussion.' U.S. envoy Jared Kushner said the number of prisoners would be finalized on Thursday, and Russian state media reported the exchange could take place in two to three weeks. Alexander Zorin, a deputy chief of the Russian General Staff's information department, said it would be one of the biggest swaps after the 1,000 soldiers returned home last year.

The talks focused on post-war security architecture for Europe, with Kyiv seeking reassurances that allies would come to its aid if Ukraine is attacked again despite it not being a NATO member, and Moscow demanding limits on NATO's influence in Eastern Europe. Kushner said the U.S. hoped to 'create a new framework' of security between Ukraine, Russia and Europe that could 'secure lasting peace,' while Kremlin negotiator Vladimir Medinsky said the world needed a 'new' and 'fair' security system. Kushner acknowledged that territorial issues, particularly over Ukraine's Donetsk region, would be the hardest to settle, but said 'progress' was made on a post-war security framework. He added that 'there is no trust right now between the sides and that is to be expected after a conflict like this.'

Kushner and U.S. special envoy Steve Witkoff are scheduled to travel to Moscow on Thursday to meet Russian President Vladimir Putin, then to Dublin to meet Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy. Kushner said one goal of the Moscow meeting is to try to get European countries added to the talks, which Moscow has opposed. European officials from Britain, NATO and various EU countries were in Abu Dhabi and met with U.S. and Ukrainian negotiators, but the Kremlin said its delegation had no contact with them. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said European representatives could eventually join peace talks but added their current statements 'don't indicate any desire on their part to contribute to movement along the path toward peace.'

Ukraine's peace negotiator Arakhamia said there was 'good momentum' from the talks toward reaching a solution before the end of the year and urged a second round to be organized as soon as possible. Medinsky said the meeting helped Moscow and Kyiv edge closer to peace, while Kremlin foreign affairs adviser Yuri Ushakov described the talks as 'substantive and quite useful.' Zelenskyy, who did not attend, said on X that 'Ukraine's priority is to end the war as soon as possible with a dignified peace' and that 'Europe must be represented at every stage of real diplomacy.' Kushner cautioned that any agreement on a peacetime security framework is contingent on the war ending, but that cannot occur 'until there's an agreement on territory and on all other bilateral issues.'
