COP31 in Turkey: Guterres calls for climate justice tax, Thunberg blasts 'hypocrisy', US absent

International & World desk · anchored on Reuters

World leaders gathered in Antalya, Turkey, on Wednesday for the COP31 climate summit, where UN Secretary-General António Guterres warned that the planet is heading toward more than 2.5°C of warming by century's end and proposed a 'climate justice compact' that includes taxing fossil fuel companies. 'Our planet is heating up. The clock is winding down. It's make or bake time,' Guterres said, urging governments to tax windfall profits from oil giants. He contrasted the $95 billion earned by the world's nine largest oil companies in one quarter with the roughly $600 million received by the international fund for climate-related loss and damage—equivalent to just 14 hours of those companies' profits. 'Polluters must pay,' he declared, earning an ovation. The United States is not attending the summit, which runs until Nov. 20.

Climate activist Greta Thunberg, attending her first COP since 2021, condemned the talks as 'hypocrisy' and accused host countries of using the forum to 'whitewash themselves.' 'Our political and economic systems that have become incompatible with human nature,' she said, warning that 'everything and everyone we love is at stake.' Her arrival energized youth activists, including Amber Turner of the Alliance of Small Island States, who said Thunberg's presence 'reminds us of why we started this work in the first place.' Protests at the conference demanded more climate finance and criticized growing interest in nuclear energy.

Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan, co-host with Australia, acknowledged that three decades of climate negotiations had failed to deliver sufficient results. 'This success at the level of ideas has not produced the same results when it comes to implementation,' he said. Brazilian President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva warned that wars and trade disputes are overshadowing climate action: 'We started talking more about weapons and tariffs than the survival of future generations.' Turkey is promoting global electrification, aiming for electricity to account for 35% of global energy consumption by 2035, up from 23% today, though both co-hosts still rely heavily on fossil fuels, especially coal.

Methane featured prominently during Wednesday's 'Zero Waste' thematic day. Guterres called on the UN Environment Programme (UNEP) to develop a global standard for near-zero methane emissions from oil and gas. UNEP's Julia Ferini described methane as 'our climate emergency brake,' noting that cutting this short-lived gas could slow warming relatively quickly. UNEP has tracked major leaks from space, issuing more than 5,000 alerts since 2023, but barely one in eight has received a response. The summit also highlighted youth-led solutions, such as a Botswana company using worms to compost organic waste, processing over 1,000 tonnes. COP32 is scheduled for Addis Ababa, Ethiopia, in November 2027.
