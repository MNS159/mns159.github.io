Indonesia's vice president offers resignation amid court ruling and political rift

International & World desk

Indonesia's Vice President Gibran Rakabuming Raka has offered his resignation to President Prabowo Subianto, an aide confirmed, as a Supreme Court ruling questioning his educational credentials and growing tensions between Prabowo and former President Joko Widodo threaten the ruling alliance. Gibran's aide Budi Arie told the BBC that the resignation letter was submitted directly to Prabowo on Wednesday; it remains unclear whether the president has accepted it. State Secretariat Minister Prasetyo Hadi said the two held over two hours of talks on Tuesday, and that Prabowo advised Gibran to 'continue pursuing legal avenues to defend the validity of his academic documents.'

The resignation offer follows an Indonesian Supreme Court ruling that a high-school document used during Gibran's 2024 election campaign was invalid. The court found 'no evidence was found to show Gibran had a foreign diploma or certificate that could serve as the basis for equivalency with a senior high school or equivalent qualification.' Gibran, who studied in Singapore and Australia, had used an Indonesian equivalence certificate to meet legal requirements. The ruling prompted more than 100 lawmakers to support a parliamentary initiative that could have led to impeachment proceedings. Gibran previously argued that undertaking university education was sufficient proof of completing senior high school; on Tuesday he asked for a chance to challenge the ruling.

Gibran's resignation comes amid signs that the political alliance between Prabowo and former President Joko Widodo (Jokowi) is fraying. Gibran, Jokowi's son, was added to Prabowo's 2024 ticket after a controversial Constitutional Court ruling exempted him from the minimum age requirement of 40. Since taking office, Prabowo has shifted focus away from Jokowi's legacy project of building a new capital, Nusantara, toward his own free school meals programme. He has also reshuffled personnel, moving Jokowi loyalists including long-serving finance minister Sri Mulyani Indrawati into new roles. Last week, authorities arrested presidential chief of staff Listyo Sigit Prabowo on suspicions of wiretapping the president; Prabowo sacked him a day later. Listyo, a former national police chief, is widely considered a longtime Jokowi ally.

Within the government, allegiances appear to be shifting. On Monday, three members of Prabowo's cabinet collectively resigned from the party led by Kaesang Pangarep, Jokowi's youngest son, citing a desire to focus on their duties in Prabowo's administration. Separately, Jokowi faces unrelated allegations that he used a fake university diploma, which he denies.

The political turmoil unfolds at a turbulent period for Prabowo's presidency. The free school meals programme is struggling for funding, parts of Indonesia have battled the country's worst wildfires in a decade, and the national stock market faces crisis amid allegations of widespread market manipulation. On Tuesday, students across the country staged demonstrations to protest the government's handling of environmental issues and corruption. In a brief statement, Gibran said: 'I respect all criticism and feedback from the public regarding the developments that are taking place. I do not want the current situation to become a prolonged political controversy.'
