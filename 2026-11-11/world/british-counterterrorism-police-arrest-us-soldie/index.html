British counterterrorism police arrest US soldier accused of spying for Russia

International & World desk

British counterterrorism police have arrested a 41-year-old member of the US Army near Gloucester, England, on suspicion of espionage activity in connection with Russia. The Metropolitan Police confirmed the arrest on Tuesday, November 10, 2026, and said authorities searched two locations in the Gloucestershire region. The man was taken into custody at a London police station, with investigators from the US and UK involved in the probe.

Deputy Assistant Commissioner Vicki Evans, Senior National Coordinator for Counter Terrorism Policing, called it a "significant arrest" resulting from "close collaboration between UK and US authorities." She added: "The allegations are serious and our investigation is very much ongoing." FBI Director Kash Patel said the case was of "utmost concern" to the US and its allies, and declared: "This FBI has no tolerance for anyone who breaks their oath to the United States and whose actions endanger our national security and that of our partners." Patel thanked UK investigators and the US Military Counterintelligence Command for their work.

Brigadier General Sean Stinchon, commanding general of US Army Counterintelligence Command, said the case demonstrated the "resolve of the counterintelligence community." He described the alleged conduct of a senior soldier entrusted with sensitive information as "a sobering reminder that insider threats are real and persistent."

The Gloucestershire area, about two hours' drive from London, hosts several sensitive military and intelligence sites, including RAF Fairford, a large airbase used by both UK and US planes, and the nearby town of Cheltenham, home to the headquarters of Britain's signals intelligence agency GCHQ. The Metropolitan Police noted that US authorities, the US Military, and the FBI were closely involved in the investigation, which remains ongoing.
