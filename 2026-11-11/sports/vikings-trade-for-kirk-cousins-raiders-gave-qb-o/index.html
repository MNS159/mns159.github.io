Vikings trade for Kirk Cousins after Raiders gave QB option to stay or leave

Sports desk

The Minnesota Vikings acquired quarterback Kirk Cousins from the Las Vegas Raiders at Tuesday's 4 p.m. ET trade deadline, sending a 2027 third-round pick to reunite with their former starter. Before the deal was finalized, the Raiders gave Cousins the choice of whether to leave the organization or remain with the club he had started the first nine games of the 2026 season for, according to The Ringer's Tom Pelissero. After consulting with his family, agent Mike McCartney, and Vikings head coach Kevin O'Connell, Cousins decided by midday Tuesday to return to Minnesota and informed Raiders general manager John Spytek of his decision.

"So much respect for John Spytek and the Raiders organization," McCartney told Pelissero. "There aren't many leaders in this league who would've done that. Very appreciative of Spy." Part of Cousins' calculus was the presence of rookie Fernando Mendoza, the No. 1 overall pick in the 2026 draft. The report indicated Cousins knew it was only a matter of time before the Raiders turned the franchise over to Mendoza, making his status as QB1 fleeting. With an opportunity to help a Vikings team he spent six years with and won the NFC North in 2022, the pieces fell into place for the deal.

Cousins, 38, has the most single-season passing touchdowns (20) and career passing touchdowns (318) by any quarterback changing teams midseason in NFL history. During his first six-year stint with the Vikings, he ranked second in completion percentage (67.9%), fourth in passing yards (23,265), and third in passing touchdowns (171) among all quarterbacks with at least 50 starts. He reunites with wide receiver Justin Jefferson, who averaged 98.9 receiving yards per game with Cousins — an NFL record for most receiving yards by a quarterback-receiver duo over at least 50 games. The Vikings' offense is tied for the fewest offensive touchdowns (11) in the NFL this season.

FOX Sports' Henry McKenna graded the trade an A for the Raiders and a C+ for the Vikings, noting that Las Vegas gets a Day 2 draft pick while moving on to Mendoza. McKenna expressed skepticism about Minnesota's move, citing the team's recent instability at quarterback — including moving to J.J. McCarthy last year and then moving on from him, and Carson Wentz's poor performance filling in for an injured Kyler Murray. Cousins is on a $10 million deal for this year, but McKenna called the third-round pick a high price given that the Murray signing was supposed to provide value. "In Minnesota, there's a plan. Then there's a new plan. Then there's a new plan again," McKenna wrote. "Forgive me for not fully buying this one."
