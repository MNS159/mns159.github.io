Astros hire Brad Ausmus as manager, bringing franchise icon back to Houston

Sports desk

The Houston Astros have named Brad Ausmus as their next manager, the team announced Wednesday. Ausmus, 57, replaces Joe Espada, whose contract was not renewed after three seasons in which he led the Astros to the playoffs twice. The hiring is the first major move by general manager Randy Flores, who was named to his post earlier in the offseason.

Ausmus, a former Astros catcher who spent 10 of his 18 major-league seasons in Houston, had served as bench coach for the New York Yankees since late 2023. He previously managed the Detroit Tigers from 2014 to 2017, compiling a 314-332 record and winning the American League Central in his first season, and the Los Angeles Angels in 2019, going 72-90. His career managerial winning percentage is .478.

"I am very excited to be back in Houston," Ausmus said in a statement. "The Astros and the city of Houston have been a huge part of my life, and I'm thrilled to create a new chapter as the team's manager. I'd like to thank [general manager Randy Flores] and the entire Astros organization for this opportunity, and I can't wait to get to work." Flores added: "Brad impressed us throughout the interview process, demonstrating the leadership, energy, and expertise critical to driving a winning culture both on and off the field."

The Astros are coming off a 2026 season in which they won the American League West with an 81-81 record and a negative run differential, then were swept out of the Wild Card round by the Chicago White Sox. The team has made the ALCS in seven straight seasons from 2017 to 2023, winning two World Series titles, but has not returned since. The roster features several core players—including Yordan Alvarez, Jeremy Peña, Isaac Paredes, Hunter Brown, Christian Walker, Yainer Diaz, Cristian Javier, and Josh Hader—who are slated for free agency after 2027 or 2028, and the farm system is considered weak after years of contention and draft penalties related to the sign-stealing scandal.

Ausmus becomes the first manager hired under Flores, who replaced Dana Brown earlier in the offseason. The hiring also reunites Ausmus with former teammate Jeff Bagwell, a key advisor to owner Jim Crane. Ausmus was connected to previous Astros managerial openings and even to the general manager position over the years. With the move, the Los Angeles Angels are now the only team with a managerial vacancy. The Yankees will need to find a new bench coach.
