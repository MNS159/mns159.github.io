Rex Ryan arrested on 6 charges after striking pedestrians with truck

Sports desk

Former NFL head coach and current ESPN broadcaster Rex Ryan surrendered to Nashville police Wednesday morning and faces six charges after allegedly striking two pedestrians with his pickup truck on Nov. 5. The incident occurred shortly before midnight as the pedestrians were crossing Broadway from 4th Avenue with a walk signal, according to Metro Nashville Police. Both victims suffered minor injuries; Ryan's truck ran over their feet and made contact with one individual's leg.

Police said the pedestrians had the right of way. After striking them, the vehicle continued driving but stopped at a red light at Broadway and Representative John Lewis Way. The pedestrians approached the truck and confronted the driver. According to police, 'The pedestrians said the vehicle kept going, but stopped for the red light at Broadway and Representative John Lewis Way where they waved down the driver. They said he stepped out of the truck to argue with them, got back in, and drove away.' The pedestrians took pictures of the driver and the truck's license plate, which was registered to Ryan.

Ryan, 63, was charged with two counts of leaving the scene of an accident, two counts of failure to give information or render aid, and two counts of failure to obey a traffic control signal. His bond was set at $6,000 by a judicial commissioner. He is scheduled to appear in a Nashville courtroom on December 18.

Ryan began his NFL coaching career with the Arizona Cardinals in 1994 as a defensive line and linebackers coach. He later joined the Baltimore Ravens in 1999 as a defensive line coach, helping the team win Super Bowl XXXV, and became defensive coordinator in 2005. He became head coach of the New York Jets in 2008, leading them to consecutive AFC Championship Game appearances. After being dismissed by the Jets in December 2014, he coached the Buffalo Bills until his tenure ended in December 2016. Ryan's head coaching record is 65-68 (4-2 in the playoffs). He has since worked as an NFL analyst for ESPN.
