House blocks Hegseth impeachment, sparing defense secretary for now

Politics desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum
✓ Facts
  • The House voted 211-204 to refer Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth's impeachment articles to the House Judiciary Committee, a procedural move that effectively ends the impeachment effort before the current Congress session concludes.
  • Rep. Thomas Massie (R-Ky.) reintroduced the impeachment articles on Monday, accusing Hegseth of high crimes tied to U.S. military action against Iran and strikes on drug boats in the Caribbean and Eastern Pacific.
  • All Republicans except Massie voted to send the measure to committee; more than a dozen members missed the vote, including 11 Democrats, some of whom cited traffic issues for their absence.
  • During a briefing, Hegseth called the impeachment effort a 'farce' and 'sabotage.'
  • President Trump posted on Truth Social that the impeachment effort 'has miserably FAILED.'
  • House Democratic Leader Hakeem Jeffries said after the vote, 'This is not the end of it. It's just the beginning.'
  • Separately, House Republicans elected Rep. Jim Jordan (R-Ohio) as the new minority leader, replacing Speaker Mike Johnson, who is stepping down after the midterm losses.
  • Per BBC, Democrats are set to take control of both chambers of Congress in January following midterm election gains, which could change the political calculus around future impeachment attempts.
○ Analysis

The vote itself was not a judgment on Hegseth's guilt or innocence but a procedural maneuver: by referring Massie's impeachment articles to the GOP-led Judiciary Committee rather than allowing a direct floor vote, Republican leadership ensured the measure would stall with little time left in the current Congress. The outcome hinged largely on attendance — enough Democrats were absent that Republicans had a working majority in the chamber. Massie, the lone Republican to vote against tabling the measure, pressed the issue over Hegseth's role in military actions against Iran and drug-trafficking vessels. The episode also arrives amid broader shifts in House leadership, with Jim Jordan stepping into the minority leader role as Mike Johnson departs following midterm losses, setting up a House dynamic in which the Judiciary and Oversight committees are expected to play a role in future investigations into the Trump administration.

◐ Debate

Democratic

  • Sen.-elect Abdul El-Sayed (D-Mich.) publicly called on Hegseth to resign, telling him on CNN, 'Why are you still doing this job? You are really bad at this.'
  • House Democratic Leader Hakeem Jeffries said the blocked impeachment vote was not the end of the matter, stating, 'This is not the end of it. It's just the beginning.'

Republican

  • Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth dismissed the impeachment push as a 'farce' and 'sabotage.'
  • President Trump celebrated the vote's outcome on Truth Social, declaring the impeachment effort had 'miserably FAILED.'
  • Rep. Warren Davidson (R-Ohio) said he believes the war with Iran is unconstitutional but argued Hegseth was being used as a scapegoat for broader 'status quo politics in Washington.'
Steelman

Massie's vote against tabling the measure, together with Davidson's comment that he believes the war with Iran is unconstitutional, shows that at least some Republicans raised genuine constitutional concerns about executive war powers, even though the broader GOP conference moved to shut the impeachment effort down procedurally.

Implications

With Democrats poised to gain control of both chambers in January, they could revisit the underlying questions raised by Massie's impeachment effort under different political leadership, making this procedural defeat likely a pause rather than a resolution.
