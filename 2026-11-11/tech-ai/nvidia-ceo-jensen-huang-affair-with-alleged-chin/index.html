Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang's affair with alleged Chinese spy triggers board investigation, stock slide

Tech / AI desk · anchored on Reuters

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang has admitted to an extramarital affair with Alice Huang, an executive at Nvidia customer Megaspeed whom U.S. intelligence agencies believe was spying for China, plunging the world's second-most-valuable company into a governance crisis. Nvidia's board announced Sunday it would conduct an independent investigation led by outside counsel Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz. The stock fell 5.47% on Tuesday and 4% on Monday, a two-day loss of about 9.5%, as investors absorbed the scandal and the prospect that Huang, who co-founded the chipmaker more than 30 years ago, could be forced out.

The board said in a Sunday statement that Nvidia 'has no reason to believe that either national security or NVIDIA secrets were compromised.' However, U.S. officials allege Alice Huang was a Chinese spy, and the affair has drawn scrutiny from Washington policymakers pressuring Huang to appear before congressional committees. Huang hired a PR crisis firm known for handling sex scandals a full year ago, according to Wired, though it is unclear when he learned of the espionage allegations. Corporate governance experts say there is no precedent for a CEO caught in a suspected honeypot, with the investigation's outcome depending heavily on what Huang knew and when.

Nvidia's chips are central to the AI boom, powering tools like ChatGPT, and Huang has personally backed hundreds of billions of dollars in investments and guarantees for AI startups and data center providers. Analysts at Bernstein wrote, 'Nvidia is not an ordinary company and Jensen is not an ordinary CEO; he IS Nvidia.' Sequoia Capital partner Shaun Maguire posted, 'Jensen is irreplaceable for America’s AI leadership.' Yet the company has no obvious successor, with a famously flat structure of more than 60 direct reports to Huang. His children also work at Nvidia: son Spencer as a director of product management, daughter Madison as a senior director of product marketing, and son-in-law Nico Caprez in business development. Huang did not rule out Madison as a potential successor when asked by Bloomberg last month.

The scandal has also highlighted broader concerns about Chinese economic espionage in the semiconductor industry. Nate Singer, CEO of due diligence firm Heron Intelligence, told TechCrunch that similar cases involving senior executives and state-sponsored actors are 'an open secret' in the industry, with many going unreported. 'There are a lot of senior people in the industry that know this is happening, and are effectively condoning it,' Singer said. He described a case where a CEO's wife confronted executives over a corporate jet used to fly with 'young ladies who also turned out to be state-sponsored actors.'

Nvidia already faces a proposed class action lawsuit alleging that Huang and the company misled investors by concealing the relationship while describing dealings with Alice Huang's former employer as routine. University of Colorado corporate governance professor Ann Lipton said securities fraud claims are 'only about lying,' adding, 'You can have as many affairs with Chinese spies as you like — you just can’t lie about it under the federal securities laws.' A mismanagement claim under Delaware law would be harder, requiring proof that the board cannot handle the problem independently. Huang owns about 3.6% of Nvidia and does not have dual-class voting control, making him 'easier to dislodge,' Lipton said.
