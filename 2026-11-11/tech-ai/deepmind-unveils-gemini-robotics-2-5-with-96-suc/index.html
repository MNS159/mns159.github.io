DeepMind's Gemini Robotics 2.5 hits 96% success rate packing parts on Mercedes-Benz lines

Tech / AI desk

Google DeepMind has unveiled Gemini Robotics 2.5, its latest vision-language-action (VLA) model for robots, reporting a 96% success rate in a real-world pilot on active Mercedes-Benz production lines. The model was tested alongside Apptronik's Apollo 2 humanoid at plants in Kecskemét, Hungary, and Sindelfingen, Germany, where the robots performed automotive kitting — sorting individual parts from bulk bins into sequenced trays for vehicle assembly. According to DeepMind, the pilot shows promising progress toward widespread industrial use, though company director Kanishka Rao cautioned that closing the remaining 4% gap to human-level reliability will be a steep road.

DeepMind says the new model marks a significant advance in dexterity, reliability, and safety. In laboratory evaluations spanning more than 200 tasks — including serving food with tongs, tying a bow on a gift box, and pipetting liquids — Gemini Robotics 2.5 achieved success rates 30% to 100% higher than previous baselines, while completing tasks 20% to 30% faster. Specific improvements over the prior Gemini Robotics 2 model included packing pastries with tongs rising from 5% to 100% success, closing a toolbox lid from 0% to 93%, untying ribbon bows from 0% to 70%, hanging a cardigan from 0% to 62%, and sweeping trash into a dustpan from 0% to 52%. The model works natively with multiple robot platforms, including Apollo 2 with its five-fingered SharpaWave hand (22 degrees of freedom) and the Franka Duo system with two-fingered grippers.

The architecture behind the system combines two components: Gemini Robotics ER, an embodied reasoning model that takes instructions and live video to plan how a robot can accomplish a task, and Gemini Robotics 2.5, the VLA action model that generates the actual movements of actuators, hands, and arms. During the Mercedes-Benz test, the robots picked up metal automotive brackets from supply bins and placed them into sequencing containers, correcting for gaps as they worked. Gemini Robotics ER managed the workflow while Gemini Robotics 2.5 controlled Apollo's hands and arms. Apptronik co-founder and CEO Jeff Cardenas said the pilot is exactly what Apollo was built for — useful work alongside people in real industrial settings — and noted that the models improved dramatically within a few weeks of post-training and carried over to production with very little drop-off.

DeepMind also highlighted safety advances. Gemini Robotics 2.5 uses inference-time reasoning to steer around physical hazards in real time, and company evaluations showed safe task completion increased by 33% around obstacles and 43% near people compared with previous baselines. The company separately introduced ASIMOV-Jailbreak, an open-source benchmark containing hundreds of prompts designed to test whether robotic AI systems resist disguised unsafe instructions. In those tests, the Gemini 4 Argon model achieved a 99.5% resistance rate, compared with 82% for the earlier Gemini Robotics ER 2 model.

Gemini 4 Argon also played a key role in accelerating development of Gemini Robotics 2.5. According to DeepMind, Argon can generate thousands of simulated practice runs from a real-world camera scene, providing varied conditions for training and evaluation. It can also analyze videos of unsuccessful task attempts, suggest possible causes of failure, and help researchers test changes in simulation and on physical robots. Gemini Robotics 2.5 is now rolling out to Google's robotics partners, including Agile Robots, Apptronik, and Boston Dynamics.
