Anthropic IPO demand surges as Nvidia faces scrutiny; Microsoft stake raises questions

Financial & Markets desk

Anthropic, the U.S.-based artificial intelligence company behind the Claude model, said in an updated filing Tuesday that it will offer 825 million shares in its upcoming initial public offering at a price range between $70 and $78 per share. Trading on the Nasdaq is expected to begin on Nov. 19, according to people familiar with the details. The IPO is reportedly seeing very strong demand, with big money already lining up during the roadshow, despite broader concerns over the AI trade.

Microsoft, a strategic investor in Anthropic, is taking a roughly $1 billion stake in the concurrent placement, representing about 13,513,513 shares at the $74 midpoint. The stake equals approximately 0.03% of Microsoft's $3.97 trillion market value. The filing discloses that Anthropic sells Claude through AWS, Google Cloud and Microsoft Azure under joint go-to-market arrangements in which it pays each cloud provider a fee. Anthropic also reports about $900.9 billion of aggregate committed spend on purchase commitments and leases, mostly for compute and infrastructure, without a counterparty breakdown. Fortune reported the offering could value Anthropic at up to $1.4 trillion on a fully diluted basis. Microsoft shares declined 2.5% to $532.44 in regular trading Wednesday, trading 11.5% below GuruFocus's GF Value of $601.35, which the firm calls 'Modestly Undervalued.'

Meanwhile, Nvidia faces mounting scrutiny. A Baird analyst downgraded the stock, warning of major scrutiny ahead and no longer recommending buying the chip maker's shares. News of Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang's affair with an alleged Chinese spy involved with an entity that short seller Culper Research alleged helped Alibaba obtain restricted Nvidia chips has dragged down the stock to its lowest valuation since late March, weighing on the overall U.S. stock market. Despite the downgrade, Nvidia shares rose nearly 4% in pre-market trading Wednesday. Goldman Sachs said the company's third-quarter results are expected to exceed expectations. A Seeking Alpha analyst rated Nvidia a Hold at $169, with a fair value estimate of $145, citing risks including unsustainable 75% gross margins, customer financing exposure, and aggressive revenue guidance.

In China, Alibaba announced late Tuesday that the Hong Kong Stock Exchange will allow it to spin off its semiconductor subsidiary T-Head via a public offering in Hong Kong, subject to approval from the exchange and the China Securities Regulatory Commission. The filing did not disclose a date or proposed size. Alibaba noted it had already filed confidentially on Sept. 11. Hong Kong-listed shares of Alibaba traded 1% lower Wednesday and are down about 29% year-to-date. In late September, Alibaba announced its next-generation AI chip, the Zhenwu V900, which it said delivers three times the performance of its predecessor. Alibaba is one of several Chinese companies developing homegrown semiconductors amid growing U.S. restrictions on China's access to chips. Short seller Culper Research alleged in May that Alibaba was able to obtain restricted Nvidia chips by indirectly financing entities in Southeast Asia; Alibaba did not immediately respond to a request for comment.

Other Chinese tech IPOs face delays. Baidu has reportedly delayed the listing of its AI chip entity Kunlunxin, and Chinese memory chip company YMTC has also delayed its listing, according to news reports this month. Neither company immediately responded to requests for comment. In recent months, China's financial regulators have increased their scrutiny on tech IPOs and the rush of capital into the sector. Chinese tech stocks have underperformed their U.S. peers, despite investor excitement last year around AI breakthroughs by DeepSeek.

The developments highlight a volatile period for AI-related stocks, with Anthropic's IPO drawing strong demand even as Nvidia faces reputational and competitive pressures. Microsoft's $1 billion stake in Anthropic tests the balance between partner and rival, as Claude routes revenue through Azure while potentially competing with Microsoft's own software. Investors will watch for any Azure-specific disclosure of Anthropic spending and monitor Nvidia's upcoming earnings for signs of sustained growth.
