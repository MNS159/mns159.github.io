Ukraine Strikes Russian Grain Terminals at Novorossiysk; Moscow Warns of Global Food Chaos

International & World desk

Ukraine launched a "massive" overnight drone and missile strike on Russia's Black Sea port of Novorossiysk, damaging two major grain export terminals and targeting the last large Russian naval base on the Black Sea, according to reports. Local officials said three people, including an eight-year-old child, were killed and 24 others injured. An emergency situation was declared in the city after the barrage struck areas around the port, damaged pipelines, and suspended water supply.

Ukraine's President Volodymyr Zelensky described the operation as a "unique operation" involving rockets, jet drones, and naval drones aimed at the Russian naval base, though he did not mention the grain terminals. Ukraine's military later claimed four Russian warships, including two frigates, were hit; Russia has not commented on that assertion. "The occupying fleet and all the infrastructure that supports it will not be safe as long as Russian aggression continues," Zelensky said on social media.

Three of Russia's largest grain terminals at Novorossiysk have halted operations due to serious damage, people familiar with the matter said. A loading gallery at United Grain's Novorossiysk Grain Plant collapsed and several silos were damaged; a gallery and truck unloading facility at Demetra's terminal were also hit, and the KSK terminal was struck. The Russian agriculture ministry announced it was working to redirect cargo flows to ports in the Baltic and Caspian Seas as well as land routes, citing "current logistical constraints" but not referring directly to the attacks. Maria Zakharova, the Russian foreign ministry spokeswoman, accused Ukraine of striking grain and agricultural infrastructure, pushing up global food prices and exacerbating grain shortages. "It is evident that the Kyiv regime’s cynical military campaign to incite chaos in the global food market … serves the interests of certain Western nations," she said. Wheat futures jumped as much as 4 percent in Chicago on Wednesday, the biggest intraday advance in three weeks.

The attack follows an uptick in Black Sea strikes this summer after a period of relative calm. Most of Russia's Black Sea fleet is now docked at Novorossiysk after being forced to vacate its former base at Sevastopol in Crimea due to exposure to Ukrainian attacks. Separately, Russian aerial overnight strikes killed at least 11 civilians in Ukraine between Tuesday and Wednesday, the majority in the southern city of Zaporizhzhia, which was hit with ballistic missiles and glide bombs.

Ukraine has grown increasingly adept at long-range strikes on Russian territory, and recent attacks on vessels in the eastern Black Sea have disrupted Russian grain shipments. Russia is the world's largest wheat exporter, but a Moscow-based analytics company said exports for August could fall by more than half compared to the same month over the last five years due to disruptions in the Black and Azov seas. Concerns about global food supplies have persisted since Russia's 2022 invasion, as the two nations together account for a significant share of global grain trade, particularly for buyers in North Africa, the Middle East, and parts of Asia. The 2022 Black Sea Grain Initiative, which temporarily allowed Ukraine to export agricultural products, collapsed after Moscow resumed attacks on vessels using the maritime corridors to Odesa the following year.

Zakharova said Russia would continue striking "facilities that [Ukraine's armed forces] use on the Black Sea to destabilise the navigation situation" until "the full elimination of threats to security projected to the Sea of Azov and the Black Sea." In an interview with CNN, Zelensky said Ukraine would need a tenth of the current U.S. stockpile of interceptor missiles to stop all Russian ballistic missile attacks, and five percent to get through the winter. He noted that this year Ukraine has two and a half times fewer interceptors than in 2025, while Russia has twice as many ballistic missiles per month as before.
