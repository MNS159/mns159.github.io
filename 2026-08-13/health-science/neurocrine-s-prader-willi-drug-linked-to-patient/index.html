Neurocrine's Prader-Willi drug linked to patient deaths; company defends risk-benefit profile

Health & Science desk

A group of physicians and experts in Prader-Willi syndrome (PWS) has notified clinicians of a series of patient deaths and severe side effects potentially associated with Vykat XR, a drug approved in March 2025 by the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) to curb intense hunger in people with the rare disease. According to the FDA’s Adverse Event Monitoring System, seven patients prescribed the drug have died since its clearance, and more than 100 reports of serious adverse events—primarily hospitalizations for swelling, respiratory, and heart complications—have been filed.

Neither the deaths nor the severe side effects have been definitively linked to the drug, the group of physicians and experts noted in their statement. The experts wrote that their intention was “to increase awareness of the risks for people with PWS when starting Vykat XR.” The drug, sold by Neurocrine Biosciences, was approved based on a small clinical trial in which no deaths were reported and side effects were characterized as manageable. However, real-world use has revealed a safety profile that appears worse than what was seen in the trial, with a small number of patients dying and a sharp rise in serious side effects requiring hospitalization.

Vykat XR is the first FDA-approved treatment for the hyperphagia (intense, insatiable hunger) associated with PWS, a devastating genetic disorder. The drug’s approval reflected a regulatory judgment that its modest efficacy and manageable side-effect profile in the trial outweighed the risks, given the severity of the disease. But the post-market reports have prompted a group of PWS specialists to issue a warning to clinicians, highlighting the discrepancy between the trial data and real-world outcomes.

Neurocrine Biosciences has defended the drug’s risk-benefit profile. The company has not publicly commented on the specific adverse event reports, but the broader context of the drug’s evaluation—a small trial for a rare disease—underscores the challenge of balancing benefit and risk when real-world use reveals safety signals not seen in pre-market studies. The FDA and Neurocrine are expected to continue monitoring the situation, and the physicians’ statement serves as an alert to the medical community to exercise caution when initiating Vykat XR in PWS patients.
