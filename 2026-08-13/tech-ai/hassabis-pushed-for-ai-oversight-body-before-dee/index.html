Hassabis Pitched AI Oversight Body Before DeepMind Leadership Reshuffle

Tech / AI desk · anchored on Reuters

Demis Hassabis, the co-founder of DeepMind, proposed the creation of a US-led coalition to act as an AI watchdog in the weeks before stepping down as chief executive of Google DeepMind on August 5, according to reporting by the Wall Street Journal. Hassabis, who sold DeepMind to Google for an estimated $650 million in 2014, warned on Substack that humanity needs to do more to prepare for highly powerful artificial general intelligence (AGI). "What we collectively do now will determine how the next phase of civilisation unfolds," he wrote. "By safely stewarding AGI into the world, we can enter a new golden age of scientific discovery and progress."

Hassabis has not left Google. He assumed two new roles: Chair of Google DeepMind and Chief Scientist of Alphabet, giving him continued oversight of AI evolution and more time to focus on research into applying AI to medicine and curing complex diseases at Isomorphic Labs, the London-based startup he founded in 2021 with Alphabet backing. His day-to-day operational responsibilities at Google DeepMind were handed to Koray Kavukcuoglu, the company's chief technology officer for 13 years, who moved from London to Mountain View last year and also took on the role of Alphabet's Chief AI Architect. Kavukcuoglu will oversee Gemini model development, which is expected to be steered more directly toward products, particularly cloud-related ones, and will have final say on major DeepMind decisions, a Google spokesperson told Reuters.

The reshuffle concentrated Google's AI leadership in Mountain View and saw the departure of several prominent London-based researchers. Jeff Dean, co-founder of Google Brain and DeepMind's chief scientist who had been with Google for 27 years, and Oriol Vinyals, former vice president of research at DeepMind and a technical lead on the Gemini models, left to co-found Discovery Loop, a new research-focused startup. They were joined by Quoc Le, a Google Brain co-founder and former DeepMind scientist, and Sanjay Ghemawat, a senior fellow with 25 years at Google. Discovery Loop is a public benefit corporation focused on machine-learning-driven scientific research, aiming to build AI systems that can propose, execute, analyze, and iterate on parallel experiments autonomously in domains such as chip design, material science, drug discovery, and cybersecurity. Alphabet is a founding institutional investor alongside venture capital firms Radical Ventures and Khosla Ventures, and Google is providing the startup with specialized cloud computing infrastructure for at least its first year. David Silver, best known for AlphaGo, left to launch a startup called Ineffable Intelligence, and Simon Bouton, DeepMind's chief experience officer, plans to join London-based CuspAI.

The leadership changes followed months of pressure from Google co-founder Sergey Brin, who has been informally shaping model training without a formal role. According to Reuters, Brin has pressed key staff to go all in on Gemini and close the distance with rivals. In April, he addressed hundreds of employees at a town hall and urged Google DeepMind to move faster after Anthropic previewed its Claude Mythos model. Brin has used the implicit weight he carries as co-founder to steer resources toward specific areas, including recursive self-improvement, the point at which AI can improve without human intervention. A DeepMind engineer told Fortune that Brin is constantly around and hands-on, while they had never seen Hassabis walk through the Gemini area. The Pathfounders newsletter, citing industry sources, reported that Hassabis wanted to exit alongside Dean but was persuaded to take the chair role over fears of a share price collapse.

Google's AI efforts have faced competitive and structural challenges. Reuters reported that by August the company had delayed its new flagship version of Gemini by two months after internal testing showed it still trailed rivals on coding. Fortune, citing DeepMind engineers, reported that Gemini 3.5 Pro missed three release deadlines after being unveiled at I/O in May with a promise to ship the following month. Constrained computing capacity and disagreements among Gemini's many project leads contributed to the lag, while Google's bureaucracy meant slower release cycles than nimbler competitors, four sources told Reuters. Engineers traced the coding gap to the previous summer, when the company was preoccupied with protecting search traffic while rivals ran ahead on agents. Alphabet's stock dropped around 4% to 5% following the announcements, representing a loss of some $170 billion in market capitalization.

At an all-hands meeting on August 6, Hassabis and Kavukcuoglu tried to calm staff, most of whom learned about the reshuffle when everyone else did, three sources with direct knowledge told Reuters. Day-to-day work would stay the same, but leadership confirmed that several nontechnical teams were moving out of DeepMind and into Google's corporate reporting structure. The Financial Times, which spoke to a dozen people familiar with the company, framed the reorganisation as a shift of AI control from London back to Silicon Valley. In his message to DeepMind staff on August 5, Hassabis wrote: "We have arrived at a pivotal moment in human history. I've been working towards AGI my whole life and now, like many of you, I feel it is close at hand. It's critical that we collectively get the next steps right."
