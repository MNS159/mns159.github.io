White House press secretary Karoline Leavitt to step down

Politics desk
✓ Facts
  • President Trump announced via Truth Social on Wednesday that White House press secretary Karoline Leavitt will leave her post at the end of the month.
  • Leavitt, 28, said she is departing to spend more time with her two young children, including a daughter born in May, after recently returning from parental leave.
  • Trump said Leavitt will become one of his top outside advisers and 'an influential voice' within the Republican Party.
  • Leavitt became the youngest White House press secretary in history when she took the role in January 2025, and is the second-longest-serving press secretary of either Trump administration after Sarah Huckabee Sanders.
  • During her tenure, her office took control of press pool logistics from the White House Correspondents' Association and initially barred the Associated Press from events after it declined to use the term 'Gulf of America.'
  • Journalist access to the 'upper press' area of the White House was restricted starting last October, requiring appointments that were not previously needed.
  • Leavitt previously worked as an assistant press secretary in Trump's first term, ran unsuccessfully for Congress in New Hampshire in 2022, and later worked for the pro-Trump group MAGA Inc. and Trump's 2024 campaign before returning as press secretary.
  • It has not yet been announced who will replace her; several Cabinet secretaries filled in at the podium during her recent maternity leave.
○ Analysis

Leavitt's exit marks the end of an 18-month tenure as the public face of the Trump White House, a role she took on at 28, making her the youngest person ever to hold the job. Her departure follows a now-familiar Washington pattern in which high-profile administration jobs collide with the demands of parenthood, and it comes as Trump's sixth press secretary changeover across two terms. The transition also lands amid broader political headwinds for the administration, with polling showing voter unease over the economy and inflation as the war involving Iran continues, though no successor has yet been named.

Steelman

Beyond the personnel change itself, Leavitt's tenure reshaped the mechanics of White House press access — from the AP dispute to new appointment rules — in ways that will outlast her regardless of who succeeds her, meaning the bigger story may be the institutional precedent she leaves behind rather than the departure alone.

Implications

Attention now shifts to who Trump names as his sixth press secretary and whether that person continues Leavitt's more combative, access-restricting approach to the press corps or pursues a different course, all against the backdrop of a midterm cycle in which the administration is already facing scrutiny over economic conditions and the Iran conflict.
