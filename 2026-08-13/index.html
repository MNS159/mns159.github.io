Colombia earthquake death toll rises to 265 as rescue operations enter final phase

International & World desk

The death toll from the 7.4-magnitude earthquake that struck western Colombia on Monday has risen to 265, President Abelardo de la Espriella said Wednesday, as rescue operations entered what officials described as a final phase. The quake, the strongest to hit Colombia this century, has injured approximately 3,500 people and left nearly 500 missing, according to the president. A crowdsourced tracking site used by families put the number of missing far higher, at more than 4,100. More than 25,800 families have been affected, and 140 buildings collapsed entirely, with thousands of homes damaged or destroyed.

The quake struck at 07:34 local time (12:34 GMT) on Monday, with its epicentre near the village of San José del Palmar in the remote, impoverished Chocó province. Seismometers near the epicentre registered shaking lasting between 90 seconds and two minutes. More than 100 aftershocks have been recorded since. The cities of Cali and Pereira were among the hardest hit, along with Manizales and Quibdo. In Cali, Colombia's third most populous city, 95 people are confirmed dead, and more than 200 remained trapped as of Tuesday afternoon. In Pereira, 79 people have died, at least 92 buildings collapsed, 15 people are known to be trapped, and 37 are reported missing, according to the Association of Colombian Capital Cities (Asocapitales).

Ebola outbreak in DR Congo on track to be deadliest ever, WHO chief warns

International & World desk

The current outbreak of Ebola in the Democratic Republic of Congo is on track to surpass the deadliest outbreak of the disease ever recorded, the director-general of the World Health Organization (WHO) has said. Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus told reporters that the 2026 outbreak, 'at its current pace,' would 'eclipse' the 2014-2016 occurrence, which killed at least 11,000 people. The outbreak, declared on 15 May, has seen at least 4,300 cases and caused more than 2,000 deaths.

July CPI rises 0.1% as expected, annual rate at 3.4%; markets rally

Financial & Markets desk

The consumer price index rose 0.1% in July, matching economist expectations, and the annual inflation rate eased to 3.4%, the Bureau of Labor Statistics reported Wednesday. Excluding volatile food and energy, the core CPI advanced 0.2% for the month and 2.5% from a year earlier, also in line with the Dow Jones consensus. The tame readings helped lift stocks, with the S&P 500 closing 0.26% higher at 7,748.50 and the Nasdaq Composite adding 0.54% to 26,588.49, while the Dow Jones Industrial Average slipped 0.04% to 53,770.27. Treasury yields were negative across the board.
