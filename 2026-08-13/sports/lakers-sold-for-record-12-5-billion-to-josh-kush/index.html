Lakers sold for record $12.5 billion to Josh Kushner and Bob Iger

Sports desk

Mark Walter has agreed to sell his majority stake in the Los Angeles Lakers to Bob Iger and Josh Kushner at a $12.5 billion valuation, according to news that arrived Wednesday. The sale comes less than 14 months after Walter initially agreed to buy majority control of the team on June 18, 2025, for $10 billion, making it perhaps the most stunning change of ownership in NBA history. Walter, who also owns the Los Angeles Dodgers, will remain the controlling owner of the Dodgers, according to The Athletic, and is not expected to sell that team at least for now.

Iger and Kushner had been pursuing a different NBA opportunity before pivoting to the Lakers. "We were involved in pursuing the new franchise in Vegas," Iger told The California Post on Wednesday. ESPN's Ramona Shelburne said on the Pat McAfee Show that Iger and Kushner "were in the expansion process trying to bid on the Vegas team" and that "the number for the Vegas expansion team is climbing close to what Mark Walter paid for the Lakers last year, which was $10 billion." The sale of the Lakers at a $12.5 billion valuation establishes a new benchmark 25% higher than the previous sale, which is expected to impact what the NBA can demand for expansion franchises.

The Buss family's stewardship of the Lakers, which spanned 11 championships under patriarch Dr. Jerry Buss, ended with the sale to Walter last year. Jeanie Buss retained the title of team governor for a reported period of at least five years. Iger told The California Post that he and Kushner "have every intention to honor the agreement that was made between Mark and Jeanie," but added, "If things change, they'll change." Neither Iger nor Kushner ever owned minority stakes in the Lakers. Iger previously ran Disney and ESPN; Kushner is a former minority owner of the Memphis Grizzlies and currently holds a stake in the Miami Heat.

The sale puts the "Dodger-fication" of the Lakers on hold. Walter's ownership was expected to transform the Lakers into the NBA's equivalent of the Dodgers, known for spending not just on players but on scouting, analytics, health and performance. Over the past year, the Lakers had begun overhauling their front office, consulting Dodgers president of baseball operations Andrew Friedman and former general manager Farhan Zaidi. Rob Pelinka remains atop basketball operations, but most of the scouting staff was fired, Rohan Ramadas was hired as an assistant general manager, and former Dodgers executive Lon Rosen was hired as president of business operations less than six months ago. With Walter no longer owning the Lakers, the vision of replicating the Dodgers' model is now uncertain.

The sale also raises questions about the Buss family legacy and the team's stability. Quite a few longtime team employees, including two Buss brothers, were fired last year by an owner who will no longer oversee their replacements. If the new ownership leads to further staff turnover and disappointing on-court results, it could affect how the Buss family's stewardship is ultimately remembered. For now, the NBA expansion process appears to be moving forward, with the league likely confident it still has enough bidders to draw a huge price for a Las Vegas team. The status of a potential Seattle expansion team is less clear, though Samantha Holloway, owner of the Seattle Kraken and Climate Pledge Arena, has expressed readiness to work with the league to bring the NBA back to Seattle.
