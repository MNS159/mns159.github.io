Russell Westbrook announces NBA retirement after 18 seasons

Sports desk

Russell Westbrook, a nine-time All-Star and the 2017 NBA MVP, announced his retirement Wednesday after 18 seasons in the league. The 37-year-old point guard, who was the No. 4 overall pick in the 2008 NBA Draft by the Seattle SuperSonics, finishes his career as the NBA's all-time leader in triple-doubles with 209. In a social media video narrated by actor Michael B. Jordan, Westbrook wrote: "Sometimes you don't even know when you've already watched the end. You had to be there. And now it's over."

Westbrook averaged 20.9 points, 6.9 rebounds and 8.0 assists over 1,301 career games with the Oklahoma City Thunder, Houston Rockets, Washington Wizards, Los Angeles Lakers, Los Angeles Clippers, Denver Nuggets and Sacramento Kings. He is one of only three players — along with Oscar Robertson and Nikola Jokić — to average a triple-double for an entire season, and he did so four times, a record. Westbrook also made nine All-NBA teams, won two scoring titles, led the league in assists three times, and is 14th on the all-time scoring list (27,176 points) and fifth in assists (10,351). He is one of two players, alongside LeBron James, to surpass 25,000 points and 10,000 assists in a career.

Westbrook's most memorable years came early in Oklahoma City, where he teamed with Kevin Durant and James Harden to reach the 2012 NBA Finals, losing to the Miami Heat. After Durant's departure in 2016, Westbrook produced an MVP season in 2017, averaging 31.6 points, 10.7 rebounds and 10.4 assists while leading the Thunder to 47 wins. He later played for six teams over his final seven seasons, and last year averaged 15.2 points, 5.4 rebounds and 6.7 assists for the Sacramento Kings. Despite interest from the Kings and Washington Wizards, Westbrook chose to retire rather than see his role diminish further. On the international stage, he won gold medals with Team USA at the 2010 World Championship and the 2012 Olympics.

Reactions poured in from across the sports world. Singer Ciara, wife of recently retired NFL quarterback Russell Wilson, posted a message on social media: "Congrats on an incredible career and being one of the most Legendary players that ain't afraid to sauce it up! It was a blessing to rock with you!" Westbrook had collaborated with Ciara in 2025 on the music video for 'Dance With Me.' LeBron James, Westbrook's teammate on the Lakers, wrote on Instagram: "HELLUVA CAREER BRODIE. HOF next!!" However, some fans criticized James, blaming him for the decline in Westbrook's later career. One fan commented: "Tell LeBron to shut up; he's the one who ruined the back end of Westbrook's career."

Westbrook's legacy is secure as a future Hall of Famer and a member of the NBA's 75th anniversary team. His retirement comes as the NBA and NFL both head into the 2026 season without their iconic 'Russ' — Westbrook and Russell Wilson — on a roster. Westbrook's decision to step away on his own terms, after proving he could still contribute at an NBA level, caps a career defined by relentless competitiveness and statistical dominance.
