Job openings surge to 7.52 million in September, signaling labor market improvement

Financial & Markets desk

Job openings in the U.S. surged to a four-month high in September, the Bureau of Labor Statistics reported Tuesday, offering a positive signal for the labor market ahead of the October payrolls report. The Job Openings and Labor Turnover Survey showed openings rose by 333,000 to 7.52 million, well above the Dow Jones consensus estimate of 7.15 million. The job openings rate increased to 4.5% of the total workforce, up 0.2 percentage point from August. There are now about 414,000 more job openings than unemployed workers, translating to roughly 1.05 openings per available worker.

The number of workers quitting their jobs also spiked, rising by 321,000 to 3.36 million, the highest level since March 2025. Quits are considered a barometer of worker confidence in finding new employment after leaving a position. Hires increased by 131,000 to 5.4 million, while layoffs and discharges fell by 127,000 to 1.64 million, the lowest since March 2025. Openings rose in manufacturing (54,000), state and local government (108,000), and professional and business services (106,000).

"There are more job openings than unemployed people in the United States right now. That doesn't solve all problems, but it's a big change from a year ago," said Heather Long, chief economist at Navy Federal Credit Union. "It's an encouraging sign that despite all the headwinds right now with high diesel and gas prices and ongoing inflation problems, companies are slowly increasing their hiring."

The report comes ahead of the October nonfarm payrolls report, due Friday from the BLS. The Wall Street consensus calls for an increase of 59,000 jobs, after September eked out a gain of just 29,000, while the unemployment rate is expected to hold at 4.2%. In other economic news Tuesday, September factory orders for manufactured goods jumped 1.8%, well above the 1.3% estimate and up from a 0.3% increase in August.
