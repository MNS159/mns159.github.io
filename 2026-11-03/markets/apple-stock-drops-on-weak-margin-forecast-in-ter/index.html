Apple stock drops on weak margin forecast in Ternus' first earnings report

Financial & Markets desk

Apple shares slid 3% in extended trading Monday after the company beat fiscal fourth-quarter revenue and earnings estimates but issued a disappointing gross margin forecast for the current quarter. Apple reported earnings per share of $2.10 versus the $1.99 expected by analysts, and revenue of $114.48 billion against the $113.39 billion forecast, according to LSEG. The quarter's revenue rose 12% from $102.5 billion a year earlier, and Apple said the period capped off its "best fiscal year ever."

The quarter's results were mixed across product lines. iPhone revenue came in at $57.58 billion versus the $56.54 billion estimate, and Mac revenue reached $10.79 billion, beating the $9.61 billion forecast. However, iPad revenue of $6.23 billion fell short of the $6.7 billion expectation, and Wearables, Home and Accessories revenue of $8.3 billion missed the $9.06 billion estimate. Other Products revenue was $31.57 billion, essentially in line with the $31.58 billion forecast, and gross margin of 47.9% topped the 47.5% estimate.

The report was the first since John Ternus took over as CEO on Sept. 1, succeeding Tim Cook. Apple said on its earnings call that it expects December-quarter revenue to rise between 7% and 10% annually, ahead of the 6.1% average analyst estimate. But its gross margin forecast for the current quarter of 45% to 46% trails the 46.3% average estimate, a key factor in the stock's decline.

Apple is contending with a global memory crunch and rising component costs. The company said memory costs were higher in the September quarter than in the June period, and it "reluctantly" raised prices across its product lineup in June, including a $100 increase on iPhones, to offset component costs. Tariff refunds added 5 cents to earnings per share. Ternus told CNBC's MacKenzie Sigalos that growth came despite supply constraints and foreign exchange headwinds, adding, "I think at this point, with everything we've achieved, it just feels like we've never been more confident that the best is yet to come."

The quarter included only eight days of sales of the new iPhone 18 Pro, and Apple's new flagship device, the iPhone Duo, did not reach store shelves until after the period ended.
