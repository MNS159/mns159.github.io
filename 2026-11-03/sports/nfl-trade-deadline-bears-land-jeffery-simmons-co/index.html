NFL trade deadline: Bears land Jeffery Simmons, Colts rebuild, rumors swirl

Sports desk

The 2026 NFL trade deadline has reshaped the league with a series of blockbuster moves, headlined by the Chicago Bears' acquisition of All-Pro defensive tackle Jeffery Simmons from the Tennessee Titans. The Bears sent the Titans a 2027 first-round pick, a 2028 first-round pick, and a 2027 fourth-round pick in exchange for Simmons and a 2028 fifth-round pick. Simmons made an immediate impact in his Chicago debut, recording a sack and a team-high six tackles in a 34-17 road win over the Seahawks. The trade signals the Bears' belief that their roster is ready to compete for a Super Bowl, while the Titans, now holding two future first-round picks, face questions about whether to use them on a new quarterback as they deepen their rebuild.

The Indianapolis Colts emerged as the most aggressive sellers, trading two star players on Oct. 27. The Seattle Seahawks acquired six-time All-Pro guard Quenton Nelson for a 2027 third-round pick and a 2028 fifth-round pick, marking the fourth consecutive year Seattle has made a major midseason addition. The Seahawks hope Nelson will elevate a struggling run game, though his debut ended in a loss to the Bears. Later that day, the San Francisco 49ers reunited with defensive tackle DeForest Buckner, sending the Colts a 2027 fourth-round pick. Buckner, who began his career with the 49ers as a 2016 first-round pick, returns to bolster a defensive line hit by injuries to CJ West (bone chip in knee, injured reserve) and Alfred Collins (torn patellar tendon, out for the year). The Colts received a Day 3 pick for the 32-year-old Buckner, who was on an expiring contract and had grown frustrated with Indianapolis' struggles.

Other notable trades include the Houston Texans acquiring wide receiver Marvin Harrison Jr. and a 2027 seventh-round pick from the Arizona Cardinals for a 2027 second-round pick and a 2027 sixth-round pick. Harrison, reunited with his college quarterback C.J. Stroud, has been mostly underwhelming in his first two games but caught a touchdown in Week 7. The Cardinals admitted the former first-round pick was a poor fit in their system but secured a second-rounder in return. The Baltimore Ravens addressed their interior offensive line by acquiring Cam Jurgens and a 2027 seventh-round pick from the Philadelphia Eagles for a 2028 second-round pick and a 2027 fifth-round pick. The move allows the Ravens to move Olaivavega Ioane from center to right guard and provides a long-term replacement for Tyler Linderbaum. The Eagles, mired in a six-game losing streak, are now facing a rebuild after also seeing tackle Lane Johnson and position coach Jeff Stoutland retire.

The Dallas Cowboys acquired cornerback Joey Porter Jr. from the Pittsburgh Steelers for a 2028 second-round pick and a conditional 2027 sixth-round pick that vested after Porter played two games. Porter has allowed a 39.6 passer rating on 11 targets in his first four games, but the Cowboys' defense remains in shambles, and Porter is set to become a free agent this offseason. The Steelers, unwilling to meet Porter's contract demands, secured a second-rounder as the next-best return. The New York Giants, desperate for quarterback help after Jaxson Dart tore knee ligaments in Week 2, acquired J.J. McCarthy from the Minnesota Vikings for a 2027 fifth-round pick. McCarthy, who had fallen to third on Minnesota's depth chart, has since earned the starting job over Jameis Winston and delivered a road win over the Texans, placing the Giants in command of the NFC East.

With one week remaining until the Nov. 10 deadline, trade watch continues for a myriad of stars on struggling teams, including Jonathan Taylor, Khalil Mack, and George Pickens. The Arizona Cardinals, at 3-5, are at the forefront of rumors, with star pass rusher Josh Sweat potentially on the move. According to NFL insider Jordan Schultz, Sweat would be open to a trade, and multiple teams have interest, but the Cardinals' asking price is believed to be high—reportedly a second-round pick or equivalent. Arizona has given the impression it is fine keeping Sweat if the price is not met. As contenders and pretenders become clearer, a flurry of moves is expected in the coming days as teams gear up for the home stretch or look ahead to the future.
