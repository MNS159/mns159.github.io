Live results and key races to watch in 2026 midterm elections

Politics desk
✓ Facts
  • Thirty-five Senate seats are up for election in the 2026 midterms, with Democrats needing a net gain of four seats to flip control of the currently Republican-controlled chamber.
  • Cook Political Report rated seven Senate races as toss-ups heading into Election Day, including Alaska, Maine, Texas and Michigan.
  • In Montana, Republican Kurt Alme, Democrat Alani Bankhead and independent Seth Bodnar are competing for the open seat being vacated by retiring GOP Sen. Steve Daines, the state's first open Senate race since 1976.
  • In Ohio, former Sen. Sherrod Brown (D) is challenging GOP Sen. Jon Husted for the Senate seat, while Democrat Amy Acton faces Republican Vivek Ramaswamy for governor to succeed term-limited Gov. Mike DeWine (R).
  • Governor's races also include Nebraska (GOP Gov. Jim Pillen vs. Democrat Lynne Walz), New York (Gov. Kathy Hochul, D, vs. Trump-backed Republican Bruce Blakeman), and Rhode Island (Democrat Helena Foulkes, who defeated incumbent Gov. Dan McKee in the primary, vs. Republican Aaron Guckian).
  • Contested House races include Pennsylvania's 7th, 8th and 10th Districts, Wisconsin's 3rd District (Rep. Derrick Van Orden vs. Rebecca Cooke) plus an open Wisconsin seat after Rep. Tom Tiffany left to run for governor, and South Dakota's at-large seat after Rep. Dusty Johnson's unsuccessful run for governor.
  • In Texas, Republican Ken Paxton faces Democrat James Talarico in what has become the most expensive Senate race in U.S. history, with Paxton backed by more than $170 million from Elon Musk.
  • In Alaska, GOP Sen. Dan Sullivan faces Democrat Mary Peltola and an independent candidate who shares his name; the matter is the subject of a state investigation and a Justice Department lawsuit that a judge rejected.
○ Analysis

The 2026 midterms will determine control of the U.S. Senate for the remainder of President Trump's term, with Democrats needing to flip four seats to take the majority and gain power over judicial confirmations and oversight of the administration. Analysts have flagged a relatively small number of races—spread across states as varied as Alaska, Maine, Texas, Michigan, Ohio, Montana and Nebraska—as competitive enough to decide that outcome, while a broader map of governor's races and House contests in states like Pennsylvania, Wisconsin, South Dakota, New York and Ohio will shape state-level power and the House majority. Several races feature unusual circumstances worth understanding before watching returns: Alaska's ranked-choice system and a ballot dispute over two candidates sharing the name Dan Sullivan could slow and complicate that state's count; Rhode Island's incumbent governor lost his own primary; and Texas's Senate race has become the most expensive in American history, underscoring the national financial stakes both parties are placing on traditionally safe territory.

Implications

The combined results across Senate, governor and House races will indicate whether Republicans retain full control of Washington or Democrats gain enough ground to check the administration's final two years, while unusually competitive contests in traditionally safe states like Texas, Alaska and Montana may offer early signals about party strength heading into the 2028 presidential race.
