Compass Pathways says psilocybin depression drug filing imminent after stock drop on rival's submission

Health & Science desk

Compass Pathways expects to complete a rolling submission of its psilocybin depression drug COMP360 to the Food and Drug Administration “in the next two weeks,” the company said Tuesday, after its stock fell more than 35% on Monday following news that the nonprofit Usona Institute had submitted its own psilocybin candidate for FDA approval. Compass shares rebounded somewhat after the announcement but remained 23% below the start of the week at $9.01 as of publication.

Both Compass and Usona hold Commissioner’s National Priority Vouchers (CNPVs) for their candidates—COMP360 for treatment-resistant depression (TRD) and Usona’s PSIL201 for major depressive disorder (MDD)—which reduce FDA review periods from 10–12 months to 1–2 months. Stifel analysts said in a note Tuesday that the CNPV “gives us more confidence approval [for COMP360] should firmly come within the 1Q27 timeframe (if not earlier) based on prior CNPV timelines.” Compass said it is “on track” for a launch in the first half of 2027, subject to FDA approval.

In the Phase 3 COMP006 trial, Compass reported in August that 39% of patients with TRD who received two fixed doses of COMP360 achieved a clinically meaningful reduction in depression severity by week six, with a durable response maintained through week 26. Treatment-emergent adverse events were transient and typically occurred on the day of dosing. Usona’s PSIL201, by contrast, targets MDD rather than TRD, and its Phase 3 data have never been disclosed. In a Phase 2 placebo-controlled trial of 104 adults with MDD published in JAMA in 2023, a 25-mg dose of PSIL201 met the primary endpoint with a “rapid and sustained antidepressant effect” compared with active placebo and no serious treatment-emergent adverse events.

Stifel analysts said the Usona filing “will be an overhang for a bit, though CMPS remains confident with NDA completion imminent.” They noted that Compass emphasized that its COMP360 data are “inherently differentiated in that they come from a highly refractory TRD population.” RBC Capital Markets analysts suggested the stock dip created a buying opportunity, writing Monday that “given the size of the market - we think there is room for multiple competitors” and that “even modest commercial success is not being priced in to shares at these levels.”
