Washington state resident dies from bird flu after exposure to infected cattle

Health & Science desk

A person in Washington state has died from complications of bird flu after exposure to infected cattle, marking the first such U.S. death in a year and the third overall since the virus became a major public health concern in 2024. The Yakima County resident, who had underlying health conditions, was hospitalized and died Sunday, according to county health officials. The infection was caused by the H5N1 strain of the virus, the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention said.

The person was exposed to avian influenza at a dairy with infected cattle. Yakima County officials reported that 33 dairy herds have recently tested positive for H5N1, and three human infections have been linked to exposure at a dairy. The Yakima Health District is working with the state health department to identify and monitor close contacts for symptoms.

The H5N1 bird flu has been spreading widely among wild birds, poultry, cows, and other animals for several years. Health concerns escalated in early 2024 when infections spread among U.S. livestock. More than 70 human infections have been reported in the U.S. since the beginning of 2024, according to the CDC. However, there is no known person-to-person spread, and the risk to the general public remains low.

The two previous U.S. deaths tied to bird flu occurred in January 2025 (an elderly person in Louisiana with H5N1) and in November 2025 (a Washington state resident with a rarer version, H5N5). The recent death is the first in a year.

The CDC analyzed the genetic sequence of the virus from the Washington patient and found no changes that would make the virus spread more easily or cause more severe illness, a spokesperson said Monday. Officials continue to urge people who have contact with sick or dead birds to take precautions, including wearing respiratory and eye protection and gloves when handling poultry. People who work on farms are considered at higher risk.
