Child dies of measles in New York, seventh U.S. death this year as outbreaks spread

Health & Science desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

A child in rural northern New York has died after contracting measles, marking the state’s first death from the disease in 30 years, state officials announced Monday. The child died in Lewis County, about 93 miles north of Syracuse, Gov. Kathy Hochul said. The death is the seventh measles-associated death reported in the U.S. this year; the other six were in Pennsylvania, which is experiencing a dramatic outbreak of more than 1,430 cases. Prior to this year, Pennsylvania had not seen a measles-related death in 35 years.

The U.S. stopped the domestic spread of measles in 2000 by maintaining high vaccination rates, but those rates have fallen in recent years to below the 95% coverage needed to prevent outbreaks. Indiana has now recorded more than 100 confirmed measles cases for the first time in the 21st century, jumping from eight cases on Sept. 2 to 104. Nearly a quarter of those infected have been hospitalized, according to epidemiologist Thomas Duszynski of IU Indianapolis. “Measles hasn’t changed. What has changed is we’ve left gaps in our vaccination status,” Duszynski said. Riley Hospital for Children is now screening patients and visitors for symptoms and exposure.

The resurgence of vaccine-preventable diseases is fueled by slipping vaccination rates nationwide as health misinformation and anti-vaccine sentiments have taken hold. One of the most prominent anti-vaccine activists in the U.S., Robert F. Kennedy Jr., now serves as health secretary. Since taking office, Kennedy has waged a multi-front war on vaccines, including dismantling federal vaccine recommendations and continuing to spread misinformation. Days before the New York death, The Washington Post reported that career scientists at the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention—under Kennedy’s control—were blocked from publishing an urgent health alert to clinicians about the risk and management of measles cases during pregnancy.

In a statement, Gov. Hochul directly tied the child’s death to federal policies, calling it “a devastating reminder of what’s at stake when Washington deliberately erodes decades of progress in protecting Americans from preventable illnesses.” She declared a disaster emergency in early October that expanded the pool of people who can administer measles vaccines to include paramedics and midwives, and allowed nurses to order testing. Overall, New York has reported 170 measles cases in over a dozen counties. Experts stress that vaccination is the only way to control the disease. “The risks of getting the disease and a bad outcome are much higher than getting vaccinated,” Duszynski said.
