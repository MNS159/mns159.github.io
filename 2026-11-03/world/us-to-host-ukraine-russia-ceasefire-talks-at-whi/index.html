Ukraine Diplomacy

White House to host Russian, Ukrainian representatives as U.S. mediates Abu Dhabi peace talks

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts slightly center-left

The White House will bring Russian and Ukrainian representatives together this week for talks on the ceasefire in Ukraine's southern Kherson region, as Russia on Tuesday confirmed that U.S.-mediated peace negotiations between the two countries will take place in Abu Dhabi on Nov. 10-11.

U.S. special envoy Steve Witkoff said the working-level discussions in Abu Dhabi would "focus on post-war security arrangements, de-escalation, humanitarian efforts, and next steps toward peace." Putin aide Vladimir Medinsky will serve as Moscow's chief negotiator, according to Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov. Witkoff added that American negotiators will meet this week in Brussels with European and NATO officials, including France, Germany and the United Kingdom, to discuss security guarantees for Ukraine and the country's reconstruction. "We look forward to building on the progress made in Kyiv, Oslo, and St. Petersburg toward a comprehensive, durable peace agreement that delivers lasting security for generations to come," he said.

Separately, First Lady Melania Trump will host representatives from both countries at the White House on Thursday to discuss the Kherson ceasefire, the White House said Tuesday; she will deliver remarks on the Oleshky ceasefire at 11 a.m. ET. The White House described the summit as "an extraordinary moment in American history" but did not name the attendees. The session follows a six-day ceasefire that ended earlier this week in the Russian-occupied city of Oleshky and surrounding areas, allowing humanitarian supplies to enter and an undisclosed number of residents to evacuate. The truce has been credited by both sides to Melania Trump and Pope Leo; Ukraine accused Russia of using it to reinforce its military positions.

Peskov also said the Kremlin hopes to finalize plans in the coming days for a trilateral meeting between Presidents Vladimir Putin, Donald Trump, and Xi Jinping to end the war — a proposal that excludes Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy. Zelenskyy has insisted that any negotiations on Ukraine's future must include Kyiv. "It can be acceptable only when we are at the table of negotiations," he said on Oct. 20.

The talks are the latest in several rounds of U.S.-mediated exchanges during President Trump's second term, which have often yielded prisoner exchanges or other low-level agreements. But Putin has refused to abandon his demand that Ukraine accept Russia's unilateral annexation of territory seized during the nearly five-year war, which began with the full-scale invasion of Feb. 24, 2022. Zelenskyy has consistently ruled out accepting Russia's seizure of territory by force, and many U.S. allies in Europe agree that legitimizing Putin's land grab would set a dangerous precedent. The diplomatic activity comes amid heightened tensions between Washington and Kyiv, with a series of Trump administration moves in October raising questions about the United States' reliability as Ukraine's most important military partner.

Violence has continued despite the localized truce. At least 29 people were killed in a strike on a village in the southeastern Zaporizhzhia region, authorities said Tuesday. In the northeastern city of Kharkiv, a Russian strike hit a medical facility, wounding 25 people and forcing the evacuation of about 200 patients. Russia's defense ministry said it carried out a "massive strike" on energy facilities across Ukraine and port infrastructure in the Odesa region. Moscow's mayor said 500 Ukrainian drones heading toward the capital were destroyed overnight.
