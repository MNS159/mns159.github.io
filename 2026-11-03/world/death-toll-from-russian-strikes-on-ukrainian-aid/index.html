Ukraine War

Death toll from Russian strikes on Ukrainian aid hub and power grid rises to at least 38

International & World desk

Russian strikes across Ukraine over the past 24 hours have killed at least 38 civilians and wounded scores more, Ukrainian authorities said Tuesday. The deadliest attack occurred in the village of Komyshuvakha in the southern Zaporizhzhia region, where at least 33 people were killed when nine 3,000kg aerial bombs struck a school building being used as an aid distribution centre. President Volodymyr Zelensky said the number of victims was expected to rise, with more than 50 people possibly trapped under the rubble. The attack, which he called "one of the largest mass killings committed by Russia during this war," came as residents had gathered to receive humanitarian aid. Local officials said the aid centre collapsed from a direct hit, and that when people took refuge underground, the roof collapsed, trapping many. Among the dead were two rescuers and four police officers, according to Ukraine's state emergencies service. Three days of mourning were declared in the region.

In the central city of Dnipro, a Russian missile destroyed a hospice caring for nearly 50 terminally ill patients, the oldest of whom was 90 years old. Patients were being transferred to other facilities. A separate strike in Dnipro killed two people and wounded 18 others. In the north-eastern city of Kharkiv, a combined missile and drone attack wounded at least 73 people, most of them elderly patients, and damaged a medical facility. Across the country, Russia struck energy infrastructure in nine regions, affecting water and heating. Kyiv's water supply was entirely cut off due to damage to the capital's energy infrastructure, and the entire metro network was suspended. Mayor Vitali Klitschko said the situation surrounding the city's power supply was becoming "very difficult."

Russia's defense ministry said the attack in Komyshuvakha had targeted Ukrainian military units and killed 25 servicemen. However, BBC News Russian defence correspondent Ilya Abishev reported that there was no credible evidence the site was being used by the Ukrainian military and that there were no major military or industrial facilities in the settlement. The UN's human rights monitoring mission said the distribution of humanitarian aid at the local school had been highly publicised and the attack "seems to have violated the most fundamental principles of international humanitarian law."

Zelensky said the attack was "yet another proof that Russia does not want any de-escalation." He noted that after a recent successful joint Russian-Ukrainian operation to evacuate civilians from the Russia-occupied city of Oleshky during a temporary ceasefire brokered by US First Lady Melania Trump, "there was a moment when the next steps could show that peace is possible." He added: "With such killings, Russia is sending the exact opposite signal — Russia is counting on further killings."

The Kremlin announced it would take part in three-way peace talks with Ukraine brokered by the US in Abu Dhabi on 10-11 November, the first trilateral meeting since February. US President Donald Trump's special envoy Steve Witkoff said the discussions would focus on "post-war security arrangements, de-escalation, humanitarian efforts, and next steps toward peace." Speaking in Berlin, Zelensky said it was important for Russia to engage seriously in negotiations but that he had so far seen no indication of that. The UN said last month that civilian casualties in Ukraine had reached a "record level" as Russian armed forces resumed systematic attacks on energy infrastructure. Ukraine faces a critical air defense shortage due to stalled Western deliveries and a lack of American Patriot interceptor missiles, Zelensky acknowledged, saying that while the front line was stable, "the situation in the air was not as positive."
