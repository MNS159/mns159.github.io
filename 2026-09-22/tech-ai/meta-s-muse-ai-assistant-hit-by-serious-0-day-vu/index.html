Meta's Muse AI assistant hit by serious zero-day vulnerability, blocked by Amazon

Tech / AI desk

Meta's new AI assistant Muse, which has already outpaced ChatGPT in early mobile downloads and daily active users, is facing two major setbacks: a serious zero-day security vulnerability that could give attackers full control over users' accounts, and an outright block from Amazon's website. The vulnerability, reported by Ars Technica, allows any locally installed app or terminal command to steal the authentication token linking a user to their Muse account, potentially enabling malicious actors to take over the assistant's capabilities. Meanwhile, TechCrunch reported that Amazon began blocking Muse from its site Sunday night, displaying a message that said, 'Continued access by an unauthorized AI agent violates Amazon’s Conditions of Use, to which our customers have agreed.'

The zero-day flaw centers on a previously undocumented setting in Muse's macOS app. According to Ars Technica, Meta developers designed Muse so that any local app or executed code—regardless of the macOS permissions it holds—can change a range of settings, including one that controls the endpoint where transcription occurs. Normally, that endpoint points to a Meta server. By changing it to a server they control, attackers can capture the token that grants complete control over the victim's Muse account. This undermines Meta CEO Mark Zuckerberg's claim that Muse was 'built from the ground up for privacy and security,' Ars noted. The assistant requires broad access to users' accounts and macOS device resources, undoing Apple's default security measures.

Amazon's block appears to stem from both competitive and practical concerns. TechCrunch noted that Amazon has its own foundation models and inference platforms, giving it little incentive to welcome a rival's AI agent. Additionally, if Muse makes a bad purchase or order, Amazon would have to handle the fallout with both customers and vendors. Although Muse has a lower hallucination rate than many AI models, it is still far from error-free, making agent-driven commerce a risk Amazon may prefer to postpone.

Muse, launched only a few weeks ago, is an AI assistant that 'books appointments, fills out forms and handles customer service,' and can 'make purchases, generate images, create documents, and connect with your favorite apps and services,' according to Meta's description cited by Ars Technica. The macOS app (there is no Windows version) works with users' WhatsApp, email, calendar, and social media accounts, and can even create tools on the fly when needed. Users must grant the assistant extensive permissions, including access to the microphone, camera, location, and file system.

Despite these issues, early adoption data suggests strong user interest. TechCrunch reported estimates from market intelligence provider Apptopia showing that in the first 12 days after launch in the U.S. and Canada, Muse's iOS app saw 1.8 million downloads, compared to 1.3 million for ChatGPT in the same period. Muse had 642,000 daily active users in the U.S. across both iOS and Android. Even when comparing only iOS, Muse's 359,000 daily active users surpassed ChatGPT's figures. Overall, Muse saw 2.8 million total installs globally in its first 12 days. Over 95% of Muse users are also Facebook users, and 63% are Instagram users.

Meta has not shared public figures on Muse's adoption. The twin developments raise questions about the assistant's security posture and its ability to integrate with major e-commerce platforms, even as it gains rapid early traction.
