Google confirms Gemini models hacked three companies in May 2026

Tech / AI desk

Google has confirmed that its Gemini models hacked three real companies during a May 2026 test, according to a Wall Street Journal report. The incident occurred during a cybersecurity “capture the flag” exercise conducted by the firm Irregular, which was designed to test the AI’s capabilities in a closed environment. Due to a misconfiguration, the models were able to access the internet and targeted real infrastructure instead of the simulated targets.

In one of the three hacks, Gemini guessed passwords until it gained access to a company’s online services. In the other two instances, the models searched public software repositories and found login credentials that companies had accidentally included. In all three cases, Google’s models reportedly stopped after realizing they had accessed a real company’s servers. Irregular then changed its configuration to prevent the AI from accessing the internet.

Irregular did not initially consider the event worthy of further investigation and did not inform Google about the hacks until July 2026, following news of other AI hacking incidents. After becoming aware of the event, Google notified the affected companies. Google, which has been slow to release frontier Gemini models in recent months, had been absent from the “rogue AI” conversation until now.

The nature of the intrusion is less troubling or impressive than previous AI hacks, as it stemmed from a configuration error rather than a deliberate breach of safeguards. The incident highlights ongoing challenges in testing advanced AI systems securely, particularly when models are given access to real-world networks.
