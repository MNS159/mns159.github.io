OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind Discuss AI Safety as UK Parliament Invites Them to Hearing

Tech / AI desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

OpenAI, Anthropic, and Alphabet’s Google DeepMind have been holding discussions on AI safety for several weeks, OpenAI global policy chief Chris Lehane said Tuesday, as the companies face mounting pressure to address risks from increasingly capable AI systems. Lehane said at a briefing in Washington that the three rivals are coordinating on safety measures, adding, “It’s better to try to work together to prioritize safety.” He stated that OpenAI does not believe an antitrust waiver is necessary for the current talks, according to a Bloomberg report.

The discussions come as Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei has called on the industry to “pace the frontier,” arguing that AI development should slow enough for safety measures and independent evaluations to keep up. Amodei has also proposed giving independent evaluators permanent, employee-level access to frontier AI labs to assess safety practices. OpenAI CEO Sam Altman has backed that proposal, signaling growing support among major labs for coordinated safety measures.

Separately, the UK parliament’s Business, Innovation, Science and Trade Committee has invited OpenAI, Meta, Anthropic, and Google DeepMind to give evidence at a hearing on AI security scheduled for October 13. The committee published letters to the four companies as part of its work examining the UK’s economic strategy for AI. Lawmakers plan to question the companies on mandatory pre-release testing and incident reporting, AI Security Institute access to their models, and whether AI development should slow to allow for safety safeguards.

In the U.S., Lehane said OpenAI would support bipartisan legislation aimed at mitigating catastrophic AI risks, including a federal AI governance framework proposed by Republican Representative Jay Obernolte and Democratic Representative Lori Trahan. OpenAI is also open to a Senate proposal from Majority Leader John Thune, Republican Sen. Ted Cruz, and Democratic Sen. Amy Klobuchar that would require developers of advanced AI models to implement safeguards and give the federal government a role in testing and oversight. Separately, Republican Sen. Jim Banks and Democratic Sen. Adam Schiff have proposed legislation creating a narrow antitrust carveout allowing AI companies to share information about loss of control over AI systems, cyber or biological threats, and data exfiltration by Chinese companies. That bill has been folded into an annual defense bill, according to Bloomberg.

The safety discussions and regulatory scrutiny come as lawmakers consider whether AI companies should receive limited antitrust protection to share information about serious AI-related threats. On investor sentiment, retail sentiment on Stocktwits was “Bullish” for OpenAI and “Extremely Bullish” for Anthropic, with both tickers seeing extremely high message volume.
