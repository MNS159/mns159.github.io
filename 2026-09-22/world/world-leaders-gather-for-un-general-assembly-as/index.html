World leaders gather for UN General Assembly as wars in Iran, Ukraine, Yemen dominate

International & World desk

Nearly 130 world leaders gathered in New York on Tuesday for the 81st UN General Assembly High-Level Week under the theme 'Restoring trust, managing transformation'. UN Secretary-General António Guterres opened the session, warning that 'the fault lines in our world are widening' due to geopolitical conflicts, the climate crisis, and the rapid development of artificial intelligence, and called for a world built on 'interdependence'.

The US-Israel war on Iran dominates discussions. Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian, heading to New York, accused the US of 'doing everything they can to block every possible route to force our nation to surrender' and vowed to highlight US 'oppression'. Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said the UN has lost trust because of its 'ineffectiveness in establishing international peace and security'. Iran demands a return to the memorandum of understanding signed with the US in June to reopen the Strait of Hormuz and has communicated seven conditions for reopening, including an end to the war on all fronts, the release of frozen assets, and the lifting of the blockade on Iranian ports. Iran also warned it could abandon the Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty depending on future US actions.

US President Donald Trump, who launched the war on Iran with Israel on February 28, is set to meet representatives from Gulf Arab states (Saudi Arabia, UAE, Qatar, Oman, Kuwait, Bahrain) to discuss the conflict. Trump said he would 'probably be open' to meeting Pezeshkian, but Iran's IRNA news service dismissed this as a 'new propaganda game'. Trump also plans to meet Chinese leader Xi Jinping in Washington on Wednesday. Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, who launched the war alongside the US, will be in New York for less than 24 hours but is not expected to meet Trump; his speech Thursday is expected to draw protests both inside and outside the UN.

The conflict in Yemen has reignited, with the Iran-aligned Houthis capturing the port city of Mokha. At least six people, including two children and a woman, were killed in airstrikes Tuesday in Mokha. The Gulf Cooperation Council called on Iran to halt attacks on Arab states and allow free transit through the Strait of Hormuz and the Bab el-Mandeb Strait. The foreign ministers of Canada, France, Germany, Italy, Japan, the UK, and the US called on Iran to stop supporting the Houthis, warning their actions constitute a 'dangerous pattern of escalation' that risks exacerbating the conflict, undermining trade, and creating global economic instability.

Other conflicts on the agenda include Russia's war on Ukraine, Israel's wars with Hamas in Gaza and Hezbollah in Lebanon. Guterres also warned about unregulated AI, saying 'killer robots must have no place in our future' and that 'life and death decisions must never be surrendered to machines'. He noted that governments with the greatest AI capabilities, China and the US, have the greatest responsibility to establish safeguards.

Iranian public expectations are skeptical of any breakthrough. One Iranian told Al Jazeera: 'there is no hope that anything comes of it.' Another said: 'I think if [the politicians] want to, then yes, there can be an opening, but they don't want to.' Iran's delegation will be in New York for five days; the US denied visas to Pezeshkian's accompanying media team and imposed restrictions on movement and luxury goods purchases.
