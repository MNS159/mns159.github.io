Iran War Spreads to Red Sea as Trump Seeks Diplomatic Exit; Oil Falls on Pipeline Hopes

International & World desk

Nearly seven months into the Iran war, the conflict has expanded from the Strait of Hormuz to the Red Sea, as Iran-backed Houthi militia in Yemen seized critical coastal positions and threatened trade routes, compounding a global energy crunch. Saudi Arabia blamed Iran-backed militias in Iraq for blowing up its critical East-West oil pipeline on Sept. 10, though Riyadh is now testing the pipeline and could restart it as soon as this week, according to a person familiar with the matter. Brent crude futures fell 1.8% to $98.56 a barrel Tuesday, while U.S. West Texas Intermediate dropped 2.3% to $93.60, as hopes for diplomacy rose ahead of the United Nations General Assembly.

U.S. President Donald Trump, facing record-high gasoline and diesel prices ahead of the Nov. 3 midterm elections, is under pressure to show results. According to The New York Times, Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman called Trump on Sunday to urge intervention against the Houthis. Fox News reported that Trump said the Houthis had agreed not to attack U.S. targets, and Axios reported that Trump rejected a Saudi request for strikes against the Houthis last week. Trump is due to meet Chinese President Xi Jinping in Washington this week, likely pressing Beijing to use its influence over Tehran—China is the largest buyer of Iranian oil—particularly on Strait of Hormuz security.

Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian is traveling to New York for the U.N. General Assembly, where he will present Iran's positions on the war and hold talks with several leaders from Sept. 22 to 26 and 28, according to Iran's semi-official Tasnim news agency. Trump has signaled openness to meeting Pezeshkian. Axios reported that Trump plans to discuss the Iran war with representatives of several Gulf states on the sidelines of the assembly. A planned meeting involving Iran, the Gulf states and Iraq was canceled last week, possibly linked to Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia.

U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent told CNBC that all Iranian airlines will be shut down from Wednesday, saying, 'If they land, you cannot provide them with fuel. You cannot provide them with landing services, you cannot sell them tickets, or you will be knocked out of the dollar system.' Meanwhile, Tehran is demanding an end to fighting on all fronts, the release of billions in frozen assets, and the lifting of the naval blockade in the Strait of Hormuz, according to Iranian security official Mohsen Rezaei. Talks with Qatar and Oman are reportedly continuing.

Iran expert Fatemeh Aman told DW that Trump wants to end the war from a position that allows him to claim Iran's nuclear and military capabilities have been weakened, freedom of navigation restored, and Tehran forced back to negotiations, but a coherent strategy remains unclear. Farzan Sabet, a Middle East expert at the Geneva Graduate Institute, said Gulf states recognize they would likely not prevail in a direct confrontation with Iran and favor de-escalation. Aman added that Iran could pursue a two-track strategy: resisting a U.S.-led regional containment while offering Gulf states an alternative security model based on accommodation.

Lukman Otunuga, head of market research at FXTM, said confirmation of direct talks between Washington and Tehran could place additional selling pressure on oil prices by improving expectations for regional supply, but warned that Tehran has said renewed escalation would trigger a significant response that could push crude prices higher. For now, Washington, Tehran and the Gulf states continue to pursue different roadmaps for ending the conflict, with no lasting ceasefire agreement in sight.

Sources: DW·CNBC
