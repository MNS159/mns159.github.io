Artiva Biotherapeutics shuts down after trial failure and two patient deaths

Health & Science desk

San Diego-based Artiva Biotherapeutics said Tuesday it is winding down after abandoning its lead immunotherapy candidate, AlloNK, for rheumatoid arthritis and laying off about 94% of its workforce. The decision follows updated clinical data showing the off-the-shelf natural killer cell therapy was initially effective but that the durability of the response did not meet the company's threshold to support continued development. The company also faced scrutiny after two patients in its clinical trials died, though neither death appeared to be related to the AlloNK therapy itself.

Artiva, founded in 2019, had 115 full-time employees in June and planned to grow, according to federal filings. The layoffs will affect nearly all staff, many of whom work at the company's headquarters at 5505 Morehouse Drive in San Diego. An official WARN notice detailing the layoffs has not yet been filed. The company said it is exploring alternatives to maximize shareholder value, including a possible sale, merger, or liquidation. “We don't have a specific timeline that we can share at this point. AlloNK was the only program we were developing, and now that we are no longer continuing development, we are exploring alternatives that maximize shareholder value,” said Noopur Batsha Liffick, head of investor relations.

The two patient deaths were assessed by an investigator and an independent safety review board. In one case, a Phase 2a trial participant suffered a fatal neurological event, leukoencephalopathy, following treatment. The investigator determined the death was likely related to fludarabine, a mild chemotherapy regimen typically administered before cell therapy to lower white blood cell counts and make room for the new cells, rather than to the cell therapy itself. In the second case, a participant died suddenly approximately two months after initiating treatment. The investigator ruled the event unrelated to AlloNK or any other component of the study regimen, but the exact cause of death remains undetermined.

“Throughout the development of AlloNK, the safety and well-being of patients have remained our highest priority. We are deeply saddened by the deaths of two patients who participated in our clinical studies and extend our sincere condolences to their families,” said Dr. Fred Aslan, Artiva's chief executive officer. The company said in a statement that “longer-term data showed that durability of clinical response did not meet the threshold the Company required to support continued development.”

Artiva held approximately $319.1 million in cash and investments as of Sept. 30, according to federal filings. The company's largest expenses were staffing and clinical development; of the roughly $25 million spent last year, $11.8 million supported drug development and $10.8 million went toward personnel. However, Aslan acknowledged that Artiva still faces substantial wind-down costs related to severance, trial terminations, site obligations, lease commitments on its San Diego facility, and partner agreements. Following the announcement, Artiva's shares fell approximately 31% to around $4.23 on Tuesday.
