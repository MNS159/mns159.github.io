Lilly's retatrutide beats Ozempic in head-to-head diabetes trial

Health & Science desk · anchored on Reuters

Eli Lilly announced Wednesday that its investigational drug retatrutide outperformed Novo Nordisk's Ozempic in a large Phase 3 clinical trial of adults with type 2 diabetes. In the head-to-head study, patients on the highest dose of retatrutide achieved a 2.2 percentage-point reduction in blood sugar (A1c) after 80 weeks, compared with a 1.8 percentage-point reduction for those on Ozempic. The results, based on participants who stayed on treatment, also showed that those on the highest dose of retatrutide lost 20.9% of their weight, while those on Ozempic lost 9.4%.

In absolute terms, patients on the 9 mg dose of retatrutide lost an average of 44.3 pounds, and those on the 12 mg dose lost 47.4 pounds, versus 20.9 pounds for the highest Ozempic dose (2 mg). The drug also provided better heart benefits, including improvements in cholesterol and blood pressure. Retatrutide is a once-weekly injection that mimics three hunger-regulating hormones: GLP-1, GIP, and glucagon. By contrast, Ozempic and Wegovy (also from Novo Nordisk) mimic only GLP-1, while Lilly's own Mounjaro and Zepbound mimic GLP-1 and GIP. The addition of glucagon, a hormone that promotes fat burning even at rest, is what sets retatrutide apart.

Lilly said it plans to submit retatrutide for approval by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration in early 2027. The drug is not yet FDA approved, and the FDA has warned that any versions consumers have obtained online come from the black market and should not be used. Novo Nordisk also has an experimental drug, CagriSema, which expects FDA approval by the end of the year. Neither CagriSema nor retatrutide is currently approved. Ozempic and Mounjaro are FDA-approved to treat type 2 diabetes, while Wegovy and Zepbound are approved for weight management. As with any prescription medication, individuals should consult a health care provider about whether these drugs may be appropriate for them.
