Senate Race

Senate control remains toss-up as key races tighten

Politics desk
✓ Facts
  • Republicans currently hold a 53-47 Senate majority and need to retain 50 seats to keep control, since Vice President JD Vance casts tie-breaking votes.
  • Fox News' final Power Rankings rate overall Senate control a toss-up, with Democrats favored in 48 seats and Republicans favored in 47.
  • A Suffolk University poll released Wednesday shows Democrat Sherrod Brown leading Sen. Jon Husted, R-Ohio, 47% to 44%, with a margin of error of ±4.4 points, 4% undecided and 3% backing another candidate.
  • A Siena College/New York Times poll shows Sen. Darline Graham, R-S.C., tied with Democrat Annie Andrews at 48% each, with 3% undecided and under 1% for another candidate.
  • Fox News shifted its rating on three races: Ohio moved from toss-up to Lean Democrat, South Carolina moved from Likely Republican to Lean Republican, and Mississippi moved from Solid Republican to Likely Republican.
  • Other races rated toss-up in the Power Rankings include Alaska, Iowa, Maine, Texas and Michigan; New Hampshire and Minnesota are rated Lean Democrat; Kansas and South Carolina are rated Lean Republican.
  • Sen. Jon Husted was appointed to the Ohio seat vacated by JD Vance, and Sen. Darline Graham was appointed in July to the South Carolina seat following the death of her brother, Sen. Lindsey Graham.
  • The midterm elections are scheduled for Nov. 3, with the Power Rankings and polls released about six days beforehand.
○ Analysis

Control of the Senate hinges on a small number of competitive races, several of which are in states that President Trump carried in 2024. Historically, the party controlling the White House tends to lose congressional seats in midterm years, and Fox News' reporting notes that inflation, an unpopular conflict involving Iran, and Trump's approval ratings form the backdrop against which Republicans are defending their majority. Because Vice President Vance can break a 50-50 tie, Republicans have a one-seat cushion in the chamber, needing only 50 seats to retain control while Democrats must win enough seats outright to overcome that advantage.

Steelman

Both marquee races in Ohio and South Carolina are within or at the edge of each poll's margin of error, meaning the numeric leads reported are not statistically decisive — the honest takeaway is that these contests, and the Senate majority itself, remain genuinely unresolved with days to go.

Implications

With several races rated toss-up or near-toss-up and notable shares of undecided voters in states like Alaska, Iowa and Kansas, the battle for the Senate majority remains unsettled heading into the final days before Election Day.
