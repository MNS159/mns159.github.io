Rays force World Series Game 5 with 12-inning walk-off win over Brewers

Sports desk

ST. PETERSBURG, Fla. — Pinch-hitter Ben Williamson singled home the winning run in the bottom of the 12th inning, and the Tampa Bay Rays staved off World Series elimination with a 4-3 victory over the Milwaukee Brewers on Tuesday night, forcing a Game 5. The Rays, who trailed 3-2 entering the bottom of the 12th after Milwaukee rookie Cooper Pratt hit a tiebreaking solo homer off Ian Seymour, rallied after a throwing error by third baseman Joey Ortiz on Junior Caminero's leadoff grounder. Liam Hicks singled, and Jonny DeLuca's single to center drove in pinch-runner Cedric Mullins to tie the score. Chandler Simpson's single loaded the bases, and Williamson — who had been 1 for 15 in the postseason — hit a chopper over first baseman Jake Bauers and into right field to end the game.

Pratt, a 22-year-old rookie who also tied the score at 1 with an RBI double in the second, became the first rookie to hit an extra-inning homer in the World Series. David Hamilton had earlier homered for Milwaukee, and Jackson Chourio collected a single and double, improving to 10 for 20 in the Series. The Brewers had won eight straight games and were bidding to become only the second team to sweep a League Championship Series and the World Series. Tampa Bay ended that streak in front of a crowd of 40,514 at Tropicana Field, where many Brewers fans waved yellow towels and some wore cheeseheads.

Tampa Bay became just the fifth of 26 teams that fell behind 3-0 in the World Series to win Game 4; the previous four all lost Game 5. Of 43 teams to trail 3-0 in any best-of-seven postseason matchup, the only one to rebound and win the series was the Boston Red Sox against the New York Yankees in the 2004 American League Championship Series. The Rays, seeking their first championship, will try to extend the series again in Game 5 on Wednesday night at Tropicana Field.

The game also carried broader significance beyond the diamond. The New York Post noted that the thriller served as a reminder of what baseball fans will be missing once the season ends, as a lockout by owners is considered almost certain when the current collective bargaining agreement expires on Dec. 1. For now, the Rays have ensured at least one more day of baseball.
