Eagles' Hurts recovering after playing with life-threatening blood clot

Sports desk

Philadelphia Eagles quarterback Jalen Hurts is expected to miss the rest of the season after multiple blood clots were found in his lungs, a life-threatening condition if not treated quickly. The 28-year-old played a full game hours after being diagnosed with a superficial blood clot in his calf during the team's visit to London earlier this month, raising questions about the NFL's handling of player health and safety.

Hurts first reported an issue to the team on 8 October, after another player stepped on his left calf during a game on 4 October. He trained in full and received treatment. On 10 October, the day the Eagles arrived in London, Hurts visited the Cleveland Clinic, where an ultrasound showed a superficial blood clot (vein thrombosis) in his calf. He was cleared to play. Hurts returned to the team hotel at about 3am on 11 October and played the full game against the Jacksonville Jaguars at Tottenham Hotspur Stadium. A follow-up ultrasound was recommended within 72 hours, and he was cleared for the flight home that evening.

On 14 October, after daily treatment, a second ultrasound showed the clot had moved, and Hurts was ruled out of the next game. A joint statement on 19 October said further tests revealed deep vein thrombosis and bilateral pulmonary emboli—multiple blood clots in both lungs. His treatment involves blood-thinning medicine, and he is expected to make a full recovery but will miss the remainder of the season.

Dr. Allen Sills, the NFL's chief medical officer, who was in London that weekend and arranged for Hurts to be evaluated at the Cleveland Clinic, said the league does not have a protocol for such situations. He stated that those who dealt with Hurts were "incredibly conservative" and that he had "complete confidence" in how the Eagles handled the matter. The NFL Players Association (NFLPA) initially called for a full investigation, noting the NFL had said it did not plan to review the care Hurts received. However, the league and NFLPA later released a joint statement saying they are "working together to review the matter."

The case has drawn scrutiny to the NFL's approach to player health during its growing international schedule, which includes a record nine games this year. Dr. Niko Mihic, the local medical coordinator for the NFL's next international game in Madrid on 8 November, told BBC Sport that blood clots among athletes are "not that common" and that decisions on whether to allow a player to sleep, stay active, or fly long-haul involve medical judgment on a case-by-case basis. "It's not as simple as some people think," he said. "There's an amount of judgement that goes into this."

Hurts is expected to make a full recovery, but Dr. Mihic noted that "the worst risk factor for recurrent embolisms is having had one previously." The Eagles, who have lost five straight games and lack an experienced backup quarterback, face a challenging season. With the trade deadline two weeks away, the team that reached two of the past four Super Bowls could be facing a rebuild.
