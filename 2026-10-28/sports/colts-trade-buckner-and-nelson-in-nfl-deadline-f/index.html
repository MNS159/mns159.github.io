Colts trade Buckner, Nelson in NFL deadline fire sale; Taylor could be next

Sports desk

The Indianapolis Colts set off the NFL trade deadline season Tuesday, Oct. 27, shipping defensive tackle DeForest Buckner to the San Francisco 49ers and guard Quenton Nelson to the Seattle Seahawks within hours of each other — and attention immediately turned to whether running back Jonathan Taylor will be next before the Nov. 10 deadline.

The Colts sent Buckner, 32, back to the team that selected him seventh overall in the 2016 draft, receiving a 2027 fourth-round pick in return. Seattle, meanwhile, acquired Nelson, an eight-time Pro Bowler and six-time All-Pro, for a 2027 third-round pick and a 2028 fifth-round pick. Both players are in the final year of their contracts and can become free agents after this season.

Analysts across outlets read the moves as the start of a full rebuild. The Colts fired general manager Chris Ballard after a disappointing start, and they are rebuilding without a first-round pick. NFL.com wrote that Indianapolis needs "as many lotto tickets as possible for the next general manager" and expected Ed Dodds to listen to offers on pending free agents and "potentially others under contract beyond 2027." Grades were mostly strong for the buyers — FOX Sports gave the 49ers a B+ and Seahawks an A, while NFL.com gave the 49ers an A- and Seahawks an A — but mixed for the Colts, who received a C+ and B+ for the Buckner deal and a B+ and B for the Nelson trade. Some analysts said Indianapolis might have gotten more by waiting closer to the deadline, though the fourth-round pick for Buckner was still seen as better than a possible 2028 compensatory fifth-rounder.

Buckner, who had grown vocal about the Colts' mounting losses, gives San Francisco an interior boost after the 49ers' pass rush managed a league-low 20 sacks last season and only 15 this year. He had four sacks, eight quarterback hits and 31 total tackles in seven games, and his 47 sacks since 2020 are tied for second among defensive tackles behind Chris Jones. NFL.com noted signs of decline, however: his pressure rate is down to 8.3% this season from 10.3% in 2025 and 11.7% in 2024, and his run-stop rate has dipped to 12.4%. Paired with Osa Odighizuwa — and eventually with Nick Bosa, who is expected back from a calf injury — Buckner should bolster a defensive line for a team in a Super Bowl window.

Nelson gives Seattle a major upgrade on the offensive line, with NFL.com calling him "the NFL's most complete guard" and noting he never allowed more than eight quarterback hits in any of his eight seasons in Indianapolis. The Seahawks have struggled to open running lanes, producing just 92 yards before contact on 184 running back carries with 37 stuffs, per Next Gen Stats. The addition should help quarterback Sam Darnold and cover for left tackle Charles Cross, who is expected to miss a week or two with a high ankle sprain; second-year player Grey Zabel is expected to slide to right guard, a trouble spot for Seattle. General Manager John Schneider cushioned the cost with expected compensatory picks, and NFL.com noted Schneider has previously re-signed players acquired in-season.

Jaguars coach Liam Coen, whose team hosts the Colts on Sunday, acknowledged the trade made his opponent less formidable: "Obviously really good players that I have a lot of respect for... It's two good players, you can't deny that." But he said he does not think Taylor will be dealt, and he warned his players not to fixate on Indianapolis's roster moves. "You try not to get into that world because it's just rat poison either way," Coen said. "Philly a couple weeks ago had a lot of guys out and played us to the last second... In this league, any given Sunday, we all know anything can happen." The Colts, meanwhile, have shifted their focus from winning this season to building for the future, with more moves possible before the Nov. 10 trade deadline.
