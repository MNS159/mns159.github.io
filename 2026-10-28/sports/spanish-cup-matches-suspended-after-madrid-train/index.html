Spanish cup matches suspended after Madrid train crash kills 68

Sports desk

All 24 matches in the Spanish Copa del Rey scheduled for Wednesday have been suspended as a mark of respect after at least 68 people were killed in a train crash near Madrid. The Spanish FA (RFEF) announced the decision in a statement, saying it was made in solidarity with the victims. More than 500 passengers were travelling on the trains when they collided and derailed in the province of Toledo.

Five top-flight teams—Levante, Alaves, Valencia, Celta Vigo and Real Betis—were due to play in the first-round matches on Wednesday. Spanish giants including Real Madrid, Barcelona and Atletico Madrid are not scheduled to enter the competition until the last-32 round.

Barcelona expressed its "deepest condolences" to the victims on social media, offering support and solidarity to the families and friends of those affected and wishing a speedy recovery to the injured. Real Madrid issued a statement on its website expressing "deepest sorrow" over the accident, extending condolences to the families of the deceased and offering "full support" to the emergency services and all those involved in the rescue efforts.

Thursday's matches remain unaffected for now. The RFEF has not yet released a revised schedule for the postponed Wednesday games.
