Ukraine War

Zelensky says Ukraine recaptured territory in secret pipeline and air assault operations

International & World desk · anchored on Reuters

President Volodymyr Zelensky said Ukraine's battlefield position is the strongest it has been in some time after his forces recaptured territory through a series of offensives, including a previously undisclosed operation that used underground gas pipelines to outflank Russian positions near Kupiansk in the northeastern Kharkiv region.

In his nightly video address, Zelensky cited a report by senior commanders to say that the "Veles" operation, conducted near Kupiansk, marked the first time Ukrainian troops used gas pipelines in the northeast. "It is a strong operation, well planned and technology-driven. The Russians tried to use underground gas pipelines to advance past our positions," he said. "Our fighters turned this tactic against the Russians and used the underground infrastructure against the occupiers with extraordinary success."

Zelensky also referred to an air assault operation in eastern Ukraine and the "Vivaldi" operation further north, aimed at preventing Russian advances in key areas. In the Vivaldi offensive, one senior commander reported last month that Ukraine's Third Army Corps had cleared an area of 75 square kilometres (29 square miles) of Russian infiltration groups and recaptured a further 10 square kilometres, focusing on the north of the "fortress belt" cities Sloviansk and Kramatorsk in the Donbas, whose capture would bring Russia closer to seizing the entire region.

His remarks came as Finnish President Alexander Stubb and Norwegian Prime Minister Jonas Gahr Støre held a rare direct phone call with Russian President Vladimir Putin, lasting around 90 minutes on Tuesday, before holding a separate conversation with Zelensky. The call, which Kyiv says it was involved in planning, drew criticism from other European leaders opposed to direct engagement with Putin. Støre defended the call as an effort to ensure Putin heard European views as well as those from the US, saying he did not think Putin would change his mind "but he gets to hear our position very clearly. I am worried that some of these messages do not reach him when the Americans talk to him." The Kremlin showed no softening, insisting that any lasting peace must "unconditionally" take Russia's interests into account, while Foreign Minister Sergei Lavrov dismissed the call as part of the "information war," saying European countries were seeking a role in the negotiation process.

Asked whether peace could come without Ukraine ceding territory, Støre said: "Today Russia controls nearly 20 per cent (of Ukrainian territory). Ukraine will never sign a deal that recognises that they have lost territory, but whether they can take the current frontline as a reality and as a starting point, that is a different question." He said the big question was what security guarantees Ukraine could receive after a peace deal, noting Putin "recognises that this is the big question." Støre also said Norway was keen to introduce de-escalation steps such as stopping attacks on energy infrastructure, halting strikes on ships exporting Ukrainian grain, and conducting prisoner-of-war exchanges.

In other developments, Russian Foreign Minister Sergei Lavrov said the information war around Ukraine would continue and likely escalate; Ukrainian drones struck the Azot chemical plant in Novomoskovsk, Tula Oblast, with unverified videos showing smoke from a fire; a Russian drone strike on Kharkiv injured five people, including a 4-year-old child; and Zelensky said Russia had launched more than 100 ballistic missiles at Kyiv and the surrounding region in two weeks, with air defence package deliveries delayed. A UN-backed inquiry reported Monday that Russia committed war crimes and crimes against humanity by intentionally attacking Ukraine's energy infrastructure, and found evidence that Russia recruited men from 29 countries through deception or coercion, mostly from vulnerable and marginalized communities. Russia's largest lender Sberbank said a Ukrainian drone attack on its data centre in the Saratov region did not cause abnormal cash outflows.
