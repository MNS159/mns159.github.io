At least 68 killed in Spanish high-speed train crash

International & World desk

At least 68 people were killed when a high-speed train slammed into the rear of another train that had stopped on the tracks in central Spain on Wednesday morning, in the country's second deadly high-speed rail collision this year. The crash occurred just after 09:00 (08:00 GMT) between the municipalities of Mora and Orgaz in Toledo province, about 140km (100 miles) south of Madrid, and knocked two carriages off the track and down an embankment, scattering debris across a gravel slope. Train operator Adif said a state-owned Renfe train collided with a private Iryo train, which covered the Madrid to Seville route. The Iryo train was carrying 146 passengers plus one baby, while the Renfe train had 397 passengers, according to Castilla-La Mancha regional government spokesperson Esther Padilla. A passenger on the Renfe train told broadcaster TVE: "We all felt the train abruptly slowing down and then the impact. The car leaned to one side like it was about to tip over. I hit my nose, everything went flying, luggage, cell phones. Everything shook."

Spain's Transport Minister Óscar Puente said the crash was caused by what he called an "inexplicable" decision by a central command centre after a computer failure. He said the security system on a 63km (39-mile) stretch stopped working at 8:30 a.m., causing all trains on that stretch to stop, including the six moving trains. The control centre then ordered the Renfe train to start moving again without any speed restrictions, even though a stop signal was still active on the line. Puente said the train should never have been allowed to proceed, and if it had, it should have been told to go at reduced speed. He said engineers were not to blame, and investigators were still studying how fast the train was travelling, but it could have been at up to 200 kph (124 mph). "I am not attributing responsibility to anyone, I am just stating the facts," he said.

Rescue crews were focusing on extracting survivors and bodies from the two most damaged carriages, Spanish Health Minister Mónica García said. At least 80 people were being treated for injuries, with 50 reported in serious condition and 15 in critical condition. Hospitals in the area suspended planned surgeries, and a Madrid blood transfusion centre appealed for urgent donations. A military emergency brigade, firefighters, police, ambulances and medical helicopters were deployed to the rural site, while uninjured survivors were put on buses. The nearby town of Urda prepared a sports pavilion to receive the injured.

Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez, who cancelled all public engagements including election campaigning, said "the whole of Spain is grieving" and promised a thorough investigation. "The pain is immense," he said at the crash site. "We will get to the bottom of this, and if there has been any negligence, it will come to light. If there have been failures in the security system, they will be addressed with the utmost firmness." King Felipe VI and Queen Letizia said they were "deeply saddened and concerned," and European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said, "My heart goes out to the victims' families and loved ones. Today, Europe mourns with you." Spain's football federation postponed all 24 Copa del Rey games scheduled for Wednesday, with Barcelona, Real Madrid and other clubs expressing condolences.

The crash is the second high-speed rail tragedy in Spain this year. In January, 46 people were killed in southern Spain when an Iryo train jumped the track and struck an oncoming Renfe train. That was the deadliest rail accident in Spain since 2013, when 80 people died in a derailment. Until this year, no fatal crash had occurred on Spain's dedicated high-speed lines since the network opened in 1992, a source of national pride. Spain has invested heavily in high-speed rail, laying about 3,900km (2,400 miles) of track, second only to China's 45,000km, according to the International Union of Railways. Rail service between Madrid, Ciudad Real and Andalusia was suspended, and all train traffic on the affected line was halted.

Iryo, an Italian-owned company that began operating in Spain in 2022, said it had "activated its security protocols and is closely working with authorities." A spokesperson for Spain's train workers' union suggested that privatisation had increased use of the network and placed extra strain on infrastructure. "We are not convinced that the liberalization of the high-speed passenger rail service was matched by the necessary upkeep of the lines," said Joan Rodríguez. "I am not saying it was a determining factor (in the crashes), but yes, the increase in use of the network is a factor to keep in mind." The government said it was still determining the exact origin of the tragedy.
