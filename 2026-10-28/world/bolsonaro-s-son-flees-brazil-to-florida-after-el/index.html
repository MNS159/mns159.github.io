Flávio Bolsonaro lands in Miami after fleeing Brazil following election loss

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts slightly center-left

Flávio Bolsonaro, the far-right Brazilian senator who lost Sunday's presidential election to incumbent Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, landed in Miami, Florida, on Wednesday morning after fleeing Brazil in a journey that took him through Argentina and Ecuador. He arrived at the Miami-Opa Locka executive airport with his wife and two daughters and left under police escort without speaking to reporters.

Argentina's migration directorate said Bolsonaro and his family crossed the border by land late Tuesday via the Tancredo Neves International Bridge in Misiones province, "with valid travel documentation and without any legal impediment to entering the country." They boarded a private Gulfstream jet at Puerto Iguazú airport at about 11:30pm bound for Guayaquil, Ecuador, reportedly to avoid Brazilian airspace, before continuing to Miami. In Miami, the family, shielded by black umbrellas, climbed into a dark gray Ford Expedition MAX and sped away under police escort; Bolsonaro is expected to hold a press conference Wednesday or Thursday. The US State Department said it would grant visas to Bolsonaro and his family, and President Donald Trump said he would approve the move, calling them "fine people" and repeating his criticism of the election: "The election was very strange, and I thought it was terrible." He also said the extension of voting times was a "very bad look."

Bolsonaro's departure came despite a Brazilian Supreme Court order to surrender his passports in connection with a money-laundering investigation in which he denies wrongdoing. He had refused to concede defeat, pledged to challenge the results, and was due to lose his immunity from prosecution in February when his term in the Senate ends. According to the AFP news agency, he also holds Italian citizenship, allowing him to travel even after his Brazilian and diplomatic passports were canceled. On Monday, he had written on Instagram that he had no intention of leaving the country and would "hand over my passports and defend myself against this travesty, which is a clear attempt to intimidate me."

Lula, marking his 81st birthday at an event in Brasília, called the flight "shameful." "This is what the family Bolsonaro are like. Brazil is their second homeland. Their first homeland is licking the boots of the US, as he's become accustomed to doing," he said. "I keep imagining what's on the conscience of a citizen who just lost the elections and flees his country … He who owes nothing, fears nothing. A citizen who sneaks out of his country owes something. The act of leaving Brazil is shameful." Lawmakers from Lula's Workers' Party asked the Supreme Court to order Bolsonaro's arrest.

Bolsonaro is the son of jailed former president Jair Bolsonaro, who is imprisoned on charges of leading a conspiracy to remain in power after his 2022 defeat. The elder Bolsonaro spent three months in Florida after that election and later spent two nights at the Hungarian embassy in Brasília while facing trial over the attempted coup; while under house arrest after a 27-year prison sentence, he attempted to destroy his electronic ankle monitor with a soldering iron. Flávio Bolsonaro's arrival in Florida could deepen a rift between the United States and Brazil: during the campaign, Lula accused the Trump administration of colluding with the Bolsonaro family to help them return to power, and Trump has previously alleged that Jair Bolsonaro was a victim of judicial persecution in Brazil. About a dozen politicians and influencers aligned with the elder Bolsonaro have fled Brazil in recent years.

Bolsonaro is under investigation in at least two criminal cases, including one examining suspicions that he was involved in a money-laundering scheme worth about £680m ($905m) involving betting companies. Shortly after the election result, Brazilian police asked the Supreme Court to retain his passport, citing a podcast interview last Friday in which his brother, former congressman Eduardo Bolsonaro, said that if his brother lost, he should not concede and should go to the US. Reports said the escape had been planned for weeks as a contingency in case of defeat, organized mainly by Eduardo, who lives in self-imposed exile in the US and lobbies the Trump administration against Lula, with the plan "organized and enabled by a lawyer close to the Bolsonaro clan." Brazilian federal police face criticism for failing to prevent the departure, and some in the government suspected allies had planted reports that he was already en route to the US to distract from his real plan to go through Argentina. There remains a gap between the last time Bolsonaro was seen by allies, about 6pm Monday, and his entry into Argentina late Tuesday; it is unclear how or when he reached the border, and he had disappeared from public view for more than 24 hours. The aircraft he boarded had been parked at Puerto Iguazú airport since early Tuesday, when reports first emerged that he intended to flee, and the Argentinian newspaper Página/12 reported that it belonged to businessman Sergio Daniel Mastropietro, who was investigated in a scandal involving the alleged payment of bribes linked to medicine purchases for people with disabilities.
