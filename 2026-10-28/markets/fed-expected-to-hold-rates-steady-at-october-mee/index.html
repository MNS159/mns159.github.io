Fed expected to hold rates steady at October meeting ahead of midterms

Financial & Markets desk · anchored on Reuters, AP

The Federal Reserve is expected to leave interest rates unchanged at the conclusion of its two-day policy meeting on Wednesday, even as inflation accelerates, according to surveys of market participants and economists. Investors put the chance of a pause at about 90%, based on CME Group’s FedWatch Tool, while CNBC’s latest Fed Survey of 30 fund managers, strategists and economists found that 93% expect no rate hike this week. The decision comes less than a week before the midterm elections, which have focused heavily on concerns about the cost of living.

The Fed’s benchmark interest rate currently stands between 3.75% and 4%, a level that marks a significant drop from a recent peak in 2023 but remains well above the near-zero rate set at the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic. The central bank raised its key rate last month for the first time in three years, from about 3.6% to 3.9%. Inflation rose to a four-month high in September, with the Consumer Price Index increasing 3.7% from a year earlier, more than a percentage point above the Fed’s 2% target, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics. Fuel prices climbed in response to unrest in the Middle East, and the U.S. crude oil benchmark is forecast to be at $88 a barrel six months from now, up from $82 in the September survey but below the current level of $101.

Despite the pause expected this week, 76% of respondents in the CNBC survey anticipate a rate hike at the Fed’s next meeting in December, and more than half expect the central bank to hike next year by an average of about two rate increases. The odds of a quarter-point rate hike in December are pegged at 75%, according to the FedWatch Tool. At least two Fed voters are expected to dissent from the decision to keep rates unchanged, the CNBC survey found. A plurality of 40% of respondents said the Fed must slow growth below potential to solve the inflation problem, while 27% said the Fed does not need to slow the economy and 23% said it needs to slow it only somewhat.

The economic picture is complicated by a weak jobs report: employers added just 29,000 jobs in September, a sharp slowdown from the 162,000 added in August and well below economists’ expectations. The unemployment rate is forecast to remain steady at 4.2% through the remainder of the year but rise to 4.4% in 2027. Respondents in the CNBC survey put the risk of a U.S. recession in the next year at 26%, down from 29% in the prior survey. The outlook for the S&P 500 stayed stable for this year at around 7,600 but moved down to 8,033 for next year from 8,274 in the prior survey. The index closed at 7,676 Monday.

President Donald Trump has repeatedly criticized the Fed for raising interest rates, including a social media post hours after last month’s hike that said: “LOWER THE INTEREST RATES FOR THE UNITED STATES OF AMERICA, AND FAST!” Fed Chair Kevin Warsh, whom Trump appointed, faces a host of challenges, including that some drivers of inflation — such as the rapid buildout of artificial intelligence infrastructure and higher energy prices — will not easily be tamed by higher interest rates. The congressional election is also seen as a market risk: 63% of CNBC survey respondents said there is some market risk from legal challenges that could extend uncertainty over which party controls Congress. More than half of respondents said the most likely outcome is Democratic control of both houses, up from 29% in the last survey.

The Fed’s rate decision may also draw attention to the challenge posed by inflation, a top issue among voters. In a memo to clients, Deutsche Bank Research said the upcoming midterms could factor into a potential decision to hold rates steady, though the election would not be a “primary consideration.” The memo added: “We could imagine that all else equal the Fed might prefer to stay out of the headlines for raising rates just ahead of a midterm election where affordability is the principal concern.” Fed Chair Warsh is expected to hold a news conference at 2:30 p.m. EDT on Wednesday to discuss the decision.
