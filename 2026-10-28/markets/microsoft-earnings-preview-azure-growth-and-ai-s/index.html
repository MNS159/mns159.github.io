Microsoft Q1 Earnings Preview: Azure Growth, AI Spending in Focus

Financial & Markets desk

Microsoft (MSFT) is set to report fiscal first-quarter earnings after the bell Wednesday, with Wall Street focused on growth in its Azure cloud business and the financial impact of its massive artificial-intelligence build-out. The report comes two days after the company announced it is offering buyouts to up to 60,000 eligible employees as part of cost-cutting efforts tied to its AI investments.

Analysts surveyed by Bloomberg expect earnings per share of $4.27 on revenue of $90.6 billion, up from $4.13 per share and $77.6 billion in revenue in the same period last year. The quarter is the first under Microsoft’s new segment structure, which replaces the previous three-segment model with two: Agents and Infra, and Devices and Consumer. Agents and Infra, which includes Azure, Microsoft 365 cloud services, productivity and server licensing, industry solutions, and frontier and support services, is expected to generate the most attention. Revenue for that segment is forecast to rise 22.5% to $75.6 billion, with Azure contributing $32.2 billion and Microsoft 365 adding $27.4 billion. Devices and Consumer, encompassing search and advertising, Xbox, and Windows OEM and devices, is projected to decline 6% to $15 billion.

Capital expenditures, including leases, are expected to top $45.9 billion in the quarter, more than double the $19.3 billion Microsoft spent in Q1 last year, underscoring the scale of its AI infrastructure push. Remaining performance obligations (RPO)—contracts Microsoft has booked but not yet recognized as revenue—are projected to reach $705.6 billion, up 77% from $389 billion a year earlier, signaling strong future demand.

Microsoft stock drifted lower in premarket trading Wednesday ahead of the report. The shares lagged for most of fiscal 2026 but rallied about 35% since July after the company’s fiscal fourth-quarter results. Investors will be looking for updates on Azure and Copilot growth as Microsoft continues to expand its AI services. The earnings release will also provide the first look at how the new segment structure affects financial reporting and investor analysis.
