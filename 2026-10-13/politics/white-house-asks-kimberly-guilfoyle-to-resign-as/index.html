White House asks Kimberly Guilfoyle to resign as ambassador to Greece amid misconduct allegations

Politics desk · anchored on Reuters
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum
✓ Facts
  • Kimberly Guilfoyle was appointed U.S. ambassador to Greece in December 2024, before President Trump's January 2025 inauguration, and was confirmed by the Senate in July 2025.
  • On Tuesday, new White House press secretary Katie Zacharia announced at her first press briefing that the Trump administration had asked Guilfoyle to resign.
  • Guilfoyle confirmed her departure in a lengthy X post the same day, calling her tenure 'the honor of a lifetime' while denying the allegations against her.
  • President Trump, speaking to reporters before departing for Alaska, said of Guilfoyle, 'She was doing a good job, but we didn't like what we were reading.'
  • The Wall Street Journal reported that Guilfoyle asked Trump donor Eric Deters to pay roughly a $100,000 credit card bill shortly before her July 2025 Senate confirmation hearing; Deters said he never sent the money.
  • Guilfoyle's attorney, Jesse Binnall, disputed the authenticity of the messages and said Deters owed her money for prior speaking and consulting work.
  • The State Department's Office of Inspector General reportedly opened an investigation into allegations involving Guilfoyle's treatment of embassy staff, private travel, gifts, and business relationships, according to the Daily Mail.
  • Sen. John Curtis (R-Utah) publicly commended the Trump administration's handling of the matter on social media after Guilfoyle's resignation.
○ Analysis

Kimberly Guilfoyle, a former Fox News host and the ex-fiancée of Donald Trump Jr., left her post as U.S. ambassador to Greece after a series of Wall Street Journal reports alleged financial impropriety, favoritism toward a Greek company, and mistreatment of embassy staff. The White House's decision to ask for her resignation—delivered by a new press secretary on her first day in the briefing room—marked an unusually swift and public break between the administration and a diplomat with close personal ties to the Trump family. Guilfoyle's exit came amid a reported State Department inspector general inquiry and shifting signals from senior officials: Secretary of State Marco Rubio initially defended her performance before declining to address the allegations directly, while Vice President JD Vance urged patience pending verified facts.

◐ Debate

Democratic

  • Sen. Jeanne Shaheen (D-N.H.) and Rep. Gregory Meeks (D-N.Y.) called for a review of Guilfoyle's conduct, with Meeks urging her removal if the allegations were substantiated.

Republican

  • Sen. John Curtis (R-Utah) praised the administration's 'willingness to act,' saying public service is a public trust and commending the handling of the allegations.
  • Vice President JD Vance said the facts should be established before judgment, while adding that if wrongdoing occurred, 'people have to suffer consequences for it.'
Steelman

Across party lines, the episode is being read less as a referendum on Guilfoyle's guilt and more as a test of how quickly and transparently the administration will act on credible allegations against its own appointees, regardless of personal ties.

Implications

Guilfoyle's departure comes amid reports of a State Department inspector general inquiry into her conduct, and further disclosures from that reported probe or additional reporting could shape both her legal defense and future congressional scrutiny of vetting standards for political appointees.
