Trump's Russia diesel deal draws criticism from Congress

Politics desk · anchored on AP
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts slightly center-left
✓ Facts
  • The Trump administration has allowed Russia to sell diesel fuel despite existing U.S. sanctions on Russian energy.
  • Trump posted on social media suggesting the U.S. would be a buyer of Russian diesel, then clarified that it was 'a world deal' and that Russia would not be selling diesel directly to the U.S.
  • A bipartisan group of senators, including Democratic Sen. Jeanne Shaheen of New Hampshire, formally objected to the administration lifting sanctions on Russian energy.
  • A 2022 law passed by Congress restricts Russian energy imports unless the administration first consults with and meets conditions set by Congress.
  • The Kremlin said Trump and Putin spoke by phone for about an hour, with Trump reportedly wanting to talk longer.
  • Trump has pointed to Ukrainian strikes on Russian energy infrastructure as a factor contributing to higher U.S. diesel and gas prices.
  • Trump told reporters the U.S. has not stopped sharing intelligence with Ukraine.
  • Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said in a post on X that Ukraine did not receive advance warning from partners before a Russian ballistic missile barrage on Kyiv.
○ Analysis

The dispute centers on whether the Trump administration's move to permit Russian diesel sales complies with a 2022 law that restricts Russian energy imports unless Congress is consulted and specific conditions are met. The deal also surfaces amid broader scrutiny of the administration's posture toward Moscow, including a reported hour-long Trump-Putin call, as lawmakers from both parties weigh whether loosened sanctions undercut pressure on Russia over its war in Ukraine.

◐ Debate

Democratic

  • Sen. Jeanne Shaheen (D-NH) says there is no reason to believe the diesel deal is authentic and that the administration bypassed the 2022 law requiring congressional consultation before resuming Russian energy imports.
  • Shaheen argues Trump is using the deal to deflect blame for high gas and diesel prices that she attributes to the war in Iran and broader cost-of-living pressures, rather than acknowledging his own policy choices.
  • Shaheen contends that lifting sanctions on Russian energy funds Russia's war in Ukraine and undermines pressure needed to bring Putin to serious negotiations, and says Democrats would act as a check on the administration if they win a Senate majority.

Republican

  • Republican lawmakers, alongside Democrats, are described by AP and WRAL as criticizing the diesel deal as fueling Russia's war against Ukraine, violating existing U.S. law, and drawing the U.S. closer to Russia, though no specific Republican official's comments on the deal are quoted in the source material.
Steelman

Beyond partisan framing, the episode raises a genuine institutional question: whether the executive branch followed the 2022 law's requirement to consult Congress before easing sanctions on Russian energy.

Implications

The outcome of the November midterms, which could shift Senate control, may determine how much legislative pushback the administration faces on Russia sanctions going forward.
