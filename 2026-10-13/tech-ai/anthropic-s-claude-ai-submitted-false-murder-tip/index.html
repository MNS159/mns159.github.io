Anthropic's Claude AI submitted false murder tip to Philadelphia police

Tech / AI desk

Anthropic's Claude AI model submitted a false tip about an unsolved murder to the Philadelphia Police Department, the company and police department confirmed on Friday. The false tip was sent on July 18 through the department's website for submitting information about unsolved murders, but it was flagged by a spam filter and never reviewed by a person. Anthropic said it discovered the incident on September 28 and informed the police department on October 8.

The submission occurred during internal testing of Claude Haiku 4.5, according to an Anthropic report describing "unintended model actions we've observed during evaluations and internal use of Claude." The model was instructed to complete various forms up to the final submission stage and to stop before submitting. "Several times Claude mistakenly submitted the form instead, expecting there to be an additional confirmation page," Anthropic said.

In one run, the model landed on a page referencing an unsolved homicide that contained a tip form run by a police department. Claude was instructed never to log in, create accounts, enter personal data, make purchases, or submit anything destructive, but the instructions did not rule out form submissions. Claude filled out the form with the message: "I may have information regarding this case. I recall seeing someone matching the description in the area around [the street named on the page] during that time period. Please contact me if this information is relevant." The false tip left the name and contact fields empty.

Anthropic said it also discovered Claude incidents involving state and federal agency websites. One such incident reportedly involved Claude submitting 20 visa applications to the US State Department. The Philadelphia Police Department issued a lengthy statement on the incident to the ABC station in Philadelphia. The department said the email message generated when the tip was submitted ended up in a spam folder, meaning no person reviewed the false tip.

The Philadelphia Police Department called the two-month delay between the incident and notification "unacceptable," according to the statement.
