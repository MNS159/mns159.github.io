Character.AI files for Chapter 11 bankruptcy, agrees to sell some assets to Disney for $20M

Tech / AI desk · anchored on Reuters

Character.AI, the five-year-old platform that lets users create and chat with AI characters, has filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy and agreed to sell some of its assets to Disney for $20 million in a court-supervised sale process. In a court document, the company said it had assets and liabilities of between $50 million and $100 million.

The $20 million deal covers some of Character.AI's assets and a non-exclusive perpetual license for its technology, with Disney providing transition services via former Character.AI employees who joined the entertainment giant last month alongside former CEO Karandeep Singh Anand. "Over the past year, Character.ai has taken meaningful steps to sustain and improve its business in the face of significant legal, regulatory, and operational headwinds," CEO Colin Adams said in a statement. "After a comprehensive review of the available alternatives, the board determined that this court-supervised sale process provides the Company with the best opportunity to maximize value for all stakeholders."

Adams said the company would continue operating during the sale process, which is expected to close in late November or early December, but noted that no bid for the company's assets or a buyer has yet emerged. "We hope that the sale process will yield an actionable proposal that will allow continued operations of the Character.ai platform. If such a bid is unavailable, however, the platform will be wound down," he wrote.

Founded in late 2021 by former Google engineers Noam Shazeer, a co-author of the landmark Transformer paper, and Daniel De Freitas, Character.AI initially focused on training foundation models and developing a platform for custom AI characters. The startup reached unicorn status in September 2022 with a $150 million fundraise, saying users had exchanged more than 2 billion messages with its bots in five months and averaged more than two hours on the platform daily. Its apps drew more than 1.7 million installs in their first week in May 2023. In August 2024, Google struck a $2.7 billion deal to license the startup's models, and both co-founders returned to the search giant; general counsel Dominic Perella stepped in as interim CEO as the company shifted away from training foundation models to focus on growth. By October 2024, the platform had 20 million monthly active users.

The company's fortunes turned after it was sued following the suicide of a 14-year-old Florida boy whose mother said he became obsessed with a chatbot on the platform, and after Texas launched an investigation into whether the platform complied with child privacy and safety laws. Character.AI subsequently settled some of the lawsuits, added more safety features, and banned minors from its platform. In June of last year, the company appointed Anand, a former Brex and Meta executive, as CEO and expanded into AI video generation and social feeds, later introducing interactive storytelling formats and microdramas. The bankruptcy filing shows those changes could not move the needle on sustainability.

The filing also underscores how difficult it is to scale and monetize consumer-facing AI products. Character.AI was one of the top apps in terms of time spent, according to analytics firm Sensor Tower, but high engagement is a double-edged sword: AI costs rise the more people use the app, increasing the pressure to monetize users. The startup raised more than $193 million in funding from investors including Lightspeed, SV Angel, Greycroft, and a16z, according to PitchBook.
