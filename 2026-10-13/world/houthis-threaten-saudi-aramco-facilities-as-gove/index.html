Houthis threaten Saudi Aramco facilities as government forces advance in Yemen

International & World desk · anchored on Reuters
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

Yemen’s Houthi group warned civilians and employees to evacuate Saudi Arabia’s Aramco oil facilities on Tuesday, calling them “a legitimate target,” after the Saudi-led coalition ordered an evacuation near a Yemeni oil station in the Houthi-controlled capital Sanaa. Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree said any strikes on Sanaa would be met with a “harsh and painful” response in Riyadh. Shortly after the coalition’s evacuation order, explosions were reported in Sanaa; Houthi-affiliated media said Saudi-backed air strikes hit an oil facility in the al-Sabaha area. Al Jazeera correspondent Yousef Mawry reported that Saudi Arabia also struck oil storage tanks at Ras Isa port and an oil station in Hodeidah province.

The Houthi threats follow a weekend of deadly attacks on Saudi Arabia. At least 14 people were killed and more than 300 wounded in separate Houthi missile strikes on Riyadh’s King Khalid International Airport, according to multiple sources. Another person was killed and 11 wounded on Sunday after a projectile struck the airport. Several international airlines, including Air France-KLM, Air India, Etihad, Lufthansa, Delta, Gulf Air and Qatar Airways, suspended flights to Saudi Arabia and neighboring countries. The Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted three drones north of Riyadh on Tuesday. Saudi Arabia’s cabinet, chaired by Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman, said the Houthis would face “consequences” for the attacks, according to the Saudi Press Agency.

On the ground in Yemen, government forces advanced as part of operation “Dawn of Yemen,” a counteroffensive launched just over a week ago after the Houthis seized strategic territory along the Red Sea coast in September, including the port city of Mocha and areas around the Bab al-Mandeb strait. Military spokesman Colonel Majid al-Nuzaili said operations had involved 1,879 precise strikes, neutralizing 950 Houthi fighters and destroying 380 military vehicles. Government troops captured strategic heights around the Maas camp in Marib’s Medghal district and positions north of the Harib district. In Taiz governorate, government forces conducted nine air strikes and reported capturing 81 Houthi fighters in al-Dhabab. Fierce clashes continued across multiple axes in Taiz, where the Houthis have the city under siege.

The escalation has caused heavy civilian casualties. The Yemeni Network for Rights and Freedom said the Houthis killed at least five civilians, including a five-year-old girl, and wounded 17 others in Taiz over 24 hours, firing more than 180 projectiles at residential areas. A Houthi attack struck al-Safwa Hospital, shattering windows and injuring at least one person. Yemen’s Ministry of Public Health said medicines and fuel at Taiz health facilities were “nearing depletion.” The International Committee of the Red Cross warned that civilians across the Middle East are paying an “unacceptable price.” At least 46 civilians have been killed in Yemen since August, and more than 200,000 people have been displaced since September, according to the United Nations and its migration agency.

The conflict, which began in 2014 when the Houthis seized Sanaa, has drawn in a Saudi-led coalition supporting Yemen’s internationally recognized government since 2015. A UN-brokered truce in 2022 largely held until renewed escalation in 2026. Analysts describe Saudi Arabia as facing an asymmetric struggle: the Houthis can impose high costs without defeating Saudi forces, and Riyadh must weigh its military options against economic security and regional alliances. German Chancellor Friedrich Merz told Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman that Iran must engage in serious negotiations, accusing Tehran of destabilizing the region. Egyptian Foreign Minister Badr Abdelatty urged his Iranian counterpart to halt Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia, though analysts say Iran holds limited sway over the group. The Houthis have also threatened a “swift and painful” response if the United States intervenes.
