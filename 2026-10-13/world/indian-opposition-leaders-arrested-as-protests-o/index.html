Indian opposition leaders arrested as protests over voter rolls intensify

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts slightly center-left

Leaders of India's Cockroach Janata Party (CJP) were detained early Tuesday after they entered a heavily-guarded protest site in New Delhi, as demonstrations demanding the resignation of the country's chief election commissioner over a controversial revision of electoral rolls continued to escalate. Party founder Abhijeet Dipke shared a video showing CJP co-conveners Ashutosh Ranka and Saurav Das being taken away by police from Jantar Mantar, a historic Delhi observatory long used for protests. Footage showed the two, along with others, climbing over police barricades before chanting slogans while holding the Indian flag. The detentions came a day after the federal home ministry announced that Jantar Mantar would no longer be a designated protest venue, instead identifying three alternative locations where demonstrations can be held with permission.

The protests, led by the CJP and joined by opposition parties, are demanding the resignation of Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar after more than 130 million names were reportedly removed from electoral rolls nationwide. Critics say the voter list revision is politically motivated and risks disenfranchising eligible voters, alleging it benefits Prime Minister Narendra Modi's Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP). The BJP and the Election Commission deny the allegations, saying the move is needed to clean up the rolls. Anger over the revision intensified after The Indian Express reported that Kumar had disregarded concerns raised by the two other election commissioners over changes to voter registration; the Election Commission has said its decisions were unanimous.

Over the weekend, authorities imposed extensive security restrictions across the capital, deploying thousands of police and paramilitary personnel, closing several metro stations, and disrupting train services. According to local media, at least 3,000 people were detained across the city. Police also detained CJP founder Abhijeet Dipke from the plane in which he landed in New Delhi on Saturday. Videos and photos from the protest show police dragging people, including women, by force and pushing them into buses. On Monday, opposition parties held a march from Connaught Place to the Supreme Court, and the Congress party, India's main opposition party, petitioned the court challenging the Election Commission's voter revision exercise.

The CJP began as a satirical online movement after India's Chief Justice Surya Kant referred to unemployed young people as 'cockroaches' during a court hearing in May. The group led weeks of protests earlier this year over exam irregularities and demands for education reforms, which culminated in the resignation of the federal education minister. Analysts say the government's response this time — including mass security deployments, preemptive detentions, and the removal of Jantar Mantar as a protest site — reflects lessons learned from the earlier protests. However, experts note that the current protests, focused on voter rolls, have not matched the cross-cutting appeal of the education issue, and they consider an abrupt change of government unlikely given India's strong state institutions.
