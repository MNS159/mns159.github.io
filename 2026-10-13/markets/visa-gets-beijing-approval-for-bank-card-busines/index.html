Visa receives Beijing approval to set up bank card clearing business in China

Financial & Markets desk

Visa announced Tuesday that it had received approval from the People's Bank of China (PBOC) to set up a bank card clearing business in the country, opening the door for the U.S. payments giant to serve mainland China's domestic payments market for the first time. "This marks an important milestone toward the company serving China's domestic payments market," Visa said in a statement.

Visa CEO Ryan McInerney said: "For more than 40 years, Visa has been an active contributor to China's dynamic payments landscape. We look forward to taking the necessary steps in the process, pursuant to the PBOC's regulations, to begin operating domestically in China." Visa previously said it applied with the PBOC in May 2020 to operate a bank card clearing institution.

The approval arrives as U.S. President Donald Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping plan their third meeting this year. McInerney joined Trump's business delegation to Beijing in May and became chair of the U.S.-China Business Council in June; he also led a trip to Beijing in early September. Trump told Fox News after his Beijing trip that he raised Visa's request for China market access, and after the September summit China said it would approve applications from all foreign financial institutions to operate in the country.

"The timing is notable," said George Chen, partner and chair of digital practice at The Asia Group. "Beijing chose to approve Visa's license just ahead of the APEC Leaders' Meeting in Shenzhen next month, where Xi and Trump are expected to meet again. The move suggests that Beijing is keen to demonstrate tangible progress for American businesses before Trump's next visit to China."

Mainland China has historically maintained tight control over its financial sector, favoring domestic players and restricting cross-border capital flows. Visa has worked with Chinese banks on dual-branded cards, for which China's UnionPay processes transactions domestically and Visa handles payments overseas; in January, Visa said mainland China-issued cards could be linked to Apple Pay for use overseas. Rivals have already entered the market: Mastercard's joint venture began processing payments in 2024 after receiving PBOC approval for clearing preparations in February 2020, while American Express was the first, obtaining its processing license in June 2020 after a PBOC nod in 2018.

Mobile payments dominate daily consumer transactions in China, led by Tencent's WeChat and Alibaba-affiliate Ant's Alipay. Visa said it would take the necessary steps under PBOC regulations to begin operating domestically in the country.
