South Korean stocks plunge 5.5%, stoking global chip fears

Financial & Markets desk

South Korean stocks plunged on Tuesday, with the KOSPI benchmark falling 5.5% to its lowest level in about three months and deepening a global selloff in chip stocks. The index now sits roughly 38% below its June all-time high. Chipmakers SK Hynix and Samsung Electronics, which together account for more than half of the South Korean market's capitalization, fell 10% and 7%, respectively.

The selloff was fueled by mounting worries over the artificial-intelligence trade. Broadcom CEO Hock Tan warned that speculative AI investing resembles gambling in a casino, according to the Financial Times, intensifying questions about whether infrastructure spending will generate sufficient returns. At the same time, Chinese memory maker CXMT announced progress on a new memory-cell design that packs more storage into less space, with plans for a server memory product by the end of 2026. The advance poses a competitive threat to Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron, which together control roughly 90% of the global DRAM market.

The weakness spilled into U.S. premarket trading, with Micron and SanDisk down around 3% and SK Hynix's U.S.-listed shares off 6% ahead of the opening bell. The iShares Semiconductor ETF fell more than 1% while major U.S. index benchmarks were roughly flat, with the S&P 500 down 0.1% and the Nasdaq 100 off 0.35% at 10:15 a.m. ET. Chip stocks have now sold off for five consecutive sessions, the longest streak since Liberation Day of last year, according to Jefferies.

Analysts linked the selling to the new competitive dynamic in memory chips. "This development suggests that supplies of DRAM chips may not be as tight as expected going forward. The news also provided investors with an excuse to cut their exposure to AI-adjacent companies," David Morrison, a senior market analyst at Trade Nation, wrote in a note. Economist David Rosenberg called the tech selling a symptom of "fragility" in the AI trade.

The stock slump contrasts with bitcoin's recent resilience. Bitcoin bottomed near $58,000 shortly after the KOSPI peaked and has recovered to as high as $87,000, a gain of roughly 40%. CoinDesk noted a moderately negative 20-week rolling correlation of -0.41 between bitcoin and the KOSPI, though it cautioned that correlation alone does not establish capital rotation. Separately, the memory-chip "Big Three" each reached $1 trillion valuations over the summer but have since pulled back; Micron remains up roughly 240% year to date, and Samsung last week reported a preliminary record third-quarter profit of $80 billion that did little to lift the sector.

Investors are now looking to Thursday's earnings from Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Co., widely viewed as the next major test of the AI investment thesis. In the meantime, U.S. markets are awaiting Wednesday's September consumer price index, while the 10-year Treasury yield eased 5 basis points to 5.19%. Bank stocks wavered after JPMorgan missed on revenue and earnings and Wells Fargo missed earnings expectations for the third quarter.
