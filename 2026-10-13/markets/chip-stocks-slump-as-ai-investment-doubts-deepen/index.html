Chip stocks slump as AI investment doubts deepen

Financial & Markets desk

Shares of semiconductor companies fell sharply on Tuesday, driven by a combination of AI investment skepticism and new competitive pressure from China. The tech-heavy Nasdaq dropped more than 100 points, or 0.35%, in early trading, while Asian markets saw steeper declines: South Korea’s Kospi index tumbled 6.25%, Japan’s Nikkei fell 1.8%, and the Taiwan exchange lost nearly 2%. Major chip stocks including Samsung Electronics (down 7.4%), SK Hynix (down 10%), Tokyo Electron (down 7.15%), and TSMC (down about 2%) all retreated. On Wall Street, Micron Technology slid 4.8%, Advanced Micro Devices dropped 1.4%, Nvidia lost 0.8%, and Broadcom slipped 0.2%.

The sell-off was fueled by two developments. First, Broadcom CEO Hock Tan likened many AI investors to casino gamblers, saying at a conference in Singapore that “a lot of investors have no clue what they are investing in” and that they “set very extreme expectations only to find out that it’s almost like they are in a casino.” Tan noted that AI companies such as OpenAI remain lossmaking despite $50 billion in annualized revenue at the end of September, adding that “it doesn’t tell anything at this point except that there is a lot of potential revenue growth.” He also warned that a sizeable portion of AI labs’ enterprise revenue comes from pilot projects, comparing it to a child spending tokens at a video arcade without learning anything.

Second, Chinese memory chip maker ChangXin Memory Technologies (CXMT) announced progress on a new “4F square” stacked DRAM architecture that promises to increase storage density. CXMT, which debuted on the Shanghai stock market in late July with a $487 billion market capitalization, plans to begin producing high-bandwidth memory (HBM) later this year. Investors fear the company could challenge dominant memory chip makers Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron. “The rout in chip stocks seems set to continue thanks once again to news from China,” said Chris Beauchamp, chief market analyst at IG. “CXMT’s new developments remind investors of the huge competition in the sector.”

The broader market showed mixed results. The Dow Jones industrial average rose nearly 0.4% in early trading, while the FTSE 100 in London hit a three-week high, gaining 0.73% to 10,762, supported by gains in financial stocks, retreating oil prices, and easing bond yields. Brent crude dropped 1.2% to $105.36 a barrel. AI shares have been volatile in recent months amid concerns that companies are spending heavily on AI infrastructure funded by debt.

Sandisk stock fell an additional 3.5% on Tuesday after shedding 6% on Monday, as CXMT’s advances renewed fears of competition in memory chips. However, analysts noted that CXMT’s chips primarily target the low-end mobile phone market rather than AI data centers, and CXMT is not yet producing NAND chips, which are Sandisk’s specialty. “I see greater risk to Micron stock than to Sandisk,” one analyst wrote. The broader concern remains that Chinese competition could pressure margins across the sector, even as memory supply remains in deficit and may tighten further next year.
