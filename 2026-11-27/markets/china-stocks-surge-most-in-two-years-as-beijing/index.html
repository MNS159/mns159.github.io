China Stocks Surge Most in Two Years as Beijing Vows Stronger Market Support

Financial & Markets desk · anchored on Reuters

Chinese stocks surged on Friday, with the CSI 300 index climbing 4% in its biggest jump since October 2024, after Beijing issued a rare midday statement pledging stronger state-backed market support. The CSI Semiconductor index of 40 chip stocks rose 17.6%, its largest single-day gain on record, while the STAR index jumped 14%, led by Chinese chipmakers such as CXMT, Cambricon and SMIC. The Hang Seng index rose 2%. Full-day trading volume across the Shanghai, Shenzhen and Beijing exchanges was 52% higher than the previous session.

The rally followed a morning meeting of the State Council, China's top governing body, chaired by Premier Li Qiang. In a readout published around noon local time by state media Xinhua News — an unusual timing, as such statements are typically released after trading hours — officials pledged to strengthen the role of the "National Team," referring to state-backed funds, to stabilize markets and ensure ample liquidity. The explicit reference to the "national team" was a first for a State Council meeting, said Bob Chen, a Shanghai-based venture capitalist and economist. Authorities also stressed that "capital markets are crucial to the broader economy," a rare comment from a government that has not always looked favorably on the financial sector.

Policymakers vowed to expand the role of "stabilization funds" such as Central Huijin Investment, a state-owned firm that holds equity stakes on behalf of the State Council. The message "effectively institutionalized Central Huijin's role as a state stabilization fund," said Hong Hao, managing partner of Lotus Asset Management, adding that he expects this rally to prove more durable than past "fleeting bounces" triggered by stimulus talks. The State Council also pledged better policies to steer medium- and long-term funds, such as insurance and social security funds, into equities, and encouraged listed companies to pay more dividends and step up share buybacks, while vowing a crackdown on financial fraud to protect investors.

Friday's surge came after the CSI 300 on Thursday sank to its lowest level since the escalation of the U.S.-China tariff war in spring 2025, according to Wind data. In recent months, investors have weighed the commercial feasibility of Chinese artificial intelligence developers and tighter U.S. restrictions on advanced chips. The U.S. unveiled a new rule last week to further restrict Chinese companies' access to advanced AI chips, including through overseas data centers, adding pressure on Beijing to accelerate its push for semiconductor self-sufficiency.

"Stock market performance is now prioritized at the highest level for the Chinese government," Chen said, which is boosting investor confidence. "That's very strong language from the top officials," said Zong Ke, a portfolio manager at a Shanghai-based quantitative fund. Ke said stock trading volumes surged rapidly after the readout was released around 1:00 p.m. local time, and that the national team will accelerate asset purchases. He expects more supportive policies to follow, such as further loosening institutional constraints that have limited long-term funds from investing in stocks. Ke added that Chinese AI-related stocks carry less downside risk than their U.S. counterparts, as the companies have spent less on AI so far and their shares have already been through a sharp correction, giving investors more confidence that the market may be nearing its bottom.
