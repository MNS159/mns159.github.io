Marc Marquez announces retirement from MotoGP; Aldeguer sees opportunity

Sports desk

Marc Marquez, the eight-time MotoGP world champion, announced his retirement from the sport on Friday, days after clinching a record-equalling eighth premier-class title. The 10-time world champion across all classes had been hampered by physical issues and double vision, which returned on two occasions after he re-injured his shoulder in Indonesia last season. His departure leaves the most coveted seat on the grid vacant — the one he was due to occupy alongside Pedro Acosta in 2027.

Fermin Aldeguer, one of the leading candidates to replace Marquez at the factory Ducati MotoGP team, said the retirement took him by surprise but that he had partly expected it. "At the end of the day, we see each other from time to time, we have a chat every now and then. And, obviously, you can tell he's struggled physically throughout the season," Aldeguer said. "I think next year was one of the biggest challenges he could have set himself, with a new bike, new tyres. For that, as I'd already discussed with him, you have to push the limits, you have to crash, you might even get injured, and I don't think he's at that stage anymore, of going through that. It's the best way to retire – as world champion."

Motorsport.com understands that Ducati will turn to one of the riders from its satellite teams to fill the void left by Marquez, and that Aldeguer is the top choice on the Borgo Panigale-based manufacturer's list. Asked whether Marquez's retirement weighed more heavily on him than the prospect of a move to the Ducati factory team, Aldeguer said: "Marc leaving opens the door for me to keep developing further and gives me the chance to join them. But this is a very self-centred world; I have to look out for myself, and if I have an opportunity to grow within the brand, I'd prefer that. Being able to battle with Marc over these past two years has been an honour."

Aldeguer finished eighth on Friday at the Valencia GP and said his target for the weekend is a top-five finish. "Judging by today's performance, we're not that far off the rest. In the end, we're a tenth and a half – not quite two – behind second place. And we're eighth. The gap to Marc is a bit bigger, but we've got his benchmark lap time. We're going to try to learn as much as we can, copy what he does, and then tomorrow, depending on our grid position, we can set a clear target in terms of results. But a top-five finish would already be good."
