Ukraine War

Zelensky criticizes Trump's 'strange' friendship with Putin, urges more US pressure on Russia

International & World desk

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky has voiced deep frustration with the United States, criticizing President Donald Trump for calling Russian President Vladimir Putin 'my friend' and saying delays in delivering vital anti-ballistic missiles were costing Ukrainian lives. In an interview with the BBC at his central Kyiv office, Zelensky complained that the US was not pushing Russia hard enough in peace talks and called it 'strange' that Trump posted a photo of himself embracing Putin on Truth Social. 'Of course it's up to the president of the United States, but... it's strange,' Zelensky said, speaking in English. 'He's an aggressor, and a killer... He can't be a friend. Just cannot be,' he added, comparing it to saying one was friends with Adolf Hitler during World War Two.

Zelensky called for 'real helpful allies, who can push Russia' to achieve results and urged the US to live up to its reputation as a strong country with 'power and respect.' 'When you try to be very neutral with the aggressor... it means you support victims and you support also aggressor. It's not right,' he said. The US argues that it is neutral in the complex search for a peace deal between the two sides that can hold. Questioned again on Thursday about calling Putin a friend, Trump told reporters: 'We want to stop killing. I could say he's not a friend... I want to make a deal.' He added: 'I could walk in and say, I don't like you, and then we have the same problem that we have with Zelensky and Putin. You know... the biggest problem to that settlement is the fact that you have two leaders that really hate each other.'

Since a bruising encounter with Trump in the Oval Office in 2025, Zelensky has been careful how he handles relations with the US. Trump's two envoys, Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff, have been trying to negotiate a peace deal between Russia and Ukraine, but they often seem more in tune with Moscow's position than that of Kyiv. Kushner, Trump's son-in-law, said on Wednesday that Putin had shown 'restraint' by not using nuclear weapons in Ukraine and described Putin as a 'grand master of politics.' The latest round of peace talks in Abu Dhabi earlier this month made no obvious progress: 'The more we know, the more problems,' Zelensky said. He said talks were expected to resume in December. On Thursday, Trump posted on Truth Social that at his own summit with Putin in China last week he had urged Russia to 'pull back all military aggression against territories throughout Europe' and said he believed a peace deal with Ukraine was 'a strong possibility.' But from its public statements, Russia appears to have regressed to its initial, hardline negotiating positions. Zelensky revealed that it was not clear whether European negotiators would be at the table, as the US envoys had announced.

Russian strikes across Ukraine have been growing more frequent and more deadly, with the number of civilians being killed at an all-time high. Zelensky said the military can now destroy about 70% of Russia's fastest drones, a statistic he said was improving. But there is a 'very, very big' deficit of interceptors to stop ballistic missiles, and he said the Americans were delaying deliveries of its Patriot systems. 'It's a pity they made a focus on Middle East and they help them more… It's a pity. We have huge questions with this,' Zelensky said. 'The deficit of these missiles - that is people's lives.' He also confirmed that the US satellite communications system, Starlink – and its military version, Starshield – were still being restricted in occupied areas of Ukraine. Asked whose decision that was, Zelensky replied: 'Technically SpaceX, politically - I don't know, I really don't know. It's like... ping pong. We are asking one side, they say it's the other.'

Ukraine's own long-range strikes against Russian targets have increased in recent weeks, including oil refineries and ships carrying weapons or fuel. On Thursday, Italy's Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni said an attack that sank a Russian warship docked in eastern Libya this week risked spreading war to the Mediterranean, which was unacceptable. Zelensky defended the move, calling the warship Merkury an 'absolutely fair goal.' He accepted that such strikes were 'very dangerous' but said Ukraine's forces 'never attack' without coordinating with its partners. He acknowledged that Ukraine was bracing for a difficult and dangerous winter. Recent attacks on Kyiv have targeted bridges over the Dnipro River, apparently trying to cut the city in two. Some residents have already left town for the winter and businesses closed, as power cuts and strikes on logistics networks have pushed operating costs unmanageably high. '[Russia] wants us to run away, of course,' Zelensky said. 'People can go, but we will try to defend our capital in any way. We need our capital. Without a capital, you don't have a country.'
