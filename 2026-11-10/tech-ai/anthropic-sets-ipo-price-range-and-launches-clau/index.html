Anthropic sets IPO price range, launches Claude Meetings office suite

Tech / AI desk

Anthropic on Tuesday set an initial public offering price range of $70 to $78 per share for 825 million Class A shares, implying a fully diluted valuation of up to $1.404 trillion. The offering is expected to raise between $57.75 billion and $64.35 billion, with net proceeds estimated to reach $84.6 billion to $93.7 billion if underwriters exercise an option to buy additional shares. The company plans to begin trading on the Nasdaq near November 19 under the ticker symbol “ANT,” with up to 20% of shares allocated to retail investors. The IPO comes despite volatile markets and a broader pullback from AI investing, and investors will watch Anthropic’s valuation as a gauge of the AI boom’s strength.

In a separate private sale, Anthropic agreed to sell $26 billion of non-voting Class N stock at the IPO price to AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia, and TSMC, with Google and Nvidia each investing $10 billion. The updated filing also revealed that co-founders CEO Dario Amodei, president Daniela Amodei, chief compute officer Tom Brown, Anthropic Institute head Jack Clark, chief science officer Jared Kaplan, chief architect Sam McCandlish, and interpretability research lead Christopher Olah are each selling nearly 3.4 million shares, which at the $74 midpoint of the price range would net roughly $250 million each. As a public benefit corporation, founders hold a Class F share with 50.1% voting power, and the Long-Term Benefit Trust will elect a board majority, limiting influence for Class A buyers.

The company reported run-rate revenue of $81 billion by the end of October, up about $10 billion from the end of September. Third-quarter revenue was $17 billion, and nine-month revenue reached $33.2 billion. Anthropic posted a nine-month net loss of $104 billion, with compute and infrastructure commitments of $900.9 billion, and holds nearly $132 billion in cash and short-term investments. The updated filing also adopted language from a Trump administration executive order, using “super intelligence” and “SI” interchangeably with “artificial intelligence” and “AI.” OpenAI, Anthropic’s biggest rival, was reportedly valued at $1.25 trillion last week after raising $11 billion.

Alongside the IPO news, Anthropic unveiled a new AI-powered office suite at a San Francisco event on Tuesday, targeting non-technical knowledge workers. The suite includes Claude Meetings, which joins video calls as a visible participant to draft plans, map timelines, answer questions, and access connected apps in real time, then provides meeting notes and action items afterward. Claude Whiteboards offers a collaborative real-time board where teams can brainstorm with Claude and turn results into documents or slide decks, and Claude Sheets lets teams create and edit spreadsheets with Claude handling setup and direct cell edits. Claude Meetings is available in beta on Team and Enterprise plans, initially supporting Google Meet, while Whiteboards and Sheets are in beta on all paid plans across web, desktop, and mobile. The company also made Claude Data (formerly Claude Dashboards) generally available to all paid plans, enabling users to query data and receive dashboards, written stories, or narrated videos.

Anthropic introduced interactive connectors that allow users to open full versions of apps from partners such as Adobe, Atlassian, and HubSpot inside Claude, embedding existing workflows into the same workspace. The company’s Model Context Protocol marketplace now includes more than 9,000 plugin options. Nicholas Lin, an Anthropic product lead, said Claude Code “changed how engineers work a year ago” and that the company now aims to close the adoption gap for non-developers, noting “there’s still a lot of friction when it comes to adopting AI for non-developers.” The tools follow similar moves by Google, OpenAI, and Meta as AI companies compete to build office suites that challenge Microsoft and Google. Anthropic faces pressure to generate revenue and drive engagement ahead of its IPO, and these products represent some of its first native offerings for non-technical workers.
