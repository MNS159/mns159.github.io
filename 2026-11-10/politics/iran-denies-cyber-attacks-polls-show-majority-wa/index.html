Iran denies cyber attacks; polls show majority want Congress to end war

Politics desk · anchored on Reuters
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum
✓ Facts
  • France's national cybersecurity agency ANSSI announced Monday that it has attributed a mid-October wave of cyberattacks on roughly 700 French retail sites — which raised refrigeration temperatures and spoiled food worth millions of euros — to the Iranian military hacking unit Shahid Kaveh, which France links to the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC).
  • Iran's Foreign Ministry rejected the allegations on Tuesday as 'baseless and malicious,' denying involvement.
  • The Paris Prosecutor's Office has opened an investigation and is requesting international arrest warrants for members of the Shahid Kaveh unit.
  • ANSSI also alleged the same campaign affected several small French hydroelectric plants and a major French blood bank's refrigeration system; similar refrigeration-system attacks hit retailers in Sweden and Ireland, a food plant in Israel, and U.S. military base commissaries over the summer, though the Pentagon has not officially attributed the U.S. incidents to any actor.
  • President Trump said Monday that further U.S. military strikes on Iran are 'highly unlikely,' describing Iran as 'militarily... effectively over,' after meeting with Qatar's prime minister to discuss ending the conflict.
  • Iran's Supreme National Security Council Secretary Mohsen Rezaei said Monday the U.S. is 'manufacturing fake news about imaginary talks' to end the war.
  • Republican Rep. Thomas Massie filed a House resolution Monday seeking to impeach Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth over his handling of the Iran war; Trump publicly backed Hegseth and called the effort a 'sham.'
○ Analysis

Two related but distinct threads are unfolding: a cybersecurity dispute over who sabotaged refrigeration systems across Europe, Israel and U.S. military bases, and a broader political reckoning over the ongoing U.S.-Iran war that began in February. On the cyber front, France has become the first government to formally attribute the refrigeration-system attacks to a specific Iranian military hacking unit, a claim Iran denies outright; cybersecurity experts note the pattern — testing tactics against Israeli targets before shifting to Western infrastructure — matches known IRGC-linked operations, though U.S. officials have stopped short of making a formal attribution for attacks on American bases. Separately, the war itself is entering a politically fraught phase: Trump is signaling a pause in offensive action and crediting diplomatic momentum with Qatar, while Iranian officials dispute that any real negotiations are underway. Domestically, Republican losses in the midterms have intensified scrutiny of the war's management, fueling an unusual intra-party clash over Defense Secretary Hegseth's future.

◐ Debate

Democratic

  • Democrats have signaled they would pursue impeachment proceedings against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth over the Iran war once they gain control of the House and Senate in the new Congress (per BBC).

Republican

  • Rep. Thomas Massie (R) requested a House vote to impeach Hegseth over the war's conduct (per BBC).
  • President Trump defended Hegseth against the impeachment push, calling Massie's effort a 'sham' (per BBC).
  • Vice President JD Vance and other senior Republicans attributed the GOP's midterm losses to the Iran war (per BBC).
Steelman

Both the cyberattack attribution dispute and the war's endgame hinge on the same underlying tension: official government claims (France's attribution of hackers, Trump's claims of diplomatic progress) are being met with direct denials from Iran, and in both cases independent, verifiable confirmation — whether from U.S. intelligence on the hacks or from neutral mediators on peace talks — remains incomplete.

Implications

Watch for whether the Paris Prosecutor's international arrest warrants prompt further diplomatic or security responses, whether the U.S. government follows France in formally attributing any of the cyberattacks on American targets, and how the Hegseth impeachment effort and Republican-Democrat dynamics evolve once the new Congress convenes in January, particularly if ceasefire or negotiation signals with Iran solidify or collapse.
