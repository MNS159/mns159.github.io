House pushes to impeach Hegseth as he defends Iran war actions

Politics desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum
✓ Facts
  • Rep. Thomas Massie (R-KY) reintroduced articles of impeachment against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, setting up a possible House vote within days.
  • The resolution lists eight charges: three for illegally waging war against Iran in violation of the War Powers Resolution, one for failing to minimize civilian casualties, one for overseeing 'extrajudicial killings' in strikes on alleged drug boats in the Caribbean and Eastern Pacific, one tied to efforts to punish Sen. Mark Kelly (D-AZ) over a video on refusing illegal orders, one for the capture of Venezuelan President Nicolás Maduro without congressional authorization, and one for illegally waging war against Yemen.
  • Hegseth called the articles a 'sham' and said, 'Didn't work against President Trump and it won't work with me.'
  • Trump told reporters, 'I'll stand by him... He won two wars. He was in charge. We all won.'
  • Impeachment and removal would require a House majority followed by a two-thirds vote (67 senators) in the Senate to convict.
  • Massie first tried to force a floor vote before the midterms, but Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) called an early recess, causing that resolution to expire; Massie reintroduced it afterward.
  • Hegseth campaigned for Ed Gallrein, who defeated Massie in the GOP primary.
  • Presumptive incoming House Speaker Hakeem Jeffries (D-NY) has signaled support for the impeachment push.
○ Analysis

The impeachment push centers on a decades-old, largely unresolved dispute over the War Powers Resolution, which requires presidential administrations to seek congressional authorization for military action but which presidents of both parties have routinely sidestepped. Massie's resolution attempts to use Hegseth's handling of strikes on Iran, Yemen, and alleged drug boats, along with the Maduro operation, as a test case for enforcing those limits. Because an outright Senate conviction requires 67 votes — a high bar even under divided government — the practical significance of the House vote is more symbolic and political than an imminent threat of removal, serving instead as a marker of how many Republicans are willing to break with the administration over war powers.

◐ Debate

Democratic

  • Presumptive incoming House Speaker Hakeem Jeffries (D-NY) has signaled support for impeaching Hegseth.

Republican

  • Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth calls the impeachment articles a 'sham,' saying efforts against Trump failed and 'it won't work with me.'
  • President Trump says he will 'stand by' Hegseth, crediting him with having 'won two wars.'
  • Rep. Warren Davidson (R-OH) argues impeachment would be hypocritical since past administrations of both parties have ignored war powers limits, calling it 'scapegoating' Hegseth and favoring using the power of the purse instead.
  • Rep. Thomas Massie (R-KY), who filed the articles, argues Congress must hold the administration accountable for violating war powers and frames impeachment as simply 'firing someone.'
Steelman

The split is less partisan than ideological: a Republican author (Massie) is leading the impeachment push against a Republican defense secretary over war-powers enforcement, while other Republicans defend Hegseth and some Democrats back removal — showing the fight is really about executive war authority, not a clean party-line battle.

Implications

A House vote appears likely to proceed soon, and with Democratic support plus a handful of Republicans, passage in the House is plausible; however, conviction in the Senate remains a high bar requiring two-thirds support, making Hegseth's removal unlikely in the near term even if the House impeaches him.
