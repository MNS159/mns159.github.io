FDA approves Bayer's asundexian, first factor XIa inhibitor, for stroke prevention

Health & Science desk · anchored on Reuters

The U.S. Food and Drug Administration on Monday approved Bayer's asundexian, a once-daily oral pill to be sold under the brand name Sunharbo, for reducing the risk of recurrent stroke in certain adults. The drug is the first approved in the novel class of factor XIa inhibitors, which are designed to prevent blood clots without increasing the risk of major bleeding, a limitation of many existing blood thinners.

The FDA cleared Sunharbo for use in combination with antiplatelet therapy in adults who have had a recent mild-to-moderate non-cardioembolic ischemic stroke—a stroke caused by a clot that did not originate in the heart—or a high-risk transient ischemic attack (TIA). The approval is based on the phase 3 OCEANIC-STROKE trial, which enrolled 12,327 adults. Over a median treatment period of 14.2 months, 6.2% of patients receiving Sunharbo experienced a recurrent ischemic stroke, compared with 8.4% of those on placebo, representing a 26% relative risk reduction. The rate of major bleeding was similar between groups: 1.9% for Sunharbo versus 1.7% for placebo. Diarrhea was the most common adverse reaction, occurring in at least 3% of patients. The FDA does not recommend Sunharbo with combined P-gp and strong CYP3A4 inducers, such as carbamazepine, or in patients with moderate or severe hepatic impairment, and advises withholding the drug at least 24 hours before elective surgery or invasive procedures.

The approval marks a significant advance in stroke prevention, a field that has seen little innovation in the past two decades. Stroke is the fourth-leading cause of death and a leading cause of disability in the United States, with about 795,000 Americans experiencing a stroke each year. Roughly 1 in 10 ischemic stroke survivors will have another stroke within a year, and 1 in 5 within five years. "Stroke remains a very significant health burden in the United States, and the need for continued innovation is abundantly clear," said Sebastian Guth, chief operating officer of Bayer Pharmaceuticals and president of Bayer U.S. Hylton V. Joffe, director of the Office of Cardiology, Hematology, Endocrinology, and Nephrology in the FDA's Center for Drug Evaluation and Research, said in a statement: "Stroke is a leading cause of disability and death in the United States, and survivors are at risk of having another stroke."

Asundexian works by inhibiting factor XIa, a protein involved in the coagulation cascade, with the goal of preventing the formation of harmful clots while interfering as little as possible with the body's natural repair mechanisms. Christoph Koenen, M.D., Bayer's chief medical officer, explained that genetic evidence—showing that people with naturally lower levels of factor XI have fewer cardiovascular events—supported the target. The drug had previously received its first worldwide approval in China in late October. Bayer's approval comes as the company faces generic competition to its established anticoagulant Xarelto and its eye treatment Eylea. A rival factor XIa inhibitor, milvexian, being developed by Bristol Myers Squibb and Johnson & Johnson, is in phase 3 trials for secondary stroke prevention. Bayer said it is ready for a U.S. launch at scale, with an established commercial and medical team. A 30-day supply of Sunharbo is listed at $748.50.
