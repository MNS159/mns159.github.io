Fed's Waller signals December rate hike, opens door to half-point increase; Logan calls for two quarter-point moves

Financial & Markets desk · anchored on Reuters

Federal Reserve Governor Christopher Waller said Tuesday he is leaning toward a rate increase in December and would support a larger-than-usual half-percentage-point hike if inflation readings come in higher than forecast, warning that failing to follow through on expected tightening would risk the central bank's credibility. Speaking in London, Waller noted that the October consumer price index, released earlier Tuesday, showed headline inflation of 0.4% and core inflation of 0.3%—both 0.1 percentage point above the Dow Jones consensus. "Hiking only 25 basis points would put our credibility at risk, a risk I am not eager to take," Waller said, adding that a 50-basis-point hike "should be considered at the December meeting" if data come in stronger.

Waller emphasized that the precise timing of rate increases matters less than ensuring promised hikes are eventually carried out. "When this communication is clear and credible, what really matters is that those hikes are eventually carried out—it is not necessary, for example, to raise rates at consecutive meetings," he said. "If, however, the hikes are not carried out, markets will reduce expectations of rate hikes and rates will immediately fall, which would loosen monetary policy in an undesirable way." He characterized the economy as growing at a healthy pace with a solid labor market. Traders after the CPI report fully priced in a quarter-point hike in December and assigned 12.5% odds of a half-point move.

Separately, Dallas Federal Reserve President Lorie Logan said the central bank needs to deliver at least two more quarter-percentage-point rate increases to bring down inflation, and may need to move more rapidly this winter. "It remains clear to me, given the current outlook, that the FOMC needs to implement at least two more 25-basis-point rate increases," Logan said in remarks prepared for an energy conference. She noted that October core consumer prices rose 0.3%, a pace that if sustained would translate to year-over-year inflation above 4%, well above the Fed's 2% target. "Excess inflation is unacceptable," she said, adding that "tighter policy will be necessary to appropriately balance the outlook and risks for inflation and employment."

Logan supported the Fed's decision to skip a rate hike in October, saying gradual increases allow households, businesses, and markets to adapt and reduce the risk of slowing the economy more than necessary. However, she warned that the longer above-target inflation persists, the greater the risk it becomes baked into behavior. "The exact pace of increases could depend on the near-term economic and financial environment," she said. "It may be possible to move more rapidly this winter than the FOMC has moved so far this fall." The Fed's next policy meeting is scheduled for December 8-9.

The remarks from Waller and Logan come after the Federal Open Market Committee held rates steady at its October meeting, a decision that was not unanimous—Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack dissented. Multiple Fed officials have recently counseled patience while stressing the need to bring inflation back to target. Chicago Fed President Austan Goolsbee said Monday the Fed must be careful not to let the economy run too hot.
