Bengals trade with Eagles fails hours before deadline

Sports desk

The Cincinnati Bengals' proposed trade to acquire Philadelphia Eagles outside linebacker Arnold Ebiketie fell through hours before Tuesday's NFL trade deadline, according to NFL insider Jordan Schultz. The Bengals had agreed to send a 2027 sixth-round pick to the Eagles for the 27-year-old defender, but the deal collapsed for reasons unrelated to a physical or performance issue.

Ebiketie signed a one-year, $7.3 million contract with Philadelphia in March after spending his first four seasons with the Atlanta Falcons. In nine appearances this season, he recorded 25 tackles and two quarterback hits, and he has 16.5 career sacks. The trade would have added another pass rusher to Cincinnati's defensive rotation as the team looked to strengthen its linebacker corps.

The failed transaction carries significant implications for the Bengals' draft plans. Because Philadelphia originally signed Ebiketie as a compensatory free agent, acquiring him would have counted against Cincinnati's compensatory pick formula. The Bengals lost three qualifying compensatory free agents this offseason—Trey Hendrickson, Joseph Ossai and Noah Fant—while signing two qualifying players, Bryan Cook and Boye Mafe. Cordell Volson does not qualify.

Avoiding the deal preserves Cincinnati's projected third-round compensatory selection tied to Hendrickson's $28 million annual contract with the Baltimore Ravens. The Bengals entered the deadline exploring multiple options to bolster their defense but ultimately will not add Ebiketie to their roster.
