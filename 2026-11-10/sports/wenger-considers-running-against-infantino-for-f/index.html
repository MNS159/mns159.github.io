Wenger considers running against Infantino for FIFA presidency

Sports desk

Former Arsenal manager Arsène Wenger is considering standing against Gianni Infantino as a candidate in FIFA's presidential election, according to sources close to the Frenchman. Wenger, 77, currently serves as FIFA's head of global development and has been urged by senior figures in European football to run. The deadline for candidates to confirm their candidacy for the election, scheduled for March, is 18 November.

Wenger's potential candidacy comes amid a crisis for Infantino, triggered by the backlash against his failed plan to sell commercial stakes in the World Cup to private investors. The proposal, known as the FIFA Forward Enterprise (FFE), was withdrawn after widespread opposition. Wenger distanced himself from the plan, stating, 'I was not involved in this strategic plan and first became aware of the project through media reports. The decision to withdraw the project was absolutely necessary and beyond question, because I firmly believe in an independent FIFA that serves our game with commitment, transparency and integrity.'

Infantino retains support in South America, Africa, and Asia, and recently insisted he has the backing of the 'vast, vast majority' of world football. He received a major re-election boost when all 54 African member associations backed him after he promised an increase in funding. However, Wenger has a global profile, including in Africa, where he is known as a champion of African players during his time at Arsenal. His support for an expanded 48-team World Cup to drive development beyond traditional powers has been well received on the continent.

If Wenger stands, he would have the backing of UEFA, which has spearheaded opposition to Infantino. Some of his supporters have told BBC Sport that they would be happy for him to win the election and then stand down before serving a full four-year term. Sources close to Wenger say he is yet to decide whether to stand, and they still believe Infantino is favourite to win a fourth and final term.

Other candidates have already entered the race. Former Tottenham defender Ramon Vega was the first to put his name forward in October. Japan Football Association honorary president Kozo Tashima has also announced his intention to stand. Meanwhile, FIFA has said that 'key documents' relating to the World Cup stake sale plan will be made available to members of its council, but they must travel to its Zurich base and access will be under 'strict protocols'. UEFA gave FIFA until 2 November to release details about the FFE proposal and answer questions about its decision-making, following a council meeting at which Infantino apologised for the abandoned plot but failed to explain how it was devised.
