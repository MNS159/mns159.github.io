Bears acquire All-Pro DT Jeffery Simmons in blockbuster trade with Titans

Sports desk

The Chicago Bears acquired four-time Pro Bowl defensive tackle Jeffery Simmons from the Tennessee Titans, sending first-round picks in 2027 and 2028 along with a 2027 fourth-round pick to Tennessee in exchange for Simmons and a 2028 fifth-round pick, according to multiple reports. The deal marks the biggest pre-deadline trade this season and significantly alters the trajectory of both franchises.

Simmons, 29, is coming off a first-team All-Pro season in 2025 and signed a three-year, $105.8 million extension with the Titans this offseason. He has played 86.7% of Tennessee's defensive snaps this season (425 of 490), ranking among the highest usage rates for an interior lineman. Known as a high-motor pass rusher and elite run stopper who draws frequent double teams, Simmons recorded seven pressures in a Week 6 game against the Colts despite facing multiple blockers. He instantly becomes the Bears' best defensive player, according to Yahoo Sports, and is expected to start immediately alongside Grady Jarrett on the interior.

The Bears have been aggressive in upgrading their talent ahead of the deadline after previously expressing interest in Colts defensive tackle DeForest Buckner before he was traded to San Francisco. Chicago's defense ranks eighth in yards allowed and 10th in points allowed but is near the bottom in several per-play metrics, buoyed by an offense that leads the league in time of possession (35:30 per game). The team faces a brutal remaining schedule, including two games against the Lions and matchups with the Jaguars and Bills. The addition of Simmons, combined with the return of pass rusher Montez Sweat from suspension and the eventual returns of Shemar Turner and Dayo Odeyingbo, could transform a below-average defensive line into a strength.

For the Titans, the trade continues a full-scale rebuild under first-year general manager Mike Borgonzi. Tennessee is 1-6 and holds the No. 2 overall pick in the upcoming draft. By acquiring two future first-rounders along with a fourth-round pick, the Titans now have six selections in the first four rounds of the 2027 draft and significant flexibility for 2028. Yahoo Sports notes the deal gives the Titans protection if quarterback Cam Ward, the No. 1 overall pick in 2025, does not develop as hoped. Borgonzi likely had multiple bidders for Simmons and was able to extract a premium price, including a fourth-round pick in 2027 for a 2028 fifth-round sweetener.

History offers mixed results for teams that stockpile multiple first-round picks, as CBS Sports notes. The Chiefs (2022), Eagles (2023) and Seahawks (2023) all used multiple first-round selections to build Super Bowl winners, while the Jets used three first-rounders in 2022 on Sauce Gardner, Garrett Wilson and Jermaine Johnson—each earning individual accolades—yet never made the playoffs and saw two of those players traded within four years. The Browns similarly had five first-round picks in 2017 and 2018, won one playoff game, but failed to sustain success. The Titans will need to pair their draft capital with sound quarterback play and roster construction to avoid a similar fate.

Yahoo Sports graded the trade a B for the Bears, noting the steep cost of two first-round picks but that the move could prove smart if Chicago makes a Super Bowl run. The Titans received an A-minus, reflecting strong value for an elite player on a team facing a multi-year rebuild. Simmons is under contract for 3.5 more seasons after signing his extension, giving the Bears a premier interior presence in his prime as they push for a championship.
