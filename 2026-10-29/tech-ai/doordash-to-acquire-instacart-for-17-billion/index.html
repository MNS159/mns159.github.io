DoorDash to acquire Instacart for $17 billion

Tech / AI desk

DoorDash announced Thursday it has agreed to acquire Instacart for approximately $17 billion, combining two major players in on-demand delivery. The deal, valued at $65 per Instacart share, has been approved by both companies’ boards and is expected to close near the end of 2027 or in the first half of 2028, subject to regulatory approvals.

The acquisition will give DoorDash a larger foothold in online grocery delivery by adding Instacart’s established grocery marketplace and retailer partnerships. “Grocery is one of the hardest things to get right online,” said DoorDash CEO Tony Xu in a statement. “Someone has to pick a ripe avocado, find a substitute you’d choose yourself, and get the ice cream to you before it melts. Instacart has spent years learning to do that well for grocers, while DoorDash has built a delivery network and technology that local stores rely on every day. Together, we could give stores of every size more of those tools and unlock the potential of grocery delivery.”

Instacart CEO Chris Rogers said the deal will “make online grocery more seamless and affordable” for millions of households, and that “joining forces with DoorDash allows us to do even more.” DoorDash plans to preserve Instacart’s brand, enterprise technology products, and app, meaning the two services will remain separate rather than merging into a single platform. The company said local grocers will not face platform exclusivity requirements, and Instacart shoppers will retain flexibility over their hours and orders, with the option to accept DoorDash orders or continue only with Instacart.

The deal comes amid increasing competition in the delivery market. DoorDash, which began as a restaurant courier, already operates its own grocery delivery business but faces rivals including Amazon, Walmart, and Uber. Uber has expanded into food delivery through acquisitions of Postmates and Delivery Hero. Rumors of a potential Instacart sale surfaced last week, with reports that Instacart had been in talks with Uber before discussions ended. By Monday, The Wall Street Journal reported DoorDash was in “advanced talks,” and the deal was confirmed Thursday.

DoorDash said the combination will give Instacart users access to more than 50,000 additional stores and allow DashPass and Instacart Plus members to pay a single membership fee instead of two. The company also noted that the deal could help grocers build their own online ordering and advertising businesses using Instacart’s technology, while taking advantage of DoorDash’s fulfillment services to offer lower-cost delivery. TechCrunch reported that Instacart, which has a market cap of more than $13 billion and a stock price up 25% year to date, may have viewed this as an opportune time to sell ahead of intensifying competition.

The acquisition is subject to regulatory review and other closing conditions. DoorDash said that until the transaction closes, the two companies will continue to operate independently. Both CEOs expressed optimism that the combined company can better serve local stores and consumers in the growing online grocery market.
