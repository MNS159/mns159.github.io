Apple launches touchscreen MacBook Pro with OLED display starting at $2,899

Tech / AI desk

Apple today announced a touchscreen MacBook Pro with an OLED display, marking the company's first major redesign of the laptop in more than five years and ending a long-standing resistance to touch input on macOS. The new MacBook Pro is available in 14-inch and 16-inch sizes, powered by M5 Pro or M5 Max chips, with up to 128GB of unified memory and up to 16TB of storage. Preorders open October 30, with availability beginning November 6.

The display, branded as PROLED XDR, achieves 2,000 nits peak brightness for HDR and SDR, a 10,000,000:1 contrast ratio, and retains a 120 Hz variable refresh rate. Apple redesigned the hinge to stabilize the screen and reduce wobble, though demos showed some movement remains. The 14-inch model weighs just 3 pounds, about half a pound lighter than its predecessor. A Dynamic Island cutout replaces the previous notch, and the Apple logo on the back now glows again.

Pricing starts at $2,899 for the base configuration, nearly $1,000 more than the regular MacBook Pro. The fully loaded 16-inch model reaches $14,549, up $4,400 from the previous generation's top-tier price of $10,149. Upgrades include a 16TB SSD for an additional $7,000, 128GB of memory for $2,000, and nano-texture glass for $150.

Apple had long resisted a touchscreen Mac. In 2010, Steve Jobs called such laptops “ergonomically terrible.” Tim Cook later described hybrid designs as a compromise, saying “you can converge a toaster and a refrigerator, but those things are probably not going to be pleasing to the user.” Craig Federighi in 2014 called the Mac a “sit-down experience” and said a touchscreen was “not the right interface.” However, Apple marketing chief Greg Joswiak acknowledged the shift, telling Wired: “There’s a generation of young creators and young people using our MacBook Pros that expect to touch.” Apple CEO John Ternus added, “You’ve gotta touch it to believe it.”

Apple has adapted macOS for touch, redesigning the menu bar to allow easier selection and enlarging window-control buttons when touched. The Dynamic Island can be tapped for quick controls, and gestures such as swiping between spaces, accessing Mission Control, and pinch-to-zoom are supported. The company is relying on developers to support touch in their apps, though basic functions like scrolling and zooming work out of the box. This approach contrasts with the Touch Bar, a touchscreen strip introduced in 2016 that failed due to inconsistent app support and was removed in 2023.

The launch validates years of effort by Microsoft to bring touch to laptops, with Windows incorporating multitouch support since Windows 7 and the Surface Pro line demonstrating the utility of touch on a PC. Apple’s decision raises questions about future convergence of macOS, iOS, and iPadOS. The new MacBook Pro does not feature a convertible design; users reach up to touch the screen in a traditional clamshell configuration.
