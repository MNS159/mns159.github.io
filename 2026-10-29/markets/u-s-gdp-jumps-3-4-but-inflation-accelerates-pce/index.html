U.S. GDP grows 3.4% in Q3, inflation accelerates with PCE at 3.6%

Financial & Markets desk

The U.S. economy expanded at a faster-than-expected annualized pace of 3.4% in the third quarter, the Commerce Department reported Thursday, while the Federal Reserve’s preferred inflation gauge accelerated in September, signaling that the central bank’s fight against rising prices is far from over.

Gross domestic product grew at a seasonally adjusted annual rate of 3.4%, beating the 3.1% estimate and accelerating from 2.2% in the second quarter. The personal consumption expenditures (PCE) price index rose 0.5% in September from August, the largest monthly increase since March, pushing the annual inflation rate to 3.6%. Excluding food and energy, core PCE rose 0.3% month over month and 3.1% year over year. Both the headline and core monthly readings were in line with consensus estimates.

The data come one day after the Federal Reserve voted to hold its benchmark interest rate in a range of 3.75%–4%. Fed Chair Kevin Warsh said Wednesday that policymakers are not on a preset path, noting, “If we had a firm conclusion about the conduct of monetary policy, we'd announce it to much fanfare today.” However, Thursday’s reports show inflation remains well above the Fed’s 2% target, and markets are now pricing in an 83% chance of a quarter-point rate hike in December, according to the CME FedWatch tool.

September’s PCE increase was driven largely by a 9.2% jump in gasoline and other energy goods, with energy goods and services broadly rising 5.4%. Services prices excluding energy and housing rose 0.4%, and housing costs increased 0.3%. Despite the inflation pressure, consumer spending remained resilient, climbing 0.5% in September — slightly below the 0.6% forecast — while personal income rose 0.2%, missing the 0.3% estimate.

Third-quarter growth was supported by robust consumer spending, which rose 3.8%, and a surge in investment. Gross private domestic investment soared 19.4%, led by a 20.9% increase in equipment spending and an 8.4% rise in intellectual property products. A separate Labor Department report showed initial jobless claims fell to 194,000 for the week ended October 24, below the 200,000 estimate and near late-1960s lows, indicating a still-tight labor market.

Following the releases, stock market futures turned negative and Treasury yields fell. The 10-year yield hovered near 4.97%, while the 2-year yield traded at 4.635%. “Net, net, economic growth is powering ahead despite the uncertainty of geopolitical risks and the damage posed to many sectors of the economy by higher energy costs,” said Chris Rupkey, chief economist at Fwdbonds. Stephen Coltman, head of macro at 21shares, noted that Fed Chair Warsh sees the economy as strong and inflation above target, and that today’s data will not change that assessment, meaning investors should expect the Fed funds rate to continue to rise.
