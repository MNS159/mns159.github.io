Alphabet plunges 13%, heads for record $500B value loss on AI-driven miss

Financial & Markets desk

Alphabet shares plunged about 13% in early trading Thursday, putting the Google parent on track for its worst single-day loss since going public in 2004 and threatening to erase nearly $500 billion in market value. The sell-off follows a third-quarter earnings report that missed analyst estimates on search revenue and revealed a deeper-than-expected swing to negative free cash flow, raising fresh concerns that artificial intelligence services are beginning to cannibalize Google’s core search business.

Alphabet reported earnings per share of $2.12 on revenue of $127.58 billion, compared with analyst expectations of $3.01 and $127.93 billion, respectively. Revenue excluding traffic acquisition costs came in at $111.21 billion, slightly below the $111.29 billion estimate. Search and other advertising revenue grew 12% year over year to about $63 billion, falling short of the roughly $65 billion Wall Street had forecast. Google Cloud revenue more than doubled to about $31 billion, exceeding the $29.1 billion estimate, and Alphabet said its Gemini app passed 1 billion users. However, free cash flow swung to a loss of about $9.07 billion in the quarter, three times the $2.70 billion deficit analysts had anticipated, as spending on AI data centers and infrastructure climbed.

The gap between search query growth—which Alphabet said was the strongest in five years—and decelerating search revenue has sharpened investor anxiety. In the first quarter, search revenue grew 19%; it slid to 17% in the second quarter and then to 12% in the third. “The bears will argue this is the first quarter that AI is starting to seriously hurt their search business,” said Jeffrey Wlodarczak, CEO of Pivotal Research. Alphabet’s chief business officer, Philipp Schindler, attributed part of the miss to lower monetization within the company’s AI Mode and said Google is scaling ads in AI Overviews and AI Mode products internationally. Despite the pullback, CFRA analyst Angelo Zino called the decline “probably a buying opportunity.”

Alphabet maintained its full-year capital expenditure guidance of between $195 billion and $205 billion and said spending will increase significantly in 2027, as it invests in tensor processing units and data centers to meet cloud and AI demand. The cloud backlog reached roughly $560 billion, reflecting contracted demand for AI infrastructure, with management expecting just over half to be recognized over the next 24 months. CFO Anat Ashkenazi noted that cash is going out to build inventory and will come in as systems are delivered. The company’s stakes in AI labs also contributed to the free-cash-flow shortfall.

The losses underscore the market’s heightened sensitivity to AI spending and search trends. Alphabet’s previous worst one-day market-value decline was about $275 billion on July 23; the $500 billion drop would be the second largest ever for a U.S.-listed company, trailing only Nvidia’s $593 billion loss on Jan. 27, 2025, after China’s DeepSeek rattled AI investors. US-listed stocks have shed $200 billion or more in a single session 18 times so far this year, up from 13 in all of 2025 and none before 2022. Alphabet alone has lost about $1.4 trillion since its record in May.

Microsoft, which reported earnings Wednesday, saw its shares trade largely flat as its free cash flow remained positive at $18.63 billion despite a 27% decline, and capital expenditures of $45.2 billion came in below analyst projections. “Alphabet, by contrast, saw its share price hit after hours as a result of a fairly messy set of results and a poorly handled earnings call,” said Ben Barringer of Quilter Cheviot. Investors now await Amazon’s quarterly report after Thursday’s close; faster growth at Amazon Web Services without a hit to cash flow would point to a problem specific to Google’s search business, while another quarter of cash burn would keep pressure on the broader AI spending narrative.
