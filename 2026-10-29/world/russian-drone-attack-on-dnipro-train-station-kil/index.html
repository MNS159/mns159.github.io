Russian drone attack on Dnipro train station kills at least 14

International & World desk · anchored on Reuters

At least 14 people were killed and 14 others wounded, including an 11-year-old boy, when a Russian jet-powered drone struck the main railway station in the central Ukrainian city of Dnipro on Thursday, local officials said. The attack hit a crowded platform where a passenger train headed for Zaporizhzhia and a commuter train were standing, according to Ukrzaliznytsia, Ukraine's state railway operator. Dnipropetrovsk regional head Oleksandr Hanzha confirmed the death toll and injuries.

Ukrzaliznytsia head Oleksandr Pertsovsky said an elevated threat level had been announced ahead of the strike, 'but unfortunately there were still a large number of people on the platform' when the drone hit. Dnipro Mayor Borys Filatov called the incident a tragedy, adding that a drone hit a civilian electric train. Photographs from rescue services showed a large hole in the platform near a blast-damaged train with smashed windows.

The Dnipro attack came as other Russian strikes hit Ukraine on Thursday. In the northern city of Chernihiv, two 15-year-old girls were killed and at least 25 others, including four children, injured in a missile strike, according to regional head Vyacheslav Chaus. In Kremenchuk, Poltava region, three people were killed and 28 injured in a drone attack that hit residential buildings and a school, regional head Vitaliy Diakivnych said. Chernihiv announced a day of mourning for Friday.

The attacks occurred during a fragile local ceasefire in the Russian-occupied city of Oleshky, brokered by U.S. first lady Melania Trump. Ukraine's human rights ombudsman Dmytro Lubinets said the truce was holding into its third day and that he had set out with a Ukrainian delegation toward Oleshky via Belarus and Russia. The ceasefire is set to last until 10:59 p.m. local time on November 1. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky condemned the Dnipro strike as 'horrific' and called for tougher sanctions from the U.S., EU, and G7, criticizing a recent deal between Donald Trump and Vladimir Putin that eased sanctions on Russian diesel imports. Ukrainian sanctions commissioner Vladyslav Vlasiuk told CNN: 'You cannot lift sanctions and then expect the Russians to take any action. Constructive actions must come first, and then everything else.'

Meanwhile, the Kremlin condemned Ukrainian drone attacks on Moscow during the Russia-Africa Summit, according to Tass. Moscow's mayor said 613 Ukrainian drones were launched overnight toward the region, most of which were shot down. Russia's defense ministry has denied deliberately targeting civilians, saying its strikes target infrastructure used to transport military personnel and equipment. However, data from Ukrzaliznytsia show attacks on railway facilities have risen sharply: of 521 locomotives hit since the start of the full-scale invasion in February 2022, almost 60% have been struck this year, with overall attacks on railway facilities rising from 1,206 in 2025 to 1,700 so far this year.
