Spain Train Crash

Audio recordings show control room errors before deadly Spain train crash; at least 68 dead

International & World desk

Audio recordings released by Spanish media reveal that a railway control center gave a high-speed train clearance to proceed at normal speed moments before it slammed into a stationary train in central Spain on Wednesday, killing at least 68 people and injuring about 80 others, 15 critically. The crash, which occurred near Urda in the Toledo province, involved a state-owned Renfe train carrying 397 passengers and a private Iryo train with 147 passengers that had stalled ahead on the same line after losing communication with the automatic signaling system.

In the recordings published by outlets including El País and RTVE, a control operator tells the Renfe driver to continue under a backup system called Lateral Signalling Block 'at normal speed with normal operation.' When the driver asks for confirmation—'At normal speed?'—the controller replies, 'Yes.' Earlier exchanges show that the Iryo driver had notified the control center that the train was stationary and could not be restarted because the security system prevented it. Spain's transport minister, Óscar Puente, called the decision to order movement without speed restrictions 'inexplicable.'

The crash is Spain's second deadly high-speed rail disaster this year, following a January collision that killed 46 people. Until 2024, the country's high-speed network—the second largest in the world after China's—had not suffered a fatal accident in over three decades. Spain only liberalized its high-speed service in 2021, with the Italian operator Iryo beginning operations the following year. The Spanish Union of Railway Drivers (Semaf) proceeded with a planned strike on Thursday, citing concerns over railway safety.

Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez, who visited the crash site, declared three days of national mourning and vowed a thorough investigation. 'It is not acceptable for a country like Spain to endure two high-speed train accidents in the same year,' he said. The disaster comes less than a month before a snap national election scheduled for November 29. Most political parties cancelled their campaigns, but the far-right Vox party continued, with leader Santiago Abascal accusing rivals of trying to 'use mourning to silence criticism' of infrastructure. Opposition leader Alberto Núñez Feijóo said 'Spain has the right to know what is happening with its infrastructure.'

Among the victims was 24-year-old long jumper Julieta Gallego, who competed for Madrid-based club AD Sprint. The Royal Spanish Athletics Federation confirmed her death, with federation president Raúl Chapado saying, 'There are blows that strike at the very heart of the athletics family.' Emergency crews deployed more than 900 personnel from the Civil Guard, using drones and helicopters to search the wreckage. Survivors described scenes of smoke, fire, and trapped passengers. Late Wednesday, about 1,500 protesters gathered outside the Transport Ministry in Madrid, demanding the resignations of Sánchez and Puente; police used tear gas after some threw bottles and flares.
