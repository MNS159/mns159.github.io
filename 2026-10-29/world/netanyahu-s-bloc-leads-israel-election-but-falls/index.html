Israel Election

Netanyahu's bloc leads Israel election but falls short of majority

International & World desk

Partial results from Israel's general election show Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's right-wing bloc leading but falling short of a majority in the 120-seat Knesset. With 99% of ballots counted, Netanyahu's Likud party and its far-right and ultra-Orthodox Jewish allies were on track to win 53 seats, while former military chief Gadi Eisenkot's Yashar party and three other Jewish opposition parties in his bloc were set for 48. Arab-led parties, on course to win an unprecedented 19 seats, could emerge as kingmakers, but all but one of the Jewish parties have pledged not to work with them.

Tuesday's election was the first since the deadly Hamas-led attacks on Israel on 7 October 2023 and the subsequent wars in Gaza, Lebanon and Iran. Key campaign issues included accountability for the security failures that led to the attacks, the conduct of regional wars, conscripting ultra-Orthodox Jewish men into the military, judicial reforms, and Israel's international standing. Turnout was 77.7%, the highest in more than two decades, which President Isaac Herzog called 'a badge of honour for Israeli society.'

Both Netanyahu and Eisenkot have insisted they should be tasked with forming a governing coalition. Netanyahu declared his right-wing bloc had won a 'great majority' and said it was 'forbidden to endanger Israel by forming a government that relies on anti-Zionist Arab parties.' Eisenkot told supporters that 'the coalition of failure has lost the people's trust' and that he would work to ensure 'the people's decision is translated into a Zionist government.'

The two Arab parties — the United Arab List and the Joint List — are set to almost double their representation due to a surge in turnout among Arab Israelis, who account for about a fifth of the population. Joint List head Yousef Jabareen said Arab voters were motivated in part by the opportunity to remove far-right National Security Minister Itamar Ben-Gvir, whom he accused of racism. United Arab List leader Mansour Abbas expressed hope that his party, the Change Bloc, and the UAL could secure 61 Knesset seats. However, Eisenkot has previously raised objections to such an alliance, including conditions that Arab parties recognise Israel as Jewish and democratic.

Netanyahu's Likud party has accused the Arab parties of 'stealing' the election, filing a complaint with the Central Elections Committee (CEC) alleging 'bribery, threats, violence, fraud and the use of prohibited software to encourage voters' without providing evidence. On Thursday, CEC chairman Justice Noam Sohlberg instructed the Shin Bet domestic security service to investigate 'serious suspicions' regarding 'improper influence through foreign funding by external actors operating against Israeli interests.' The Haaretz newspaper cited a source saying the committee had been presented with a claim that Qatar had provided funding aimed at increasing voter turnout in Arab communities. Likud called it 'an attack on democracy aimed at enabling foreign control of the Knesset.' Balad chairman Sami Abu Shehadeh dismissed the allegation as a 'racist blood libel.'

Qatar's Foreign Ministry spokesman Majed al-Ansari denied the allegations, calling them 'ridiculous and dangerous in equal measure' and part of a 'disinformation campaign.' He said Qatar has 'no involvement' in Israel's political or electoral competition. Dean Livine, acting director for Israel's CEC, rejected allegations of foreign interference, stating that 'the Knesset elections were conducted lawfully.' Once results are confirmed, President Herzog will hold consultations with all factions and invite the leader he believes has the best chance to form a government, a process that could take weeks. If no coalition is formed, another election would be required.
