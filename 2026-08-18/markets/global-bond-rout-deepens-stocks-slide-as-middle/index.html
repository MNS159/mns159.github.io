Global bond rout deepens, stocks slide as Middle East tensions and inflation fears rattle markets

Financial & Markets desk · anchored on Reuters

Global financial markets faced renewed turmoil Tuesday as a deepening bond rout and declining stock futures reflected escalating Middle East tensions and fresh inflation fears. Futures on the Dow Jones Industrial Average were flat, while S&P 500 futures fell 0.5% and Nasdaq-100 futures retreated 1%, extending losses after all three major U.S. indexes closed lower Monday. The Dow fell 272 points (0.51%), the S&P 500 shed 0.52%, and the Nasdaq composite declined 0.32% in the previous session.

Oil prices surged to multi-week highs after President Donald Trump threatened to bomb Oman if the country impedes U.S. efforts to reopen the Strait of Hormuz. "If Oman gets in the way, we'll bomb the s*** out of them," Trump told Fox News, as a 60-day ceasefire with Iran expired without a breakthrough. Brent crude futures hit $91 per barrel, while West Texas Intermediate crude rose above $85 a barrel. The moves pushed the U.S. Strategic Petroleum Reserve to its lowest level since 1982.

The bond market rout intensified, with longer-term yields climbing sharply. The 10-year Treasury yield rose to 4.72%, while the 30-year yield advanced to 5.31% — its highest level since 2007 — even as the implied probability of a Federal Reserve rate hike at the September 16 meeting fell from nearly 100% in late July to roughly one-third. Analysts noted the divergence suggested the bond market was pricing in persistent inflation pressures independent of near-term Fed policy expectations.

Geopolitical risks compounded existing concerns over rising oil, corporate borrowing for artificial-intelligence investments, and elevated government debt. Iran and Oman are negotiating management of the Strait of Hormuz, with Washington not participating, while U.S. Energy Secretary Chris Wright told Fox News the country was "playing the long game" with Iran. Oil has rallied nearly 50% this year, though Persian Gulf producers have increasingly diverted shipments around the chokepoint.

On the corporate side, earnings season continued with reports expected from Home Depot, Toll Brothers, and Klarna, among others. Separately, Nasdaq announced plans to seek regulatory approval for nearly continuous U.S. equity trading, proposing a schedule from 9 p.m. Sunday through 8 p.m. Friday with a daily one-hour processing break. The exchange aims for a December 6, 2026 launch, pending SEC approval and system readiness.
