Nvidia invests $1.5B in SoftBank-linked data center developer and backs $105B in financing for OpenAI Ohio project

Tech / AI desk

Nvidia said on Monday it will invest $1.5 billion in SB Energy, a data center developer tied to SoftBank and OpenAI, and provide up to $105 billion in credit to help finance a new artificial intelligence data center for OpenAI in Pike County, Ohio, according to securities filings. The facility, located on U.S. Department of Energy land that previously enriched uranium, will be built at the PORTS-Pike Technology Campus and is expected to have an initial 4.25 gigawatts of computing capacity, with an option to expand to 8 gigawatts. Nvidia will be the sole supplier of compute infrastructure for the project, providing its chips and technology.

The deal marks Nvidia’s latest financial maneuver to support the sprawling AI data center buildout and underscores the chipmaker’s strategy of forging complex financial links across the AI industry. SB Energy, which already counts SoftBank and OpenAI among its investors, will build and manage the data center under a 20-year lease to OpenAI. OpenAI has a stake in SB Energy, and CEO Sam Altman was an early investor in the company. The power for the site will come from a 9.2-gigawatt natural gas power plant expected to cost $33 billion, reflecting a 66% rise in natural gas plant construction costs over the past two years, according to BloombergNEF. SB Energy and SoftBank will also invest at least $4.2 billion into regional grid infrastructure to support 10 gigawatts of total energy.

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang said in a release: “We are securing long-lived infrastructure for NVIDIA compute so OpenAI can deploy the most productive AI factories that can be upgraded repeatedly with each new generation delivering more intelligence and better economics.” OpenAI President Greg Brockman told CNBC's "Squawk Box" that compute is a “fundamental resource” for the industry, adding: “Compute is really becoming the new oil, the new limited resource of the AI age.” The data center is expected to create 35,000 construction jobs through 2032 and 2,500 long-term positions, according to OpenAI. Capacity is expected to come online in phases starting in 2028.

CNBC reported earlier that Nvidia had been in discussions to provide up to $250 billion in financing for a 10-gigawatt facility at the Ohio location, but the Wall Street Journal later reported that guarantee would be cut to less than $120 billion. The final $105 billion figure is lower than both. The deal comes a week after Nvidia teamed up with six large asset managers to build financing platforms aimed at deploying $500 billion in third-party capital for data center projects. Separately, a Friday SEC filing revealed that Nvidia holds a roughly $21 billion stake in SpaceX, now worth about $17 billion after a post-IPO decline, signaling the company’s growing financial entanglement with major customers. During SpaceX’s first public earnings call, CEO Elon Musk said his company has an exclusive relationship with Nvidia for its data centers, citing its Vera Rubin architecture as “the best AI computer.”
