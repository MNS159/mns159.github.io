International & World US-South Korea Alliance

Trump orders cuts to joint US-South Korea drills; Seoul urges military independence

International & World desk

President Donald Trump has ordered the Pentagon to substantially reduce this week’s joint military exercises with South Korea, the Ulchi Freedom Shield drills, citing his “very good relationship” with North Korean leader Kim Jong-un and expressing displeasure that Seoul declined to join the US campaign against Iran. The 11-day exercise, which began Monday and involves about 18,000 South Korean troops alongside US forces and personnel from 11 United Nations Command member states, is one of the two main annual combined drills. Trump said in a social media post Sunday evening that the drills are costly and “send a signal that is totally inappropriate and hostile” to a country he described as “unthreatening and respectful” during his presidency. He added that he had instructed Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth to “substantially reduce” the exercises because it was too late to cancel them outright.

The move comes despite warnings from US and South Korean military officials that North Korea is learning new battlefield tactics from its deployment of troops to the Ukraine war—lessons, including drone warfare, that the allies had planned to incorporate into the drills. Last week, Col Ryan Donald, public affairs director for US-led commands in South Korea, said the exercise scenario would reflect “drones, GPS disruption, cyber-attacks” and that North Korean forces “have taken those lessons they learned there and brought them back to North Korea.” Lt Gen Scott Winter, deputy commander of the UNC, said the drills would be “bigger and better than ever” and that the threats being countered “reflect the increased use of these technologies and the understanding that North Korea has contemporary battlefield experience.” The US military had previously described the exercises as reinforcing “the role of the alliance as the linchpin for regional peace and security.”

Tech / AI AI / Infrastructure

Nvidia invests $1.5B in SoftBank-linked data center developer and backs $105B in financing for OpenAI Ohio project

Nvidia said on Monday it will invest $1.5 billion in SB Energy, a data center developer tied to SoftBank and OpenAI, and provide up to $105 billion in credit to help finance a new artificial intelligence data center for OpenAI in Pike County, Ohio, according to securities filings. The facility, located on U.S. Department of Energy land that previously enriched uranium, will be built at the PORTS-Pike Technology Campus and is expected to have an initial 4.25 gigawatts of computing capacity, with an option to expand to 8 gigawatts. Nvidia will be the sole supplier of compute infrastructure for the project, providing its chips and technology.
