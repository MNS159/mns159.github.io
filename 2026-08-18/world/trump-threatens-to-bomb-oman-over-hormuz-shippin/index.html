Trump threatens to bomb Oman over Hormuz shipping deal, lashes out at reporter

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts slightly center-left

US President Donald Trump on Monday threatened to bomb Oman if it “gets in the way” of a deal over shipping in the Strait of Hormuz and called for Iran to “put up the white flag of surrender,” according to Fox News journalist Trey Yingst, who interviewed the president. In a separate exchange, Trump lashed out at a CNN reporter, calling him a “fake reporter” after the reporter asked whether North Korea’s leader had requested a scaling back of US military drills with South Korea.

Trump’s threat came amid ongoing talks between Oman and Iran on managing transit through the strategic waterway, which Iran effectively closed after Israel and the US attacked the country on 28 February. Iran’s foreign affairs spokesperson, Esmail Baghaei, said Monday that an understanding had been reached on a map for the transit route, with ships entering near Iran and exiting near Oman without paying fees during an interim period; the sides are working to finalize a joint statement. Trump had previously threatened Oman in May, telling reporters that “Oman will behave just like everybody else, or we will have to blow them up.”

The US has sought an acceptable deal to reopen the strait before ending its blockade of Iranian ports. A 60-day negotiating period for a US-Iran peace deal is expiring, but Iran’s foreign ministry spokesperson, Esmaeil Baqaei, said Monday that the timeline was “irrelevant” because the US had violated the agreement early on, adding that “widespread violations of the agreement began, thereby rendering the 60-day provision irrelevant.” Trump told Yingst that the 60-day deadline was irrelevant and that there was no timeline, and that the US has a back channel for talks with the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps. A senior Iranian official warned that Iran would escalate tensions in the Strait and wider region if diplomacy fails, pointing to a policy shift relying on offense rather than defense.

Other regional developments Monday included two Iranian drones targeting the Iraqi Kurdistan prime minister’s office and the home of the autonomous region’s intelligence chief, according to Kurdish leader Masrour Barzani, who called it a “dangerous escalation.” In Israel, Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and US envoy Jared Kushner discussed putting an American general in charge of verifying Hamas’s disarmament, as Netanyahu insisted there would be no pullout from Gaza until that occurs. Eight Muslim-majority countries—Egypt, Jordan, Qatar, Indonesia, Saudi Arabia, Turkey, the United Arab Emirates, and Pakistan—issued a joint statement condemning Israel for rejecting Trump’s Gaza roadmap, saying it “fundamentally undermines the collective efforts undertaken to achieve a just and lasting peace.”

Elsewhere, Israeli flags planted on a main coastal highway in southern Lebanon were deemed a violation of a UN Security Council resolution by the UN peacekeeping force UNIFIL, which urged all sides to avoid “provocative actions.” Yemen’s Houthi rebels claimed they targeted a Saudi military vessel and four accompanying boats in the Red Sea using ballistic missiles, though the claim could not be independently verified. Israeli soldiers oversaw the demolition of a Palestinian building in the West Bank, citing a lack of a building permit. The UN rights chief voiced alarm at the rising civilian toll in Yemen, urging all parties to halt actions that risk a return to full-scale conflict.

US forces stationed in the Kurdish region of Iraq have begun withdrawing ahead of a 30 September deadline to end the US military presence there. In Israel, Likud members voted Monday to choose candidates for the October 27 general election. The day’s events underscored the volatile security landscape across the Middle East, with multiple flashpoints involving Iran, the US, and regional allies.
