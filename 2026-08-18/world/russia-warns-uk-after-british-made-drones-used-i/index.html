Russia warns UK after British-made drones used in Ukraine strikes; 10 killed in Kharkiv

International & World desk · anchored on Reuters

Russia has warned the UK of "consequences" after reports confirmed that British-made drones were used in recent Ukrainian strikes on military and industrial targets inside Russia. The Russian embassy in London accused the UK of "deliberately opting for an escalation of the Ukraine crisis," stating that "the deeper its involvement in the conflict... the higher the price it will pay." The embassy added that "London's actions will inevitably carry consequences for which it will have to answer" but did not specify the type of threat Moscow is considering. A UK Ministry of Defence spokesperson responded that the government stands "shoulder to shoulder" with Ukraine, saying, "Russia should be in no doubt about the resolve of this government to stand against Russian aggression."

The warning came after a wave of Ukrainian drone attacks on Russia. Moscow Mayor Sergei Sobyanin reported that approximately 620 drones headed toward the capital overnight, with 180 shot down over the region and 17 more intercepted later. Moscow regional governor Andrey Vorobyov said three people were injured, including a 10-year-old girl with a shrapnel wound, and that a house in the village of Kuznetsy "burned down completely," with several other residential buildings damaged. A Wildberries logistics complex east of Moscow sustained minor damage when debris struck the building wall. Russia's state news agency TASS described the barrage as the heaviest drone attack on Moscow in two years. Ukraine has repeatedly targeted Wildberries facilities, saying the company supplies components for Russian drones.

In the deadliest attack on Ukraine overnight, Russian missiles struck the village of Pechenihy in the Kharkiv region, killing 10 civilians and wounding eight, according to regional governor Oleh Syniehubov. He said the strike damaged 10 private homes, a cafe, a post office, a shop, and at least seven cars. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said the attack hit a busy intersection. Elsewhere, three Ukrainians died in Sumy, two in Zaporizhzhia, and two in Kherson, while five people were wounded in Izium. Kyiv came under five waves of attack overnight. The Ukrainian air force reported intercepting or suppressing 111 of 147 enemy drones.

The UK has been one of Ukraine’s largest international supporters since Russia launched its full-scale invasion in February 2022. In April, the MoD announced it would supply Ukraine tens of thousands of drones, including long-range strike devices, in its biggest-ever package. A military source confirmed to the BBC that UK drones had been used in the recent attacks inside Russia, which are said to have caused more than £35bn in economic damage. Russia currently controls approximately a fifth of Ukrainian territory. Russia has threatened the UK previously over its role in providing drone technology and Storm Shadow missiles to Ukraine. The latest phase of the war has directly affected Russia’s domestic fuel supplies and dented its economy, while the UN has warned that civilian deaths in the conflict rose in July to levels not seen since May 2022.
