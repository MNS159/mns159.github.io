Iran-US Tensions

Iran threatens new offensive; oil tops $90 on Middle East tensions

International & World desk · anchored on Reuters
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

Iran has threatened a new offensive, and the United States has ruled out extending a ceasefire deal, according to reports on Tuesday. The developments come as oil prices have topped $90 a barrel, fueled by renewed tensions in the Middle East. ICE Brent traded just below $90 per barrel in early Asian trading, extending Monday's gains, supported by renewed fighting in Lebanon and attacks on vessels in the Strait of Hormuz.

Several vessels, including ships linked to the Abu Dhabi National Oil Co., were reportedly targeted in the waterway late last week, raising concerns over regional supply disruptions and complicating prospects for a US-Iran deal. The rise in oil prices reflects growing market anxiety about potential supply disruptions from the region, a key transit chokepoint for global crude shipments.

Speculative positioning in oil markets has turned more bullish. Money managers increased their net long position in ICE Brent by 76,026 lots to 240,748 lots as of last Tuesday, the largest bullish position since early June 2026. The increase was primarily driven by fresh long positions, with gross longs rising by 51,818 lots week-on-week. In NYMEX WTI, net longs increased by 2,665 lots to 103,715 lots.

Meanwhile, US drilling activity continued to expand. The US oil rig count rose by one to 455 active rigs last week, marking a third consecutive weekly increase, according to Baker Hughes. The count is now 43 rigs higher than a year ago and at its highest level since May 2025. Drilling activity has trended higher since the start of the US-Iran conflict in late February. The US Energy Information Administration estimates US crude oil production will average 13.8 million barrels per day in 2026, up from 13.6 million in 2025, before increasing further to 14.2 million in 2027.

In other commodity markets, copper prices hit a record high, with LME copper rising 1.7% to $14,396 per ton, extending its rally for a third session and marking a seventh consecutive week of gains. The cash/3M spread widened to a backwardation of $518.5 per ton, the strongest since October 2021, highlighting acute near-term supply tightness. LME inventories fell for a 42nd consecutive session to 204,975 tonnes, their lowest level since February. Chile's copper commission Cochilco expects national copper output to fall 2.6% year-on-year to 5.3 million tonnes in 2026, reflecting weaker production from Codelco and BHP operations.

In agriculture, Ukraine's grain exports remain under pressure as the Black Sea corridor stays closed. Data from the Agriculture Ministry showed grain exports reached just 590,000 tonnes during the first 12 days of August, around 30% of the pace needed to meet demand. While rail and Danube routes are helping to offset some disruptions, they are unlikely to fully compensate for the loss of Black Sea access. The Danube route is also facing drought-related constraints, although capacity could rise to around 1.5 million tonnes per month by year-end.
