Russell Westbrook announces retirement from NBA after 18 seasons

Sports desk

Russell Westbrook announced his retirement from the NBA on Wednesday, ending an 18-season career that included an MVP award, nine All-NBA selections, and the league's all-time triple-doubles record. The announcement came via an Instagram video narrated by Michael B. Jordan, which opened with the words "The Museum of Unspoken Answers" and featured Westbrook wearing a shirt that read "Don't Rock With What They Say." He did not speak in the video.

Westbrook, 37, played 1,301 games across seven organizations: 11 seasons with the Oklahoma City Thunder, roughly a year and a half each with the Los Angeles Lakers and Los Angeles Clippers, and one season apiece with the Houston Rockets, Washington Wizards, Denver Nuggets, and Sacramento Kings. He retires 14th on the NBA's all-time scoring list and fifth in career assists, and is one of only two players in league history—alongside LeBron James—to record more than 25,000 points and 10,000 assists. He is also one of three players, with James and Oscar Robertson, to surpass 25,000 points, 7,000 rebounds, and 7,000 assists. His career honors include two scoring titles, three assist titles, the 2017 MVP, and a spot on the NBA's 75th Anniversary Team.

The retirement came after reported contract talks with the Washington Wizards, for whom Westbrook played during the 2020-21 season. According to NBA insider Marc Stein, the Wizards made a two-year offer to bring Westbrook back, but Westbrook—not the team—ended those discussions, apparently balking at the specific terms or the role presented. The Wizards, who added Trae Young and Anthony Davis last season and won the NBA draft lottery to land top overall pick AJ Dybantsa this summer, were seen as a potential fit for Westbrook as an experienced backup point guard. He spent last season with the Sacramento Kings, averaging 15.2 points, 6.7 assists, and 5.4 rebounds per game, largely as a starter.

Westbrook's career was defined by a ferocious, high-octane style that made him one of the most electrifying players of his generation. He averaged a triple-double for a season four times—three consecutive years in Oklahoma City and once more in Washington—becoming the first player since Oscar Robertson to accomplish the feat. Among his most memorable performances was a 20-point, 20-rebound, 21-assist game against the Lakers in 2019, two days after the death of his friend Nipsey Hussle. He also played through a fractured cheekbone in 2015, missing just one game after surgery and returning with a protective mask to post 49 points, 15 rebounds, 10 assists, three steals, and a block against Philadelphia.

Westbrook's impact extended beyond statistics. He was known for his relentless competitiveness, signature poster dunks, and distinctive fashion sense, from custom Bode at the Met Gala to glasses without lenses. His decision to stay in Oklahoma City after Kevin Durant's departure in 2016 was widely credited with saving Thunder basketball. He retires as a future first-ballot Hall of Famer and one of the defining players of his era, with a legacy that The Ringer described as "one of the greatest to ever pick up a basketball."
