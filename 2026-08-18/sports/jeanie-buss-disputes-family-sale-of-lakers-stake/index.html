Jeanie Buss opposes family sale of Lakers stake, setting up ownership fight

Sports desk

Jeanie Buss is contesting her family’s plan to sell its remaining 17.8% stake in the Los Angeles Lakers to new majority owners Bob Iger and Josh Kushner, according to a letter from her attorney obtained by CNBC. The dispute pits Buss against her five siblings — Joey, Jesse, Jim, Johnny and Janie — who have all reportedly agreed to sell the shares as part of a larger transaction that values the franchise at $12.5 billion. The Buss family has held an ownership interest in the Lakers since Dr. Jerry Buss bought the team in 1979.

The five siblings favoring the sale invoked a tag-along clause to force the sale of the family’s shares alongside Iger and Kushner’s purchase of Mark Walter’s controlling stake, which closed last week. In a statement released Monday through ESPN, the siblings said, ‘We have decided as a family to sell the remaining Buss Family Trust shares to the Bob Iger group as part of the ongoing transaction. We love the Lakers, Laker fans and will continue to support Los Angeles; but it is time to use this opportunity to move on and exit gracefully while we still can.’ However, Jeanie Buss’s attorney, Adam Streisand, wrote in the CNBC letter that any vote to sell ‘would be and is void,’ arguing that a 2017 court order requires unanimous approval of the trust’s co-trustees — Jeanie, Janie and Joey Buss — and that the sale would breach fiduciary duty and violate the court order.

The 17.8% stake is significant because NBA rules require a team’s governor to own at least 15% of the franchise. If the family sells its shares, Jeanie Buss would lose her position as governor, the role that gives her final authority over basketball operations and decisions such as retaining president of basketball operations Rob Pelinka. Jeanie Buss had driven the initial sale of the majority stake to Walter last year, which allowed her to remain governor for at least five years as part of a special provision. The current dispute follows a lengthy history of Buss family infighting, including a 2017 conflict in which brothers Jim and Johnny Buss tried to remove Jeanie from the board after she fired Jim as head of basketball operations; the two sides ultimately settled with Jeanie remaining in control.

Iger and Kushner have not commented on the family dispute, though Iger told The California Post that while the plan was for Buss to remain governor, ‘if things change, they’ll change.’ The outcome of the fight could have broader implications for the Lakers’ on-court future. Luka Dončić, who signed a contract extension last offseason, can become a free agent in 2028. The Lakers, currently considered a tier below Western Conference contenders Oklahoma City and San Antonio, face the risk that off-court instability could affect his long-term plans.

The Lakers’ on-court performance has declined sharply since Dr. Jerry Buss’s death in 2013. From 1979–80 through 2012–13, the team posted a regular-season record of 1,749–991 (about 64% wins), winning 10 championships. Since 2013–14, the Lakers are 483–562 (about 46% wins) and endured a franchise-record six-year playoff drought from 2013 to 2019. The team won the 2020 championship with LeBron James and Anthony Davis but has not advanced past the first round since. James left the Lakers this offseason to sign a minimum-salary deal with the Philadelphia 76ers.
