Public Health Policy

Trump administration has deleted or altered nearly 400 US datasets, endangering public health

Health & Science desk

The Trump administration has deleted or altered at least 366 federal datasets over the past year and a half, according to a new online tracker released last week by dataindex.us, a group of data policy experts, researchers and developers. The tracker identifies 28 datasets that have been completely deleted and 338 that have been modified, with health-related data collections accounting for about 40% of the total. The changes include the removal of questions about gender identity from multiple Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) surveys, following President Donald Trump's executive order 'Defending Women from Gender Ideology Extremism and Restoring Biological Truth to the Federal Government.'

Woman's suicide after confiding in ChatGPT raises AI safety questions

Health & Science desk

Sophie Rottenberg, a 29-year-old former public health policy analyst, died by suicide in Ithaca, New York, in early February 2025. Her mother, Laura Reiley, later discovered that Rottenberg had been sharing her deepest struggles with a ChatGPT-based therapist named "Harry" for months, a trove of nearly 1,800 pages of interaction. Rottenberg had told no one else—not her parents, her best friend, or her flesh-and-blood therapist—about the extent of her suicidal thoughts, according to Reiley, who spoke with NPR.

EyePoint's Wet AMD Treatment Fails to Meet Primary Goal in Phase 3 Trial, Stock Plunges

Health & Science desk

EyePoint announced Monday that its experimental wet age-related macular degeneration (wet AMD) treatment, Duravyu, failed to achieve its primary endpoint in the first of two pivotal Phase 3 clinical trials, causing the company's stock to fall sharply. The LUGANO trial did not demonstrate non-inferiority in the average change in best corrected visual acuity (BCVA) from baseline to weeks 52 and 56 compared to the standard treatment, on-label aflibercept, in the full dataset. Shares of EyePoint cratered 69% on the news, while shares of Ocular Therapeutix, which is developing a competing treatment, rose.

Gene Therapy

After undisclosed gene therapy deaths, China's regulatory path questioned

Health & Science desk

Two deaths in Chinese investigator-initiated trials (IITs) of experimental gene therapies—both involving severe immune reactions to high-dose viral vector delivery and a lack of timely disclosure—have drawn international scrutiny to the oversight and transparency of China's alternative regulatory pathway for early-stage cell and gene therapy studies. In the first case, reported by Science and Retraction Watch, a 6-year-old girl with a rare, nonfatal neurodevelopmental disorder died last year from a severe immune response days after receiving a base-editing therapy funded in part by her parents. In the second, uncovered by Stat, a young boy enrolled in HuidaGene Therapeutics' IIT for Duchenne muscular dystrophy died last year of acute respiratory distress syndrome following a CRISPR-based treatment.
