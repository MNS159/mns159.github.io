EyePoint's Wet AMD Treatment Fails to Meet Primary Goal in Phase 3 Trial, Stock Plunges

Health & Science desk

EyePoint announced Monday that its experimental wet age-related macular degeneration (wet AMD) treatment, Duravyu, failed to achieve its primary endpoint in the first of two pivotal Phase 3 clinical trials, causing the company's stock to fall sharply. The LUGANO trial did not demonstrate non-inferiority in the average change in best corrected visual acuity (BCVA) from baseline to weeks 52 and 56 compared to the standard treatment, on-label aflibercept, in the full dataset. Shares of EyePoint cratered 69% on the news, while shares of Ocular Therapeutix, which is developing a competing treatment, rose.

The primary endpoint was confounded, according to EyePoint, by an asymmetric cohort of 9 out of 211 patients (4%) in the Duravyu arm who experienced vision loss of 15 or more letters unrelated to wet AMD. No patients in the aflibercept control arm experienced such vision loss. In an ad hoc analysis excluding those patients, Duravyu was non-inferior to aflibercept (nominal p-value = 0.0096). The company noted that in prior similar-scale pivotal Phase 3 trials, approximately 3-5% of aflibercept patients lost 15 or more letters, suggesting the control group in LUGANO overperformed.

Despite the primary endpoint miss, Duravyu met several key secondary endpoints. The treatment achieved a 42% reduction in treatment burden versus on-label aflibercept (nominal p-value < 0.0001), translating to an average of two fewer injections through week 56. Additionally, 76% of Duravyu patients were supplement-free up to week 32, and 54% were supplement-free up to week 56; 79% received zero or one supplemental injection through week 56. A pre-specified analysis of supplement-free patients showed Duravyu was non-inferior to aflibercept on BCVA change (nominal p-value = 0.0035). The drug also demonstrated a favorable safety profile with repeat dosing, with no observed cases of insert migration, retinal vasculitis, or severe intraocular inflammation.

Wet AMD is a leading cause of vision loss in people over 50, caused by abnormal blood vessel growth under the retina that leaks fluid and blood. Current treatments require frequent eye injections. Duravyu is a sustained-release intravitreal insert designed to be administered every six months, potentially reducing the treatment burden. The LUGANO trial randomized patients 1:1 to receive either Duravyu 2.7 mg every six months or on-label aflibercept as a control.

EyePoint President and CEO Jay S. Duker said the company remains confident in Duravyu's potential, citing the positive secondary endpoints and the ad hoc analysis. The company plans to present further LUGANO data at retina conferences beginning in September 2026. Topline results from the second pivotal Phase 3 trial, LUCIA, are expected in the fourth quarter of 2026, with a potential New Drug Application submission to the FDA planned for the first half of 2027, pending those results. EyePoint also has two Phase 3 trials for Duravyu in diabetic macular edema, with topline data expected in the fourth quarter of 2027.
