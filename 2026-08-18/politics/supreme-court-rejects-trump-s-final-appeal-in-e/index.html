Supreme Court rejects Trump's final appeal in E. Jean Carroll case

Politics desk
✓ Facts
  • On Monday, the US Supreme Court denied Trump's petition for rehearing in the E. Jean Carroll civil case ("Carroll II"), the second time the Court has declined to disturb the verdict after an initial denial in late June 2025.
  • The case originated from a 2023 federal jury verdict finding Trump liable for sexually abusing Carroll in a Manhattan department store dressing room in the mid-1990s and for defaming her in 2022 Truth Social posts that called her claim a "hoax" and said she was "not my type."
  • The jury awarded Carroll $5 million total: $2 million compensatory plus $20,000 punitive damages for the abuse claim, and $1.7 million for a reputation-repair program plus $1 million in other damages for defamation.
  • Following the Court's June denial, Trump paid Carroll the judgment plus post-judgment interest; outlets report the total payout as more than $5 million, with figures cited between roughly $5.6 million and $5.8 million.
  • The Court's order gave no explanation, consistent with its custom for rehearing denials issued during recess; no dissents were noted, and the same order list denied 33 requests in total.
  • Trump's rehearing request argued that a separate pending case, "Carroll I" (an $83.3 million defamation verdict over 2019 statements, on which he filed a cert petition in July 2025), raises presidential-immunity questions that should affect the evidentiary rulings—including admission of the 2005 Access Hollywood tape—underlying Carroll II.
  • Carroll's attorney, Roberta Kaplan, said the jury's verdict finding Trump sexually assaulted and then defamed Carroll "is now final and cannot be challenged in any court."
  • The $83.3 million Carroll I case remains pending before the Supreme Court; Carroll's response was due later in the month the ruling came down, and the Court is not expected to act on it until after its October term begins.
○ Analysis

This dispute involves two separate civil suits E. Jean Carroll brought against Donald Trump: one ("Carroll II") over 2022 statements calling her rape allegation a hoax, which produced the $5 million verdict at issue in Monday's order, and another ("Carroll I") over 2019 statements he made while president, which produced an $83.3 million verdict still on appeal. Trump has argued throughout that statements made while serving as president are protected by presidential immunity and should not have been used as evidence against him, while Carroll's team has maintained the verdicts rest on a unanimous jury's factual findings. Monday's denial closes off further judicial review of the smaller, $5 million verdict, but the larger $83.3 million case—raising similar immunity arguments—remains unresolved and will not be taken up by the Court until at least October.

Steelman

Both sides can agree on the narrow legal fact that matters most: the $5 million verdict is now final and unappealable, even as the far larger $83.3 million verdict and its presidential-immunity question remain unresolved and pending before the same Court.

Implications

With the $5 million verdict now final, attention shifts to the pending $83.3 million Carroll I case, where Trump is expected to press similar presidential-immunity arguments regarding statements made while in office; the Supreme Court is not expected to act on that petition until after its term begins in October, meaning the broader legal questions about immunity for official statements remain open.
