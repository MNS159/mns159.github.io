Trump reduces joint military drills with South Korea, citing relationship with Kim

Politics desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum
✓ Facts
  • President Trump posted on Truth Social that the US will "substantially reduce" joint military exercises with South Korea, citing his "very good relationship" with North Korean leader Kim Jong Un.
  • Trump said he had asked South Korea's president to join the US in the "denuclearisation" of the Islamic Republic of Iran and that South Korea responded "No thanks!"; he called this decision to reduce the drills a matter of "fairness."
  • Trump said it was "too late to cancel" the exercises entirely because the US-South Korea Ulchi Freedom Shield drills were scheduled to begin the following Monday.
  • South Korea's government said it was reviewing Trump's comments and that it hoped improved US-North Korea relations could lead to meaningful talks, while earlier stating the drills would proceed "as scheduled."
  • Ulchi Freedom Shield was described by US and South Korean military leaders as an 11-day exercise similar in scale to previous years, involving about 18,000 South Korean soldiers; the US has roughly 28,500 troops stationed in South Korea, though Trump cited a figure of 39,000 in Oval Office remarks.
  • Trump previously suspended the joint exercises entirely in 2018 during denuclearization talks with Kim, and the drills were scaled back again in 2020 during the Covid-19 pandemic.
  • North Korea conducted ballistic missile tests this month that are banned under UN resolutions, and North Korea's foreign ministry condemned the joint drills as "a rehearsal for an aggressive war."
  • South Korea's president recently proposed talks with North Korea aimed at officially ending the Korean War, which has remained technically active since the 1953 armistice; South Korea separately committed $350 billion to US investment earlier this year.
○ Analysis

The dispute centers on Ulchi Freedom Shield, the annual US-South Korea joint military exercise that Washington and Seoul view as central to deterring North Korea, which still has nearly 30,000 US troops stationed on the peninsula. Trump's move follows a familiar pattern from his first term, when he suspended the drills in 2018 amid direct diplomacy with Kim Jong Un, a relationship that collapsed by 2019 without a denuclearization deal. The new reduction is explicitly tied by Trump to a separate grievance: South Korea's refusal to assist US efforts related to Iran, a request Trump has also made of other allies including Japan, France and the UK. Experts note the scaled-back exercises save relatively little money while carrying real coordination and deterrence costs, especially given North Korea's recent missile tests and its deployment of troops to support Russia in Ukraine.

◐ Debate

Democratic

  • Democrats, per The Hill, have expressed derision at the decision, framing it as another instance of Trump discounting longstanding US defense commitments to allies.
  • Critics warn — echoed by allied nations' broader unease described in The Hill — that unpredictability in alliance management could embolden adversaries and unsettle partners who rely on US security guarantees.
  • Foreign policy analysts cited by BBC (e.g., Leif-Eric Easley) argue the move risks weakening deterrence and slowing South Korea's assumption of defense responsibilities for marginal cost savings — a critique aligned with concerns raised by Trump's opponents.

Republican

  • Trump and allies frame the reduction as a cost-saving, fairness-driven correction, arguing the US bears disproportionate financial burden for exercises that Trump calls "provocative" and unnecessary given his rapport with Kim Jong Un.
  • Supporters point to precedent from Trump's first term, when a similar suspension in 2018 was tied to direct diplomatic engagement with North Korea, framing this as a continuation of a proven de-escalation strategy.
  • Trump's camp ties the decision to reciprocity, arguing South Korea's declining to assist on Iran while the US provides extensive troop protection against North Korea justifies recalibrating the relationship.
Steelman

Both supporters and critics agree the exercises carry more symbolic and alliance-management weight than financial significance — the real question is whether reduced drills buy diplomatic goodwill with North Korea or simply weaken deterrence and coordination without a clear payoff.

Implications

South Korea's government has said it is reviewing Trump's remarks and continuing consultations on both the Iran-related request and the joint drills, so the practical scope of the reduction — and any linkage to renewed Trump-Kim diplomacy — remains unsettled. How North Korea, which has already condemned the drills and expanded its missile program, responds may shape whether this move opens a diplomatic opportunity or is read as a signal of weakened US commitment in the region.
