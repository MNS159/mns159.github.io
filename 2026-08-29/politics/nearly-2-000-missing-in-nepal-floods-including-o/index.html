Disaster

Nearly 2,000 missing in Nepal floods including over 100 Americans; families await news

Politics desk
✓ Facts
  • A flash flood and landslide struck the Nepal-Tibet border region on 26 August, near pilgrimage routes to Mount Kailash.
  • Nepali authorities report more than 575 deaths and nearly 2,000 people missing, including tourists from the US, Australia, Canada, India, and the UK.
  • The US State Department said about 90 US citizens may have been impacted, with five confirmed to have reached safety as of Thursday.
  • Nepal police report more than 500 missing tourists from 28 different countries.
  • Chinese state media (CCTV) reported three dead and at least 558 missing on the Tibet side, including 260 foreign nationals; it is unclear whether these figures overlap with Nepal's count.
  • At least 77 people, including US citizens, were part of a pilgrimage organized by the Isha Foundation, a nonprofit spiritual organization based in India with a US location in McMinnville, Tennessee.
  • Canada's foreign office said it was aware of 32 Canadians and permanent residents in Nepal and Tibet whose status was unconfirmed, out of 820 Canadians registered as being in Nepal and five in the Tibet Autonomous Region.
  • The US Geological Survey said the seismic energy detected was generated by a landslide, not an earthquake as initially suggested by some reports.
○ Analysis

The disaster struck a remote, high-altitude border region popular with Hindu and Buddhist pilgrims traveling to Mount Kailash, a site visited by tens of thousands annually. Because the area spans the Nepal-China border and involves nationals from at least 28 countries, the response has required coordination among multiple governments and consular services, complicating efforts to locate the missing. Scientists note that while climate change is known to accelerate glacier melt in the Hindu Kush Himalaya region, it is too early to attribute this specific landslide event to climate change with confidence. The scale of infrastructure damage—estimated at over 15 billion Nepalese rupees ($98 million) in roads and bridges—adds to the difficulty of rescue and communication efforts in the area.

Implications

As search-and-rescue efforts continue, the number of confirmed dead or found alive is likely to change as government agencies in Nepal, China, the US, and Canada update their counts. The disaster may also renew scientific and public attention on landslide and flood risks in the Hindu Kush Himalaya region, particularly the role of glacier melt, even as officials caution that a direct climate link to this specific event has not yet been established.
