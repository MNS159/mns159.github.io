AI Stocks Drive Market Gains as Magnificent Seven Earnings Surge 118.5%

Financial & Markets desk

The S&P 500 and Nasdaq Composite posted gains last week, driven by a concentrated rally in artificial-intelligence-related stocks after a wave of earnings reports showed the AI buildout is boosting both hardware and software companies. The S&P 500 rose 0.5% and the tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite gained 0.9%, while the Dow Jones Industrial Average added 0.5% for its first winning week in three. Much of the advance came Thursday, when the ripple effects of Nvidia's quarterly results lifted all three indexes to their best day since Aug. 4.

Nvidia, the AI chip leader, reported better-than-expected fiscal second-quarter results on Aug. 26, marking its fourth straight quarter of accelerating revenue growth, and issued a surprisingly strong outlook for fiscal 2028, forecasting roughly 70% revenue growth. The company's finance chief, Colette Kress, said growth in the current quarter will be driven primarily by non-hyperscaler customers, including so-called neoclouds like CoreWeave and Nebius, as well as enterprises. CEO Jensen Huang said the return on investment capital for customers is now less than a year, adding, "Now, compute is revenue." Nvidia shares ended the week up 1%.

The strong results from Nvidia and other AI-related companies lifted the broader software sector. Salesforce reported better-than-expected revenue and an upbeat outlook, with CEO Marc Benioff calling fears of a "SaaSpocalypse" "nonsense" and noting that nine of the 10 leading AI companies use Salesforce products and that their spending is up 435% year over year. CrowdStrike reported revenue rose 26% as AI-powered attacks drive demand for stronger defenses. Salesforce surged 22% and CrowdStrike jumped about 20% on Thursday, making them the two best-performing stocks in the CNBC Investing Club portfolio for the week. The iShares Expanded Tech-Software Sector ETF (IGV) is up about 6% so far this month.

In aggregate, the seven stocks known as the "Magnificent 7" — which include Nvidia, Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, Apple, Meta Platforms, and Tesla — reported actual earnings growth of 118.5% for the second quarter, the highest growth rate for the group since at least Q4 2020, according to FactSet. The group exceeded earnings estimates by 66.2% in aggregate, compared with 26.5% for all S&P 500 companies. Amazon, Alphabet, and Eli Lilly are among the stocks analysts favor for profit growth prospects, according to Investor's Business Daily. Microsoft extended a breakout, and Palantir Technologies, Workday, JFrog, and Shopify are other software stocks near buy points.

Wall Street is also preparing to put a tradable price on AI computing power. The CME Group plans to launch futures on Oct. 5 tied to the hourly rental cost of Nvidia's H100 and B200 graphics processors, pending regulatory approval. The contracts would settle against benchmarks from Silicon Data, which tracks what companies pay to rent those chips. Silicon Data's H100 benchmark is currently around $2.68 per GPU-hour, while its B200 benchmark is about $5.66. Both have moved substantially over the past year, and not always together. The effort follows a mixed history of creating new markets: DRAM-chip futures were proposed in 1989 but never launched, and bandwidth trading during the late-1990s fiber boom failed to become a benchmark. Weather futures survive but remain specialized, while iron ore derivatives took hold alongside index pricing.

In other developments, Meta Platforms agreed to an $18 billion settlement with attorneys general from 48 states, Washington, D.C., and three U.S. territories over claims that social media platforms harm young users. As part of the settlement, Meta will implement additional protections for younger users, including default daily time limits, enhanced parental controls, age-verification measures, and restrictions on push notifications during school hours. Shares of Meta ended the week up 5%. Meanwhile, Federal Reserve Chairman Kevin Warsh said in a Jackson Hole speech that recent inflation data has not eased his concerns about underlying price pressures, keeping the possibility of another rate hike on the table. Traders raised the odds of a September hike to 55%, up from about 35% a day earlier, according to the CME FedWatch tool.
