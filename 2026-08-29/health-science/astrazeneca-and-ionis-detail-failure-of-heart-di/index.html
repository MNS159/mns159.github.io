AstraZeneca and Ionis detail failure of heart disease silencer drug, raising Alnylam questions

Health & Science desk

AstraZeneca on Friday detailed the surprise failure of a closely watched Phase 3 clinical trial for its experimental heart disease drug, explaining that the treatment offered no additional benefit for patients who were largely already taking another type of medicine. The study involved patients with ATTR-CM, a progressive condition, who received AstraZeneca's drug, a type of therapy known as a silencer. The lack of improvement among patients already on a different class of drug called a stabilizer was so pronounced that it sank the entire trial.

The trial's miss has implications not only for AstraZeneca and its development partner Ionis Pharmaceuticals, but also for other companies in the multibillion-dollar ATTR-CM market, including Alnylam Pharmaceuticals, which sells another silencer, and BridgeBio, the maker of a new stabilizer. Most participants in the study were on a stabilizer at the outset of the trial, and the results showed that the silencer added no benefit on top of that existing treatment.

Analysts have said the outcome bolstered the case that stabilizers, which are oral drugs, may be superior to injectable silencers. The failure represents the latest twist in the competitive race over treatments for ATTR-CM, a condition in which abnormal protein buildup damages the heart. AstraZeneca's detailed autopsy of the study data was presented at a meeting of the European Society of Cardiology, according to STAT.
