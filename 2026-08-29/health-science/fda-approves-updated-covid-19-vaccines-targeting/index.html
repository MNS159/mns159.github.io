FDA approves updated COVID-19 vaccines targeting XFG variant for fall 2026

Health & Science desk

The Food and Drug Administration has approved updated COVID-19 vaccines for the 2026-2027 respiratory disease season, targeting the XFG strain—a recombinant omicron variant currently accounting for about 40% of U.S. cases, according to July data from the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention. The approvals cover four shots from Moderna, Pfizer-BioNTech, and Sanofi (in partnership with Novavax), each designed to prevent severe disease caused by the dominant JN.1 lineage XFG subvariant. Vaccine distribution has already begun, with doses expected to arrive in pharmacies, hospitals, and clinics across the country in the coming days.

The approvals are restricted by age and risk factors, aligning U.S. policy with most other countries. Moderna’s mRNA vaccine Spikevax is approved for people aged 6 months through 64 years with an underlying condition that raises risk for severe disease, and for all adults aged 65 years and older. Moderna also received approval for mNexspike, a lower-dose mRNA vaccine for individuals aged 12 through 64 with a high-risk condition and all adults 65 and older. Pfizer’s mRNA vaccine Comirnity XFG is approved for adults 65 and older and for people aged 5 through 64 with an underlying condition. Sanofi’s Nuvaxovid, the only protein-based, non-mRNA COVID-19 vaccine available in the U.S., is approved for adults 65 and older and for individuals aged 12 through 64 who are at high risk for severe COVID-19.

According to the CDC, underlying conditions that increase the risk of severe COVID-19 include asthma, cancer, chronic lung or kidney disease, diabetes, heart conditions, mental health conditions, obesity, pregnancy, and smoking. Dr. William Schaffner, a professor of preventive medicine at Vanderbilt University Medical Center, noted that the virus mutates similarly to influenza, requiring regular vaccine updates. “The better the match we have with what's in the vaccine and the circulating strain, the more effective the vaccine will be,” he said. Dr. Peter Chin-Hong, an infectious diseases specialist at the University of California, San Francisco, identified four groups at highest risk: people aged 65 and older, very immunocompromised individuals, pregnant women, and the very young (six months to 23 months). More than 1.2 million Americans have died from COVID-19 since the pandemic began, the CDC reports.

Most insurance plans are expected to cover the updated shots at no cost. America’s Health Insurance Plans has said its members will cover the total cost of vaccines recommended by the CDC’s Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices through at least the end of 2027. CVS is currently advertising free COVID-19 vaccines with most insurance. However, the ACIP has not met to make formal recommendations for this season’s vaccines after a federal judge stayed Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr.’s appointments to the panel, essentially disbanding it. Medical groups including the American Academy of Pediatrics and the Infectious Diseases Society of America have strongly supported vaccination. Public health specialists say it is safe to receive a COVID-19 vaccine at the same time as a flu shot or RSV vaccine.

According to the CDC, COVID-19 cases are “growing or likely growing” in 48 states as of late August. While immunity from prior vaccination or infection can wane, the updated vaccines aim to reduce the risk of severe illness, hospitalization, and death. Moderna CEO Stéphane Bancel said in a press release, “COVID-19 remains a serious public health threat, responsible for up to 2.3 million outpatient visits and 250,000 hospitalizations in the U.S. last year alone.” The status of free access for uninsured individuals remains unclear following the end of the CDC’s Bridge Access Program in August 2024.
