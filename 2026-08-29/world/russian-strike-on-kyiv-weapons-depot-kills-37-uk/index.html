Russian strike on Kyiv-area weapons depot kills 37; Ukraine opens negligence probe

International & World desk · anchored on AP

At least 37 people were killed and nearly 400 evacuated after a Russian drone strike hit a weapons storage depot in the town of Myla, west of Kyiv, late Friday, triggering a massive secondary explosion that damaged dozens of residential buildings and a care home, Ukrainian officials said Saturday.

The strike, which Russia’s Defense Ministry said targeted an ammunition depot holding components and launch boosters for long-range drones, caused shells, mines and drones stored at the site to detonate. Kyiv regional head Tymur Tkachenko said 42 people were injured, including four children, and at least 50 residential buildings were damaged. Among the dead was Taras Didych, head of the Dmytrivka community, who was helping with rescue efforts.

President Volodymyr Zelensky said the depot “definitely shouldn’t have been there” and announced a criminal investigation into “terrible negligence on the part of those who stored explosives right next to people.” Ukraine’s prosecutor general’s office said it is reviewing the legality of storing explosive objects near homes and whether the unnamed company responsible had the necessary permits. Prime Minister Serhiy Koretskyi said the attack showed that a “lesson” had not been learned from a similar July strike on a munitions depot in Vyshneve; that investigation found a state-owned company had failed to comply with storage standards.

In addition to the Myla attack, Ukrainian officials reported that a 2-year-old was killed in Kyiv and a 14-year-old died in the Boryspil district in separate Russian strikes Saturday morning. The Ukrainian Air Force said Russia launched at least two missiles and 267 drones across the country overnight. In Russia, local officials said two people were killed and 25 injured in Ukrainian drone strikes on the Belgorod and Rostov regions, and the Russian-appointed governor of occupied Zaporizhzhia reported four injured in a strike on a shopping center. Russian online retailer Ozon said one of its logistics centers was hit in a Ukrainian drone strike.

Both sides have intensified long-range aerial attacks in recent months, with Ukraine increasingly targeting Russian economic infrastructure. Zelensky said the investigation into the Myla depot would focus on officials who made the storage decisions, adding: “Appropriate conclusions will certainly be drawn.”
