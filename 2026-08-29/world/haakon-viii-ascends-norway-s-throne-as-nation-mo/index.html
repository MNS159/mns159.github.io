Haakon VIII ascends Norway's throne as nation mourns King Harald

International & World desk

Norway began a period of national mourning on Friday after the death of King Harald V, Europe’s oldest monarch, at the age of 89. Harald, who reigned for 35 years, died at 06:35 local time (04:35 GMT) at Oslo University Hospital, where he had been treated since August 17 for haemolytic anaemia, a rare blood condition. His son, 53-year-old Crown Prince Haakon, immediately ascended the throne as King Haakon VIII, holding an extraordinary council of state meeting with government ministers and adopting the royal family's motto 'All for Norway'.

Thousands of Norwegians gathered outside the Royal Palace in Oslo to lay flowers and flags, with many mourners in tears. Norway’s public broadcaster reported that an estimated 10,000 people lined the streets of the capital to see the late king’s coffin transported from the hospital chapel to the palace in a public procession. The Norwegian Armed Forces honored Harald with a 21-gun salute from Akershus Fortress at noon. The king will lie in state in the palace chapel until his funeral, the date of which has not yet been announced, allowing the public to pay their last respects.

Harald was widely described as an inclusive, transparent and empathetic 'people's king' who modernized the Norwegian monarchy. He broke with tradition by marrying a commoner, Queen Sonja, and served as a unifying figure during the country’s most difficult moments. Prime Minister Jonas Gahr Støre, who laid flowers at the palace and signed a book of condolences after greeting the new king, said in a televised memorial address that the country was 'united in grief and gratitude' and that Harald's 'legacy lives on'. Oslo Mayor Anne Lindboe postponed her wedding, which had been scheduled for Friday, writing in a tribute: 'It's hard to imagine an Oslo and a Norway without you.'

European royals and political leaders paid tribute, including King Charles III, whose mother Queen Elizabeth II was Harald’s second cousin. Norway’s star footballer Erling Haaland thanked Harald for 'everything you meant to Norway' in an Instagram post, adding that it had been an honor to meet him earlier in the summer. Biteya Terfassa, an Oslo resident born in Ethiopia, told Reuters outside the palace: 'He made us feel at home and he made us feel loved. So he was like a grandfather that I never had.'

King Haakon VIII assumes the throne at a challenging time for the royal family. His wife, Queen Mette-Marit, received a lung transplant earlier this year after suffering from pulmonary fibrosis since 2018, and doctors will not declare her stable until next year. She also faced scrutiny after it was revealed she had a three-year friendship with the late convicted sex offender Jeffrey Epstein, for which she publicly apologized. Additionally, Mette-Marit’s son from a previous relationship, Marius Borg Høiby, was convicted in June of rape and other offenses and sentenced to four years in prison; he is appealing. A poll for the newspaper Aftenposten earlier this year found that support for the monarchy had dropped from 72% in 2024 to 54%, though royal experts said Haakon was respected and hard-working.

During the national mourning period, flags will fly at half-mast across Norway, Harald’s portrait will be displayed, candles lit, and moments of silence held at professional events. All non-work-related social events are cancelled or postponed. King Haakon is due to take an oath before the Norwegian parliament on Tuesday, swearing to defend the constitution and laws, and will later participate in a consecration service—a stripped-down version of a coronation, which Norway no longer holds. His daughter, 22-year-old Ingrid Alexandra, who joined him at the palace on Friday, is next in line to the throne as crown princess after succession rules changed in 1990 to allow firstborn women to inherit the crown.
