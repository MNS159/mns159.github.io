Trump announces US oil agreement with Venezuela; opposition cries foul

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts left

President Donald Trump announced Friday a new US oil agreement with Venezuela’s interim president Delcy Rodríguez, saying the US will have majority control of more than 65 billion barrels of proven oil reserves at no cost to American taxpayers. Trump wrote on his social-media platform that the deal was brokered by secretary of state Marco Rubio and defense secretary Pete Hegseth through a partnership with private business, and that it would more than double American oil reserves. The announcement provided few details on the structure, the fields or companies involved, or how the US would exercise control over the reserves.

Venezuela holds an estimated 303 billion barrels of proven oil reserves, the world’s largest. The Wall Street Journal reported Thursday that the Trump administration was in advanced talks to take a direct stake in more than a dozen oilfields containing 90 billion barrels, nearly a third of Venezuela’s energy reserves, in the Orinoco Belt and Lake Maracaibo regions. Bloomberg, citing sources, said one deal under discussion was a 100-year lease on several oilfields. Chevron and other US energy companies are expected to sign a deal next week investing billions in Venezuela’s oil fields, according to the Journal.

The agreement follows the US capture and removal of former Venezuelan president Nicolás Maduro in January. Since then, Washington has sought a stable flow of Venezuelan crude oil for US refineries while promoting American investment in the country’s deteriorated energy industry, which produces 1.25 million barrels per day. Trump is under pressure before November’s midterm elections over rising gasoline prices and has been looking for ways to replenish the strategic petroleum reserve. The policy is part of the so-called ‘Donroe Doctrine,’ a 21st-century adaptation of the Monroe Doctrine aimed at countering China’s footprint in the region.

The reports of a US stake in Venezuelan oil have sparked anger among Venezuela’s opposition, who are already critical of Trump’s failure to bring about genuine political change after Maduro’s removal. ‘It’s a land grab – a massive land grab,’ said one well-known opposition figure who asked not to be named. ‘This is not the United States of the Marshall plan. This is a rapacious, mafioso United States.’ Exiled former minister Ricardo Hausmann called the deal ‘unconstitutional,’ and economist Francisco Rodríguez urged the national assembly to reject what he called a ‘predatory deal’ done ‘at gunpoint.’ Energy historian Gregory Brew compared the arrangement to colonial oil concessions of the early 20th century, saying, ‘It sounds colonial, because it is.’

Venezuela’s post-Maduro government is led by interim president Rodríguez, a former Maduro vice-president, with other regime officials retaining power. The exiled opposition leader María Corina Machado, whose movement is believed to have won the 2024 presidential elections, remains sidelined, and no date has been set for fresh elections. Trump last month praised Rodríguez, saying she was doing a ‘fantastic job’ and that Venezuela was ‘not really ready’ for a vote. The agreement’s legality is contested, with critics arguing that the interim government lacks legitimacy to strike such a deal.
