Nepal-Tibet floods: missing number rises to 2,478 as rescue efforts continue

International & World desk

The number of people missing after catastrophic flash floods and mudslides struck the Nepal-Tibet border on Wednesday has risen to 2,478, according to authorities in both countries. Nepal’s disaster agency reported 1,924 missing on its side, while China’s state news agency Xinhua put the figure in Tibet at 554. The confirmed death toll stands at 579 in Nepal and seven in China, with Indian authorities reporting seven additional bodies recovered from rivers believed to be flood victims. The International Red Cross estimates that more than 93,000 people have been directly affected and are in urgent need of humanitarian assistance.

The disaster, triggered by a glacial collapse that sent rock and meltwater surging into rivers, devastated the Gyirong Port border crossing in Tibet and swept through Nepal’s Rasuwa district. Chinese state media reported that a rescue team reached the Gyirong complex for the first time on Friday, describing it as “nothing but ruins.” Rescue official Zou Mingqi told state broadcaster CCTV, “As far as I can see, it’s nothing but debris.” China has evacuated 555 stranded tourists and 499 Chinese residents from Tibet. In Nepal, more than 3,700 people have been rescued, but rescue operations were temporarily suspended on Friday after a barrier lake formed by debris began overflowing, threatening further flooding. Authorities later said the threat had eased but continued to monitor two lakes upstream.

Nepal initially declined foreign assistance, with Foreign Ministry spokesperson Lok Bahadur Chhetri stating, “Our security agencies are doing the search and rescue operation. For now we don’t require such help.” However, later on Friday, Foreign Minister Shisir Khanal reversed course, saying the country needs specialised and technical help for tunnel rescue and for identifying and storing bodies. Nepal has also requested bailey bridges from India and China to restore transport links after about two dozen bridges were destroyed. At least 777 foreigners are missing, including 517 in Nepal and 260 in Tibet, according to separate tallies. Among the missing are 90 U.S. citizens, 41 Australians, 33 British nationals, and 98 Indians, with many of the foreigners believed to be pilgrims en route to Tibet’s Mount Kailash or workers at hydropower projects.

In China, information about the disaster has been heavily restricted. Dramatic CCTV footage of the flood sweeping through Gyirong Port spread rapidly on social media worldwide, but only a single still frame was broadcast by China’s state-run news bulletin. The International Campaign for Tibet said Beijing had been removing videos and first-hand information from social media. Foreign journalists cannot travel to Tibet without government permission, and the BBC reported that it is “near-impossible” to contact people in the region. The Communist Party issued a notice urging local party members to “make every effort to maintain social order and ensure stability and public confidence in the disaster areas.” Chinese Premier Li Qiang was dispatched to the region, and President Xi Jinping chaired an emergency meeting. The World Health Organization has warned of increased risk of infectious diseases, and the European Union pledged €2 million ($2.3 million) in humanitarian aid to Nepal. The UN Office for Disaster Risk Reduction cautioned that “the disaster is not yet completely over.”
