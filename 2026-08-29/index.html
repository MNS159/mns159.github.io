International & World Disaster

Nepal-Tibet floods: missing number rises to 2,478 as rescue efforts continue

International & World desk

The number of people missing after catastrophic flash floods and mudslides struck the Nepal-Tibet border on Wednesday has risen to 2,478, according to authorities in both countries. Nepal’s disaster agency reported 1,924 missing on its side, while China’s state news agency Xinhua put the figure in Tibet at 554. The confirmed death toll stands at 579 in Nepal and seven in China, with Indian authorities reporting seven additional bodies recovered from rivers believed to be flood victims. The International Red Cross estimates that more than 93,000 people have been directly affected and are in urgent need of humanitarian assistance.

The disaster, triggered by a glacial collapse that sent rock and meltwater surging into rivers, devastated the Gyirong Port border crossing in Tibet and swept through Nepal’s Rasuwa district. Chinese state media reported that a rescue team reached the Gyirong complex for the first time on Friday, describing it as “nothing but ruins.” Rescue official Zou Mingqi told state broadcaster CCTV, “As far as I can see, it’s nothing but debris.” China has evacuated 555 stranded tourists and 499 Chinese residents from Tibet. In Nepal, more than 3,700 people have been rescued, but rescue operations were temporarily suspended on Friday after a barrier lake formed by debris began overflowing, threatening further flooding. Authorities later said the threat had eased but continued to monitor two lakes upstream.

Fed Chair Warsh Signals Rate Hikes May Be Needed as Inflation Persists

Financial & Markets desk · anchored on AP

Federal Reserve Chairman Kevin Warsh said Friday that the central bank may need to raise interest rates again if inflation does not show sustained improvement, upending market expectations for a steady policy at the September meeting. In his keynote speech at the Fed's annual Jackson Hole symposium, Warsh cautioned that this summer's better-than-expected inflation readings do not indicate that underlying trends have meaningfully improved. "We must be confident that underlying inflation is moving to our objective, clearly and at sufficient speed. Otherwise, we have work to do," Warsh said.

Health & Science Vaccines

FDA approves updated COVID-19 vaccines targeting XFG variant for fall 2026

Health & Science desk

The Food and Drug Administration has approved updated COVID-19 vaccines for the 2026-2027 respiratory disease season, targeting the XFG strain—a recombinant omicron variant currently accounting for about 40% of U.S. cases, according to July data from the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention. The approvals cover four shots from Moderna, Pfizer-BioNTech, and Sanofi (in partnership with Novavax), each designed to prevent severe disease caused by the dominant JN.1 lineage XFG subvariant. Vaccine distribution has already begun, with doses expected to arrive in pharmacies, hospitals, and clinics across the country in the coming days.

NASA's Roman Space Telescope to Launch Aug. 30, Will See 100 Times More Sky Than Hubble

NASA is set to launch the $4.3 billion Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope on Aug. 30, an observatory that combines the sharpness of the Hubble Space Telescope with a field of view 100 times larger — enabling astronomers to survey vast areas of the sky quickly and potentially transform the understanding of dark energy, dark matter, and exoplanets. Named for NASA’s first chief astronomer, Roman is scheduled to begin science operations in January 2027.
