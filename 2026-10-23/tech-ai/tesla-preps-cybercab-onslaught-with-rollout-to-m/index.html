Tesla preps Cybercab 'onslaught' with Dallas launch and multi-city rollout

Tech / AI desk

Tesla is preparing to expand its gold Cybercab robotaxi service beyond its home market of Austin, Texas, beginning with a launch in Dallas next month and a rollout to additional cities by the end of this year, Chief Financial Officer Vaibhav Taneja said Wednesday on the company's third-quarter earnings call. Taneja framed the push as part of a broader ambition to 'literally take over every part of terrestrial transportation,' adding, 'We're starting with getting people from one place to the other, but we want to basically own transportation of any kind on Earth.' Tesla also said it will begin selling Cybercabs to fleet operators by the end of this year.

Tesla already operates robotaxi versions of its Model Y SUV in small fleets in Dallas, Houston, Miami, Orlando, and Tampa. A broader Cybercab rollout would set up a sharper competitive fight with Waymo, currently the U.S. robotaxi market leader with more than 4,000 vehicles deployed across more than a dozen cities. Tesla has argued that its Cybercab is cheaper and easier to build, which should let it undercut Waymo and other operators on price — but the company still has to reach real scale outside Austin to prove that point.

Financial details on the robotaxi effort remain murky. Tesla CEO Elon Musk said the new ride-hailing business won't be noticeable in the company's overall financial figures until the second quarter of 2027, 'and probably a big deal by Q3 or Q4 of next year.' Tesla also changed how it reports robotaxi activity: its second-quarter shareholder letter included a chart for 'Cumulative Paid Robotaxi Miles' that showed a 36% decline from the first quarter, while the new letter replaced that with 'Cumulative Unsupervised Robotaxi Miles,' a measure of how far the fleet drives whether or not paying riders are aboard. Asked about paid miles, Taneja said the company is 'seeing utilization improving' but offered no specifics.

Taneja praised Tesla's engineering team for producing a vehicle that manufacturing colleagues consider 'one of the easiest products for them to scale in terms of manufacturing,' adding, 'So scaling would not be that much of a challenge.' The harder part, he said, is securing 'all the regulatory approvals' so Tesla can 'start deploying the Cybercabs as fast as we can produce them.' That echoes Musk's long-standing claim that regulatory approval, not technical progress, is the main barrier to robotaxis. Tesla faces at least one concrete regulatory hurdle: in September, just hours after the Cybercab formally launched in Austin, the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration opened an investigation into whether the vehicle complies with federal motor vehicle safety standards. The Cybercab lacks features current standards require, including a brake pedal and side mirrors; NHTSA has proposed removing some of those requirements, but that rule change is likely still months away.

Not every Tesla executive agreed on the bottlenecks. Ashok Elluswamy, Tesla's vice president overseeing autonomy, said the remaining constraints are 'not purely regulatory' but 'technology-driven.' He noted the Austin service has been gradually expanding its operating hours — from 6 a.m. to 9 p.m., then to 10 p.m. to 11 p.m., and soon to 2 a.m. — and still does not run on highways or in certain conditions, although it has been observed operating in heavy rain in Austin, something Waymo's robotaxis have largely avoided. Elluswamy said Tesla plans to tackle more challenging driving scenarios by the end of this year before scaling further. 'We just want to make sure that there's not a single safety incident. That is our operating principle. And even if there's the slightest possible risk at all, we don't like to take on that risk,' he said.

Taneja's 'terrestrial transportation' comments come after Tesla previously showed a concept version of a multi-passenger vehicle called the 'Robovan,' though it is unclear whether his broader remarks signal work on other kinds of transportation. For now, the near-term focus is proving the Cybercab can expand beyond Austin and reach the scale needed to challenge Waymo, with Dallas serving as the next test market.
