Trump staffer Britt McHenry throws drink at fan during NBA game, ejected

Sports desk

Britt McHenry, a State Department press adviser and former ESPN reporter, was escorted out of Capital One Arena in Washington, D.C., on Wednesday night after throwing a drink at a fan during the Washington Wizards' home opener against the Milwaukee Bucks. Video obtained by TMZ shows McHenry tossing a beverage at someone in the crowd, and bystanders recorded her exit as she was booed and had rally towels thrown in her direction. McHenry, 40, blew kisses to the crowd as she left.

The confrontation began after a group of men behind McHenry and her male guest began heckling Bucks player Kyle Kuzma and disparaging his fiancée, model Winnie Harlow, according to a source cited by Page Six. The source said McHenry, who had been drinking, turned around and told the heckler to 'ease up,' sparking a verbal dispute. The men then allegedly threw 'misogynistic' insults at McHenry and threw a drink on her, prompting her to retaliate with her own drink, the source added. TMZ's sources said the group behind McHenry made inappropriate and misogynistic comments to instigate her, but did not recall exactly what prompted her to throw the drink.

Video shows McHenry arguing with stadium employees, yelling 'What'd I do?' while staff pointed to the front doors. Page Six's source said both McHenry and the man she fought with were escorted out by stadium staff. However, sources close to McHenry told TMZ that she was not kicked out, but left on her own to go to a bar, while her male guest stayed and met up with her after the game. The Wizards won the game 128-116.

McHenry previously worked as an ESPN correspondent for just over three years before the network suspended her in April 2015 after a video surfaced of her berating a tow truck employee. In that video, she told the employee they had 'no education' or 'skillset,' and said, 'Lose some weight, baby girl.' She was fired by ESPN in April 2017. In an August 2024 podcast, McHenry said the video was 'highly edited' and 'doctored,' but maintained her behavior was justified, saying, 'I will always kinda own it, but I look back, and I don't think that video is that big of a deal.'

After leaving ESPN, McHenry joined Fox News as a commentator and later co-hosted a show on Fox Nation. In December 2019, she sued Fox News and her former co-host George 'Tyrus' Murdoch, alleging he sexually harassed her through explicit text messages and lewd comments, and that the network retaliated after she reported the harassment. Fox News said it had fully investigated her complaints and maintained its actions were appropriate. The lawsuit was settled in July 2021 on undisclosed terms, and McHenry left the network. She now serves as a press adviser at the State Department under the Trump administration.
