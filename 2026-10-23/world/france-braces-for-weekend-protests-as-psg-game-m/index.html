France/Protests

France braces for weekend protests as PSG game moved and 70,000 officers deployed

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts left

French authorities are preparing for another weekend of violent protests, deploying 70,000 officers and reinforcing police with troops in a massive security operation. The demonstrations follow some of the worst urban violence in years that erupted on the sidelines of protests a week ago, with rioting and looting sweeping through Paris and other French cities on October 17. The Pittsburgh Steelers are scheduled to face the New Orleans Saints in an NFL regular-season game at the Stade de France on Sunday, about 10km from Place de la Bastille, where Saturday's protests are expected to begin.

Paris Saint-Germain's home Ligue 1 game against Lyon, originally scheduled for Sunday, has been postponed until Monday at 8:30pm following a request by police. The French league announced the decision on Friday, noting that the police had issued a decree prohibiting the staging of the match on Sunday. The postponement means that reigning Ballon d'Or winner Ousmane Dembele will miss Monday's awards ceremony in London, where he and several PSG teammates are on the men's shortlist. Defending champion PSG and Lyon were level on 14 points at the top of Ligue 1 heading into the weekend. Toulouse's Ligue 1 home match against Troyes has also been pushed back from Saturday to Sunday at the request of local authorities.

According to Steelers quarterback Aaron Rodgers, the NFL would have cancelled its first regular-season game in Paris if the situation were unsafe. 'I definitely think that the NFL is alert to what's going on,' Rodgers told a news conference on Friday. 'This game means a lot to the NFL. If it was unsafe, then I'm sure we wouldn't be here.' The Steelers informed fans on Thursday that a fan event in the Bastille neighbourhood would not go ahead. The US State Department issued a security alert on Thursday night, calling on its citizens to avoid demonstrations and areas of unrest. The build-up to the game has also been disrupted by Storm Austen, which prompted Meteo France to issue orange alerts for much of the northwest, including the Paris region. The Steelers cancelled their training session due to bad weather, and the NFL also cancelled a fan event on Friday.

The protests began in late September when high school students in the northern Paris suburb of Seine-Saint-Denis demonstrated about the state of their school buildings and teacher shortages, angered by proposed government budget cuts. The protests spread nationwide as public sector workers joined to oppose planned pay freezes, and later widened to include those angry about rising living costs, petrol shortages and fuel prices. Demonstrations have frequently descended into rioting, looting and arson. Similar youth-led protests have begun in Italian cities, with many turning violent. Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni's government approved measures including sending 500 soldiers to Milan and increasing financial liability on parents whose children were involved in vandalism and looting. Under new rules, anyone taking part in a group of at least five people and endangering public safety during protests faces up to five years in prison and a maximum fine of 20,000 euros ($22,460).

In France, a new bill known as 'rioters pay' is being proposed by the government in response to the violent protests. The government is scheduled to debate the draft bill on October 27. If passed, those found responsible for vandalism could be required to pay up to 10,000 euros ($11,200) in compensation. In the case of criminal damage by minors, parents could be held responsible. A provision could also introduce collective civil liability, potentially requiring someone in a group responsible for damage to pay, even if they did not cause it themselves. French President Emmanuel Macron has said that 'nothing justifies' violence and that local officials could ban certain street gatherings if deemed necessary. French authorities have also announced curfews.
