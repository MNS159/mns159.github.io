Iran warns US forces as oil tankers damaged and possible attack looms

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

Iran is bracing for a possible new wave of US and Israeli strikes after the US midterm elections on November 3 and Israel's election on October 27, according to Iranian officials and media reports. President Donald Trump posted on October 8 that the US would not attack before the midterms, citing 'productive discussions' with Tehran, but may feel less constrained afterward. The New York Times reported Wednesday that the US and Israel have developed specific plans for a 'short but intensive operation' targeting Iran's nuclear facilities, energy infrastructure, and missile and drone arsenal, aiming to force diplomatic concessions by further weakening Iran's economy.

The Pentagon has instructed US Central Command to conclude preparations for resuming major combat operations in Iran, Axios reported in early October. Two US aircraft carrier strike groups are currently in the Arabian Sea, with a third, the Theodore Roosevelt, expected to arrive by late November. Trump told Newsmax on October 15 that he was considering a strike on Pickaxe Mountain near the Natanz nuclear complex, where US officials believe Iran may have moved sensitive nuclear material underground. CNN reported the next day that Trump is considering using a Massive Ordnance Air Blast (MOAB) bomb, though its effectiveness against underground targets like Pickaxe would be limited.

Iranian officials say they are prepared for another attack and plan to respond with strikes on US bases and energy facilities in the Gulf. Ali Asghar Shafieian, a media adviser to President Masoud Pezeshkian, told DW that while the US has the upper hand in military power and technology, Iran's strength lies in how it deals with crisis. He pointed to the rapid replacement of senior officials killed in previous attacks and the rebuilding of missile and drone capabilities as evidence of Tehran's resilience. Iran has told regional governments that US military bases and critical infrastructure could become targets if neighboring countries assist another US or Israeli operation.

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi warned that participation by regional states would result in a 'decisive and proportionate response.' In a post on X Wednesday, he said Iran is prepared to give diplomacy another chance if it sees strong evidence of a sincere US desire to build trust, but added that if the US chooses war over diplomacy, it will result in an 'even greater strategic failure.' Iran's latest proposal, sent through Qatari mediators, included an offer to reopen the Strait of Hormuz under Iranian control in exchange for the US lifting its maritime blockade, releasing frozen assets, and ending military actions. However, the Wall Street Journal reported this week that oil flows through Hormuz are approaching prewar levels as the US military escorts tankers, raising questions about whether Iran's leverage over oil flows is weakening.

Inside Iran, the prospect of renewed fighting comes amid severe economic pressure and growing political polarization. A doctor in Tehran, speaking anonymously, told DW that the economic situation has become extremely bad and that she expects authorities to restrict internet access again if fighting resumes. A woman in Tehran described anger toward regime supporters and said another war combined with domestic protests could increase the risk of street confrontations. Tehran-based political analyst Rahman Ghahremanpour told DW that Iran's preparations extend beyond missiles and drones to prioritizing internal security, including confronting armed opposition groups and preventing external military pressure from combining with domestic unrest. 'In the government's asymmetric-war strategy, the ultimate objective is to convince the United States that regime change through military action is impossible,' he said.

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, who is trailing in polls ahead of Tuesday's election, has campaigned on a platform of regime change in Iran. He said the new supreme leader is attempting to develop nuclear weapons and that there is only one way to remove the threat: overthrow the regime. Former Israeli military intelligence Iran chief Danny Citrinowicz criticized the strategy, arguing that more attacks will not convince Iran to give up its nuclear or ballistic missile ambitions and that Iran could retaliate with even more severe attacks affecting global markets. NPR reported that a person briefed on the matter said Israel had initially planned a strike for mid-October but the US ruled it out. Netanyahu has denied the reports of war plans.
