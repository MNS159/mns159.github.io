GOP presses to expel Ilhan Omar as DOJ probe deepens over name, finances and home raid

Politics desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum
✓ Facts
  • Homeland Security Investigations agents searched Rep. Ilhan Omar's Washington, D.C. home on Oct. 12, beginning around 6 a.m. EDT with 15-25 agents present for roughly five hours.
  • Agents left with 17 items, including multiple cellphones, notebooks, a children's Somali-language phrasebook, Omar's passport, and her husband's 1960s camera.
  • A federal court filing identified Omar under the name "Ilhan Nur Said Elmi" and listed "Ilhan Abdullahi Omar" as an alias; Omar was born in Somalia and became a U.S. citizen in 2000.
  • More than a dozen grand jury subpoenas have been issued to members of Omar's family as part of the investigation.
  • President Trump posted on Truth Social calling on Omar to "IMMEDIATELY resign," citing the name discrepancy, and has denied any involvement in the DOJ probe.
  • Rep. Randy Fine (R-Fla.) told Fox News Digital he plans to pursue Omar's expulsion from Congress if DOJ finds her guilty of immigration fraud; expulsion requires a two-thirds House vote.
  • A separate DOJ investigation is examining Omar's financial disclosures, which showed the value of two businesses tied to her husband rising from as much as $51,000 in 2023 to as much as $30 million in 2024, before being amended to between $18,004 and $95,000.
  • No criminal charges have been filed against Omar in connection with either the immigration matter or the financial disclosures.
○ Analysis

The story involves two distinct, ongoing federal inquiries into Rep. Ilhan Omar: one tied to possible false statements on immigration and naturalization documents, triggered by a home search and a court filing listing a different legal name, and a separate Justice Department review of her financial disclosures stemming from a sharp, later-amended discrepancy in reported business assets tied to her husband. Neither probe has produced charges, and officials have not publicly detailed what crime, if any, is alleged in the immigration matter. Expulsion from Congress is a high bar, requiring a two-thirds vote of the full House, and is constitutionally separate from any criminal investigation or citizenship question, which would itself require formal legal proceedings to resolve.

◐ Debate

Democratic

  • Rep. Ilhan Omar called the raid "an outrageous abuse of power" and "weaponisation of federal law enforcement to target political enemies."
  • Former President Barack Obama said the federal probe was "so appalling."
  • Omar argued President Trump has "everything to do with" the investigation, citing his 2019 comments calling for her to be "sent back."

Republican

  • President Trump said the investigation has "nothing to do with me" and called Omar "a disaster."
  • Rep. Randy Fine (R-Fla.) said he will demand Omar's expulsion if DOJ finds she is not a citizen or committed immigration fraud.
  • House Oversight Chairman James Comer (R-Ky.) said the committee's review found discrepancies in Omar's financial disclosures, including a winery tied to her husband that he said shut down "because of fraud."
Steelman

Across both camps, the rhetoric—resignation demands, expulsion threats, and cries of political weaponization—has outpaced the legal record: two separate federal inquiries remain open, no charges have been filed, and key facts (the reason for the name discrepancy, the cause of the financial-disclosure swing) remain unresolved.

Implications

The outcome hinges on two unresolved DOJ tracks—immigration/naturalization status and financial disclosures; any move to expel Omar would require a two-thirds House vote and would likely face Democratic opposition, while the dispute is already being used for fundraising by Omar's campaign and the DNC ahead of the midterms.
