Russia strikes Ukraine security service headquarters in Kyiv as attacks intensify

International & World desk · anchored on Reuters

A Russian drone struck the central Kyiv headquarters of Ukraine’s Security Service (SBU) on Friday, Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy confirmed. The attack, which hit the building on Volodymyrska Street across from the UNESCO-listed Saint Sophia Cathedral, injured five people, according to Kyiv Mayor Vitaliy Klitschko. Zelenskyy said the drone was aimed directly at the office of the SBU head, Oleksandr Poklad, and ordered an “appropriate, tangible response” that mirrors the strike.

The strike marks a major escalation in Russia’s near-continuous aerial assaults on the Ukrainian capital, which have entered their eighth day. At least 53 people have been killed and 134 injured in Kyiv city and the wider region since the intensified campaign began last month, according to authorities. The attacks have hit multiple buildings in the city center as well as warehouses and factories in the suburbs, including a Coca-Cola plant. The SBU headquarters, situated in historic Kyiv near the Golden Gate and the National Opera, is one of the most important government buildings in the country.

Ukraine’s Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha condemned the strike as “a major escalation that demands absolute responses,” noting that the nearby UNESCO World Heritage site was placed at risk. He called on international partners to “unequivocally condemn these actions and step up pressure on the Putin regime” and urged UNESCO to react decisively. The attack comes as Ukraine struggles with critical shortages of interceptor missiles, raising questions about its ability to defend key assets. The United States and Ukraine are discussing a possible visit by U.S. negotiators to Kyiv on September 6, a source familiar with the matter said.

The strike on the SBU headquarters is part of a broader Russian campaign targeting Ukraine’s energy infrastructure ahead of winter. In the Odesa region, a separate overnight attack killed one person and injured at least five, Governor Oleh Kiper said. Russia has added daytime attacks to regular nighttime strikes, disrupting daily life. In a separate development, Air Tanzania announced it is temporarily suspending flights to Moscow, citing a routine risk assessment, after repeated warnings from Ukraine that Russian airspace is becoming unsafe due to increased military activity. Ukraine has formally notified the International Civil Aviation Organization of its concerns.

Zelenskyy said he has instructed the SBU to deliver a retaliatory strike against Russia, with military support. “Our military will support this response,” he said. The attack on the security service headquarters, in the heart of the capital, underscores the vulnerability of Kyiv’s key institutions amid the intensified aerial campaign. The United Nations and other international bodies have not yet issued formal statements on the latest strike.
