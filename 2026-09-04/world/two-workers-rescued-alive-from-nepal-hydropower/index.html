Two workers rescued alive from Nepal hydropower tunnel nine days after floods

International & World desk

Rescuers in Nepal pulled two workers alive from a blocked tunnel at the Trishuli 3A hydropower station early Friday, nine days after a catastrophic flash flood devastated the region. The rescued men were identified by Nepal’s home minister as Sanjay Shah, 30, a mechanical foreman, and Kabir Maharjan, 45, a mechanical supervisor. Local television showed images of Shah, covered in mud, being carried from the site on a rescuer’s shoulder; Maharjan, also mud-covered, pressed his hands together in prayer as he was taken away on a stretcher. Officials said both were in stable condition and flown to a hospital in Kathmandu.

The rescue followed an extraordinarily complex operation described by tunnel expert Arnold Dix, president of the International Tunnelling and Underground Space Association, as “layer upon layer of miracles.” The tunnel entrance had been buried by an avalanche, making it unrecognizable. Using old topographic maps, satellite images, helicopter footage, and surface markers such as power poles, Dix’s team triangulated the entrance location. Inside, rescuers faced a tunnel blocked by mud and rocks larger than cars, still full of water. They used explosives — a technique Dix said they would “normally never do because of the risk of blowing up people” — and dug trenches to drain water. Finally, they created a small passageway through debris at the top of the tunnel. In the early hours of Friday, rescuers heard voices; after calling out “don’t worry, we’re here to rescue you,” they heard a faint “okay,” and soon pulled the two men out.

The flood, believed triggered by a partial glacial collapse on August 26, killed more than 1,200 people in Nepal and left over 5,000 missing, according to the country’s disaster management agency. China reported 21 killed and 541 missing on the Tibet side. Multiple hydropower projects were hit; at least 557 workers are missing from the Trishuli 3A site alone, with about 115 believed trapped in the main tunnel. Across 12 hydropower projects in Nepal, roughly 900 workers are missing, local authorities said.

The rescue has raised hopes that more survivors may be found. An army spokesperson said one of the rescued men told rescuers that other people remain alive inside the tunnel. Nepal’s Communication Minister Bikram Timilsina wrote on social media that officials are “waiting for the rescue of more and more people.” The Nepali Army is continuing search and rescue operations, while rescuers proceed deeper into the tunnel. Dix estimated they had dug about 60 meters from the entrance and needed another 150 meters to reach the hydropower station, heading toward what he called a “Goldilocks zone” — the uppermost level where survivors may be located. “We got two people out,” Dix said. “If we were playing at a casino, some might say it’s time to walk away, but we say game on, time to take on the house.”

Nepal has largely refused international help, but Australia dispatched a 15-member team of drone specialists on Friday, joining operations at the government’s request. Thirty-six Australian citizens remain missing. Jeremy Fewtrell, commissioner of Fire and Rescue New South Wales, said the team hopes to locate people still surviving. The rescue of Shah and Maharjan marked the first time survivors were pulled from a hydropower tunnel since the disaster struck.
