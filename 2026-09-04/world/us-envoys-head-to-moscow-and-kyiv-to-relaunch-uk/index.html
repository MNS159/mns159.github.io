Ukraine War

US envoys Witkoff and Kushner to visit Moscow and Kyiv in renewed Ukraine peace push

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts left

US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner are expected to arrive in Moscow and Kyiv over the coming days, marking their first trip to Ukraine and the most significant effort in months to revive negotiations to end Russia’s war on Ukraine, according to officials and statements from both sides.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said in a late-night address Thursday that Washington had confirmed the envoys would hold meetings in both capitals. "We expect to see representatives of the US president here in Kyiv," Zelenskyy said. "There will be a meeting with them in Moscow and in Kyiv. We are expecting a meeting with them in the coming days. It is very important. It is important that America continues to be active." Russia’s state news agency TASS reported the visits would take place on September 5 and 6. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov declined to confirm the report. A US official told CNN on Friday that the trip was being planned. Witkoff, Trump’s real estate partner, and Kushner, his son-in-law, previously visited Moscow in January but have never traveled to Ukraine during the war.

Russian President Vladimir Putin, speaking Thursday at an economic forum in Russia’s Far East, said there was a chance of reaching a settlement. "Is there a chance? In my view, yes, there is," Putin said. He added that a number of countries, including the US and China, were ready to support a peace deal but that the conflict must ultimately be resolved by Russia and Ukraine. Putin has previously demanded that Kyiv cede large swaths of territory, abandon its NATO ambitions, and drastically reduce its military—conditions Ukraine has rejected. Ukrainian Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha said he saw a "new dynamic" in peace efforts, with more active political and diplomatic engagement.

Despite the diplomatic overtures, fighting has intensified. Russian attacks overnight Thursday into Friday killed at least three people in Ukraine, officials said, including two police officers in Dnipro and one person in the southern Odesa region, where five others were wounded. In the capital Kyiv, a drone attack on a residential building injured three people. Zelenskyy said Russian drones struck a Coca-Cola plant outside Kyiv in what he described as "a clear Russian signal to America." Ukraine has meanwhile stepped up long-range strikes on Russian energy infrastructure and economic targets. A large-scale Ukrainian drone strike hit the Black Sea city of Sochi and the nearby district of Sirius, regional officials said, with fires reported at both locations.

In Washington, President Donald Trump said he wanted to see an end to the war. "I just want to see that end. I think we’ll get it," Trump told GB News in an interview aired Thursday. He has separately said he would ask European nations to reimburse the US for military aid previously sent to Ukraine and appeared to signal a halt in weapons sales to allies while the US rebuilds its own depleted stockpiles. Trump last met Putin for US-mediated talks in February.
