NBA imposes historic penalties on Clippers over Kawhi Leonard salary-cap scheme

Sports desk

The NBA on Wednesday levied an unprecedented set of punishments against the Los Angeles Clippers, stripping the team of five first-round draft picks, banning owner Steve Ballmer from the arena for one year, and suspending team president of business operations Gillian Zucker for a year. The penalties stem from a league investigation that confirmed the Clippers orchestrated a secret $28 million deal with star forward Kawhi Leonard through a no-show endorsement arrangement with the financial firm Aspiration, a scheme designed to circumvent the NBA's salary cap.

The league's 35-page investigative report, produced by the law firm Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz, largely validated the reporting of journalist Pablo Torre and his "Pablo Torre Finds Out" podcast, which began publishing the story a year ago. Torre's reporting, which earned a Pulitzer Prize, detailed how the Clippers used a fraudulent contract that included tree-planting carbon credits to disguise additional compensation for Leonard. The NBA's report noted that Clippers president of basketball operations Lawrence Frank kept contemporaneous notes of conversations with Ballmer and Leonard's uncle, Dennis Robertson, and shared them with the league, leading to a reduced penalty for the team.

The investigation revealed that the Clippers engaged in a "deeply elaborate cap-circumvention scheme" involving four entities, including the public company Daktronics. The Securities and Exchange Commission has begun probing Daktronics, and the company's CFO stated on an earnings call the same day the NBA's punishment was announced that the company is cooperating with the SEC. Torre noted that the federal government has powers beyond the NBA's, suggesting the story may not be over.

Reaction across the league was swift. Mark Cuban, who had been skeptical of Torre's reporting, tweeted "Congrats Pablo." Torre responded that he hopes people can now trust the rigor of his reporting process. The Clippers issued statements expressing anger at the league's findings. A former team executive described the situation as "a huge test" for Commissioner Adam Silver, and a current executive said the "integrity of the league is at stake." Torre said he felt "delight" rather than vindication, adding that the punishment shows that even the wealthiest individuals can be held accountable.

The penalties are the most severe in NBA history and include a one-year suspension for Ballmer, who will be banned from the arena for that period. The Clippers will forfeit five first-round picks. The league's report also highlighted that the Clippers' cooperation—particularly Frank's notes—mitigated what could have been even harsher sanctions. Torre said he was surprised by the severity of the punishment but noted that the report left no choice given the evidence.

The investigation did not address all potential lingering questions. Torre indicated that the SEC's probe into Daktronics, a public company, could open new avenues. He said he is most interested in what the federal government might do next, given its broader powers.
