Google DeepMind launches WeatherNext 3 AI weather model with hourly global forecasts

Tech / AI desk

Google DeepMind and Google Research have introduced WeatherNext 3, an AI weather forecasting model that generates hourly, high-resolution global forecasts and, according to independent evaluations by startup Brightband, is the most advanced and accurate global weather AI model to date. On Brightband's Operational WeatherBench benchmark, WeatherNext 3 outperformed other deep-learning models from Google, Microsoft, Nvidia and the European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts (ECMWF), as well as traditional forecasts from the U.S. National Weather Service and ECMWF. The model can predict key surface variables like temperature and moisture at 5-kilometer resolution, other surface variables at 10 kilometers, and atmospheric variables such as wind speed at 25 kilometers—roughly five times sharper than Google's previous model, WeatherNext 2, which generated forecasts on a 25-kilometer grid in 6-hour increments.

WeatherNext 3 produces a new forecast every hour by ingesting a mosaic of live geostationary satellite data, rather than relying only on numerical weather prediction (NWP) data, which carries a six-hour lag and can introduce biases for fast-changing variables like rain and surface temperature. It also trains directly on sparse weather-station observations, allowing it to predict what individual stations will measure and to be evaluated against ground-truth data. Daniel Rothenberg, an atmospheric scientist at Brightband, said that capability connects the forecasting task closer to real-world use: “Adding a capability where this model is now also predicting, say, what Denver’s airport’s weather station is going to measure on an hourly basis, just connects that forecasting task closer to the core.” Google calls WeatherNext 3 the first AI model to directly incorporate raw observations for a high-resolution global forecast, though AI weather startup WindBorne says its WeatherMesh 6 model has used raw observations from weather balloons and other sources since late 2025; Google says its forecasts are higher resolution across the globe, and both models still depend on national weather datasets.

The model also targets three known weaknesses of earlier AI forecasters: coarse spatial coverage, unreliable precipitation predictions, and dependence on formatted government datasets. WeatherNext 3 is 2.4 times larger than its predecessor in parameters, with decoder heads tailored to produce more useful outputs, and it now outputs dense gridded fields, discrete cyclone tracks, and station-level sparse forecasts. It can resolve rain and snow much better: Google reports precipitation forecast improvements of up to 60% against NASA's satellite-based IMERG data, 30% against MRMS data, and 10% against rain-gauge measurements for early lead times. The system also includes forecasts engineered for renewable energy, such as 100-meter wind speeds near turbine height, high-resolution cloud cover, and solar radiation levels.

The release continues a broader shift in meteorology from government-owned supercomputers laboriously solving physics equations to machine-learning models trained on decades of historical data. After the ECMWF released more than half a century of weather data in 2018, deep-learning researchers began building models that make predictions far more quickly and with comparable accuracy to government tools. “Weather is chaotic, and so small differences really start to perturb massively…Machine learning targets the problem we are really solving, which is approximate noisy physics from incomplete information and finite compute, and so it learns patterns from a lot of data,” said Ferran Alet, a staff research scientist manager at DeepMind. European and U.S. weather agencies are already using AI models in their forecast products, and the speed and low cost of those models promise to bring better forecasting to regions where expensive sensors and supercomputers have historically been out of reach.

Google says WeatherNext 3 will start feeding into weather information users see in Search, Google Maps, and Gemini, and will be available to users and researchers on Google's cloud platforms. “This is going to be the first time that some of the core variables feed and power a lot of the Google products,” said Samier Merchant, a Google senior staff engineer. The higher-resolution forecasts are especially significant for Latin America, Africa, and Asia-Pacific, areas that have been underserved by high-resolution forecasting because of the supercomputing costs of traditional regional models. Bill Gates has cited AI-powered weather forecasting as a crucial benefit of the technology, with better forecasts improving crop yields in developing countries, and Alet said higher-resolution wind, rain, and cloud-cover forecasts will make renewable energy projects more dependable. “At the end of the day, I think Google is about providing useful information to the user, and a lot of what users are looking for has to do with the weather in some way or another,” he said.
