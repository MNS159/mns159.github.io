Federal investigation launched into Tesla Cybercab as company seeks fleet buyers

Tech / AI desk

The National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) announced Friday it has opened an audit query into Tesla’s newly unveiled Cybercab, mere hours after the vehicle appeared on public roads in Austin, Texas. The investigation focuses on whether Tesla properly self-certified the autonomous vehicle, which lacks traditional features such as sideview mirrors, pedals, and a steering wheel — all required under Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standards (FMVSS).

NHTSA said it will “examine the process and technical data on which Tesla relied when certifying the Cybercab and related issues.” The agency noted that “Tesla does not appear to have sought an exemption from these FMVSS rules,” and will consider “the extent to which Tesla’s certification depended on determinations that certain FMVSS are inapplicable to the Cybercab.” The investigation covers an estimated 1,000 Cybercabs, though only 45 are currently registered with the Texas Department of Motor Vehicles.

The probe follows a similar pattern to NHTSA’s 2022 inquiry into Amazon-owned Zoox, which self-certified a steering-wheel-free robotaxi. Zoox eventually received a temporary Part 555 exemption in July 2026, allowing it to operate a commercial fleet of up to 2,500 vehicles per year. Zoox now charges for rides in Las Vegas. The Cybercab investigation raises questions about whether Tesla will be allowed to charge fares while the inquiry is ongoing; Tesla held a private event in Austin Thursday to celebrate the Cybercab’s launch and plans to deploy a robotaxi service.

Separately, Tesla released an online form Thursday inviting businesses to express interest in buying Cybercab fleets or providing infrastructure for its robotaxi network. The form asks parties to select options including “Cybercab fleet purchasing,” “mobility hubs and infrastructure,” “event collaboration,” and “other.” This marks a shift from CEO Elon Musk’s earlier vision of a Tesla-owned ride-sharing network or individual owners renting out their vehicles. The interest form suggests Tesla is open to third-party fleet operators to accelerate scaling, similar to Waymo’s partnerships with companies like Moove.

NHTSA is also currently investigating 3.2 million Tesla vehicles equipped with Full Self-Driving software, which could lead to a potential recall. Despite Musk’s status as a major GOP donor and reported tensions with Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy — whom Musk has reportedly called “Sean Dummy” — analysts do not expect favorable treatment for Tesla in the Cybercab probe. The investigation does not block production but calls into question the vehicle’s regulatory path forward.

The Cybercab is Tesla’s purpose-built autonomous vehicle, with an estimated price of $30,000 for future individual customers. The combination of a federal safety investigation and a nascent fleet-buyer program underscores the regulatory and commercial uncertainties facing Tesla’s robotaxi ambitions.
