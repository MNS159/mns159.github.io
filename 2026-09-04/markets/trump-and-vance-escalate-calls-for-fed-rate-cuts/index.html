Trump and Vance escalate calls for Fed rate cuts as inflation forecast signals further pressure

Financial & Markets desk · anchored on Reuters
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

President Donald Trump on Friday demanded that the Federal Reserve slash interest rates or else he will cut off trade with countries with which the U.S. maintains trade deficits, according to a Truth Social post reported by CNBC. The ultimatum came a day after Vice President JD Vance told reporters the Fed should be lowering rates, adding to pressure on the central bank as its own initial September inflation forecast shows a mixed picture: headline CPI is expected to ease to 3.3% but core PCE — a preferred Fed gauge — is projected to reaccelerate to 3.49%.

Vance, speaking at a White House press briefing Thursday, said the Fed's rate cuts would make homes more affordable. "Obviously the president cares a lot about interest rates," Vance said. "One of the main reasons he cares a lot about interest rates is because he wants Americans to be able to afford a home. When interest rates go higher, that means that borrowing costs are higher." He added, "We believe that the Fed should be lowering interest rates," calling it the "proper and responsible" response to recent inflation data, and said, "We're doing a lot of things to try to keep those interest rates down, but it would be nice to have some help from the Federal Reserve."

Trump's Friday post, reacting to a stronger-than-expected August jobs report (162,000 jobs added), urged Fed Chair Kevin Warsh to "get smart" and cut rates. "LOWER THE RATE OR I'LL STOP TRADING WITH COUNTRIES WITH WHICH WE HAVE A DEFICIT," Trump wrote, citing the president's authority under a Supreme Court tariff decision. The post also said, "A STRONG COUNTRY MEANS A LOWER INTEREST RATE" and called for the U.S. to have the "LOWEST RATE of any country in the World."

The Fed's inflation outlook, based on the Cleveland Fed's Inflation Nowcasting tool, shows headline CPI falling from a three-year high of 4.2% in May to an estimated 3.3% in September. However, core PCE — which excludes volatile food and energy costs — is forecast to rise from 3.3% in June and July to 3.4% in August and 3.49% in September, signaling what Yahoo Finance described as "Trumpflation" becoming entrenched. The Fed's preferred inflation measure remains well above its 2% target.

Fed officials have offered divergent signals less than two weeks before the Federal Open Market Committee's Sept. 15–16 meeting. Warsh, speaking in Jackson Hole, said "short-term interest rates are the predominant tool to achieve the dual mandate" and committed to bringing inflation back to 2%. Governor Michael Barr said Tuesday he would back a rate increase if inflation stays elevated, while Governor Christopher Waller said Thursday he is more likely to keep rates steady. Traders are about evenly split on the odds of a rate hike, according to CME Group's FedWatch gauge. The war with Iran, which drove a brief surge in fuel prices and pushed headline inflation to a three-year high in May, continues to test the Fed's patience as oil prices stay high.

Vance's remarks, reported by CNBC, add to concerns about the erosion of the Fed's independence under Trump, who has aggressively pushed Warsh's predecessor to slash rates and is trying to fire Governor Lisa Cook. The outcome of the upcoming FOMC meeting remains uncertain, with the bond market volatile and the administration pressing for lower rates while some Fed officials signal readiness to hike if inflation does not abate.
