August jobs report crushes expectations with 162,000 payrolls added, stoking rate-hike fears

Financial & Markets desk

The U.S. economy added 162,000 nonfarm payrolls in August, far exceeding the 53,000 to 55,000 economists had expected, the Bureau of Labor Statistics reported Friday. The unemployment rate held steady at 4.1%, as forecast. The August gain was the strongest monthly increase since March, and prior months were revised upward: July swung from a loss of 23,000 to a gain of 21,000, and June was revised up to a gain of 31,000, an increase of 11,000.

Job gains were broad-based. Restaurants and bars led with 59,000 new jobs, government education added 42,000, and manufacturing contributed 16,000. Health care, typically the primary engine of job growth, added only 13,000, below its 12-month average of 32,000. Information-related industries lost 23,000 jobs, with a 12-month average loss of 8,000, suggesting some impact from artificial intelligence. Average hourly earnings rose 0.3% month over month and 3.1% year over year, 0.1 percentage point above expectations.

The household survey, used to calculate the unemployment rate, showed employment rising by 569,000 and the labor force surging by 683,000, pushing the participation rate up 0.2 percentage point. An alternative measure of unemployment that includes discouraged workers and those in part-time jobs for economic reasons fell to 7.7%, its lowest since June 2025.

The strong report shifted market expectations for the Federal Reserve's Sept. 15-16 policy meeting. Traders priced in about 60% odds of a quarter-point rate hike, according to the CME Group's FedWatch tool, up from roughly 50-50 the day before. Stock futures moved lower, with the Dow Jones Industrial Average falling 0.3% and the S&P 500 declining 0.1% in early trading. The tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite traded near flat. Treasury yields rose, with the 10-year yield increasing to 4.77%.

President Donald Trump called the report a 'great jobs number' but urged the Fed to cut rates, posting on social media: 'The Fed Board, with its great new leader, must get smart - BE PATRIOTS for a change. High interest rates put the U.S.A. at a very unfair disadvantage, and I won't allow that to happen!' He also threatened to stop trading with countries with which the U.S. has a deficit unless the Fed lowers rates.

The jobs report sets the stage for next week's Bureau of Labor Statistics readings on producer and consumer prices, scheduled for Thursday and Friday, respectively. Fed officials have indicated they are in a data-dependent mode. Governor Christopher Waller said he would favor staying on hold if inflation moderates on a monthly basis, while New York Fed President John Williams told CNBC he is in 'wait-and-see' mode. Governor Michael Barr also said he would be content to stay on hold as long as inflation is 'moderating,' but both said they would be ready to raise if data do not cooperate.
