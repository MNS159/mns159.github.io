FDA approves GSK's bepirovirsen (Hibsago) for chronic hepatitis B, offering functional cure

Health & Science desk · anchored on Reuters

The U.S. Food and Drug Administration on Tuesday approved GSK's bepirovirsen, to be marketed as Hibsago, for the treatment of chronic hepatitis B virus (HBV) infection in adults without cirrhosis. The drug is the first antisense oligonucleotide approved for chronic hepatitis B and is indicated for patients who have maintained viral suppression on nucleos(t)ide analogue reverse transcriptase inhibitor (Nrtl) therapy for at least six months and have hepatitis B surface antigen levels at or below 3000 IU per mL. The approval was granted under Fast Track, Breakthrough Therapy, and Priority Review designations.

Hibsago is administered as a weekly subcutaneous injection for up to 24 weeks alongside existing oral antivirals. It targets viral RNA directly, binding to complementary messenger RNA and recruiting RNase H1 enzymes to degrade the viral transcripts, thereby reducing viral load and surface antigen levels. In clinical trials, 24% of patients receiving bepirovirsen met the criteria to stop all hepatitis B medications, compared with none on placebo. Among treated patients, 19% maintained viral suppression with undetectable surface antigen levels 24 weeks after stopping all therapy. For patients with baseline surface antigen below 1000 IU/mL, the functional cure rate rose to 26%. The FDA also authorized the Roche Elecsys HBsAg II quant II RxDx assay as a companion diagnostic to guide patient selection and monitoring.

Chronic hepatitis B is a long-term liver infection caused by the hepatitis B virus that can lead to cirrhosis and liver cancer. Standard treatments such as entecavir or tenofovir suppress viral replication but typically require indefinite use and have limited potential to achieve a functional cure, defined as reduction of viral DNA and proteins to undetectable levels. GSK licensed bepirovirsen from Ionis Pharmaceuticals in 2019. The company said it expects Hibsago to be available to U.S. patients before the end of the year; a list price will be announced after pricing and reimbursement discussions with payers are completed.

“For many people living with chronic hepatitis B, treatment can mean taking antiviral medication for years to keep the virus suppressed,” said Wendy Carter, DO, acting director of the Office of Infectious Diseases in the FDA’s Center for Drug Evaluation and Research. “Hibsago provides a new treatment approach that gives some patients the possibility of maintaining control of the virus after all chronic HBV treatment has ended.” GSK Chief Scientific Officer Tony Wood said, “For the first time, with Hibsago, US patients have the potential to achieve functional cure after a six-month treatment course, with the added convenience of the option of self-administration after the first dose.”

Hibsago carries a boxed warning for severe liver reactions and low platelet counts (thrombocytopenia), which can increase bleeding risk and require regular hematologic monitoring. Additional warnings include worsening renal function, systemic inflammatory reactions such as vasculitis, and risks from improper administration. Common side effects include injection site reactions, fever, fatigue, and headache. Patients will need regular blood tests before and during treatment. Analysts at Jefferies said the approval “validates the functional-cure opportunity” but noted that safety and monitoring considerations may cause debate about the drug’s real-world trajectory; they project potential peak sales of approximately £2.2 billion (about $2.95 billion).
