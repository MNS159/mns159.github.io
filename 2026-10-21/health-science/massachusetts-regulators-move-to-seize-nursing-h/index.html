Massachusetts regulators move to seize nursing home licenses after Boston Globe investigation; state blocks out-of-state sale

Health & Science desk

Massachusetts regulators on Oct. 13 moved to revoke all nursing home licenses held by RegalCare, a New Jersey-based company that acquired 12 facilities in the state after the pandemic and sharply cut costs, leading to a decline in care quality. The action by the state Department of Public Health follows a Boston Globe Spotlight Team investigation that detailed how RegalCare CEO Eli Mirlis reduced nursing and other services while directing funds to his own related businesses.

Despite the license revocation proceedings, RegalCare will not immediately lose control of its homes, according to DPH officials. The facilities will instead be subject to enhanced monitoring by the department for the time being.

The Boston Globe investigation chronicled the transformation of one of RegalCare’s Massachusetts homes, a 100-bed facility in Taunton. Before Mirlis took over, the home had earned the federal government’s highest quality rating of five stars in early 2020 and held it for more than two years. After RegalCare’s acquisition, the home saw steep cuts to staffing and services.

The state’s move to revoke RegalCare’s licenses comes as Massachusetts also blocked an out-of-state firm from purchasing nursing homes in Worcester and Fitchburg, according to the Worcester Telegram. The Telegram reported that the state blocked the sale, though further details on that transaction were not provided in the available sources.

RegalCare’s troubles in Massachusetts highlight the broader challenges facing the nursing home industry, where private equity and out-of-state operators have been criticized for prioritizing profits over patient care. The state’s regulatory action signals a tougher stance on facilities that fail to meet quality standards.
