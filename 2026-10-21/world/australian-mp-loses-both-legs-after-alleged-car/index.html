Australian MP loses both legs after alleged car attack, husband says

International & World desk

Australian independent MP Helen Haines has lost both legs after being hit by a car in what authorities say was a deliberate attack, her husband Phil Haines confirmed Wednesday. The incident occurred Monday afternoon outside her electorate office in Wangaratta, about 240km northeast of Melbourne, when a Toyota LandCruiser allegedly driven by 43-year-old Brendan Nugent struck her. Bystanders pulled Nugent from the vehicle and detained him moments after the attack.

Phil Haines told reporters in Melbourne, where his wife is being treated, that the "horrendous incident" caused significant injuries but "her heart and her mind are in good shape." He added that "the good news in all this is that she's alive," describing her as a fighter whose road to recovery will be long and difficult. Haines underwent surgery after the incident and lost one leg; as of Wednesday morning she had also lost the other leg, her husband said.

Nugent, a local from the nearby town of Tarrawingee known as an environmental campaigner, appeared in court Wednesday facing charges of dangerous driving causing serious injury, intentionally causing serious injury, and causing harm to a Commonwealth public official. Police said more charges, including attempted murder, may be laid. Nugent did not apply for bail and is due back in court in February. Court documents also show he was charged with cultivating and possessing cannabis. His lawyer cited "mental health concerns" and noted Nugent has a rare neurological condition called idiopathic transverse myelitis, but gave no further detail.

Victoria Police said the investigation, with support from the Australian Federal Police, remains ongoing and a motive has not yet been established. Commissioner Mike Bush said determining the motivation for the alleged deliberate attack is a "centrepiece of the investigation." Police said Nugent was not on their radar and there was no intelligence of any threat to Haines before the incident. Investigators are also looking into whether drug use or medication played a role and whether there was any personal connection between Nugent and Haines. Nugent's family earlier said they were long-time friends with Haines, describing the apparent attack as "devastating and unfathomable."

Bystander Jacob Evans used a tourniquet from a medical kit in his car to help stem the bleeding from one of Haines's legs. He and his wife Jackie were among the first on the scene; Jackie told Nine's Today program that Haines thanked them, saying "Thank you, you lovely people," as she lay severely injured. Australia's Prime Minister Anthony Albanese said he had spoken to Haines's husband several times since the incident and noted that threats to female politicians are marked by a "different form of vilification and aggression." Police said there has been a recent growing trend of threats against high office holders, with almost one person a week being charged for such offenses.

The attack has sparked debate over security for MPs. Federal police said providing individual protection for all 226 federal MPs would require "incredible" staffing levels, so protection is assessed based on role and threat environment. Australia's Governor-General Sam Mostyn, speaking at the same press conference as Haines's husband, called for a moment "to pause, and to choose to be better," urging Australians to "exercise our muscle of decency." Phil Haines thanked hospital staff and well-wishers in Australia and internationally, and asked those wanting to show support to donate to the Save the Children fund.
