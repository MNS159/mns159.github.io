Pakistan arrests man wanted by US over $126m postage fraud case

International & World desk

Pakistan's cybercrime agency has arrested Faheem Akram, a 33-year-old man from Khanewal, Pakistan, who is accused of running a counterfeit postage operation that caused $126 million (£94m) in losses to the US Postal Service. Akram was indicted by a federal grand jury in Florida last month on charges including conspiracy to defraud the United States, making and selling counterfeit postage stamp labels, and wire fraud.

According to the US Attorney's Office for the Southern District of Florida, Akram operated LabelsBank.com, a fraudulent website that sold counterfeit US postage at a fixed rate regardless of a package's weight, size or destination. The counterfeit labels, typically sold for about $2 each, allowed customers to ship packages at rates far below legitimate prices, resulting in substantial lost revenue and shipping costs for the Postal Service.

An investigation found that more than 5,200 individuals purchased more than 5.1 million counterfeit shipping labels through the site, which authorities have now shut down. Akram is accused of having sold fake stamps via the website since 2023. Earlier this month, the US Postal Inspection Service offered a reward of up to $100,000 for information leading to his arrest.

Akram faces one count of conspiracy to defraud the United States and to make and sell counterfeit postage stamps, five counts of making and selling counterfeit postage stamp labels, and four counts of wire fraud. If convicted, he faces a maximum penalty of five years in prison on the conspiracy and counterfeit postage counts, and 20 years in prison on each wire fraud count.
