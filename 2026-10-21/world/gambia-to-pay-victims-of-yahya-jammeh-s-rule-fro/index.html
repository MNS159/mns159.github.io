Gambia to pay victims of Yahya Jammeh's rule from proceeds of his seized US mansion

International & World desk

The Gambia will use funds received from the sale of a Maryland mansion owned by former ruler Yahya Jammeh to compensate victims of his regime, authorities announced Tuesday. The Gambia Reparations Commission said it received $2,573,414.10 from the forfeiture and sale of the property, which the United States had seized on grounds that it was acquired through 'criminal proceeds and misappropriated Gambian public funds.'

The commission said it was 'pleased to inform victims, survivors and the public' of the receipt and that it was committed to managing the funds 'responsibly and transparently, with due regard to the rights and needs of victims and survivors.' The $2.5m (£1.86m) represents the proceeds from the sale of the mansion, which the US secured the right to sell in 2022.

Jammeh ruled The Gambia for more than two decades after seizing power in a 1994 coup. His tenure was marked by allegations of human rights abuses, forced disappearances, extrajudicial killings, rape, and torture. A commission of inquiry found that widespread abuses resulted in the deaths of '240-250 Gambians and non-Gambians at the hands of the state or its agents.'

Jammeh lost the 2016 election to Adama Barrow. He initially accepted defeat then rejected the results, eventually stepping down in 2017 amid intense regional and international pressure, including the deployment of troops from several West African nations. He went into exile in Equatorial Guinea.

A national commission later discovered that Jammeh owned 281 properties in The Gambia, as well as the US property and one in Morocco, and controlled more than 100 bank accounts. The commission concluded that he had misappropriated more than $300m, far exceeding what he could have afforded on his salary.
