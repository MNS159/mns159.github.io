Russia releases American teacher Stephen Hubbard; Witkoff faces backlash for calling Putin 'humanitarian'

Politics desk
✓ Facts
  • Russia released American citizen Stephen Hubbard, 74, from Michigan, after he had been imprisoned since shortly after Russia's 2022 invasion of Ukraine.
  • Hubbard was sentenced in 2024 to nearly seven years in a penal colony, accused by Russia of serving in a Ukrainian defense unit in Izyum when captured.
  • The release was announced during a roughly three-hour meeting in St Petersburg between Russian President Vladimir Putin and US envoy Steve Witkoff.
  • President Trump said he had personally appealed to Putin for Hubbard's release and stated the US was 'not required to give anything in return,' calling it not a 'swap.'
  • During the meeting, Witkoff told Putin, 'You are a humanitarian also, sir, and I salute that,' a remark that drew criticism from lawmakers afterward.
  • Hubbard, reported to be in poor health, was expected to be flown to Texas for medical treatment.
  • The same St Petersburg talks covered other topics, including Oslo-negotiated peace proposals for Ukraine, a US-Russia diesel import deal (first shipment due in 2-3 days), Iran, a potential meeting in China, and Russian fertilizer exports to the US.
  • Trump linked his recent suspension of sanctions on Russia to the Hubbard negotiations, saying imposing them would have halted talks.
○ Analysis

Hubbard's release ends a multi-year wrongful detention and comes amid broader, parallel US-Russia engagement on the Ukraine war, sanctions, and trade. Unlike past releases of detained Americans from Russia, which were often negotiated through prisoner swaps, the Trump administration frames this one as a unilateral gesture following a direct presidential request. The release occurred inside a wider diplomatic meeting that also touched on peace-process proposals from Oslo talks with Ukraine's Zelensky, a new US-Russia diesel deal, and other economic and geopolitical topics — meaning Hubbard's case cannot be fully separated from the broader, still-unresolved state of US-Russia-Ukraine negotiations. Witkoff's on-camera praise of Putin as a 'humanitarian' became a flashpoint of its own, separate from the facts of the release itself, highlighting tension between diplomatic messaging and the optics of complimenting a wartime leader.

Steelman

Across the spectrum, the core tension is this: Hubbard's release is unambiguously good news for him and his family, but it occurred inside a larger, more contested package of US-Russia dealings (sanctions suspension, diesel imports, peace-plan diplomacy), making it hard to view the release in isolation from judgments about the broader negotiating strategy.

Implications

Attention will likely turn to whether the Oslo-originated peace proposals Witkoff raised with Putin advance toward renewed trilateral talks with Ukraine, whether the diesel and fertilizer economic arrangements expand, and whether Witkoff's rhetoric prompts further scrutiny of his diplomatic approach. Hubbard's case may also inform expectations about whether additional detained Americans could be released through similar direct appeals rather than formal swaps.
