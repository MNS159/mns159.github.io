SpaceX Stock Surges 9.5% on Earnings Beat; Musk Hints at Tesla Merger, Lifting AI Sector

Financial & Markets desk

Shares in SpaceX surged 9.5% in New York trading Wednesday after the company reported its first quarterly profit as a publicly traded entity, handily beating analyst expectations and prompting owner Elon Musk to float a potential merger with Tesla. The earnings, released after Tuesday's close, showed net income of $4.4 billion for the July-September period versus a loss of $1.1 billion a year earlier. Revenue soared 161% to $14.6 billion, topping the LSEG consensus estimate of $12.72 billion. Operating income came in at $2.8 billion.

The results, SpaceX’s first since its record-breaking June initial public offering on the Nasdaq, were fueled by what the company now calls its “Super Intelligence” (SI) infrastructure business. CFO Bret Johnsen cited “continued momentum in Connectivity and a clear inflection in our Super Intelligence business,” noting that SI segment revenue surged ninefold to $7.56 billion from $813 million a year ago, making it SpaceX’s largest business unit. Recurring revenue from the Starlink satellite internet service also contributed. The company forecast first-quarter 2027 revenue of $33 billion to $37 billion, far above the $22.2 billion average analyst estimate.

On an earnings call, Musk said he is considering a combination with Tesla, which would be the largest merger in history, and that both companies’ boards have created specific committees to discuss a consolidation. “I do think there's a lot of merit to combining the companies as we are pretty much in the superintelligence singularity, and there's a lot to be gained from cooperating,” Musk said. Tesla is set to report its own third-quarter earnings after Wednesday's close, alongside Meta Platforms.

SpaceX’s commentary on computing demand provided a powerful lift to the broader AI-investment universe. “Despite market concerns relating to financing for AI cloud providers this past week, we believe SpaceX's third-quarter … represents the first data point during the earnings season to support the bull case for AI cloud providers,” Truist Securities analysts wrote. They noted that “demand appears insatiable,” with SpaceX indicating it could rent out its compute capacity many times over if it had the infrastructure. The assessment helped ease anxiety about high interest rates threatening the business models of heavily leveraged AI data-center firms such as CoreWeave and Nebius, whose shares and bonds had been battered the prior week. Market analyst Patrick O'Hare at Briefing.com called the results “relief music to the ears of investors” in those names. Server-maker Super Micro Computer also contributed to the positive sentiment by reporting preliminary quarterly earnings above forecasts.

The tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite gained 0.6% on the day, with other space stocks such as Rocket Lab, Intuitive Machines and AST SpaceMobile also rising. In Seoul, shares of tech-infrastructure firm Elice jumped nearly 30% after tanking on its trading debut Tuesday. “Clear earnings beats and constructive corporate updates are helping to ease some of the AI anxiety that has surfaced in recent sessions,” said KCM Trade analyst Tim Waterer. He added that if Tesla and Meta deliver similarly strong results Wednesday evening, it “could go some way toward reigniting broader AI enthusiasm and giving the sector more sustained momentum.”
