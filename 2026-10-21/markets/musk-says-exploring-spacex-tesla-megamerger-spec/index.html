Musk says exploring SpaceX-Tesla merger; special committees formed

Financial & Markets desk

Elon Musk said Tuesday that a merger between his two companies, SpaceX and Tesla, would be a "good idea," and that the boards of both firms have formed special committees to discuss a potential tie-up. Musk made the remarks on a conference call after SpaceX reported third-quarter earnings that beat Wall Street estimates, with revenue of $14.58 billion against expectations of $12.72 billion and adjusted earnings per share of 45 cents versus the 13 cents forecast.

SpaceX's third-quarter results showed net income of $4.4 billion, swinging from a net loss of $1.05 billion a year earlier, and operating income of $2.8 billion. The company's connectivity segment, anchored by the Starlink satellite-internet service, remains the primary profit driver: operating income in the unit roughly doubled to $2.37 billion. Starlink's subscriber base grew to 13.6 million from 12 million in the prior quarter, and total connectivity revenue jumped 69% to $5.25 billion, beating the $4.84 billion analysts expected. Consumer Starlink subscribers contributed $3.12 billion, while government and enterprise customers generated $2.13 billion.

SpaceX's artificial-intelligence business, which it now calls "Super Intelligence" and which was built largely through the acquisition of Musk's AI lab xAI earlier this year, has become the company's largest revenue source. AI revenue surged ninefold to $7.56 billion from $813 million a year earlier, above the $6.47 billion analysts had forecast. During the quarter, SpaceX also closed its $60 billion purchase of AI coding startup Cursor. The AI unit swung to a profit of $431 million. By contrast, SpaceX's launch business posted only a slight sales increase to $1.77 billion from $1.67 billion, and the space division recorded a $7 million loss.

CFO Bret Johnsen said on the call that SpaceX expects first-quarter 2027 revenue of $33 billion to $37 billion, far exceeding the average analyst estimate of $22.2 billion, citing "increased visibility into our compute contract pipeline over the next six months." Musk added that annual recurring revenue by the end of next year could reach between $500 billion and $1 trillion, a figure that dwarfs analysts' current projection of roughly $110 billion for full-year 2027 revenue. Analysts expect full-year 2027 revenue to be up more than 100% from this year.

SpaceX has also been building momentum for a U.S. mobile network after announcing the purchase of 800 MHz of low-band spectrum and receiving Federal Communications Commission approval for a 15,000-satellite constellation. Tesla is scheduled to report its own third-quarter results after the bell on Wednesday.
