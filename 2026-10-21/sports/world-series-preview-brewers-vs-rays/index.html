World Series preview: Brewers vs. Rays

Sports desk

The Milwaukee Brewers and Tampa Bay Rays will open the 2026 World Series on Friday, Oct. 23 at American Family Field in Milwaukee, with first pitch scheduled for 8 p.m. ET. Both franchises are seeking their first World Series title. The Brewers (103-59) had the best record in baseball and swept the two-time defending champion Los Angeles Dodgers in the National League Championship Series. The Rays (98-64) won the American League pennant by defeating the Cleveland Guardians in six games. Milwaukee won two of three regular-season meetings between the teams.

Milwaukee enters as a -162 favorite on the money line, with Tampa Bay at +134, according to DraftKings odds. The over/under is set at 6 runs. The expected starting pitchers are right-handers Jacob Misiorowski (16-5, 1.80 ERA) for the Brewers and Drew Rasmussen (16-5, 2.67 ERA) for the Rays, though neither team has confirmed its rotation. SportsLine's computer model, which simulates each game 10,000 times, projects 8.1 combined runs and recommends the over, citing that the over hit in each of the last two head-to-head meetings and in 68% of simulations.

The Brewers' offense scored 832 runs (5.14 per game) during the regular season, second-most in the majors, while leading the league in batting average (.258) and on-base percentage (.341). Though they tied for 28th in home runs (154), they ranked fifth in doubles (279), sixth in triples (26) and fifth in stolen bases (162). Key hitters include catcher William Contreras, who is batting .367 with three home runs and a 1.157 OPS this postseason; outfielder Jackson Chourio, who hit 25 home runs in 127 games despite missing time with an injury; and first baseman Jake Bauers, who hit 26 homers during the regular season. The Brewers have hit 10 home runs in seven playoff games.

Milwaukee's pitching staff posted a 3.51 ERA (second-lowest in MLB) and ranked second in strikeouts (1,544) and fourth in WHIP (1.18). Reliever Aaron Ashby has not allowed a run in 12 1/3 postseason innings after striking out 116 in 89 1/3 regular-season innings. Closer Trevor Megill has five scoreless playoff appearances. The Brewers' defense ranked fifth in outs above average and sixth in defensive efficiency (.712).

CBS Sports MLB experts offered World Series MVP predictions. Mike Axisa picked Ashby, noting he threw 6 2/3 scoreless innings in the NLCS and could end a reliever drought dating to Mariano Rivera in 1999. Kate Feldman picked Rays third baseman Junior Caminero, who hit .273/.351/.455 in the playoffs and went 4 for 12 with a double, a home run and three RBIs in the latter part of the ALCS. Julian McWilliams selected Contreras, calling him "the heartbeat of Milwaukee's lineup." Dayn Perry chose Misiorowski, predicting he will regain his dominant form after 11 days of rest. Matt Snyder picked Chourio, citing his five-tool ability and leadoff role. DraftKings odds list Chourio (+600), Contreras (+650), Caminero (+750) and Misiorowski (+800) as the top favorites.

The Rays counter with a lineup led by Caminero, who at 23 is one of the game's top young stars. Tampa Bay went 46-39 on the road including the postseason. The Brewers were 59-26 at home. The series marks the Brewers' first World Series appearance since 1982 and only the second in franchise history since joining the National League in 1998. The Rays are in the Fall Classic for the third time in franchise history, having previously appeared in 2008 and 2020.
