LeBron James leads 76ers past Knicks on NBA opening night

Sports desk

LeBron James made his debut for the Philadelphia 76ers on Tuesday, scoring 14 points with nine rebounds and seven assists as the 76ers defeated the defending champion New York Knicks 106-100 at Madison Square Garden on NBA opening night. The 41-year-old James, entering a record-extending 24th season with his fourth team after moving from the Los Angeles Lakers in the summer, helped spoil the Knicks' championship celebration. The Knicks had raised their third title banner to the rafters before the game in front of a crowd that included celebrity fans Ben Stiller and Spike Lee, but they could not open their season with a win after the 76ers took the lead in the third quarter.

Tyrese Maxey led all scorers with 28 points for Philadelphia. Jaylen Brown, who also debuted for the 76ers after a high-profile trade from the Boston Celtics for Paul George, added 19 points, eight rebounds and six assists. Joel Embiid recorded a double-double with 13 points, 11 rebounds, six assists and three blocks. For the Knicks, Jalen Brunson led the scoring with 21 points, while Karl-Anthony Towns had 19 points and 12 rebounds. New York shot just 37% from the field and struggled in the third quarter, when Philadelphia turned a halftime deficit into a seven-point lead going into the fourth. A key sequence came at the end of the third quarter: after a Knicks turnover with 1.5 seconds left, Jaylen Brown hit a 3-pointer at the buzzer to stretch the lead to seven points, and the Knicks could not recover.

James acknowledged he is still adjusting to his new team. 'I could be a lot better,' he told NBC. 'Still getting my rhythm, obviously just a little bit over two weeks with the new squad. But I like the way we played. I think we played exceptionally well, especially defensively.' The 76ers held the Knicks to 37% shooting and overcame a slow start in which both teams shot under 41% in the first quarter. Philadelphia's bench also contributed, with Kentavious Caldwell-Pope and Anfernee Simons providing a spark.

In the opening game of the season, the Detroit Pistons rallied from a 22-point third-quarter deficit to beat the Boston Celtics 121-113. Cade Cunningham and Jalen Duren each scored 22 points, while Duncan Robinson (21 points) and Javonte Green (19) keyed the comeback by combining to go 10 of 17 from 3-point range. For Boston, Paul George scored 38 points in his Celtics debut, a record for a player making his Celtics debut, but Jayson Tatum struggled mightily, scoring 13 points on 6-of-23 shooting, including 0 for 7 from 3-point range.

In the night's final game, the Oklahoma City Thunder defeated the San Antonio Spurs 116-104 in a rematch of last season's Western Conference finals, which the Spurs won in seven games. Shai Gilgeous-Alexander, the back-to-back NBA Most Valuable Player, scored 32 points for the Thunder. Jared McCain added 20 points off the bench, hitting six of eight 3-point attempts. Rookie Aday Mara, the No. 12 overall pick out of Michigan, impressed in his Thunder debut with shot blocking and passing. For the Spurs, Stephon Castle scored 24 points, but Victor Wembanyama had a difficult offensive night, missing his first 12 field-goal attempts and finishing with 12 points, though he contributed 13 rebounds and six blocked shots. 'Obviously, this is the team that eliminated us from achieving our goal,' Gilgeous-Alexander said. 'But for us, I think it was more like we're just happy to be back. It's a longer summer when you don't win and we felt that this summer.'
