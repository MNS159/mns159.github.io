Critical PHP vulnerabilities prompt emergency patches; potential for widespread website disruption

Tech / AI desk

PHP developers are preparing to release emergency patches for two critical vulnerabilities that could wreak havoc on the internet if left unchecked. The security updates are expected to roll out today at 10AM ET, though the developers say they may release them earlier if the vulnerability details become public or there is evidence of active exploitation. The vulnerabilities carry a severity rating of 9.2 out of 10, and according to cybersecurity firm watchTowr, they are Remote Code Execution exploits that reportedly impact every PHP version from PHP 5 onward, potentially enabling attackers to run their own code on affected servers, alter websites, steal data, and more.

The impact of these vulnerabilities could be vast, with one security researcher estimating that more than 1.3 million internet-connected hosts are likely affected. WordPress security plugin provider Wordfence warned that every WordPress site will be vulnerable because they all run on servers that use PHP, which is no small number given WordPress reported last year that it powers more than 40 percent of the internet. Wordfence also warned that the fix cannot be installed using automatic updates, so site owners need to find out who is responsible for updating PHP to apply the patch manually.

Multiple cybersecurity organizations have prompted PHP administrators to prepare, with threat researchers at Sansec saying they expect to see 'mass attacks within 15 minutes of the release.' That does not mean attackers are waiting around, however. Cloudflare chief technology officer Dane Knecht says 'we are seeing early attacks emerging' against the platform's PHP sites, and that Cloudflare is temporarily updating its firewall rules as a stopgap until the fix is available.

The European Union Computer Emergency Response Team and the UK National Cyber Security Centre have both issued advisories that encourage any companies who cannot immediately patch their platforms to instead isolate their internet-facing apps from public networks or take them offline entirely until the vulnerabilities can be fully mitigated. Mobile operating system developer GrapheneOS says they have temporarily taken their user discussion forum offline because proof-of-concept exploits are already being developed and they 'don't want to take the risk.' According to PHP developer Gina Peter Banyard, patching is the only mitigation, and otherwise turning servers off 'might be the most sensible thing to do.'

Sources: Ars Technica·The Verge
