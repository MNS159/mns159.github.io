Binance loses $6.3 billion in record crypto heist blamed on North Korea

Tech / AI desk

Binance, the world's largest cryptocurrency exchange, suspended all deposits and withdrawals Friday after hackers stole roughly $6.3 billion in digital assets, in what security researchers called the largest cryptocurrency heist on record. The exchange put the "estimated total impact" of the attack at about $6.3 billion and said around $3 billion of the affected assets — mainly stablecoins that act as cash for the crypto market — had been frozen by industry partners. Blockchain intelligence firms TRM Labs and Chainalysis attributed the theft to TraderTraitor, a North Korean cybercrime cell focused on stealing cryptocurrency.

Binance said its initial investigation indicated that attackers compromised a DevOps engineer's laptop, which "allowed an attacker to gain access to a service container and submit unauthorized withdrawal transactions." In a subsequent update, the company said the investigation "has uncovered additional points of breach," and co-CEO Richard Teng said the attack was not a "one-click" action but a more complex process. Binance said it found no evidence of a leak of its wallet private keys. Blockchain analytics firm Elliptic estimated that the attacker converted about $3.3 billion of the stolen assets into digital currencies that cannot be frozen and was working to cash those out as traditional money.

TRM said most of the theft occurred in just 20 minutes, making it roughly four times larger than the previous record, when about $1.46 billion was taken from exchange Bybit last year in a heist also blamed on North Korean hackers. Elliptic, which also tied the Binance theft to North Korea, said some of the stolen funds ended up in the same wallet used in an earlier cyberattack on crypto exchange Bitget this year, when hackers stole $351 million. The hack escalates a wave of large-scale crypto thefts that followed several heists disclosed in September, prompting new concerns about the industry's exposure to sophisticated attackers.

Binance said "user funds are completely safe" and publicly committed to covering all losses from the breach. The company, which says it holds about $140 billion in digital assets, said it had reserved more than $6.34 billion of company funds to cover the incident — with $5 billion already held in the exact token assets that were impacted — and was buying more tokens to make up the remainder. It also maintains an emergency reserve fund of around $1.3 billion. During the response, Tether froze about $1.56 billion related to the hack and Circle froze roughly $800 million in Tether as it moved through its networks.

Early estimates before Binance's disclosure had suggested at least $2 billion was drained from the exchange's hot wallets, with some unconfirmed estimates as high as $5.7 billion. The incident is a major blow to Binance, co-founded in 2017 by Changpeng Zhao, who said he expects user funds to be "fully covered" but gave no explanation. Zhao's company in 2023 pleaded guilty to U.S. criminal charges for breaching anti-money-laundering laws and paid a $4.3 billion fine; Binance was also hacked in 2019, losing $40 million. North Korea, which remains cut off from the global financial system over its nuclear weapons program, has long used crypto theft to fund it. Peter Ward of the Sejong Institute in Seoul said the country offers "near-total protection to its hackers." "Things may get a lot worse going forward," he said.

TRM chief policy officer Ari Redbord said, "This is an extraordinary hack, for its size and for the response... Few financial systems have ever shown a theft of this scale." The breach raises questions about how a single employee's laptop could reach systems controlling billions of dollars, and whether Binance had recently undergone a third-party security audit; the company did not respond to requests on those points. Binance said it will publish an updated proof of reserves once remediation is complete and continues to work with law enforcement to trace, freeze, and recover funds. In a separate incident Thursday, prediction market Polymarket briefly halted trading after hackers stole about $2 million in user funds by compromising software from a company called DebugBear. The heist also follows an OpenAI report that one of its AI models swiped fake crypto on an Ethereum test network, adding to concerns about how AI tools could be used maliciously against the industry.
