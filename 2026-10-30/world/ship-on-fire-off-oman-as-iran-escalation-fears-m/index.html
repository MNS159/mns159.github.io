Iran Tensions

Ship fire off Oman stokes Iran-US escalation fears; oil prices jump more than 2%

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

A ship was reported on fire in the Strait of Hormuz on Thursday, the British maritime agency UKMTO said, as Iran reported shooting down another US drone and oil prices jumped more than 2% on renewed supply fears. The UKMTO, run by the British Royal Navy, said a captain had reported a blaze onboard a vessel spotted 9 nautical miles (17 kilometers) north of the town of Khasab in Oman, just off the strait. The agency gave no details on the cause, any casualties, or the status of the crew, and no information has emerged on damage or environmental impact.

Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) said in a statement on Thursday that three tankers had exploded and caught fire in the past 24 hours after hitting sea mines while using an "unauthorized" route. It is not known whether the case reported to the UKMTO is one of them. On Friday, the IRGC Navy said it had halted two tankers in the strait's southern waters - one in the eastern part and one in the western part - after they attempted to pass through an unauthorized route, switched off their navigation and positioning systems, and ignored several radio warnings. The IRGC accused the US military of supporting the attempted passage and blamed what it called hostile US actions, along with the vessels' disregard for Iranian regulations, for the incidents and fires aboard the ships. It also said another vessel that caught fire in the strait's southern waters on Thursday night had sunk.

The incidents are part of a broader escalation since the Middle East war was triggered in February by US and Israeli attacks on Iran. Tehran has sought to control the strait, a key transit point for global oil and natural gas supplies, and has attacked ships it considers unauthorized users. Oil exports through the waterway fell again last week, according to shipping data provider Kpler, and attacks on ships in the strait are at their highest level since the war began, according to the US-based Washington Institute for Near East Policy. The US Navy, which is enforcing its own blockade of Iranian vessels, has recently escorted tankers through the strait in the same area where the burning ship was reportedly sighted.

The reported ship fire coincided with Iran's claim that its forces downed another US drone near the strait. The Tasnim news agency cited the IRGC as saying a new, advanced air defense system was used, and that Iran's armed forces have destroyed 14 drones of this type and more than 50 MQ-9 Reaper drones since the war broke out. The claims have not been independently verified, though the US Congressional Research Service says dozens of US aircraft and drones have been reported lost or damaged since the war began.

The latest developments come as US President Donald Trump is widely seen as considering fresh strikes against Iran after the US midterm elections on November 3. Last week, Trump said "we may do another little Epic Fury," referring to the US-Israeli attacks on Tehran in February that triggered the war, though he gave no timeframe. A third US aircraft carrier and thousands of additional troops are currently heading to the region. As reports of a possible renewed escalation circulate, US Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth has reportedly fired the Pentagon's top military planner, Army Lieutenant General Brett Sylvia. If confirmed, it would be the latest in a series of high-level departures under the Trump administration; Sylvia's predecessor, Lieutenant General Joseph McGee, was forced to retire last October. According to a report in The Atlantic, no reason was given for Sylvia's departure and no replacement has been named.
