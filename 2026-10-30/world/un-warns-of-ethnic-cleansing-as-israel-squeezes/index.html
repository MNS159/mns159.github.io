UN warns of ethnic cleansing as Israel squeezes space in Gaza

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

The United Nations human rights office (OHCHR) warned on Friday that Israeli forces are deepening the displacement of Palestinians in Gaza, raising concerns of ethnic cleansing. The OHCHR said Israel has stated its aim to expand its military deployment area to 70 percent of Gaza’s territory, pushing Palestinians into an ever-shrinking fraction of the besieged enclave.

Over the past two weeks, the OHCHR documented the westward expansion of Israeli military deployment in Al Shujaiyeh, Gaza City, and in eastern Al Bureij, eastern Deir Al-Balah and eastern Al Maghazi in central Gaza. The Israeli army is encroaching past its so-called “Yellow Line” demarcation boundary, and the OHCHR said Israeli forces appear to treat that line as a kill zone, preventing Palestinians from returning to what remains of their homes on the other side.

In Al Shujaiyeh, at least two Israeli attacks were reported on displacement camps, and forces reportedly used explosive robots to detonate the few buildings left standing. The OHCHR said it received reports that on 25 October an Israeli drone dropped munitions on tents sheltering displaced families at the Al Shujaiyeh junction near the Yellow Line, causing a fire that burned for five hours. According to the Palestinian Ministry of Public Works and Housing, the attack displaced 200 families comprising 800 people, mostly women, children and older persons.

Ajith Sunghay, head of the OHCHR in the Occupied Palestinian Territory, described the displacement nightmare as both acute and chronic. “Those displaced before the announcement of a ceasefire have little to no prospect of return to the areas they fled, most of which are inaccessible because they fall beyond the so-called yellow line. And they continue to be displaced again and again,” he said. The OHCHR stressed that as an occupying power, Israel must cease the forcible transfer of Palestinians within Gaza, a war crime that may also amount to a crime against humanity, and must facilitate safe and dignified return.

Despite a ceasefire that took effect on 11 October 2025, Israeli attacks on Gaza have continued. The enclave’s Ministry of Health reports at least 1,574 people killed and 5,687 wounded since the ceasefire began, with total casualties since October 2023 reaching at least 74,570 killed and 175,808 wounded. The displacement crisis is worsened by Israeli restrictions on the entry of shelter-related humanitarian aid, leaving many facing a fourth winter in flimsy tents while still under fire. “Let this be the last,” Sunghay said.
