DOJ accuses Peltola of misleading court in Alaska 'Decoy Dan' spoiler candidate probe

Politics desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum
✓ Facts
  • Alaska's U.S. Senate race pits Republican incumbent Dan S. Sullivan against Democrat Mary Peltola, a former congresswoman, in a contest the nonpartisan Cook Political Report rates a toss-up ahead of Tuesday's election.
  • The New York Times reported that people connected to Peltola's campaign and senior aides at the Democratic Senatorial Campaign Committee (DSCC) tried to recruit Dan J. Sullivan, a retired teacher in a remote fishing village, to run as a second 'Dan Sullivan' candidate on the ballot.
  • Alaska's Republican Governor Mike Dunleavy ordered an official state investigation into the matter earlier in the week covered.
  • The Trump administration's Justice Department filed a federal lawsuit seeking to block certification of the Senate election result if Dan S. Sullivan loses, citing the alleged recruitment scheme.
  • DOJ has issued subpoenas targeting Democratic officials allegedly connected to the effort to recruit the second Sullivan, and as of Friday morning no criminal charges had been filed.
  • In a Thursday filing, DOJ accused Mary Peltola's campaign ('Alaskans For Mary') and the DSCC of misleading the federal court.
  • Peltola publicly denied any knowledge of the alleged scheme, stating she knew nothing about any candidate on the ballot besides herself and the incumbent.
○ Analysis

The dispute centers on whether Democratic-aligned operatives tried to put a second candidate named 'Dan Sullivan' on the Alaska Senate ballot to split votes intended for the Republican incumbent, Dan S. Sullivan — a tactic sometimes called running a 'spoiler' or 'decoy' candidate. The allegations, first reported by the New York Times based on texts and interviews with a dozen people, have not been independently verified by all outlets. Election law experts cited in NPR's coverage found no law clearly prohibiting the alleged conduct, even as they described it as ethically questionable. The controversy has escalated quickly: a state investigation, a federal lawsuit seeking to block certification, DOJ subpoenas, and now a court filing accusing Peltola's side of misleading the judge — all unfolding in the final days before a closely watched election that could help determine control of the Senate.

◐ Debate

Democratic

  • Mary Peltola stated publicly that she knew nothing about any other candidate on the ballot besides herself and the incumbent, and said she is 'not interested in tricking voters.'

Republican

  • Dan S. Sullivan and his allies have accused Democrats of trying to confuse voters by recruiting a same-named spoiler candidate.
  • The Justice Department, under the Trump administration, accused Peltola's campaign and the DSCC of being 'emboldened by the success of their reported plot to deceive Alaskan voters' and of misleading the federal court.
Steelman

Despite the severity of the accusations and escalating legal action, election law experts found no clear law broken by the alleged recruitment scheme, and no criminal charges have been filed — meaning the controversy currently centers on contested ethical and political claims rather than established legal violations.

Implications

The outcome of DOJ's lawsuit and the state investigation could affect whether Tuesday's Senate election result is certified, with the Alaska race seen as potentially pivotal to Senate control; further developments—additional court filings, subpoena responses, or possible charges—are likely in the immediate aftermath of the vote.
