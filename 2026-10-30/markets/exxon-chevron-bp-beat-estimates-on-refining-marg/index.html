Exxon, Chevron, BP beat estimates on refining margins; oil majors rally

Financial & Markets desk · anchored on Reuters

ExxonMobil and Chevron delivered third-quarter earnings that topped analyst expectations, driven by a surge in refining margins as geopolitical conflicts disrupted fuel production. ExxonMobil reported adjusted earnings per share of $4.23, above the Bloomberg consensus estimate of $4.13, while Chevron posted adjusted earnings of $5.28 per share versus the $4.88 forecast. Chevron’s revenue rose 36% year over year to $67.8 billion, exceeding the $60.55 billion consensus, and its U.S. refineries achieved record crude throughput of 1.09 million barrels per day with utilization rates near 98%. The strong results lifted shares across the sector, with Chevron stock gaining 2.2% following the report.

The earnings beats were largely attributed to higher refining margins, which ExxonMobil said would add roughly $4.5 billion to its overall results after a $4.1 billion boost in the prior quarter. The margin increases were triggered by ongoing wars in the Middle East and Ukraine, which have disrupted activity at critical fuel-producing facilities and caused prices for diesel and gasoline to spike. The returns echoed similarly solid profits at other Western oil groups such as Shell and TotalEnergies, underscoring a broad trend among integrated oil majors benefiting from the conflict-driven environment.

The strong quarterly performance comes during a broader earnings season in which nearly a quarter of S&P 500 companies have reported, with an overall earnings growth rate of 29.5%. If that rate holds, it would mark the third straight quarter of earnings growth above 25%. For the oil majors, the results reflect the continued impact of twin conflicts — the U.S.-Israeli war on Iran and Russia’s invasion of Ukraine — on global energy markets.

Investors responded positively to the results, with Chevron shares rising in premarket trading and ExxonMobil stock also gaining. The sector’s rally signals confidence that the favorable margin environment may persist, though analysts will be watching for any easing of geopolitical tensions or shifts in refinery utilization that could alter the outlook. For now, the integrated oil giants have delivered a clear demonstration of how refining operations can serve as a powerful earnings driver during periods of supply disruption.
