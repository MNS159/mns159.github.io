Alphabet's record ~$500B wipeout sparks AI spending debate

Financial & Markets desk

Google parent Alphabet lost roughly $500 billion in market value Thursday, its worst day on record, after disappointing third-quarter earnings intensified investor concerns about the company's massive spending on artificial intelligence. Alphabet's Class A shares closed at $283.82, down 12.64%, erasing approximately $499.8 billion from the company's market capitalization — the second-largest single-day market value loss in stock market history.

The sell-off followed Alphabet's third-quarter report, released Wednesday, which showed revenue of $127.6 billion, up 25% from a year earlier but below Wall Street's expectation of $127.9 billion. Earnings of $2.67 per share were down 7% year over year and missed the $3.00 consensus — a double miss and the company's first since the fourth quarter of 2022, according to Kiplinger. Yahoo Finance noted that on a reported basis, per-share earnings slid to $2.12 from $2.87, with an $8.7 billion loss on equity securities cutting $0.55 from EPS and a year-earlier $10.7 billion gain adding $0.68; excluding those investment swings, earnings rose about 22% to roughly $2.67 from about $2.19. Operating income climbed 30% to $40.6 billion, or about 17% excluding last year's $3.5 billion European Commission fine.

The bigger worry for investors was the cost of Alphabet's AI buildout. The company spent approximately $51.4 billion on capital expenditures during the quarter — more than twice the year-ago total — with much of it directed at data centers and computing infrastructure. Free cash flow was negative for the second straight quarter, at $9.07 billion, and long-term debt jumped to $125.1 billion from $21.6 billion a year earlier. Alphabet maintained its full-year capex forecast of $195 billion to $205 billion, with spending projected to increase significantly again in 2027, and Chief Financial Officer Anat Ashkenazi warned that Google Cloud could face near-term pressure on profit margins as the company relies on outside computing capacity to meet demand.

The pressure to show returns on that spending is acute because growth in Alphabet's core search business is cooling: Google Search advertising revenue rose 12% from a year earlier, down from 17% growth in the prior quarter, as AI-powered search tools reshape the industry. Google Cloud, by contrast, was the standout. Cloud revenue more than doubled to $30.7 billion, up 103% and above analysts' estimate of $29.2 billion, marking the sixth straight quarter of accelerating growth. Cloud operating income roughly tripled to $11.1 billion, and the unit converted about 36% of revenue into operating profit, up from about 24% a year ago.

Analysts were divided on what the sell-off means. Wedbush's Ygal Arounian, who rates Alphabet Outperform, said the quarter-over-quarter slowdown in Search ad revenue growth "outweighs all other points in the print," but argued the company is "well positioned" and that Google "will prove a winner as AI advertising evolves." Seeking Alpha contended the post-earnings drop "looks increasingly mispriced." Alphabet shares had already fallen about 27% from their record close of $402.62 on May 13, Yahoo Finance noted. Thursday's loss surpassed Alphabet's previous record single-day market value decline of roughly $275 billion in July; Nvidia's $589 billion drop in January 2025 remains the largest on record.

The Alphabet rout weighed on the broader market. The Nasdaq Composite fell 1.4% to 26,184, the S&P 500 slipped 0.3% to 7,720, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average dropped 0.3% to 52,342. Caterpillar was the second-worst Dow stock, down 9.3% for its biggest one-day decline since March 2020, after third-quarter revenue of $20.7 billion (up 17% year over year) fell short of the $20.8 billion expected; earnings grew 45.5% to $7.20 per share. Oppenheimer analyst Kristen Owen noted strong sales across Caterpillar's businesses, led by 75% growth in its Power & Energy division — which supplies AI data centers — and said management expects moderate year-over-year growth in the fourth quarter.

Thursday's moves came as investors weighed fresh inflation data. The Federal Reserve's preferred gauge, the Personal Consumption Expenditures price index, rose 0.5% from August to September and was up 3.6% year over year; core PCE, excluding food and energy, rose 0.3% month over month and 3.1% year over year. "Although the inflation data came in line with expectations, because it is higher than the prior month, it emphasizes the problem we face — inflation is not coming down to the 2% target," said Chris Zaccarelli, chief investment officer at Northlight Asset Management. A separate report showed U.S. GDP grew 3.4% in the third quarter. Futures traders priced a 78% probability of a quarter-point Federal Reserve rate hike in December, down from 84% a day earlier.
