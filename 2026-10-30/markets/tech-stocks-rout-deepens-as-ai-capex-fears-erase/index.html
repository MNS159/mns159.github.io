Tech stocks rout deepens as AI capex fears erase over $1 trillion

Financial & Markets desk · anchored on Reuters

US technology stocks suffered a severe sell-off on Friday as investors recoiled from mounting evidence that Big Tech’s massive spending on artificial intelligence is not yet translating into commensurate profits. The tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite opened at its lowest level in more than two months, falling more than 2%, while the S&P 500 also fell. The declines were led by Intel, which plunged as much as 30% after warning of a prolonged PC slowdown, and Amazon, which fell as much as 6% after reporting that its free cash flow had swung to a record negative outflow of more than $42 billion over the past 12 months, driven by a more than doubling of capital spending on data centers and equipment in the third quarter.

The earnings season has laid bare the tension between AI ambitions and financial discipline. Alphabet lost more than $500 billion in market value on Thursday after revealing that its AI-generated search summaries were cannibalizing core advertising revenue; its shares fell another 1% on Friday. Amazon’s spending on property and equipment reached about $77 billion from July through September, more than double the $35 billion a year earlier, while its operations generated only about $42 billion in cash, leaving a roughly $33 billion gap over three months. Intel’s finance chief David Zinsner said the company did not expect to generate positive free cash flow this year and forecast lower-than-expected fourth-quarter revenue, adding that the PC slowdown would likely persist through the first half of next year. The sell-off also hit social media and gaming stocks: Reddit tumbled 24% after reporting weaker-than-expected daily active users and warning that Google’s AI-driven search was hurting referral traffic; Roblox fell 16% after its daily active users dropped 9% year over year; and Pinterest declined 6% after missing revenue and earnings estimates.

The rout hit the Magnificent Seven hard: Meta and Alphabet are effectively in bear markets—down more than 20% from their 52-week highs—while Tesla is off 19.2%, Amazon 15.1%, and Nvidia 12.2%. Analysts said the results suggested that the entire sector was facing growing pains from the aggressive AI build-out. “The thinking is that these signs suggest that the entire sector is going to slow down,” said Chris Low of FHN Financial. The losses were compounded by a record-breaking $6.3 billion hack of cryptocurrency exchange Binance, which sent Bitcoin tumbling 5% to around $76,600 and dragged down crypto-related stocks such as Coinbase, Robinhood, and Strategy by more than 6% in premarket trading. Binance co-CEO Richard Teng said the exchange would cover all losses from its company funds. The exchange also said about $3 billion in assets had been frozen with the help of partners. Blockchain analysis firms linked the theft to North Korean hackers.

Adding to market pressure, oil prices surged on renewed geopolitical risks. Brent crude rose to $113 per barrel and West Texas Intermediate reached $104 per barrel after reports that the US may resume military strikes against Iran, threatening energy flows through the Strait of Hormuz. The yen strengthened against the dollar after the Bank of Japan held interest rates steady at 1.25% but revised up its inflation forecast, signaling readiness to continue raising borrowing costs. The dollar tracked Treasury yields lower as a fresh wave of selling in AI shares triggered a flight to safety.
