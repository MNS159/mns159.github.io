MLB lockout looms as labor deal expires Dec. 1; salary cap at center of fight

Sports desk

Major League Baseball's 2027 season is threatened by a looming lockout, with the current five-year collective bargaining agreement set to expire on Dec. 1. The last game of the 2026 season, in which the Milwaukee Brewers clinched their first World Series title, could be the final one played for an extended period. Owners have proposed a salary cap for the first time since a 1994 proposal led to a 232-day strike that wiped out the playoffs and World Series, and both sides are digging in for a protracted battle.

Management is pushing for a salary cap and floor to reduce payroll disparity, along with a restriction limiting any single player to 15% of a team's payroll. The players, who have opposed a cap for decades, are seeking expanded free agency and salary arbitration rights, nearly doubling the major league minimum, increasing revenue sharing from high-revenue teams, and imposing a tax on teams that fail to maintain payroll at a certain level. Interim union chief Bruce Meyer has likened a salary cap to "a nuclear bomb," and player representatives describe negotiations as "worthless" and a "charade," with no back-and-forth on core economics.

The sides have been negotiating since May but have found no common ground. The expectation, based on past statements from Commissioner Rob Manfred, is that owners will authorize a lockout when the deal expires. The last lockout, in 2021-22, lasted 99 days and ended on March 10, forcing a truncated spring training and a compressed 162-game season. This time, with a salary cap on the table, the fight could be more protracted. Talks historically do not intensify until the threat of missing regular-season games looms in late February or early March.

The looming lockout is already shaping the offseason. Teams face a flurry of deadlines before Dec. 1, including the Nov. 17 deadline for free agents to accept or decline qualifying offers and the Nov. 20 non-tender deadline. The 2021-22 lockout created a free-agent frenzy before the expiration, with clubs committing $1.4 billion in salaries on Dec. 1, 2021. This year's free-agent class is headlined by Tarik Skubal, Freddy Peralta, Michael King, Seiya Suzuki and Randy Arozarena, with 144 new free agents as of Thursday and dozens more expected by Monday.

Players have been preparing for a lockout and a fight on a cap for years. The union's executive subcommittee, which includes holdover Marcus Semien from the 2021-22 talks and newer members such as Chris Bassitt and Paul Skenes, has worked to keep all 30 teams united. "We have made massive, massive strides in just educating players, keeping players together," Bassitt said. "Now it's all 30 teams pulling from the same rope." Owners, meanwhile, say they are united on their cap plan, which MLB argues would benefit the majority of players who make close to the minimum—about 40% of players on 40-man rosters at season's end had less than one year of service.

The stakes are enormous for a nearly $13 billion industry. Commissioner Manfred has cited a $441 million gap between the highest and lowest payrolls as evidence of competitive imbalance, saying it "defies human experience" to ask fans to believe both ends have the same chance to win. With no deal expected by the Dec. 1 deadline, the sport faces a deep freeze that will impact when next season begins and could threaten the scheduled March 24 start date.
