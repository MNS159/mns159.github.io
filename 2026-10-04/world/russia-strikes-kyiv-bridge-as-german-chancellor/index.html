Russia strikes Kyiv bridge for second day as German chancellor Merz visits Ukraine

International & World desk · anchored on Reuters

A Russian drone struck the Pivnichnyi (Northern) Bridge in Kyiv for the second consecutive day on Sunday, injuring two people, as German Chancellor Friedrich Merz made an unannounced visit to the Ukrainian capital in a show of support. The attack came as Moscow intensified strikes on critical infrastructure, hitting a second major bridge across the Dnipro River that links the city's eastern and western halves.

Kyiv Mayor Vitali Klitschko said the strike damaged the roadway and overhead trolleybus wires, causing traffic jams. Video verified by the BBC shows a drone crashing into the bridge and exploding, leaving a gaping hole in a traffic lane. The Pivdennyi (Southern) Bridge was hit multiple times earlier in the week. A separate drone strike damaged a heating main, temporarily cutting hot water in the north-eastern Desnyanskyi district. Ukraine's Air Force reported that Russia launched 135 drones overnight, of which 125 were shot down. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said a warehouse in Sumy was struck, killing one person, and a communications facility in Dnipro was hit, injuring six.

Merz met Zelenskyy and laid wreaths for fallen Ukrainian soldiers. Writing on X, Merz said: "Ukraine is facing decisive weeks. That is why I travelled to Kyiv today with the message: You can rely on us." A new aid package worth more than €1.3bn ($1.5bn) was agreed, including weapons and support for Ukraine's energy sector ahead of winter. Al Jazeera reported the package includes $1.1bn in military aid and $394m for energy repairs. Merz was accompanied by his economy minister and executives from Siemens Energy and Diehl Defence. The pair visited the National Academy of Sciences, damaged by a Russian drone on September 28, as air raid sirens sounded across the city.

Russia's defence ministry said it would intensify strikes across Ukraine in response to Zelenskyy's comments about ramping up attacks on Russian oil refineries. Moscow repeated a warning for foreign citizens, including diplomats, to leave Kyiv. EU ambassador Katarína Mathernová responded on X: "The EU Delegation is staying. Threatening us will not stop Europe's support for Ukraine." German ambassador Boris Ruge added: "Amen - we're staying." Russian President Vladimir Putin said his military was carrying out retaliatory attacks following Ukraine's strikes on Russian energy facilities, despite Moscow having launched such attacks first. Russia currently controls about 20% of Ukrainian territory.

The two bridges are still standing, but damage and traffic restrictions have caused gridlock. About 500,000 vehicles use the bridges daily. Kyiv resident Roman told the BBC: "To get from the left bank to the right one and back - it's just hell." Officials are planning temporary pontoon bridges, ferry services, and a tunnel under the river. Klitschko warned the city is in a "very dramatic situation" as Moscow batters "critical infrastructure, logistics, housing" and other key facilities. Experts say repeated drone attacks are unlikely to bring down the bridges but could force their closure and weaken their structure.

Since becoming chancellor in May 2025, Merz has made Germany the single largest donor to Ukraine. He said the country "needs all our support in the coming weeks", adding: "If Russia is not stopped, its war machine will sooner or later turn against us." The visit underscores Western backing as the war enters its fifth year, with both sides increasing drone and missile strikes on infrastructure and military sites.
