Ukrainian Robot Offensive Recaptures Territory; Russian Drones Violate Moldova and Strike Kyiv Bridges

International & World desk · anchored on Reuters

Ukrainian forces have recaptured 176 square kilometers of territory in the Donbas region through a surprise robot offensive, dubbed Operation Vivaldi, which began in May, according to reports from CNBC and security experts. The operation, conducted by Ukraine’s Third Army Corps, has inflicted over 2,000 Russian casualties and taken more than 250 Russian soldiers prisoner, Ukrainian Brigadier-General Andrii Biletskyi said on September 28. Meanwhile, Russian forces escalated long-range strikes, hitting Kyiv’s Northern Bridge on October 3 and sending five drones, including Banderol loitering munitions, into Moldovan airspace, where they crashed, according to the Institute for the Study of War (ISW).

Operation Vivaldi involved the deployment of large bomber drones and remote-controlled robots behind Russian lines around Lyman in northern Donetsk Oblast, catching Russian forces off guard, security experts told CNBC. Patrick Bolder, a defense expert at the Hague Center for Strategic Studies, described the offensive as a "big bloody nose" to Russia’s minimal war aim of seizing the Donbas. Ukrainian forces secured control of the air in the area, denying Russia use of its drones, then used their own drones to drop unmanned ground vehicles behind enemy lines. "They put this together masterly," Bolder said. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy lauded the operation, stating that replacing warriors with ground robotic systems wherever possible is "one particularly promising area" and that Ukraine will scale up the use of drones for the most dangerous tasks.

Russian drone incursions into Moldova have become increasingly frequent, with five drones—including Banderol loitering munitions—violating Moldovan airspace on October 3, the Moldovan government reported. Debris fell in the Causeni, Hincesti, Anenii Noi, and Stefan Voda districts and near Crocmaz. ISW assessed that the violation, involving more aerial vehicles than previous incidents, is part of Moscow’s efforts to normalize airspace violations with increasingly intense and frequent incursions. Since August alone, seven violations have been recorded.

Russian forces struck the Northern Bridge over the Dnipro River in Kyiv City on October 3, the sixth strike on bridges since October 1, Kyiv Mayor Vitaliy Klitschko reported. ISW noted that Russian forces have targeted both the Southern and Northern bridges to degrade transportation and logistics across the Dnipro River, aiming to split Kyiv physically and make life difficult for civilians to force Ukraine to capitulate. The Russian Ministry of Foreign Affairs issued a warning on October 3, recommending foreign citizens and diplomats leave Kyiv and stating that Russian forces will continue long-range strikes. Ukrainian Presidential Advisor on Defense Technologies Serhiy “Flash” Beskrestnov reported that Russian forces used mesh network repeaters to maintain control of drone trajectories during strikes on the bridges.

Ukraine continued its own long-range strike campaign against Russian missile and drone launch sites, President Zelenskyy stated on October 3, reporting a strike on an Iskander ballistic missile launch system in Bryansk Oblast. Ukraine’s Strategic Communications Center added that forces struck a Russian strike drone launch complex near Tsimbulova, Oryol Oblast on the night of October 2–3. Russia launched 157 drones against Ukraine overnight and Banderol loitering munitions on the morning of October 3, according to ISW.

The developments underscore the evolving nature of the conflict, with Ukraine seeking to raise the cost of Russia’s war by targeting oil refineries and logistics hubs, as noted by CNBC. Security experts cautioned that Ukraine’s robot offensive, while significant, is not a decisive breakthrough but sends "a powerful message to Moscow and to Kyiv’s partners that the highly innovative Ukrainian military remains capable of defeating Russia on the battlefield," said David Kirichenko of the Henry Jackson Society. Serhii Kuzan, chairman of the Ukrainian Security and Cooperation Center, said Operation Vivaldi "demonstrated that the systematic replacement of human personnel with robots is changing the paradigm of modern warfare."
