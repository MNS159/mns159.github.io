Brazil Election

Brazil votes in polarised presidential election between Lula and Flávio Bolsonaro

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts slightly center-left

Brazilians began voting on Sunday in a general election that pits left-wing incumbent Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva against right-wing Senator Flávio Bolsonaro, the eldest son of convicted former President Jair Bolsonaro. About 160 million registered voters are eligible to cast ballots in the election, which also chooses lawmakers, governors and senators. If no presidential candidate wins more than 50 percent of valid votes, the top two contenders will proceed to a run-off on October 25.

Opinion polls show a close race, with Lula and Bolsonaro both hovering around the 40 percent mark and Lula holding a marginal advantage. The other 11 candidates are polling at 4 percent or less. With about 9 percent of voters still undecided or planning to spoil their ballot, an outright first-round win cannot be entirely ruled out, though a run-off is considered highly likely. Voting is compulsory for literate Brazilians aged 18 to 70, with a fine for those who do not cast a ballot.

Lula, 80, of the Workers’ Party, is seeking a fourth non-consecutive term. He previously served as president from 2003 to 2010, lifting millions out of poverty through the Bolsa Família cash-transfer program. He left office with record approval ratings but was later convicted of corruption and money laundering, charges he denied; those convictions were annulled in 2021, allowing him to win the 2022 election against Jair Bolsonaro by 1.8 percentage points. During his current term, Lula has pointed to reduced deforestation in the Amazon as a policy success, but has drawn criticism from environmentalists for supporting exploratory oil drilling near the Amazon river mouth. He has also had rocky relations with US President Donald Trump, insisting that “Brazil does not fit into anyone’s backyard.”

Flávio Bolsonaro, 45, of the Liberal Party, is appealing to supporters of his father by promising to continue his tough-on-crime and pro-gun policies, and to cut regulations on farmers. He has courted evangelicals and the agricultural industry, and has actively sought alliance with the Trump administration, promising to join the US-led anti-crime alliance “Shield of the Americas” if elected. After voting in Rio de Janeiro on Sunday, Bolsonaro said he wants to “free the Brazilian people” and that “Brazil needs change … it cannot bear any more suffering, such as high taxes, so many women killed.” He expressed confidence in a first-round victory.

The election is deeply shaped by the shadow of Jair Bolsonaro, who is serving a 27-year sentence for plotting a coup after losing the 2022 election and is barred from office. Under house arrest due to health problems, he is prohibited from posting on social media or making public statements, but an AI-generated avatar of him endorsed his son’s campaign. A scandal involving jailed banker Daniel Vorcaro, accused of orchestrating a major financial fraud, has pushed corruption to the top of voters’ concerns. Flávio Bolsonaro faces a police investigation for suspected corruption and money laundering over Vorcaro’s alleged financing of a biopic about his father. Alleged links between Vorcaro and Supreme Court Justice Alexandre de Moraes, who presided over Jair Bolsonaro’s trial, have also emerged; Moraes has denied wrongdoing. Lula has said “no one is above the law,” while Bolsonaro has told supporters, “Voting for Lula means voting for Moraes.”
