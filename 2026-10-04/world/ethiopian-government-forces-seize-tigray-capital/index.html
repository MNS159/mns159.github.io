Ethiopian government forces seize Tigray capital Mekelle as TPLF withdraws

International & World desk

Ethiopian government forces and allied fighters have taken control of Mekelle, the capital of the northern Tigray region, after the Tigray People's Liberation Front (TPLF) announced it had temporarily withdrawn from the city. The TPLF said in a statement late Saturday that it had 'temporarily moved out of Mekelle' to 'operate from somewhere else,' citing concerns about risks to civilians. Two residents told the BBC that pro-government forces now control the city and that TPLF forces have abandoned it; one resident said mobile phone services appeared to have resumed in parts of Mekelle. A humanitarian source and a Mekelle resident confirmed to Reuters that the city was under government control, and a humanitarian source told AFP that staff on the ground reported the TPLF had abandoned the regional capital, with Ethiopian federal forces and allied forces observed across various locations.

The recapture of Mekelle's main airport by Ethiopia's federal army and pro-government fighters preceded the TPLF's withdrawal. The development marks a major shift in the conflict, which erupted on September 23 after seven armed and political groups—including the TPLF and the Fano militia from Amhara—announced an alliance aimed at removing Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed's government and establishing an inclusive transitional administration. The fighting has spread across parts of Ethiopia's northern regions of Tigray, Afar and Amhara.

The renewed hostilities follow the collapse of a 2022 peace deal, known as the Pretoria Agreement, brokered by the African Union that ended a devastating two-year civil war between the federal government and Tigray forces. The AU's mediator estimated some 600,000 people died in that conflict, which drove the region to the precipice of famine. In the last six months, relations have worsened, with both sides accusing each other of violating the agreement.

The latest round of violence has raised fears of a wider war that could draw in neighbouring countries. Ethiopia has accused Eritrea, Sudan and Egypt of backing the opposition coalition; Khartoum and Cairo have denied involvement. On Friday, Ethiopia and Eritrea severed diplomatic relations after Addis Ababa accused Asmara of supporting the new rebel alliance, a charge Eritrea denied. Ethiopia also expelled an Egyptian diplomat, prompting Cairo to order an Ethiopian embassy official to leave within the same period.

The United States has urged Ethiopia and Eritrea 'to respect the sovereignty and territorial integrity of their neighbors, exercise maximum restraint and return to dialogue and cooperation,' warning that the increasing tensions 'have the potential to affect the entire region.' The US Department of State called for 'restraint' and 'dialogue' on all sides ahead of a diplomatic meeting in Egypt on Sunday with leaders from some of Ethiopia's neighbours. Egypt's President Abdel Fattah el-Sisi, addressing the African Union's mid-year coordination summit in El-Alamein, said Egypt opposed foreign interference in African states and called for relations based on 'mutual respect, good neighbourliness, dialogue and consultation.' The United Nations has warned that the deteriorating security situation in northern Ethiopia is severely disrupting humanitarian work and has called on all sides to guarantee safe and unhindered access to people in need.
