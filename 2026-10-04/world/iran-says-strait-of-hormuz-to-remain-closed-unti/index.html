Iran says Strait of Hormuz will remain closed until US meets conditions

International & World desk · anchored on Reuters

Iran’s parliament speaker, Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, said on Sunday that the Strait of Hormuz will not reopen until the United States meets seven conditions based on the Islamabad Memorandum of Understanding, according to the state-linked news agency Nournews. Ghalibaf stated that Washington had recently put forward proposals through a mediator, but warned that “the era of wasting time and dictating unilateral demands is over.” He added that Iran is “present on the military battlefield and will confront them with new surprises.”

Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi separately said that a plan put forward by Tehran could lead to the route reopening within seven days if accepted. Speaking to foreign ambassadors in Tehran, Araghchi said Iran is “serious about finding diplomatic solutions just as we are serious about defending ourselves.” He warned that if the US takes military action again, Iran is “more prepared than before and will take whatever action is necessary to defend ourselves.”

Iranian Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei said that reopening the strait has been Tehran’s main goal, while the US has continued to focus on nuclear talks. The Strait of Hormuz has been largely blocked and disrupted for more than seven months since the US and Israel launched strikes on Iran on February 28, causing one of the worst maritime shipping disruptions in decades. The waterway normally handles about one-fifth of global oil and natural gas shipments.

US President Donald Trump ruled out an Iranian proposal to reopen the strait last week. On Sunday, Trump told reporters that an imminent decision will be made on Iran, warning that the only question is whether Tehran will have it the “easy way or hard way.” He claimed that Iran has been “decimated” and has “virtually given up any plans for a nuclear weapon.”

Separately, Yemen’s Houthi rebels claimed responsibility for a ballistic missile attack on a Saudi Aramco oil refinery in Riyadh, saying it was retaliation for Saudi coalition airstrikes on Sanaa and other Yemeni provinces. Witnesses reported seeing a large plume of smoke and fire near an Aramco installation in the capital. Yemeni government-aligned forces said they conducted 97 “precision targeting operations” against the Houthis, neutralizing 260 militia members, according to military spokesperson Brigadier General Majid Abdullah Al-Nuzaili.
