Rasmussen carries no-hitter into 8th as Rays take ALDS Game 1 over Yankees

Sports desk

ST. PETERSBURG, Fla. — Drew Rasmussen carried a no-hitter into the eighth inning and struck out 10 as the Tampa Bay Rays blanked the New York Yankees 1-0 on Saturday night in Game 1 of the American League Division Series before a sold-out crowd of 40,542 at Tropicana Field. Rasmussen walked two and retired 15 straight batters before Austin Wells broke up the no-hit bid with a double down the right-field line with two outs in the eighth. Wells was thrown out at third base on a relay from right fielder Victor Mesa Jr. to shortstop Taylor Walls to third baseman Junior Caminero, ending the inning and the Yankees' best scoring chance.

Rasmussen became the seventh pitcher in the Modern Era to carry a no-hit bid through 7 2/3 innings in the postseason and the first since Aníbal Sánchez did so for the Nationals in Game 1 of the 2019 National League Championship Series. He racked up a career-high 23 swinging strikes, the most by a Ray in a postseason game, mixing cutters, changeups, four-seam fastballs and two-seamers. "My heart rate was at a thousand beats per minute," Rasmussen said. "I was just trying to execute one pitch at a time."

The only run came in the third inning when first baseman Jonathan Aranda hit a solo home run off Yankees starter Gerrit Cole. Aranda crushed a 2-2 changeup that Cole tried to bury low and away but left up in the zone. "Obviously it comes down to us losing the game on that pitch; that's tough," catcher Austin Wells said. "But I would throw that pitch again probably 10 more times, and I think we'd end up with a good result." Cole allowed one run on three hits and two walks with five strikeouts over five innings. "It felt like it was leverage the whole game," Yankees manager Aaron Boone said. "I thought he threw the ball really well."

The Rays' victory gave them a 1-0 lead in the best-of-five series. In Division Series with the current 2-2-1 format, teams taking a 1-0 lead at home have gone on to win 42 of 56 times (75 percent). It was Tampa Bay's first playoff victory since Game 1 of the 2021 ALDS. "If you look it up, the definition of ace, in my opinion, is Drew Rasmussen," Aranda said. "He's done it for us the entire year."

Rasmussen, whom pitching coach Kyle Snyder called "the industry standard" and "the most underrated starting pitcher in baseball for the last five years," improved to 5-2 with a 1.81 ERA in 11 career regular-season games (10 starts) against the Yankees. Manager Kevin Cash had compared Rasmussen's work ethic to Hall of Famer Roy Halladay before his final regular-season start, when Rasmussen carried a no-hitter into the seventh inning against New York. "I've said a lot of dumb things in my life," Cash said. "That one was not."

Wells, who was thrown out trying to stretch his double into a triple, said he was "trying to make something happen" for an offense that had been held hitless until his hit. Boone called it "obviously not a good decision there." Mesa, who has one of the stronger outfield arms in the sport, said he knew Wells "is not that fast" and focused on getting the ball in. "I could just feel Wells put his head down and commit to going to third when he was maybe 10-15 feet before he hit second," Walls said. "I just knew that I had a lot of time."
