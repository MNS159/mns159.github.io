Trump creates 'Super Intelligence Force' led by DNI Jay Clayton to ensure US AI dominance

Politics desk
✓ Facts
  • On Sunday, President Trump announced via a Truth Social post the creation of a new federal body called the 'Super Intelligence Force' (SIF).
  • Trump named Jay Clayton, the current Director of National Intelligence, to lead the SIF and serve as the White House's new AI czar.
  • Clayton previously served as the top federal prosecutor in Manhattan and led the Securities and Exchange Commission during Trump's first term.
  • According to Trump's post, the SIF is tasked with coordinating the federal government's engagement with consumers, public-interest groups, religious organizations, critical infrastructure providers, and AI companies.
  • CBS News was the first outlet to report Clayton's appointment as AI czar.
  • In September, Trump had already announced plans for an 'AI Force' and a czar role to oversee AI growth, and he floated Clayton's name for the position at that time.
  • David Sacks previously served as the administration's AI and cryptocurrency czar until March 2026, when he reached his employment limit as a 'special government employee'; he continues to co-chair the President's Council of Advisors on Science and Technology.
  • On the Tuesday before the SIF announcement, Trump and six AI industry executives signed an AI accord at the White House creating internal and external monitors meant to address AI safety issues.
○ Analysis

The announcement formalizes an AI-oversight structure that Trump first previewed in September, consolidating it under Jay Clayton, who already runs the nation's 18 intelligence agencies as Director of National Intelligence. The move follows warnings from AI researchers that the technology is advancing faster than safeguards can keep pace, and it comes amid the administration's broader push to maintain U.S. leadership over China in AI development. The SIF appears designed to serve a dual function: advancing the administration's stated goal of global AI dominance while also managing public and industry-facing coordination on safety-related concerns, a balance underscored by the AI accord signed with tech executives just days before the SIF was unveiled.

Steelman

The appointment places both the nation's top intelligence official and its lead AI-policy coordinator in the hands of one person, a structural choice whose efficiency or risk depends on how one weighs speed of coordination against concentration of authority over emerging technology oversight.

Implications

Attention will likely turn to how the SIF operationalizes its coordination role with industry, religious and public-interest groups, how it interacts with the AI accord's new monitoring mechanisms, and whether Clayton's dual role as DNI and AI czar shapes how safety concerns are weighed against the administration's stated priority of outpacing China in AI development.
