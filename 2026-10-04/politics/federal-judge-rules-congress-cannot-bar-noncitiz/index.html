Federal judge rules Congress cannot bar noncitizens from voting in federal elections

Politics desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts right
✓ Facts
  • U.S. District Judge David Leibowitz of the Southern District of Florida issued a 31-page ruling on Thursday dismissing federal charges against Chelsea Cox, a woman from Jamaica accused of voting in federal elections from 2012 to 2020 while not a U.S. citizen.
  • Cox allegedly overstayed a tourist visa by 20 years and used a fraudulent U.S. Virgin Islands birth certificate to obtain a Florida driver's license, which she then used to register to vote.
  • The case centered on 18 U.S.C. § 611, the 'Voting by Aliens' law, enacted in 1996 as part of the Illegal Immigration Reform and Immigrant Responsibility Act (IIRIRA), which prohibits noncitizens from voting in federal elections.
  • Leibowitz ruled the statute unconstitutional as applied to Cox, holding that the Constitution gives states — not Congress — the authority to set voter qualifications in federal elections.
  • Leibowitz appointed Paul D. Clement, a former U.S. Solicitor General, as amicus curiae; Clement and co-counsel Chadwick J. Harper argued the law's 'unless' clause showed Congress's intent to override state voting permissions.
  • Judge Leibowitz was nominated by President Biden in 2024 and confirmed by the Senate 64-33, with then-Sen. Marco Rubio, R-Fla., voting in favor.
  • Florida retains the authority to bring separate state charges against Cox despite the dismissal of federal charges.
  • According to a Reuters investigation cited by Jurist, Cox was one of only 129 people charged under the federal noncitizen-voting law, and no defendants were accused of conspiring to sway an election, back a candidate, or accept payment for a vote.
○ Analysis

The ruling addresses a previously untested constitutional question: whether Congress had authority under the Constitution to criminalize noncitizen voting in federal elections, or whether that power belongs exclusively to the states under the Constitution's voter-qualification framework. Judge Leibowitz concluded that while Congress holds broad power over immigration and naturalization, that power does not extend to overriding the states' express constitutional authority to set voter qualifications, and that the statute in question created a federal voter qualification rather than regulating election procedures or fraud. The decision arrives amid a broader national push by the Trump administration to restrict and prosecute noncitizen voting, including efforts to verify citizenship on state voter rolls, and amid a stalled Senate push for the SAVE Act, which would impose federal citizenship-verification requirements for voter registration. Legal experts cited in the coverage noted competing precedents — including Bluman v. FEC (addressing foreign nationals' campaign spending, not voting prosecutions) and Oregon v. Mitchell (addressing federal authority over voting age) — that could factor into an appeal to the 11th Circuit Court of Appeals.

Steelman

The dispute ultimately reflects a genuine, unresolved constitutional tension between two textual provisions — the states' explicit authority to set voter qualifications and Congress's broad naturalization power — rather than a simple partisan disagreement, which is why legal experts across the spectrum expect the ruling to be closely scrutinized on appeal.

Implications

Legal experts cited in the coverage have suggested the ruling is likely to be challenged on appeal, with some predicting it could be overturned given competing precedents on federal versus state authority over voter qualifications in federal elections.
