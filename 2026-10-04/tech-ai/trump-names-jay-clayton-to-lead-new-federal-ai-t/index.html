Trump names Jay Clayton to lead new federal AI task force

Tech / AI desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

President Donald Trump on Sunday named Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton to lead a new White House task force on artificial intelligence, giving the group 120 days to research and report on the technology's risks and opportunities and to recommend how the federal government should respond.

Trump is calling the group the "Super Intelligence Force," or SI, because he prefers the term "super intelligence" over AI. In a Truth Social post Sunday, Trump said the task force "will coordinate the Federal Government's engagement with Consumers, Public Interest Groups, Religious Organizations, Critical Infrastructure Providers, and Super Intelligence Companies." The task force will report to Trump and his chief of staff, Susie Wiles.

In addition to Clayton, the task force includes Federal Trade Commission Chairman Andrew Ferguson; Emil Michael, the Defense Department's under secretary of war for research and engineering and chief technology officer; and Scott Kupor, director of the Office of Personnel Management. The Wall Street Journal first reported Clayton's new role Saturday.

Clayton, a former chair of the Securities and Exchange Commission and U.S. attorney for the Southern District of New York, was confirmed by the Senate as DNI in July, giving him authority over 18 U.S. intelligence agencies. The announcement comes days after AI industry leaders met with Trump and House Speaker Mike Johnson at the White House and signed onto a voluntary safety agreement that Trump called "morally binding."

Trump has so far opposed regulation of AI, despite calls from industry leaders such as Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei and OpenAI CEO Sam Altman for federal guardrails on frontier AI models. In a September Truth Social post, Trump said he would create a new "AI Force" to help the industry and root out bad actors, adding, "We will not in any way hinder or stifle the Growth of this incredible Industry. Rather, we will cherish it, help it, and watch over it, as it grows!" He also suggested AI fears were a hoax and blamed "Radical Left Dumocrats."

Venture capitalist David Sacks had previously served as AI and crypto czar under Trump, but in March said his time as a special government employee had ended. Sacks remains co-chair of the President's Council of Advisors on Science and Technology, a federal advisory committee of outside experts on technology, scientific research and innovation.
