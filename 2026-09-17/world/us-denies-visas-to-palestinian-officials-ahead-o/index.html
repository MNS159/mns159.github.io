US denies visas to Palestinian officials for second consecutive year ahead of UN General Assembly

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts slightly center-left

The Trump administration has denied entry visas to a high-level Palestinian delegation, including Palestinian Authority President Mahmoud Abbas, for the second consecutive year, barring them from attending the annual UN General Assembly meeting in New York in person. The State Department announced the decision on Wednesday, citing the Palestinian Authority's failure to meet commitments to seek peace with Israel.

In a statement, the State Department accused the Palestinian Authority and the Palestine Liberation Organization of continuing to pursue legal action against Israel at the International Court of Justice and the International Criminal Court, and of paying stipends to families of prisoners convicted of attacks against Israelis. The department said the groups had 'failed to live up to their commitments' and were engaging in 'ongoing activities that undermine the prospects for peace.' As a result, the United States will 'extend sanctions that deny visas to PLO members and PA officials' in accordance with standing U.S. law.

The move marks the second year in a row the Trump administration has barred the Palestinian delegation from traveling to New York for the UN General Assembly. Last year, Abbas addressed the gathering by video conference. This year, the UN General Assembly is set to vote on a draft resolution on Thursday to allow Abbas to address the gathering remotely, according to two officials familiar with the planned vote. Despite the visa denials, the Palestinians will still be represented at next week's high-level meeting by their diplomats who are already accredited to the United Nations.

The Palestinian Authority's foreign ministry denied the allegations in a statement on Thursday, describing the visa sanctions as an 'unjustified measure' that 'contradicts the efforts being made to restore trust, develop Palestinian-US relations, and prepare the necessary political climate for implementing the two-state solution and achieving peace and stability in the region.'

The visa denials are the latest in a series of restrictive measures by the Trump administration, further signaling the close U.S. alliance with Israel, which has faced international isolation over its war in Gaza following the deadly Oct. 7, 2023, attacks by Hamas. A fragile, U.S.-brokered ceasefire took hold in October, but Israel has continued to carry out strikes in the Palestinian territory. The decision also comes as the Trump administration has launched a campaign to dismantle the ICC, accusing it of trying to unfairly prosecute U.S. and Israeli soldiers. Neither Israel nor the United States are members of the global tribunal. This year's UN General Assembly general debate begins on 22 September.
