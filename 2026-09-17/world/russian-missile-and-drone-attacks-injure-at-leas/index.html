Russian missile and drone attacks injure at least 19 in Ukraine

International & World desk

KYIV, Ukraine — A Russian barrage of missiles and drones targeting Ukraine's capital and surrounding regions injured at least 19 people overnight and into Thursday morning, Ukrainian officials said. The Kyiv City Military Administration said the attacks struck more than 10 locations in the capital, with a 10-year-old girl and a 12-year-old boy among the injured. Ukraine's Air Force said it destroyed or suppressed 131 Russian aerial targets during the overnight assault, which involved ballistic and cruise missiles as well as drones and decoys, with the main areas targeted being the Kyiv, Zaporizhzhia and Odesa regions.

In Odesa, seven people were injured, including two children, according to Ukraine's State Emergency Service. Apartments on the 14th through 16th floors of a residential building were damaged, a private residential building was destroyed, and warehouses, a nursery school, cars and garages were damaged at other locations. In Kyiv, a warehouse, office building, residential buildings and cars were damaged in the Dniprovsky district, with additional damage to warehouses and vehicles in the Solomyansky and Darnitsky districts and a non-residential building in the Podilsky district. In the wider Kyiv region, civilian areas and infrastructure were targeted in the Obukhiv, Brovary, Fastiv and Bucha districts, according to Tymur Tkachenko, head of the Kyiv Regional Administration. A total of 33 sites were damaged, with the worst reported in the Brovary district, where 14 sites were hit, and the Fastiv district, where 10 were damaged; no casualties were reported there.

Russia's Defense Ministry said its forces hit metallurgical and electronics plants, factories of the military-industrial complex, and energy and transport facilities in the three regions, and that three bulk carriers and a ferry carrying supplies for the Ukrainian military were hit in the port of Chornomorsk. Separately, a Russian drone struck a civilian vessel flying the flag of Tanzania in the Black Sea on Thursday, killing the ship's captain and injuring three crew members, according to Oleh Kiper, the regional head in Odesa. Kiper said the vessel's superstructure and deck were damaged in the morning attack and accused Russia of deliberately targeting Ukrainian ports and civilian merchant vessels.

President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Ukrainian forces carried out overnight strikes against targets in Russia, including an oil refinery in Yaroslavl, northeast of Moscow, and a military airfield in the Rostov region. He said the operation against the Yaroslavl refinery was conducted jointly by Ukraine's Security Service, special operations forces, military intelligence and the Foreign Intelligence Service, and that the strike on the Rostov airfield damaged an An-12 aircraft, two An-26s and three helicopters. Regional Governor Mikhail Yevrayev said the resulting fire at the refinery had been extinguished. Zelenskyy also reported damage to Russian warplanes and helicopters in Rostov and cited "good results in the Black Sea," without giving further details.

The attacks come days after U.S. President Donald Trump claimed in a social media post Monday that Russia and Ukraine had mutually agreed to cease attacking each other's energy facilities, strikes that have led to significant supply issues in both countries. In an interview with CBS News on Tuesday, Zelenskyy pushed back on that claim, saying that while an energy truce was discussed during a recent meeting in Kyiv with Trump's representatives, Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner, he would agree only if Russia ceases its attacks on Ukrainian energy sites. Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha said Ukraine is ready to agree to a truce covering attacks on energy infrastructure and the Black Sea, but said he has yet to see any indication that Russia is prepared to do the same. He welcomed U.S. efforts and said active U.S. involvement could make a truce possible, adding that Ukraine was preparing for a meeting between Zelenskyy and Trump on the sidelines of the U.N. General Assembly this month. "I believe that achieving a truce could be one of the key outcomes of this meeting. It could be a truce covering both the energy sector and the Black Sea — specifically in that combination," he said.

Peace efforts to end Russia's 4 1/2-year full-scale invasion have stalled, with the issue of territorial concessions in eastern Ukraine among the central sticking points. Successive rounds of U.S.-brokered talks have failed to produce a breakthrough, and Ukraine is also seeking security guarantees from partners for a deal. Russian attacks "almost collapsed the Ukrainian energy grid" last winter, and Ukraine's energy providers now say they are expecting another very tough winter, according to Al Jazeera's Rory Challands reporting from Kyiv. Russia has promised to increase its "mass attacks on the Ukrainian energy grid going into the winter months," he said.

Meanwhile, Russia and Ukraine exchanged the bodies of their war dead, with Moscow handing over the remains of 252 people and receiving 23 bodies in a swap at the Belarusian-Ukrainian border, Belarusian state news agency BELTA reported. Ukraine's government agency handling prisoners of war said the bodies of 252 soldiers were received as part of ongoing exchanges and that they will be identified. Ukrainian officials also welcomed the vote in the U.S. House of Representatives on a package of sanctions targeting Russian officials and key sectors of the economy, saying the measures could increase pressure on Moscow and limit the resources available to finance its war effort. The bill was the last undertaking of the late Republican Sen. Lindsay Graham. "It is precisely Sen. Graham's bill, along with other strong sanctions measures taken by our partners against the source of this war, Russia, that represent an extremely powerful tool that can stop this terrorist war and force Russia to seek peace," Zelenskyy wrote on Telegram.
