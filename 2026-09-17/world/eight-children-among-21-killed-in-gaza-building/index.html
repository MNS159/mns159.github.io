Eight children among 21 killed in Gaza building collapse

International & World desk

At least 21 people, including eight children, were killed and 45 others injured when a war-damaged six-storey building collapsed overnight in the Tal al-Hawa area of Gaza City, rescuers and medics said. Witnesses told the BBC that the building had been left tilting dangerously after an Israeli strike more than a year ago, yet displaced families had lived inside because they had no other options. The Hamas-run Civil Defence agency initially used bare hands to search for survivors, later joined by UN and Egyptian teams, before ending the operation on Wednesday afternoon.

A woman living in a tent nearby, Umm Mohammed Bakrun, said the collapse came suddenly in the early hours: 'It felt like an earthquake. There was a loud sound and I couldn't stand up. Stones rained down on us and when I looked outside I saw a lot of dust. Within minutes, there were screams everywhere.' Another witness, Mansour Abu Mohammed, said the building fell 'in a few seconds' and that stones had been falling from it daily, but residents could not find anywhere else to live. Sherif, whose sister was killed, said: 'They couldn't find a tent or somewhere to live. This was the last place they could turn to, despite the danger. A lack of resources drove them towards certain death.'

Satellite imagery shows the area was targeted early in the war, with damage to the building itself first visible in 2024. The head of the UN Development Programme office in Gaza, Alessandro Mrakic, said the situation was 'tragic because we don't have the equipment and machinery that is needed.' He warned that approximately 400 other war-damaged buildings across Gaza were at imminent risk of collapse, particularly as winter approaches, and appealed for machinery, spare parts and oil. The UN's humanitarian relief co-ordinator, Ramiz Alakbarov, said he was 'devastated' and urged Israeli authorities to approve deliveries of heavy machinery, tents and other non-food items for displaced people before winter rains. Israel says its restrictions are meant to keep equipment out of the hands of Palestinian armed groups.

According to the UN, an estimated 80% of all structures in Gaza have been destroyed or damaged during the war between Israel and Hamas. More than 1.9 million people – about 90% of the territory's population – have been displaced, with 1.2 million currently living in tents or makeshift shelters. The UN has warned that the humanitarian situation remains dire despite a ceasefire agreed last October, as Israeli air strikes and other attacks continue to cause casualties and damage. On Tuesday, Israeli air strikes killed at least five Palestinians, including two children and a Hamas commander, according to health officials and Hamas sources. Separately, the British charity Medical Aid for Palestinians said a patient was killed by an Israeli drone at a health centre it supports in northern Gaza; the Israeli military said it was not aware of the incident.

The war was triggered by the Hamas-led attack on southern Israel on 7 October 2023, in which about 1,200 people were killed and 251 taken hostage. Since then, more than 73,790 people have been killed in Gaza, according to the territory's Hamas-run health ministry, whose figures the UN considers reliable.
