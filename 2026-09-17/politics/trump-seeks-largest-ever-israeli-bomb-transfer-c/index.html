Trump seeks largest-ever Israeli bomb transfer; Congress split

Politics desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts slightly center-right
✓ Facts
  • The Trump administration has informally notified Congress of a proposed $2.8 billion arms package for Israel that would include heavy bombs, according to multiple reports cited by BBC and Fox News.
  • Fox News reports the package includes 20,000 unguided MK-84 2,000lb bombs, 20,000 BLU-117 2,000lb precision-guided bombs, and 20,000 I-2000 penetrator warheads; BBC, citing Rep. Gregory Meeks, describes the transfer as involving 40,000 bombs overall.
  • Rep. Gregory Meeks (D-NY), ranking member of the House Foreign Affairs Committee, said he opposes the sale, citing unresolved concerns about lawful use of the munitions in populated areas of Gaza and Lebanon.
  • Sens. Bernie Sanders (I-VT) and Chris Van Hollen (D-MD) have publicly said they will work to reject or stop the package.
  • Under the Arms Export Control Act, formal notification would let Congress attempt to block the sale via a joint resolution, but blocking it would require a veto-proof two-thirds majority in both the House and Senate; the president may also bypass congressional review by declaring an emergency.
  • In 2024, the Biden administration paused a shipment of 2,000lb bombs to Israel over civilian-casualty concerns; the Trump administration reversed that pause after taking office and, in 2025, separately approved an approximately $3 billion sale of 35,500 such bombs by bypassing normal congressional review.
  • The bombs would be funded largely through the U.S. Foreign Military Financing program, which uses congressionally appropriated taxpayer funds for allied arms purchases.
  • In July, Rep. Thomas Massie (R-KY) forced a House vote on a bill to cut $3.3 billion in security aid to Israel; 103 Democrats supported it, while Massie was the only Republican to vote in favor.
○ Analysis

The proposed $2.8 billion package would be one of the largest transfers of 2,000-pound bombs to Israel in recent years, building on a similar nearly $3 billion sale earlier in 2025. Because the deal is still in the informal congressional notification stage, lawmakers can raise objections, but they have no binding power to stop it unless the administration proceeds to formal notification and a veto-proof majority in both chambers votes to block it. In 2024, a similar bomb shipment was paused during the Biden administration over concerns about civilian casualties in densely populated Gaza, only to be reversed once Trump returned to office.

◐ Debate

Democratic

  • Rep. Gregory Meeks (D-NY) says the deal raises 'grave, unresolved concerns' about lawful use of the munitions in densely populated areas of Gaza and Lebanon and reflects Congress's duty to ensure lawful, safeguarded use of U.S.-funded weapons.
  • Sen. Bernie Sanders (I-VT) called on Congress to reject the package, accusing the administration of 'doubling down' on funding what he termed Israel's actions in Gaza.
  • Sen. Chris Van Hollen (D-MD) criticized the package's use of taxpayer funds, asking 'How is this America First?' and vowed to work to stop it.

Republican

  • Rep. Thomas Massie (R-KY) was the sole Republican to support a July amendment cutting $3.3 billion in security aid to Israel.
Steelman

Both sides broadly affirm support for Israel's security, though Democratic lawmakers have raised concerns about how the munitions could be used and about congressional oversight of U.S.-funded weapons sales, given that the administration can legally bypass formal review.

Implications

Passing a resolution to block the sale would require a two-thirds majority in both chambers. Given the precedent of the administration bypassing review for a comparable 2025 sale, the bombs could be transferred regardless of congressional objections.
