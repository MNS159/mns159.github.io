FDA Approves Durvalumab Plus Enfortumab Vedotin for Perioperative Treatment of Muscle-Invasive Bladder Cancer

Health & Science desk

The U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) approved on November 13, 2026, the combination of durvalumab (Imfinzi) and enfortumab vedotin-ejfv (Padcev) as neoadjuvant treatment followed by adjuvant durvalumab monotherapy after radical cystectomy for adults with muscle-invasive bladder cancer (MIBC) who are ineligible for or have declined cisplatin-based chemotherapy. The decision, granted under priority review and Project Orbis, was based on interim results from the phase 3 VOLGA trial (NCT04960709), which showed a 49% reduction in the risk of an event-free survival (EFS) event (hazard ratio [HR], 0.51; 95% CI, 0.38–0.67; P < .001) and a 42% reduction in the risk of death (HR, 0.58; 95% CI, 0.40–0.83; P = .003) compared with radical cystectomy alone.

In the VOLGA trial, 695 patients with clinical stage T2-T4aN0/1M0 or T1N1M0 MIBC who were candidates for cystectomy but ineligible for or refused cisplatin were randomly assigned 1:1:1 to either the approved doublet (durvalumab plus enfortumab vedotin before surgery then adjuvant durvalumab), a triplet that also included tremelimumab, or surgery alone. The median EFS for the doublet arm was not reached (95% CI, 33.2 months–not reached) versus 12.1 months (95% CI, 7.6–27.0) for surgery alone, and the 24-month EFS rates were 63.5% and 45.1%, respectively. The median overall survival was not reached in either arm, with 24-month OS rates of 78.4% (doublet) and 69.4% (surgery). Pathologic complete response was achieved in 45.1% of patients in the doublet arm versus 9.6% in the surgery arm.

The recommended dosage for patients weighing 30 kg or more is durvalumab 1500 mg every 3 weeks plus enfortumab vedotin 1.25 mg/kg on days 1 and 8 of each 21-day cycle for 3 neoadjuvant cycles, followed by durvalumab 1500 mg every 4 weeks for up to 9 cycles after surgery. For patients weighing less than 30 kg, the dose is durvalumab 20 mg/kg on the same schedules. Enfortumab vedotin is administered only in the neoadjuvant phase. The FDA’s review relied on EFS assessed by blinded independent central review.

Grade 3 or higher adverse events of any cause occurred in 71.1% of patients receiving the doublet compared with 50.2% in the surgery-alone arm. Treatment-related grade 3 or higher events were reported in 30.7% of the doublet arm, with one treatment-related death (multiple-organ dysfunction syndrome). The most common any-grade adverse events in the doublet arm included pruritus (44%), urinary tract infection (36%), and anemia (32%). Skin reactions, peripheral neuropathy, and hyperglycemia were also noted. The FDA label includes warnings for immune-mediated adverse reactions, infusion-related reactions, complications of allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation, and embryo-fetal toxicity.

"Up to half of patients with muscle-invasive bladder cancer are not eligible for cisplatin and face high rates of disease recurrence, even after having their bladder removed, leaving a significant need for new effective and well-tolerated treatments," said Thomas Powles, MD, professor and chair of Barts Cancer Centre and international coordinating investigator for VOLGA, in a news release on the survival findings. "The VOLGA results show that perioperative durvalumab significantly extends event-free survival and overall survival when combined with neoadjuvant enfortumab vedotin, with a manageable safety profile, compared to surgery for patients in this curative-intent setting."

The triplet arm (durvalumab, tremelimumab, and enfortumab vedotin) also met the EFS endpoint but did not show a statistically significant overall survival benefit at the interim analysis (HR, 0.77; 95% CI, 0.54–1.07; P = .12); it is not part of the approved indication. The approval was supported by the VOLGA trial’s primary endpoint of EFS and key secondary endpoint of OS, with a median EFS follow-up of 22.9 months at the data cutoff of March 2, 2026. The FDA’s decision comes six weeks ahead of its goal date, underscoring the unmet need in this patient population.
