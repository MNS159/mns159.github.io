Nvidia scandal deepens: Huang resigns, Lutnick faces pressure, both summoned to testify

Politics desk
✓ Facts
  • Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang left the company after reports surfaced of an extramarital affair with Alice Huang, a former executive at Singapore-based Megaspeed whom US intelligence has alleged has ties to Chinese intelligence; Beijing has denied the allegations.
  • Nvidia's board said it has no reason to believe company secrets or national security were compromised.
  • Senate Banking Committee Chairman Tim Scott (R-SC) announced that both Huang and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick have been summoned to testify before the committee, with Lutnick's appearance set for December; Huang's team told lawmakers he plans to cooperate with a closed-door testimony request.
  • Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said publicly on CNBC that several government agencies had favored adding Megaspeed to the Commerce Department's export-control entity list 'for months,' but that 'Commerce has the pen' on such decisions.
  • Megaspeed CEO Tan Yong Pong (James Tan) said the company takes its US export-control compliance obligations 'extremely seriously' and has cooperated with Commerce Department inspections.
  • House Ways and Means Ranking Member Richard Neal and Senate Finance Ranking Member Ron Wyden opened investigations into how Nvidia's advanced H200 chips were allowed to be sold to Chinese firms via a process involving import through Taiwan for US inspection and a 25% tariff under the Trade Act.
  • The White House told the New York Post that President Trump has 'complete confidence' in Lutnick, calling him 'the most transformative commerce secretary in modern history.'
  • Elon Musk said on social media that Nvidia will lose 'critical deals' following Huang's departure because many supply-chain agreements were informal handshake arrangements with Huang rather than written contracts.
○ Analysis

The story sits at the intersection of a personal scandal and a long-running US policy fight over how much advanced AI chip technology should be allowed to reach China. Washington has restricted exports of Nvidia's most advanced processors since 2022 over fears they could aid Beijing's military and tech ambitions, but enforcement has been difficult because chips can be legally sold to third countries and then diverted. Huang was unusually close to President Trump, giving him direct influence over China chip policy, which is why his departure amid allegations involving a suspected Chinese intelligence contact has widened into scrutiny of the Commerce Department, the Nvidia-Megaspeed relationship, and what senior officials — including Lutnick and reportedly Trump himself — knew about the intelligence concerns before acting.

Steelman

Officials across the political spectrum agree that something in the export-control system failed to prevent advanced chips from potentially reaching China — the dispute is not over whether a failure occurred, but over whether responsibility lies with Nvidia's internal vetting, the Commerce Department's enforcement delays, or the broader White House policy balancing trade with China against national security.

Implications

Congressional testimony from Huang and Lutnick in the coming weeks, combined with ongoing Democratic-led investigations into Commerce Department records, is likely to produce new details about who knew what and when regarding the Megaspeed concerns. The outcome could affect Lutnick's standing in the administration, reshape enforcement of AI chip export controls, and influence Nvidia's ability to maintain the informal business relationships Musk warned were central to its supply chain.
