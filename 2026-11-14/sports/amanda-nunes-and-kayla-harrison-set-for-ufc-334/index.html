Amanda Nunes and Kayla Harrison set for UFC 334 showdown

Sports desk

Amanda Nunes, the most successful women's mixed martial artist of all time, returns from a three-year retirement to challenge women's bantamweight champion Kayla Harrison at UFC 334 on Saturday at Madison Square Garden in New York. The fight, originally scheduled for January, was postponed after Harrison required neck and spinal surgery. Harrison, a two-time Olympic gold medalist in judo and a two-time PFL lightweight champion, won the UFC bantamweight title in only her third fight with the promotion.

Nunes, 38, left the UFC in 2023 as a former featherweight and bantamweight champion, the only woman to hold titles in two weight classes simultaneously and the only fighter, male or female, to successfully defend both belts. She holds multiple records, including 11 wins in title fights, tied for most in women's UFC history. During her career, Nunes defeated icons such as Ronda Rousey, Cris Cyborg, and Valentina Shevchenko. She won 23 of 28 fights, with 13 knockouts.

"I'm here to stay," Nunes told CBS Sports. "I'm a fighter, man. This is a huge difference between me and Kayla. She's an athlete. I'm a fighter." Nunes insists she is not returning as a challenger. "I'm here as a double champion," she said. "I needed a break and did it at the right time for my mind, body and soul." Nunes said she was motivated to return after Harrison joined the UFC, seeing a new challenge in a division she felt she had cleaned out.

"I feel like 20, honestly. I didn't expect to feel like that," she said. "Everything is coming together perfect. The training. Everything [feels] like I never left this game." She also cited a desire to revive interest in women's MMA, which she believes has slowed since her departure. "I got upset when I left because I wanted to see the division grow," Nunes told the BBC. "I want to make women's MMA great."

Harrison, 36, described her own recovery from neck surgery as a battle leading up to the fight. She had been competing with herniated discs dating back to her judo career. The contest is being billed as one of the biggest women's fights in UFC history. Nunes enters as a betting underdog, a position she said she welcomes. "I have always been the underdog," she said. "I take that as a motivator." Nunes also expressed desire to face the team that she believes did not have her best interests at heart, as her former coaches at American Top Team will be in Harrison's corner. Nunes said she plans to fight on after Saturday "until my body tells me to stop."
