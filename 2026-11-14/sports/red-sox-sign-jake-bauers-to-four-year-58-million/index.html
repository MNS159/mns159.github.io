Red Sox sign Jake Bauers to 4-year, $58 million contract; ex-Brewer to serve as primary DH

Sports desk

The Boston Red Sox signed free-agent first baseman/outfielder Jake Bauers to a four-year, $58 million contract on Friday, according to multiple outlets. The deal was first reported by ESPN’s Jeff Passan. Bauers, who turns 31, rejected a $23.15 million qualifying offer from the Milwaukee Brewers, meaning the Red Sox will forfeit their second- and fifth-highest selections in the 2027 draft and $1 million from their international bonus pool after exceeding the competitive-balance tax threshold in 2026.

Bauers is expected to serve as Boston’s primary designated hitter, with occasional starts at first base to give Willson Contreras more rest after Contreras dealt with soft-tissue injuries late last season. Contreras will remain the primary first baseman, as he grades out as a better defender. Bauers can also play left field, but the Red Sox already have Roman Anthony, Ceddanne Rafaela, Wilyer Abreu, Jarren Duran and Eli White in the outfield. The signing increases the likelihood that Boston trades Duran and appears to mark the end of Masataka Yoshida’s tenure, as Yoshida enters the final season of his five-year, $90 million contract and is a change-of-scenery candidate. Curtis Mead, who could have logged DH at-bats, is now expected to be the everyday second baseman.

Bauers enjoyed a breakout 2026 season for the World Series-champion Brewers, hitting .266 with a .389 on-base percentage, .484 slugging percentage and .874 OPS in 144 games. He set career highs with 26 home runs, 22 doubles, 86 RBIs and 93 runs, and posted a 3.6 fWAR after entering the season with a minus-0.4 career fWAR. His .389 OBP ranked third in all of baseball, and he finished in the 91st percentile or above in hard-hit rate (51.9%), bat speed (76.5 mph), expected wOBA (.371), barrel rate (14.0%), chase rate (21.7%) and walk rate (16.3%). Bauers was named MVP of the National League Championship Series after the Brewers swept the Los Angeles Dodgers. He had previously played for the Rays, Guardians, Mariners and Yankees before signing with Milwaukee before the 2025 season.

The Red Sox are gambling that Bauers’s 2026 performance was not a fluke, similar to their acquisition of Curtis Mead last July after a first-half breakout. Bauers brings October experience from three consecutive postseason trips in Milwaukee, including deep runs the past two years, and joins a Boston clubhouse that already includes Contreras, Ranger Suárez and Aroldis Chapman. The Red Sox have not won a postseason series since 2021. The deal adds to the team’s estimated luxury-tax payroll, which now sits at approximately $269 million per FanGraphs, about $3 million less than the final 2026 figure.

The timing of the signing marks an aggressive early move for Boston, which has been slower in free agency in recent years. This offseason, Bauers is the first player to sign a contract worth more than $40 million; the only other major league deals signed to this point were reliever contracts for Emilio Pagán (Detroit) and Evan Phillips (Atlanta). The Red Sox have now accepted draft-pick penalties for signing a qualified free agent for the third straight year, following Alex Bregman in 2025 and Ranger Suárez in 2026. Meanwhile, the Brewers are considering options to replace Bauers at first base, including retaining Andrew Vaughn or promoting prospects Andrew Fischer, Blake Burke or Luke Adams. They are not expected to pursue free agent Ty France, who also broke out in 2026.
