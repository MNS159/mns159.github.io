FAA lifts rare nationwide ground stop; airline stocks fall

Financial & Markets desk

The Federal Aviation Administration lifted a rare nationwide ground stop at 8:15 p.m. ET Friday after aging infrastructure forced the agency to halt all outbound flights for hours, stranding passengers at airports across the United States. The ground stop, ordered around 5 p.m. Eastern, came after the FAA reported a temporary telecommunications outage and said it acted "out of an abundance of caution." At the time, roughly 3,500 planes were in the air, but more than 10,000 flights were disrupted.

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said at a press conference that aging infrastructure caused the outage. "Our switches, our routers, actually got overloaded with data, and they went down…and I shouldn't say they went down. They started to give us inconsistency in the data from those routers, and because of that, we shut the system down," he said. Duffy added that the system needs a complete overhaul, including investment in new towers and airports, which he estimated would cost about $50 billion.

Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said there is "zero evidence" of a cyberattack. Meanwhile, Microsoft said in a statement on X that the FAA ground stop was not related to any of its services, after the tech giant apologized earlier Friday for a separate software outage that left users unable to access Outlook email and other services.

Airline stocks fell in post-market trading Friday on the news. Shares of American Airlines, Southwest, JetBlue, Delta Air Lines, and United Airlines all moved lower. The economic impact of the outage remains unknown. High jet-fuel costs mean fewer flights are available for travelers seeking to rebook, and delays are expected to extend into the weekend, with the FAA planning to prioritize aircraft carrying more passengers.

This nationwide grounding was extremely rare. The last time the FAA halted all U.S. departures was in January 2023 due to a system outage. Prior to that, traffic was halted after the Sept. 11, 2001, attacks.
