Nvidia Scandal

Commerce Secretary Lutnick ties to Nvidia scrutinized as Bessent blames chip leakage

Financial & Markets desk

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent on Friday said his department and others in the Trump administration had long supported restricting Megaspeed, the company at the center of former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang's spy-sex scandal, but that the Commerce Department is in charge of the entity list. 'You will have to ask them about their internal processes,' Bessent told CNBC's 'Squawk Box,' pointing to Secretary Howard Lutnick's department as the one responsible for imposing the sanctions. The remarks came as new scrutiny fell on Lutnick's ties to Nvidia following Axios reports that he repeatedly resisted a crackdown on companies suspected of funneling Nvidia's advanced AI chips to China, despite President Donald Trump authorizing new export restrictions.

A CNBC analysis of securities filings found that Cantor Fitzgerald, the Wall Street firm formerly run by Lutnick and now led by his sons Brandon and Kyle, reduced its reported Nvidia holdings by 49% between September 2025 and March 2026, from 3.5 million shares to 1.78 million, as the value fell from $652 million to $310 million. The decline coincided with U.S. intelligence warnings about Huang's ties to a suspected Chinese spy and pressure on Lutnick to crack down on Megaspeed. Cantor said its 13F filings reflect client swap transactions and that its proprietary positions in Nvidia are 'only immaterial.' The firm declined further comment. There is no evidence Lutnick shared nonpublic information with his sons, ethics experts said, though Richard Painter, former White House ethics lawyer, noted 'the appearance doesn't look great.'

Even as Cantor pared its Nvidia stake, it maintained deep business ties to the company's AI empire. In May 2025, Cantor served as one of seven bookrunners on a $3 billion convertible bond offering for IREN, an Australian cloud computing provider that had just signed a $3.4 billion contract with Nvidia. By June 2026, Cantor reported $115 million in IREN shares and $362 million in Nvidia shares. Earlier, in October 2025, Cantor advised AI infrastructure developer Lancium on a $600 million financing deal for a Stargate data center campus; Nvidia later invested $2 billion for a roughly 20% stake in Lancium. Cantor also has ties to Nvidia's supply chain: Semafor reported in May 2025 that Cantor bankers informally advised TSMC on navigating Washington, which Cantor called 'baseless and false.'

The scrutiny intensified after Huang's resignation Thursday and amid reporting that Lutnick had received a classified intelligence memo in May 2025 about Huang's relationship with a suspected spy, yet appeared alongside Huang at a G20 technology summit and later cleared roughly 10 Chinese companies, including Alibaba and ByteDance, to buy Nvidia's H200 chips. Sens. Jim Banks and Elizabeth Warren in March urged Lutnick to reconsider Nvidia's export licenses after indictments in a chip-smuggling case. On Wednesday, Sens. Warren and Chris Coons demanded that Lutnick, Secretary of State Marco Rubio, and White House chief of staff Stephen Miller preserve records on Nvidia chip exports and intelligence warnings. Coons told CNBC on Friday that the administration 'has most failed here in being persuaded by Jensen Huang and others to license the export of some advanced chips.'

The White House has expressed 'complete confidence' in Lutnick, and a White House official told Reuters on Thursday that Trump is standing by the commerce secretary. Cantor's next quarterly securities filing is due Monday, which will show whether its Nvidia holdings continued at lower levels. The episode raises questions about the intersection of Lutnick's family financial interests and his role overseeing export controls at Commerce, though no evidence of wrongdoing has emerged. Painter cautioned that 'the Trump administration leaks like a sieve when it comes to nonpublic government information,' but stressed there is no proof Lutnick shared such information with his sons.
