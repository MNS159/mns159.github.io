Cloud Outage

Microsoft outage escalates into global cloud crisis, experts say worse than CrowdStrike

Tech / AI desk

A widespread outage that began midday Thursday has disrupted Microsoft 365 services globally, affecting email, cloud storage, AI tools, and security software for hundreds of millions of users. Ciaran Martin, former head of the UK's National Cyber Security Centre, called it 'one of the biggest outages of all time,' suggesting it may be 'worse' than the recent CrowdStrike and Amazon Web Services disruptions.

The outage struck Microsoft's Outlook email, Teams video conferencing, Copilot AI, Defender security screening, and Intune management dashboards, as well as cloud-based Windows computers. Businesses, hospitals, police departments, schools, and government agencies were affected. In Japan, deputy chief cabinet secretary Masanao Ozaki said government IT infrastructure was disrupted. The Supreme Court in the Philippines experienced legal work delays, and a Colorado sheriff's office reported difficulty posting bonds for prisoner release. Financial transactions were blocked when users could not receive verification codes for bank transfers. Microsoft estimated in January that it has over 450 million paid commercial subscriptions.

Microsoft attributed the failure to 'a large number of servers being unexpectedly unavailable' and 'failed server health checks.' The company said it has 'no evidence of malicious activity' and that the incident is 'not related to cyber or AI / agents.' Recovery efforts continued into Friday, with Microsoft extending email timeout to 48 hours to preserve delayed mail. Doug Madory of Kentik noted that traffic was returning to Microsoft services after roughly 18 hours of downtime.

Microsoft's executive vice president Charles Lamanna stated Friday that 'we’re seeing encouraging recovery progress, with service availability and email delivery performance improving across a growing portion of affected infrastructure. Our teams are reviewing what happened and how we can prevent similar issues going forward.' The company promised a full post-incident report with root cause analysis after mitigation is complete. The outage follows a similar dayslong cloud service disruption in September 2023, which Microsoft blamed on a 'core authentication configuration' issue.

The prolonged disruption and slow recovery have raised questions about Microsoft's ability to swiftly rebound from internal failures. While the company has ruled out malicious activity, the scope of the incident—affecting everything from hospital communications to court operations—underscores the dependence on Microsoft's cloud ecosystem and the cascading risks of a single provider's service failure.
