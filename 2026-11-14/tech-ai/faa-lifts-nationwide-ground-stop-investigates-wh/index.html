FAA lifts nationwide ground stop; officials investigate possible role of new AI system

Tech / AI desk

The Federal Aviation Administration lifted its third-ever nationwide ground stop Friday evening, clearing domestic flights to resume takeoffs at 8:35 p.m. EST after a communications outage halted departures from all U.S. airports for more than four hours. The stoppage, which began at 3:36 p.m. EST, affected more than one million travelers, with FlightAware reporting 9,124 U.S. flights delayed and 5,901 canceled as of 8:39 p.m. ET. Some airlines, including Air Canada, canceled all flights to and from the United States for the remainder of the evening.

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy blamed the disruption on an aging air traffic control system, saying in a post on X that the system relies on "DECADES-OLD analog equipment that simply cannot compete with the demands of the 21st century." In a White House press conference, Duffy said Terminal Radar Approach Control (TRACON) facilities "started to have inconsistency in the data from the radars and the communications from airplanes," and that analog telecom switches and routers "got overloaded with data." Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin confirmed that "patches" to the existing system sparked the issue, though Duffy added, "By doing nothing, you inject way more risk into the system… I didn’t anticipate that we would have an outage like this."

The outage began at the Chicago air traffic control center, according to The Air Current, followed by centers in Boston, Washington, New York, and Minneapolis, disrupting both radar and radio frequencies used by controllers and pilots. The FAA issued the ground stop "due to extraordinary circumstances and for reasons of safety," and said inbound international flights would be managed case by case. Mullin told reporters there is no reason to suspect a cyberattack or connection to a recent Microsoft Outlook outage, but that no cause had been ruled out for the "telecommunications outage."

Officials are investigating whether the FAA’s new artificial intelligence system, the Strategic Management of Airspace, Routes and Trajectories (SMART) tool, contributed to the disruption. The system, designed by Air Space Intelligence (ASI) under a 12-year, $875 million contract awarded in June, was activated nationwide on November 10 and uses real-time data on weather, congestion, and flight paths to streamline routes. Mullin said the Department of Homeland Security is working with federal agencies to examine any connection, but stressed there is no threat to the public. ASI denied any link, posting that "the current ground stop is not based on or a result of the SMART system, any product of ASI or our integration with FAA and ATC."

The last nationwide ground stop occurred in 2023 and lasted about 90 minutes after the NOTAM system went down; the one before that followed the September 11, 2001 attacks. Duffy cautioned that without massive system updates, there will be "more of these" outages ahead, and said he is pursuing funds to replace the outdated equipment. The FAA said technicians continue to investigate the communications failure affecting air traffic control centers nationwide.
