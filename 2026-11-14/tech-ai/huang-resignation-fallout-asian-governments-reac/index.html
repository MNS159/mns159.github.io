Jensen Huang leaves Nvidia after scandals; analysts say company can survive without him

Tech / AI desk

Jensen Huang, the co-founder and CEO of Nvidia, has left the company he led for 33 years following allegations of involvement in two chip smuggling scandals and three extramarital affairs, including one with an alleged Chinese spy. The 63-year-old visionary, who built Nvidia into a $5 trillion company representing roughly 6 percent of the S&P 500, had no named successor and around 60 employees reporting directly to him, making his departure a seismic event for the AI industry and stock market.

Huang resigned after reportedly enabling the smuggling scandals and amid the infidelity allegations, which surfaced as he mingled with President Donald Trump. He had once said he preferred to die on the job and suggested he would work until age 90 or 100, then continue as a robot. Analysts describe his leadership as singular: “Jensen is Nvidia, and Nvidia is Jensen,” said Jon Peddie, a computer industry analyst who has tracked Nvidia since its inception. “He was the consummate showman, salesman, and visionary,” added IDC analyst Tom Mainelli.

Huang’s legacy includes a series of high-stakes bets that transformed Nvidia from a PC graphics company into the dominant supplier of chips powering the AI boom. In 2006, he bet the company’s entire gross profit on CUDA, a technology that allowed GPUs to handle non-graphics tasks, despite no market demand. That bet paid off when University of Toronto researchers used Nvidia GPUs in 2012 to train AlexNet, a breakthrough image-recognition network widely credited with sparking the AI revolution. “He had the vision for what it could be and the courage to pursue it,” Peddie said.

Despite Huang’s departure, analysts do not expect an immediate business crisis. “This is a stock shock, not a business shock, for the next year,” said Pat Moorhead of Moor Insights & Strategy. “The roadmap and manufacturing are locked in through 2027.” Creative Strategies analyst Ben Bajarin noted that the product roadmap remains strong, with several generations already in design or on order. “The product roadmap is set and remains strong… That supply position does not walk out the door with one person,” he said.

The bigger risk, according to analysts, is longer-term strategic vision. “The question is whether Nvidia keeps inventing at Jensen’s pace once those plans run out,” Moorhead said. Huang built an exceptionally talented organization, but his departure leaves a leadership gap that may require an outsider. “No one in the company really wants to step into his shoes, so an outsider is going to have to be found,” one source noted. Nvidia’s future hinges on choosing a successor with technical credibility and a plan to protect its CUDA ecosystem, while navigating the fallout from the scandals that prompted Huang’s exit.
