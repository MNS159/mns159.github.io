US shifts to economic pressure as Iran war stalemate continues; Iran claims strike on US radar in Bahrain

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

The United States is pivoting to economic pressure on Iran as the war that began on February 28 remains at a stalemate, with President Donald Trump sidelining Vice President JD Vance from the Iran file and declaring a deal is no longer a priority. Iran claimed its Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps struck a US early warning radar system in Bahrain on Friday, but the US military's Central Command denied the claim and said it shot down two Iranian attack drones with no casualties or damage. Tehran said the strike was retaliation for a US attack on a telecommunications tower in Iran's Sirik county on the Strait of Hormuz.

Trump wrote on Truth Social that "we don't need a deal with Iran. Those days have long since passed. We've got their money." Vance, who had been the US chief negotiator and favored a negotiated resolution, was redirected to focus on alleged Democratic corruption after Republicans lost their House majority in midterm elections, losses Vance blamed on the unpopular war. Mediated contacts via Qatar continue but no breakthrough is expected; Tehran demands seven conditions and clear sequencing of concessions, which Washington finds unacceptable. Professor Nasser Hadian of the University of Tehran said Vance's sidelining signals that reaching a deal is not a priority and that the US believes economic pressure can force concessions.

The war has severely disrupted the Strait of Hormuz, a critical transit point for global oil and gas supplies. The United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations recorded 139 outbound and 169 inbound transits in the past week, approximately 65 percent below pre-conflict levels. Iran continues to attack ships it considers unauthorized users of the waterway, while the US Navy enforces a blockade of Iranian vessels and escorts tankers. The International Monetary Fund says Gulf Arab states are in economic recession despite higher oil prices: Kuwait, Qatar and Iraq are contracting approximately 16 percent this year; Bahrain's economy is down 11 percent.

Trump met Qatar's prime minister this week and said afterward that Gulf Arab allies prefer "the status quo" of continued economic pressure on Iran. He wrote that while the US remains "locked and loaded," an attack "seems to be quite unlikely." However, the US is sending a third aircraft carrier with thousands of troops to the region, keeping open the possibility of escalation. Iranian media said the US struck one of Iran's islands in the Strait of Hormuz on Friday. Trump is expected to meet Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin at an APEC summit in China next week to discuss ending the war. Senior UAE diplomat Anwar Gargash said the region must "prepare for a prolonged period of uncertainty."
