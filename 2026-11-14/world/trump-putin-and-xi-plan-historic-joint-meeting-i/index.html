Trump, Putin and Xi to hold historic joint meeting at APEC summit in China

International & World desk

US President Donald Trump, Russian President Vladimir Putin and Chinese President Xi Jinping are planning to hold a historic trilateral meeting on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) summit in Shenzhen, China, next week, the White House has confirmed. The summit runs from November 18-19. White House spokesperson Olivia Wales said Trump would arrive in Shenzhen late on Wednesday and that the trilateral meeting would conclude his visit to China. Trump will also hold bilateral talks with Xi on Thursday, their fourth meeting since he returned to office in 2025.

The White House said the talks would focus on reducing nuclear weapons, which Trump believes is very dangerous. A senior US official told news agencies that no agreement was expected to follow the discussions. The New Strategic Arms Reduction Treaty (New START) between the US and Russia expired in February, leaving the world's largest nuclear arsenals without major guardrails. Trump has insisted on a new framework that includes China, which is rapidly building up its arsenal. Beijing has said it would be 'neither fair, reasonable nor realistic' to ask China to join trilateral nuclear disarmament negotiations at this stage.

Iran is also expected to be discussed. The US and Israel launched a war on Iran on February 28; full-scale strikes have ceased but the conflict remains deadlocked. The Strait of Hormuz remains closed, affecting oil and gas prices and disrupting Chinese crude oil supplies and exports. Russia has profited from rising energy prices and recent US sanctions relief. Ukraine, however, is unlikely to be raised. A US official said Washington is pursuing peace talks through separate channels and that China is not part of that track. Trump has accused Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy of not wanting to end the war and has lifted oil sanctions on Moscow while agreeing to allow Russian diesel into global markets.

Russia has confirmed the meeting; China has not yet officially commented on the trilateral talks, though Chinese state media reported Xi would take part in bilateral meetings at the summit. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov earlier said there were 'signals confirming readiness' for such talks. Analysts suggest the meeting may be largely performative, allowing Putin to project power and sideline the Ukraine conflict, while Trump can present himself as a global statesman. The meeting comes after Trump's Republican Party suffered a crushing defeat in midterm elections, which could weaken his leverage. Some Russian officials have drawn parallels to the 1945 Yalta conference, but Peskov said any agreements reached cannot be imposed on other nations.
