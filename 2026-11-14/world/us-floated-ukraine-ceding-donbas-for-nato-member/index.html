Ukraine War

US floated Ukraine ceding Donbas for NATO membership, Zelensky says

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts slightly center-left

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky said on Friday that United States negotiators raised the idea of Ukraine withdrawing its remaining forces from the eastern Donbas region in exchange for NATO membership, marking the first time the proposal had been put forward. The suggestion came during the latest round of US-mediated peace talks in Abu Dhabi this week, following meetings between US envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff with both Zelensky in Dublin and Russian President Vladimir Putin in Moscow. Zelensky said the question made him believe "NATO is back on the agenda," but cautioned that "you have also heard Putin's signals, and this is an extremely difficult conversation."

Zelensky stated that Ukraine would not cede territory, but added that if Kyiv received a comprehensive proposal including firm security guarantees, no constraints on its military, and funds for reconstruction, he would put the question to a public referendum. "The Americans raised a question: 'What if Ukraine joins NATO? Would Ukraine withdraw from Donbas?' This was the first time this had been raised," Zelensky told reporters. The Donbas, comprising Ukraine's Donetsk and Luhansk regions, is largely occupied by Russian forces, with Russia having declared its annexation in 2022 after launching its full-scale invasion. Russia currently occupies roughly a fifth of Ukrainian territory, including most of the Donbas.

Moscow has long opposed Ukraine joining NATO, demanding instead Kyiv's guaranteed neutrality, limits on the size of Ukraine's military, and territorial concessions. Russia has also revived earlier demands for NATO to halt any expansion in its vicinity and roll back military infrastructure from central and eastern European member states. Zelensky warned that Moscow's bid to review the continent's security architecture was "dangerous because … it is precisely things like this that lead to wars or escalations," adding that "real security in Europe means the EU and NATO."

The talks come amid strained relations between Kyiv and Washington. Trump had asked Ukraine to pause strikes on Russian oil facilities to ease global fuel prices, and Zelensky accused US officials of limiting Ukraine's Starlink internet service, which it uses to coordinate defensive operations. Trump said it was "time for Ukraine to get a new president" and called for fresh elections, which are suspended under martial law. Zelensky has said he would be willing to hold elections within months of a peace deal and would give up the presidency in exchange for NATO membership.

European representatives, including from Germany, France, the United Kingdom and Poland, are set to take part in the next round of talks, despite Moscow's longstanding insistence that European states be excluded because they support Ukraine. Meanwhile, fighting continues: Russia's Defence Ministry said its forces captured the settlement of Tersianka in Ukraine's Zaporizhzhia region, and a Ukrainian drone attack killed three people, including a 24-month-old child, in Russia's Belgorod region. Russia launched 96 drones overnight, of which Ukraine said 81 were shot down or suppressed, while Russia claimed it intercepted 29 Ukrainian drones. Zelensky warned that Putin was "betting everything" on this winter's campaign, which Kyiv says is aimed at hurting public morale.
