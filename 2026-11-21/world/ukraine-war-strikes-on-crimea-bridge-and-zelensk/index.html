Ukraine war: Zelensky defends Crimea Bridge strikes as 'fair response'; Russian cargo ship hit in Gulf of Finland

International & World desk · anchored on Reuters

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky has justified Kyiv's repeated attacks on the Kerch Bridge linking Russia to occupied Crimea, calling them a "fair response" to 109 separate Russian attacks on bridges in Ukraine. In an interview with the BBC, Zelensky rejected Russian President Vladimir Putin's claim that the bridge is "exclusively civilian" in use, pointing to online images of tanks and military hardware using the route as a supply line. "If Putin used the internet, he could see that," Zelensky said. This week's combined missile-and-drone strikes by Ukrainian forces destroyed the central road section of the bridge, collapsing part of it and one of its support arches into the sea, rendering it unusable for road traffic. Ukraine's General Staff declared that "the short supply route from Russian territory has been destroyed," while the Security Service of Ukraine (SBU) said the operation "shook the myth of Russia's greatness." According to Al Jazeera, it was Ukraine's fourth and most complex operation on the bridge, possibly crippling its rail section as well. Bloomberg reported that cleanup of the downed bridge will take Russia months, according to Ukraine.

Russian attacks on Ukraine continued on Friday evening and overnight, killing a seven-year-old boy and a 20-year-old female medic in Zelensky's hometown of Kryvyi Rih in the central Dnipropetrovsk region. Local officials said the boy was in a playground at the time. A local official added that when rescuers rushed to the scene, the same site was targeted again with jet-powered drones. Another 58 people were injured in the city. Ukraine's state emergency service DSNS said 11 people were also injured in the north-eastern Kharkiv region, and one person in Rivne in the north-west. Zelensky described Putin as a "killer," in response to US President Donald Trump referring to Putin this week as his "friend."

Zelensky was speaking ahead of a meeting in Kyiv with UK Defence Secretary Wes Streeting, who told the Ukrainian president he was there to show that Europe was united in its support. "The more Putin tries to divide us, the more resolute we become," Streeting said. He called Russian missiles hitting Poland on Friday during military exercises of the so-called Coalition of the Willing an "outrageous provocation," adding that the timing seemed "more than a coincidence." Asked about NATO's response, Streeting said the best way was to "do more for Ukraine" to defend itself against Putin, whom he called a "cold-blooded killer." Streeting's visit came after European leaders meeting in Berlin on Thursday alongside US envoys said they were ready for negotiations with Moscow on ending the war. UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham said Europe has "a clear and consistent and united negotiating position," while German Chancellor Friedrich Merz stressed that Moscow's "maximalist demands are not realistic." US envoy Jared Kushner said President Trump wanted to make a deal with Moscow "with Europe by his side." Putin has so far rejected any European role in US-led peace talks but earlier this week invited European representatives to Moscow. The Berlin meeting came hours after Putin held trilateral talks in Shenzhen, China, with Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping.

In a separate development on Saturday, Finland said it "detected a drone strike on a Russian merchant vessel" in the eastern Gulf of Finland, in an international maritime area. The Finnish defence ministry said in a statement that the vessel was damaged, without giving further details. It added that temporary restrictions on aviation and maritime areas in the eastern Gulf of Finland were reinstated for safety reasons. Russia has not publicly commented on the incident.
