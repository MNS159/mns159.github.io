Denmark Accuses Russia of Plotting to Kill US-Ukrainian Rapper, Expels Diplomat

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts slightly center-left

Denmark has accused Russian intelligence services of plotting to assassinate a Ukrainian-American rapper on Danish soil and has expelled Russia’s defense attaché in response, Danish authorities said Friday. The target, identified by Danish media as rapper Kyivstoner—whose real name is Albert Vasiliev and who holds U.S. and Ukrainian nationality—was set to perform at a concert north of Copenhagen on September 25 when police arrested three men traveling in a vehicle near the venue. Firearms and knives were found in the car, and two of the individuals have since been charged with attempted murder and assisting a foreign intelligence service; both pleaded not guilty, according to local media.

Danish Foreign Minister Lars Løkke Rasmussen announced the expulsion of Russia’s defense attaché, who must leave the country within seven days, after summoning the Russian ambassador. “We are saying this very clearly: we have exposed you,” Rasmussen told a news conference. He described the alleged plot as Russian “intimidation” and an attempt to make Denmark “waver” in its support for Ukraine. The head of Denmark’s security and intelligence service (PET), Rasmus Kieffer-Kristensen, said, “It is our clear assessment that the assassination attempt was planned by the Russian intelligence services.” PET stated that the individuals allegedly involved were associated with the outlawed Danish gang Loyal to Familia, “known for extensive, serious and violent criminal activity.” Police stopped the vehicle at about 9:55 p.m.—five minutes before the concert was due to begin—and one suspect had purchased a ticket to the show, according to court documents.

The incident comes after Russia’s FSB security service in July accused Vasiliev and Ukrainian authorities of organizing a “terrorist attack” on military facilities in Russia, an allegation the rapper denied on social media. Danish Prime Minister Mette Frederiksen said the plot “is a clear illustration of the grave security situation Denmark and Europe find ourselves in. We are facing a Russia under pressure which is acting more aggressively and is more willing to take risks.” The European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen condemned what she called “the assassination attempt against a US-Ukrainan citizen in Denmark, orchestrated by Russian intelligence services,” adding, “This was an attack on EU soil. And it is unacceptable.” European Council President António Costa said, “We will not be intimidated. Russia’s tactics and threats will not weaken the European Union’s resolve to continue supporting Ukraine.”

Russia’s embassy in Copenhagen called the accusations “unsubstantiated” and said Denmark failed to provide evidence of Moscow’s involvement. In a statement, the embassy claimed a “Danish model” had emerged, which it described as a “Russian trace” allegedly being discovered and “exploited for a propaganda spectacle,” accusing Denmark of “provocations” intended to “frighten the Danish population in order to justify … a prolonged confrontation with Russia.” Russian state media cited the foreign ministry in Moscow as saying it would respond to the expulsion “accordingly.”

Rasmussen also said Denmark is pushing for the European Union to restrict Russian travel within the Schengen zone. In a message to senior European diplomats, he proposed that the EU halt tourist visas for Russians, cease recognition of Russian non-biometric passports across the Schengen area, and issue more limited visas that do not allow travel to other Schengen countries. “We must take concrete actions that project European strength and decisiveness,” his message said. Von der Leyen said the EU would soon propose a “new European security strategy” to reinforce preparedness and deterrence. The alleged plot, which PET chief Kieffer-Kristensen described as unprecedented in Denmark, has heightened concerns about Russian hybrid threats as the war in Ukraine continues.
