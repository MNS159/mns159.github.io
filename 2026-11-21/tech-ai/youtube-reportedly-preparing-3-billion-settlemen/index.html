YouTube reportedly preparing $3 billion settlement over social media addiction claims

Tech / AI desk

YouTube is reportedly preparing to pay $3 billion to thousands of U.S. school districts to settle claims that its platform's "addictive design" harmed children, according to Bloomberg. The agreement, which sources say has been reached "in principle," would also require YouTube to introduce new safeguards for teens, the report states.

The potential settlement would allow Google, YouTube's parent company, to avoid a wave of lawsuits filed by school districts accusing YouTube, Meta, Snap, and TikTok of negatively impacting the mental health of teen users. The first "bellwether" trial of these federal multidistrict litigation (MDL) cases was scheduled to begin in Kentucky in June, but all four companies settled for a total of $27 million before it went to court. The next trial is set for February 2027 before an Oakland, California, judge, with Meta, Snap, and TikTok as defendants.

Google did not immediately respond to The Verge's request for comment.

In August, Meta agreed to pay $17.1 billion over 10 years to settle lawsuits filed by a group of 47 states, along with several districts and territories. That agreement also included stricter safeguards for teens, but Meta is pushing TikTok and YouTube to implement similar measures. As part of the deal, Meta will only pay the remaining $5.3 billion of its $17.1 billion settlement if YouTube and TikTok roll out comparable changes and pay matching amounts. In March, both YouTube and Meta were found negligent in a separate legal battle over social media addiction.

Sources: The Verge
