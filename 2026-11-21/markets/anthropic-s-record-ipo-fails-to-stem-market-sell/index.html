Anthropic's record IPO fails to stem market sell-off as 'smart money' shifts

Financial & Markets desk

Anthropic's blockbuster initial public offering, which raised $81 billion after pricing above its expected range at $83 per share, failed to halt a broader market sell-off on Friday as new data shows U.S. corporations have shifted from buying back shares to issuing them, adding supply that the four-year-old bull market must absorb.

According to a recent analysis by Acadian Asset Management, net new issuance of shares equaled 3.1% of total market capitalization as of late last week. Portfolio manager Owen Lamont wrote in a letter to investors this month that 'no matter how you measure it, U.S. corporations have changed their behavior over the past two years, switching from repurchasing in 2024 to issuing today.' He added: 'The question you should be asking is: Why is the smart money selling?'

The shift marks a reversal from the era of net repurchases, which had helped boost share prices by shrinking the number of shares available for trading and increasing earnings per share. Now, increased equity supply is acting as a potential headwind. The Baker-Wurgler equity share, which measures equity issuance as a share of total stock and bond issuance, peaked at about 18% in 2021 and has risen in recent months but remains below that peak, Lamont noted.

Recent large equity deals include Oracle's filing to sell $20 billion in stock through an at-the-market program and a separate $10 billion common-share issuance in November; Meta's $25 billion common-stock offering and $12.5 billion in mandatory convertible preferred shares in October; Alphabet's $84.8 billion equity raise in June to fund AI infrastructure; and SpaceX's $85.7 billion IPO in June after underwriters exercised their overallotment. Federal Reserve data show debt gross issuance rose first in 2020, followed by equity issuance in 2021; more recently, debt issuance began climbing in 2024, followed by a surge in equity issuance in 2026.

Lamont wrote that 'before 2026, firms were net repurchasers of equity, consistent with the idea that they viewed their shares as undervalued. Today, for whatever reason, firms are issuing equity on net.' He said he cannot rule out that the market is in an AI bubble now. Anthropic's stock, which debuted this week, was falling on Friday and dragging the broader market with it, underscoring investor unease despite the strong initial demand for the frontier AI laboratory's shares.
