FDA rejects Capricor's Duchenne muscular dystrophy therapy over data integrity concerns

Health & Science desk · anchored on Reuters

The U.S. Food and Drug Administration on Friday declined to approve Capricor Therapeutics' experimental cell therapy deramiocel for Duchenne muscular dystrophy, citing serious data integrity problems in a key clinical trial that undermined the reliability of the evidence. The agency issued a Complete Response Letter, determining that the available data did not meet the required standard for approval. Shares of Capricor plunged 72% in extended trading following the announcement.

Deramiocel was intended to preserve upper-limb function in patients with Duchenne, a fatal genetic disease that causes progressive muscle weakness, loss of mobility, heart complications and premature death. The FDA's decision follows a lengthy review that included two randomized clinical trials, both of which failed to show that deramiocel could stave off heart failure or preserve muscle function in the arms. In July, an FDA advisory committee voted 9–3 that the evidence did not establish deramiocel's effectiveness for Duchenne-related heart disease, prompting Capricor to amend its application to focus on upper-limb function using additional 24-month open-label extension data from the Phase 3 HOPE-3 trial. The agency treated that amendment as a major change and moved the decision deadline to November 22.

The FDA identified deficiencies in the conduct of the HOPE-3 study that compromised the reliability of the trial data, according to a statement from the Muscular Dystrophy Association. Capricor has disputed the FDA's assessment of its clinical evidence and is evaluating its next steps. The company's CEO, Linda Marbán, had said earlier in November that the application was in its final review stages and that Capricor was advancing launch preparations while pacing spending to the regulatory timeline. The company is also pursuing independent commercialization amid an ongoing arbitration with Nippon Shinyaku's unit NS Pharma over a prior distribution agreement.

The Muscular Dystrophy Association issued a statement on Saturday expressing disappointment, calling the decision a heavy blow for the Duchenne community. 'We know this decision is a heavy blow for the Duchenne community, and our hearts are with the families who have waited, hoped, and fought for a new treatment,' the organization said. 'We are grateful to the FDA for its thorough review and to Capricor for its continued fight to bring a treatment to this community.' The MDA added that it seeks to learn more about the data integrity issues and will work with Capricor and advocates to understand next steps. The organization reaffirmed its commitment to accelerating research and advocating for regulatory processes that balance urgency with safety and effectiveness standards.

This is the second rejection for deramiocel; the FDA previously issued a Complete Response Letter in July 2025 after the initial application seeking approval for cardiomyopathy associated with Duchenne was found to lack substantial evidence of effectiveness. Patients with Duchenne, primarily boys, have few treatment options, especially those who have already lost the ability to walk. Capricor has said management does not expect the arbitration with NS Pharma to delay patient access if deramiocel is ever approved, but for now the therapy remains investigational and not FDA-approved.
