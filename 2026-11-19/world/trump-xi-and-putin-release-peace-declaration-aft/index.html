Trump, Xi and Putin release 'peace declaration' after first trilateral summit at APEC

International & World desk

US President Donald Trump, Chinese President Xi Jinping, and Russian President Vladimir Putin issued a joint 'peace declaration' on Thursday following their first-ever trilateral summit, held on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) meeting in Shenzhen, China. The 90-minute closed-door meeting, chaired by Xi, marked a historic diplomatic moment as the leaders of the world's three largest nuclear powers gathered together for the first time.

In the declaration, the three leaders stated that 'nuclear war cannot be won and must never be fought' and agreed to increase the production and supply of petroleum. They also committed to reforming the United Nations to respond more effectively to global challenges and flagged the issue of immunity for state officials, asserting that 'the International Criminal Court and other similar institutions must not abuse their jurisdiction.' Xi, in his opening remarks, urged shedding a 'Cold War mentality' and called for 'positive interaction among major powers' as a 'mandatory requirement.' Putin highlighted shared interests in ending the war in Ukraine and addressing food and energy security, while Trump said the three countries have a 'special responsibility' to maintain productive dialogue and to 'combat the threat of nuclear proliferation,' specifically referencing Iran.

The summit took place amid ongoing conflicts: the US war on Iran, which began in February 2026 and led to the closure of the Strait of Hormuz, and Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine, launched in 2022. While Trump said he and Putin had a 'long discussion' on Ukraine and expressed hope for an end to the war, Putin later told reporters that Ukraine and Europe were 'not yet ready to make compromises.' He also vowed a 'far more painful' retaliation for Ukrainian attacks on the bridge connecting Russia to Crimea. Notably, as Putin held his press conference, Kyiv was under attack from Russian drones. There was no mention of the Iran war in the declaration beyond Trump's statement that 'we're not going to let Iran have a nuclear weapon.' A Kremlin spokesperson said Ukraine was not the 'central topic' of the trilateral talks.

The meeting was reportedly Putin's idea, and Russian newspapers called it a 'diplomatic victory' after years of Western efforts to isolate Moscow. It was also the first time since the Ukraine war began that Putin participated in an international gathering attended by leaders from countries including Canada, Japan, and Australia. Xi described the talks as 'positive, constructive, and highly productive,' while Putin called them an 'important chapter in the history of diplomacy.' Trump praised the meeting as 'a great and highly consequential trip' on social media and announced he would host a news conference on Friday at the White House.

US lawmakers reacted with frustration. Republican Senator John Curtis, a member of the Senate Foreign Relations Committee, said he was encouraged by the commitment that 'nuclear war must never be fought' but expressed concern about Trump's approach, stating that 'Vladimir Putin and Xi Jinping are not our friends.' Democratic Senator Jeanne Shaheen, the ranking member on the same committee, wrote on X: 'President Trump's "friend" is a murderous war criminal.' Political commentators noted that Trump and Putin's statements at the summit distracted from APEC's planned agenda.
