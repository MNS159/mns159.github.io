Putin Vows 'Far More Painful' Response After Ukraine Strikes Crimean Bridge for Third Time in a Week

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts slightly center-left

President Vladimir Putin vowed Thursday that Russia's retaliation for Ukrainian strikes on the Crimean Bridge would be "far more painful" than the attacks themselves, as Ukraine hit the strategic bridge for the third time in a week. The 12-mile bridge, which connects Russia to Ukraine's Russian-occupied Crimean Peninsula, was struck in the early hours of Thursday by Neptune cruise missiles, according to Ukraine. The attack forced a suspension of train services, though they resumed later in the day, while car traffic remained prohibited after a previous strike on Tuesday destroyed the central road section, collapsing part of it into the sea.

Speaking at a press conference in Shenzhen, China, after meetings with President Donald Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping, Putin said he had discussed the possibility of a limited ceasefire in certain areas of Ukraine but ruled out negotiations following the bridge strikes. "Honestly, I discussed the issue with my colleagues at the Security Council, and I asked them to think about it and go back to the issue, so that we could reach some agreements," Putin said, according to Russian state news agency TASS. "However, we will not make agreements after strikes on the Crimean Bridge. We will respond to these strikes." Putin claimed the bridge was used exclusively for civilian purposes, despite Kyiv's allegations that it is used to transport military equipment and supplies for Russian troops in southern Ukraine.

The bridge, which Putin ceremonially opened in 2018 after Russia illegally annexed Crimea in 2014, holds deep symbolic and logistical importance. It is the longest bridge in Europe and serves as a key supply route for Russian forces in occupied southern Ukraine, as well as a link to the Black Sea Fleet based in Crimea. Tuesday's attack, which Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky called an "unprecedented operation" weeks in the making, involved the destruction of air defense systems, radars, and electronic warfare assets before missiles and drones dealt the final blow. The damage left a gap of about 200 meters (660 feet) in the road surface, dragging it into the sea.

Ukraine's defense ministry described the strikes as "time gained and lives saved," adding, "The less that reaches the occupiers, the less hits our warriors." Russia's transport ministry said no one was hurt in Thursday's attack and that specialists were assessing the damage. Crimea's Russian-installed governor, Sergei Aksyonov, issued a defiant video message, saying, "We have the strength, the resolve and the understanding of how to resolve this situation. We will overcome everything. Crimea is with Russia forever!" Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said of Tuesday's hit, "The bridge will be rebuilt, and it will be even better than before," though repairs are expected to take time and may be carried out under Ukrainian fire.

The strikes came as Putin met with Trump and Xi in what Moscow cast as a historic trilateral summit, though the White House readout of the meeting did not mention Ukraine. Meanwhile, U.S. peace envoys, including Jared Kushner and special envoy Steve Witkoff, met with European leaders in Germany to discuss efforts toward a "just, lasting and durable peace in Ukraine." German Chancellor Friedrich Merz called the China summit a photo-op and said the trilateral format "will not bring peace" in Ukraine. Russia has regularly targeted Ukrainian civilian infrastructure since launching its full-scale invasion in 2022, and this week, as the bridge was being hit, Moscow struck key bridge crossings over the Dnipro River in Kyiv.
