Anthropic raises over $100 billion in record-breaking IPO

Politics desk
✓ Facts
  • Anthropic, maker of the AI model Claude, began trading on the Nasdaq exchange after raising more than $100 billion in its initial public offering.
  • The company sold 956 million shares priced at $83 each, above its original target range of $70 to $78 per share.
  • The IPO valued Anthropic at an estimated $1.4 to $1.5 trillion, a level that would place it among the 10 most valuable publicly traded U.S. companies.
  • Anthropic's seven founders sold more than 23 million shares as part of the offering.
  • The company's prospectus outlines plans for an additional $26 billion in shares to be sold through private placements with Google, Microsoft and Nvidia.
  • Anthropic was founded by siblings Daniela and Dario Amodei and other former OpenAI employees who left that company over disagreements about AI safety practices.
  • Analyst estimates cited in connection with the IPO project Anthropic's revenue will reach $52 billion in 2026 and $135 billion in 2027.
  • Anthropic's IPO prospectus flagged political risk from an ongoing dispute with the Pentagon over permissible uses of its AI models.
○ Analysis

Anthropic's IPO is the largest in U.S. corporate history and marks the first time ordinary investors can directly buy shares in a company built solely around large language models, rather than the chipmakers, cloud providers or data-center firms that have so far dominated AI-related public investing. The company's own prospectus claims AI could have a bigger economic impact than industrialization, electricity or the internet, estimating an addressable market as large as $40 trillion a year. The listing arrives amid broader uncertainty about whether AI valuations reflect a sustainable business model or a speculative bubble: several AI-infrastructure stocks have fallen sharply in value in recent months, and observers note that a meaningful share of Anthropic's revenue comes from a small number of corporate customers — some of whom are also its investors and chip suppliers — raising questions about how much of the AI boom's apparent growth is circular financing among a handful of large tech firms. Anthropic's listing follows SpaceX's earlier move to the public markets, and reporting suggests OpenAI could pursue a similar IPO as soon as next year.

Steelman

The IPO's real significance lies less in its record size than in what it tests: whether public markets, not just venture investors and tech giants trading capital among themselves, believe AI revenue growth can justify trillion-dollar valuations — a verdict that will shape how the broader AI investment boom is read in hindsight, regardless of political affiliation.

Implications

The IPO's performance in coming months will be watched as a bellwether for whether AI valuations can be sustained once subjected to public market scrutiny, and may influence whether OpenAI and other major AI labs pursue similar public listings. Anthropic's unresolved dispute with the Pentagon over permitted uses of its models could also become a point of regulatory or political friction as the company's government and defense-related business grows.
