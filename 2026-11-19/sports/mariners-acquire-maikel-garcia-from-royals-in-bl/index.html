Mariners acquire All-Star third baseman Maikel Garcia from Royals in blockbuster trade

Sports desk

The Seattle Mariners acquired All-Star third baseman Maikel Garcia, right-handed pitcher James McArthur and minor league pitcher L.P. Langevin from the Kansas City Royals on Wednesday, in what multiple outlets are calling the first blockbuster of the MLB offseason. The Royals received right-handed pitchers Bryce Miller and Logan Evans, along with left-handed pitcher José A. Ferrer, the teams announced.

Garcia, 26, broke out in 2025, winning a Gold Glove at third base and hitting .286 with 16 home runs and 74 RBI, earning his first All-Star selection. He regressed offensively in 2026, hitting .268 with three home runs and 38 RBI in 82 games, limited by injury. Since becoming a full-time third baseman in 2023, he has graded as one of the best at the position. Garcia signed a five-year, $57.5 million contract extension with the Royals in 2025 and is under team control through 2030 with a team option in 2031. Mariners president of baseball operations Jerry Dipoto said in a statement, 'It is rare to have an opportunity to acquire a player signed for the long term who fits our needs as well as Maikel does. His ability to impact the game with his bat, his defense and his baserunning makes us a more athletic and well-rounded team.'

For the Royals, Bryce Miller, 28, appeared in 22 games (21 starts) last season with the Mariners, posting a 4-10 record with a 4.17 ERA in 118.2 innings. His best season came in 2024, when he had a 2.94 ERA in 180.1 innings across 31 starts. Logan Evans, 25, did not pitch in 2026 after undergoing UCL surgery; he appeared in 16 games (15 starts) with the Mariners in 2025, going 6-5 with a 4.32 ERA in 81.1 innings. The Royals are banking on Miller and Evans to slot into their starting rotation next season, as the loss of Garcia weakens an already bad lineup that averaged 4.26 runs per game in 2026, 22nd in baseball.

The trade comes as the league faces an anticipated lockout on Dec. 2, with owners widely expected to lock out players at midnight, preventing any signings or trades during that period. The early timing has heated up the hot stove. Separately, Yahoo Sports reported that the New York Yankees had inquired about Garcia before the trade, as third base is a clear area of need for them. The Yankees are now reportedly interested in other third base options, including the possibility of trading for Corey Seager and moving him to third base, though Seager's no-trade clause could be an obstacle.
