Manfred says MLB owners won't budge on salary cap, lockout looms

Sports desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts right

Major League Baseball Commissioner Rob Manfred said Wednesday that owners will not compromise on a salary cap and floor, a stance that risks a lockout when the current collective bargaining agreement expires. The two sides have roughly two weeks to reach a new deal before a potential work stoppage.

“There's plenty of room for compromise,” Manfred said. “But there's one thing I will not compromise, and that's the issue that we're hearing from the vast majority of our fans: They want salary cap and floor, and on that, we will not compromise.” The players’ union has long opposed a salary cap.

Manfred criticized the union for what he called a refusal to bargain. “Another word for 'nonstarter' over months is a refusal to bargain,” he said. “I think it's time that the MLBPA needs to realize that this proposal is serious. I'm not suggesting they need to accept it wholecloth. I think they need to look at the proposal and try to find some ways to move to the middle as opposed to making no proposal at all.”

The commissioner also drew attention for remarks about the timing of negotiations. “A decision on a lockout is one that we're going to make at the last possible moment,” Manfred said. “I'm not that big on Thanksgiving as a holiday. We'll make ourselves available wherever and whenever to bargain in an effort to make an agreement.” The lockout date is set for Dec. 2, when negotiations are scheduled to begin.

MLBPA head Bruce Meyer has not publicly responded to Manfred’s latest comments. The union has consistently rejected the concept of a salary cap, and the impasse has led to widespread concern among fans about the possibility of a prolonged work stoppage. With no deal in sight, the 2022 season remains in jeopardy.
