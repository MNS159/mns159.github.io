Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella discusses buyouts, Nvidia confidence, and AI agent economics

Tech / AI desk

Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella addressed a range of topics during an appearance at The New York Times DealBook Summit on Wednesday, including the company's offer of buyouts to 60,000 employees, his confidence in Nvidia following the departure of its CEO, and his predictions about how AI agents will reshape the economics of the internet.

Nadella explained that the buyouts, offered in October, were intended to give employees agency as Microsoft reorganizes into an AI-first company. He emphasized that the goal is not to replace workers with AI but to transform how work is done, with employees managing AI agents rather than performing tasks directly. He cited the example of the team building Cowork, the AI agent in Microsoft 365 apps, which consists of just 11 developers who use AI to build a software factory that produces the product. Nadella acknowledged that the changes are difficult for some employees and that the buyout was a way for them to choose whether to adapt or leave.

On the topic of Nvidia, Nadella expressed confidence in the company after former CEO Jensen Huang stepped down amid reports of an affair with an executive believed to be a Chinese spy and relationships with two Nvidia employees. Nadella said he has known Huang for a long time and admires his work but wants to give him space. He noted that interim CEO Colette Kress previously worked at Microsoft and that he remains in touch with the same Nvidia team, expressing confidence in their ability to continue their work. Nadella also reflected on the role of founders, saying they are singular but not gods, and that companies must eventually have successors.

Nadella also warned about vulnerabilities in critical infrastructure, noting that much of it is unpatchable and connected to the internet, making it susceptible to AI-aided cyberattacks. He referenced a recent Microsoft Outlook outage caused by a latent bug, which the company is addressing through architectural and operational fixes. He highlighted the challenge of patching software vulnerabilities quickly enough to match the speed of exploitation, which he said can create instability.

Regarding the rise of AI agents, Nadella predicted that they will reshuffle the economics of the web, impacting business models reliant on advertising and human web traffic. He suggested that companies blocking AI agents, such as Amazon, could lose out to competitors that embrace them. Nadella compared the shift to the transition from web portals to search engines, noting that companies attached to existing business models will need to adapt or scramble.
