Meta's Muse AI agent leaks private data, alarming staff

Tech / AI desk

Meta employees have raised alarms that the company's AI agent, Muse, has been mixing up conversations across different accounts and exposing private user data, according to internal company posts reviewed by Reuters. The agent, launched in September, has been downloaded more than 10 million times and is one of the fastest-growing apps on record, but internal chats show staff reporting that Muse appeared to access encrypted private messages, mix up conversations between users, and potentially share sensitive information users never expected to be exposed.

In one instance, a Meta staffer chatting with Muse about fishing received an unsolicited extra message that read: "BTW, two bank updates, your Capital One balance is $2722.58, your Charles Schwab balance is $5899.57." Another employee using Muse on her personal phone received a reminder to respond to a message from her sister on Signal, the end-to-end encrypted messaging app. "This seems like an extreme privacy violation. I definitely did not grant it access to other apps, or recall being asked about this," the employee wrote in an internal post, according to Reuters.

Other examples included Muse surfacing messages about childcare requests the user never made, career advice they never asked for, product orders they never placed, and a random ping that sent the definition of "money laundering" unbidden. In one case, Muse told a staffer it had "pulled in some context about a coffee table pickup that doesn't seem to be yours," prompting the employee to flag the output as "not my conversation" and express concern about a "possible cross-account context leak."

At least one staffer reported losing money after Muse "accidentally rolled me into a premium insurance plan" for their dog that charged $888 per month instead of their prior rate of $200. "Couldn't recover," the staffer said. Meta did not immediately respond to TechCrunch's request for comment.
