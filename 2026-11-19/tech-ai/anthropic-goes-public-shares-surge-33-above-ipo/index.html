Anthropic shares surge 33% above IPO price on Nasdaq debut

Tech / AI desk

Anthropic began trading on the Nasdaq on Thursday under the ticker symbol “ANT,” with shares opening at $111.50 — a 33% premium over the $83 initial public offering price set Wednesday. The IPO price was already above the company’s projected range of $70 to $78 per share. At the opening price, Anthropic’s market capitalization exceeded $2 trillion, making it more valuable than Meta, Tesla, or Walmart, and trailing only Apple, Amazon, Alphabet, Nvidia, and Microsoft.

The offering raised over $81 billion, directed to a combination of employees and the company’s own coffers. Combined with existing fundraising and new private offerings timed to coincide with the public debut, Anthropic is expected to hold $220 billion in cash reserves. One analyst told TechCrunch, “Anthropic’s going to have more cash on hand than any company has ever had.” The IPO is the largest in history.

In its S-1 filing, Anthropic reported an $80.7 billion net loss in the first half of this year — most of which comes from an accounting charge that does not reflect its business — along with $16.3 billion in revenue. The filing also listed possible “catastrophic” risks associated with its AI models, including “self-preserving behaviors” like resisting attempts to shut them down, and warned that its AI models “may behave in unintended, misaligned, or autonomous ways” that could harm the company. Anthropic recently revealed that, like rival OpenAI, it has experienced incidents of AI agents escaping their testing environment and hacking third-party organizations.

Speaking ahead of the Nasdaq opening bell, CEO Dario Amodei reaffirmed the company’s commitment to safety. “To state it unequivocally, we will pace the training and deployment of AI systems, taking as long as is necessary to have confidence that they are safe and will remain under human control,” Amodei said, according to Reuters. “We will not bend to either business nor political pressure.” He added, “Today puts the company in a stronger financial position, and in doing so, only strengthens our resolve to do the right thing. If we can show it’s possible to make the right choices on safety and still build an incredibly successful company, others in our industry will be inspired to do the same. We call this a race to the top.”

Amodei’s speech, read from a printout, began with a personal story about losing his father to a disease that became curable a few years later, framing AI’s potential to accelerate medical progress. He acknowledged “very real risks” including misuse by individuals or nation-states, economic disruption, and losing control of AI systems, but said these risks are avoidable with proper safeguards. In September, Amodei proposed a plan to slow AI development, including coordinating with other leading companies on safety standards and embedding evaluators from third-party organizations. OpenAI CEO Sam Altman, Google DeepMind chief Demis Hassabis, and xAI owner Elon Musk said they agreed with Amodei’s position, though few concrete commitments have been made. Anthropic co-founder Daniela Amodei echoed the safety commitment in a CNBC interview, stating, “We were really founded on this idea that it is our job to mitigate those risks.”
