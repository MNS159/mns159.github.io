Anthropic surges 27% in record $81B IPO, topping $2 trillion market cap on Nasdaq debut

Financial & Markets desk · anchored on Reuters

Anthropic, the artificial-intelligence company behind chatbot Claude, made a historic stock-market debut Thursday, raising $81.3 billion in the largest initial public offering on record. Shares opened at $111.50 on the Nasdaq and surged 27% in early trading, pushing the company’s market capitalization above $2 trillion. The IPO eclipsed SpaceX’s $75 billion listing in June and marked the first time a pure-play AI firm has sold shares to the public.

Anthropic sold more shares than originally planned, pricing them at $83 each, above the proposed $70-to-$78 range. The offering was heavily oversubscribed; one early investor told the BBC that demand was ten times the number of shares available. In addition to the $81.3 billion from the public sale, a group of technology companies including Google, Microsoft and Nvidia committed $26 billion in separate investments, and an overallotment provision could raise up to $12 billion more. On a diluted basis, the company is valued at about $1.5 trillion, while the basic share count puts its worth at roughly $1.1 trillion—more than Walmart and Samsung but less than Tesla and Meta.

Founded in 2021 by seven former OpenAI employees, including siblings Dario and Daniela Amodei, Anthropic has seen revenue surge to $33 billion in the nine months through September, driven by demand for tools that accelerate coding, writing and data analysis. Yet net losses widened to $104 billion over the same period, underscoring the high cost of building frontier AI models. “AI is financially demanding,” said Marta Norton, head of strategy at investment firm Empower. “The public markets open up the financial spigot a little bit more and so the time has come.” Yang Wang, a principal financial analyst at Counterpoint Research, said the central question is whether Anthropic can “keep pace” as hundreds of billions of dollars in financial obligations come due in the next few years.

The debut rattled broader markets. The Dow Jones Industrial Average dropped 800 points, and the S&P 500 and Nasdaq composite slid sharply as investors took profits. The selloff reflected anxiety that even a blockbuster AI IPO may not be enough to reignite the sector’s rally, analysts said. Leah Bramwell of Canaccord Wealth noted strong demand for Anthropic shares “across the board” from both institutional and retail investors.

Anthropic’s listing is seen as a bellwether for the AI industry, which faces mounting headwinds: competition from cheaper rival models, a shortage of expensive computer chips, and Federal Reserve concerns about “complex and opaque” AI-related debt structures, according to minutes from the central bank’s October meeting. The company has called for a slowdown in AI development to focus on safeguards, even as it pushes ahead with growth plans. OpenAI, the maker of ChatGPT and Anthropic’s biggest rival, is expected to go public next year. Until then, Anthropic will be the only publicly traded U.S. company entirely focused on AI.
