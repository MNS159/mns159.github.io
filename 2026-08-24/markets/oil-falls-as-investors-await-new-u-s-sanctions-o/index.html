Oil Falls as Investors Await New U.S. Sanctions on Iran

Financial & Markets desk

Oil prices fell more than $1 a barrel on Monday as investors took profits after two weeks of gains and awaited details of expected new U.S. sanctions on Iran. Brent crude futures dropped $1.55, or 1.64%, to $92.84 a barrel by 0911 GMT, while U.S. West Texas Intermediate crude slid $2.04, or 2.34%, to $85.02 a barrel.

Both contracts had posted a second consecutive weekly gain last week, rising more than 5%, as peace negotiations between the U.S. and Iran stalled. The standoff has constrained oil shipments through the Strait of Hormuz, a route that once carried a fifth of global supplies. Saxo Bank analysts noted in a note that oil slipped after the two-week rally as traders awaited the U.S. plan to economically isolate Iran, due later Monday.

U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent is scheduled to hold a press conference at 1 p.m. EDT (1700 GMT) on Monday and has threatened to impose 'the toughest sanctions in history' on Iran. President Donald Trump has also threatened sanctions on Iran's trading partners. PVM analyst Tamas Varga said that should the pledged embargo be launched, oil supply from the region will fall, adding that the U.S. would likely tighten its naval blockade against Iranian oil exports and that Iran could retaliate with fresh strikes against oil installations in the Middle East.

Shipping data on Monday showed fewer than 20 commodity vessels transited the Strait of Hormuz over the weekend, as Iranian and U.S. blockades restrict traffic through the chokepoint. Offers of Iranian crude to Chinese buyers have declined and prices have jumped as the U.S. blockade has cut Tehran's shipments, according to trade sources. However, Iran has granted permission for a number of Iraqi oil tankers to pass through the strait following repeated requests from Baghdad, Iran's state news agency IRNA reported on Saturday. Iraq's SOMO and QatarEnergy both offered crude for loading inside the strait in tenders, trade sources told Reuters.

Iran condemned the U.S. plans to announce new sanctions, and President Masoud Pezeshkian has called for a diplomatic solution. Pakistan's army chief was visiting Tehran on Monday for mediation talks ahead of the U.S. announcement.

Morgan Stanley analysts noted that Brent has climbed back to around $92 per barrel from as low as $71 in June due to inventory draws and a growing sense that Middle East disruptions can last longer. The bank increased its Brent forecasts, projecting a peak at $100 in the fourth quarter.
