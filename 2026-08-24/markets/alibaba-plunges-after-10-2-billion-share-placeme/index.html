Alibaba Shares Plunge 10% on $10.2 Billion Share Placement to Fund AI Infrastructure

Financial & Markets desk

Alibaba Group shares dropped as much as 10% in Hong Kong trading on Monday after the Chinese e-commerce and cloud computing giant priced a 80 billion Hong Kong dollar ($10.20 billion) placement of newly issued shares to non-U.S. investors. The company will issue 710 million new shares at HK$112.70 apiece, a discount from Friday's closing price of HK$123. Shares were last trading 8.4% lower at HK$112.7.

Alibaba said it plans to use all of the net proceeds to invest in its full-stack AI capabilities, including expanding and enhancing its AI infrastructure. The placement, expected to close Wednesday, comes just days after Alibaba reported a 75% drop in profit for the June quarter as heavy AI spending weighed on results. Capital expenditure jumped 75% to 67.7 billion yuan. The company has announced plans to invest at least 380 billion yuan in cloud computing and AI infrastructure over the next three years.

The market’s negative reaction to the share issue underscores a broader skepticism among global investors about the eventual payback on present AI spending, according to MarketWatch. Alibaba's U.S.-listed shares fell 3.4% in premarket trading.

Vey-Sern Ling, senior equity advisor at UBP, told CNBC last week following Alibaba's latest earnings that the company was well-positioned to pursue AI growth. 'I think Alibaba clearly is well positioned to chase that growth, given that they have a cloud computing arm, they have a very strong AI model,' he said, adding that profits might weaken in the near term while capex might rise.

Chinese tech peers have also been ramping up AI spending. Tencent's capital expenditure rose 65% from the previous quarter to 52.8 billion yuan in the June quarter as the company continued to invest in computing infrastructure to monetize its AI models.
