Stock Futures Slip as Tech Shares Fall Ahead of Nvidia Earnings and Jackson Hole Symposium

Financial & Markets desk · anchored on AP, Reuters

Stock futures slipped Monday, weighed by declines in technology shares, as markets entered a critical week featuring Nvidia's earnings report, the Federal Reserve's Jackson Hole symposium, and key inflation data. Dow Jones Industrial Average futures traded 11 points lower, while S&P 500 and Nasdaq-100 futures lost 0.1% and 0.5%, respectively. The State Street Technology Select Sector ETF (XLK) fell nearly 1% in premarket trading, with chip-related stocks Coherent and Lumentum dropping 5% and 4.5%, respectively, and Sandisk shedding 4%.

The declines follow a losing week for major U.S. indexes. The S&P 500 fell 1.4% last week, the Nasdaq dropped 2%, and the Dow lost 0.9%, with the Dow posting its second consecutive weekly pullback and the S&P 500 and Nasdaq snapping three-week winning streaks. Global bond yields remained elevated, with the 30-year U.S. Treasury bond yield topping 5.3% last week, reaching levels not seen in nearly 20 years. Rates in Japan, France, and Germany also scaled multiyear highs, pressuring equities.

Investors are focused on Nvidia's second-quarter earnings, due Wednesday, which will test the resurgent AI trade. Nvidia is expected to report adjusted earnings per share of $2.09 on revenue of $92 billion, according to Bloomberg consensus estimates, marking a 96% year-over-year revenue jump. Data Center revenue is anticipated to top $85.4 billion, up 107%. However, Nvidia shares have fallen in response to earnings in six of the past eight quarters, including the last four, as the market prices in high expectations. UBS analyst Tim Arcuri said in a note that he expects investors to gain greater confidence in a path to $15-plus EPS in 2027E and $20 in 2028E, numbers that should keep the stock grinding higher. HSBC analyst Frank Lee wrote that the next major re-rating for Nvidia will be driven by a new narrative, such as the company positioning itself as the world's largest contributor to open-source AI.

The economic calendar is also busy. The July personal consumption expenditures (PCE) price index, the Fed's preferred inflation gauge, will be released Wednesday. The Federal Reserve's annual symposium in Jackson Hole, Wyoming, begins this week, where Chairman Kevin Warsh is expected to deliver a speech. The Conference Board's consumer confidence survey is due Tuesday, and the University of Michigan's consumer surveys on inflation expectations will round out the week. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent announced measures last week to help stabilize the long end of the U.S. yield curve, but the reprieve was short-lived. David Zervos, chief market strategist at Jefferies, described the Treasury's decision to upsize tactical long-end buybacks as a Treasury-led 'Operation Twist' designed to counter shifts in shorter-term market conditions, rather than a form of quantitative easing.

In Asia, Japan's Nikkei 225 closed 0.74% lower, South Korea's Kospi dropped 3.12%, and mainland China's CSI 300 closed 1.21% lower. Australia's S&P/ASX 200 rose 0.49%. Oil prices dropped ahead of a press conference by Bessent on Iran, as investors weighed the potential for a prolonged U.S.-Iran conflict that could keep oil prices elevated and drive inflation higher. Retail earnings continue this week, with reports from DICK'S Sporting Goods, Five Below, Urban Outfitters, Bath & Body Works, Ulta Beauty, Dollar General, and Dollar Tree offering further insight into the American consumer.
