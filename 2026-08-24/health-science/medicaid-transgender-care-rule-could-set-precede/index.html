Medicaid transgender care rule could set precedent restricting other drugs

Health & Science desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

WASHINGTON — The Trump administration plans to use a new approach to deny Medicaid coverage of drugs for gender-affirming care, a move that Medicaid experts say could set a precedent for restricting coverage of other medications as well. The Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services issued a final rule this month barring the use of federal Medicaid and CHIP funding to pay for pediatric gender-affirming medications and surgery. The rule is set to go into effect Oct. 13.

The rule attracted attention as the latest development in the administration’s campaign to halt transgender health care for young people. But experts warn that the precedent it establishes could be used to avoid paying for other drugs, upending the general expectation that Medicaid covers the vast majority of prescription medication.

Under current law, Medicaid must cover most outpatient drugs approved by the Food and Drug Administration for medically accepted indications, as long as drug companies agree to pay significant rebates to Medicaid to be part of the program. The new rule introduces a mechanism that could allow the administration to carve out exceptions beyond gender-affirming care.

The implications extend beyond transgender health, potentially affecting coverage for a wide range of drugs. The rule signals a shift in how the administration interprets Medicaid’s obligations, and experts say it could be used to deny coverage for other treatments in the future.
