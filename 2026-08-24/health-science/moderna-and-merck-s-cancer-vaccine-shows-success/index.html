Moderna and Merck's cancer vaccine shows success in trial

Health & Science desk

Moderna and Merck announced last Wednesday that a Phase 2 trial of their experimental cancer vaccine, intismeran autogene, reduced the odds that surgically removed melanoma would return or spread. The 1,137-volunteer study marks a significant milestone for the mRNA-based treatment, which has long been met with skepticism, even from the companies’ own executives.

The candidate, which uses Moderna’s core mRNA technology, is designed to target melanoma that has already been removed by surgery. Top-line results from large pharmaceutical companies are rarely reversed when full data emerge, but the vaccine’s success had been doubted by investors—who were betting against Moderna’s stock before the announcement—and by longtime skeptics of mRNA technology. Some observers still say researchers should wait for fuller data before drawing firm conclusions.

Moderna CEO Stéphane Bancel and President Stephen Hoge both expressed initial disbelief in the treatment’s performance. At a March 2023 off-site leadership meeting, after mid-stage results showed promise, Bancel asked the roughly 100 executives present whether any of them had expected the experimental treatment to succeed. Hoge, then standing on stage, sat down. Bancel sat down as well. ‘None of us believed it,’ Hoge recalled in an interview. He said it took time before he felt, ‘Oh my gosh. This is real.’

The vaccine’s progress challenges the narrative that Moderna—best known for its COVID-19 shot—cannot extend its mRNA platform to other diseases. If full data confirm the early findings, the treatment could become a new option for melanoma patients and a proof of concept for personalized cancer vaccines. The partnership with Merck, a major pharmaceutical firm, adds weight to the results’ credibility, though the firms have not yet set a timeline for regulatory submissions or further studies.
