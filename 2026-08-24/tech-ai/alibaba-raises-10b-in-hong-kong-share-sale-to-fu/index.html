Alibaba raises $10.2 billion in Hong Kong share sale to fund AI investments

Tech / AI desk

Alibaba Group on Monday completed a HK$80 billion ($10.2 billion) share placement, marking its first primary share issuance since its 2019 secondary listing in Hong Kong, as the company ramps up spending on artificial intelligence. The offering of 710 million newly issued shares, representing about 3.7% of Alibaba’s total outstanding shares, was met with fierce investor demand, with the order book reaching $28 billion — nearly three times the $10 billion target, according to a person familiar with the matter.

The placement price was set at an 8.4% discount to the stock’s closing price in Hong Kong on Friday and a 3.6% discount to the Friday closing price of its New York-listed shares, the company said in a stock exchange filing. Alibaba shares in Hong Kong plunged on Monday morning following the announcement, reflecting the dilutive effect of the new shares and the discount pricing.

Alibaba is doubling down on artificial intelligence at a time when the race to build frontier models and the computing infrastructure behind them is intensifying across both China and the West. The proceeds from the share sale are specifically earmarked to fund the company’s AI investments, according to the company. The deal is one of the largest AI-related fundraising efforts by a Chinese company.

The placement drew strong interest from institutional investors, with sources confirming the order book was quickly filled after launch. The company’s move to raise capital through a primary share issuance rather than debt or asset sales signals its strategic commitment to competing in the AI arms race, a sector that has seen massive capital expenditure from tech giants globally.

Alibaba’s Hong Kong-listed shares had closed at HK$138.50 on Friday before the placement was announced. The 8.4% discount on the offer price and the subsequent sell-off on Monday highlight the market’s immediate reaction to the dilution. The company, which owns the South China Morning Post, did not disclose further details on specific AI projects to be funded.

Sources: South China Morning Post·Nikkei Asia
