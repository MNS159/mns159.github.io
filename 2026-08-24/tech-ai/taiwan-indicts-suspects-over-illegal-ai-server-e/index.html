Taiwan indicts suspects over illegal AI server exports to China

Tech / AI desk · anchored on Reuters

Taiwanese prosecutors have indicted three technology executives for allegedly smuggling advanced Nvidia artificial-intelligence servers to China in violation of U.S. export restrictions, according to a statement from the Keelung District Prosecutors Office on Wednesday.

The defendants — an Albatron Technology Co. vice president surnamed Lu, and two Super Micro Computer Inc. Taiwan branch managers surnamed Wang and Lin — are accused of conspiring to purchase high-performance AI servers from Super Micro, based in San Jose, California, and then using falsified export declarations to smuggle them to China, Hong Kong and Macau. The shipments were valued at approximately NT$700 million (US$21.99 million). The Keelung District Court approved the prosecutors' request to detain the three.

The indictments follow a broader investigation that began in May, when prosecutors raided residences, warehouses and workplaces of three other individuals — surnamed You, Wang and Chen — and discovered 50 AI servers containing Nvidia's high-end GB300 chips, along with NT$9 million in cash. Those three have been detained since May. In Monday's raids, prosecutors also questioned a Chief Telecom manager surnamed Wang and an employee surnamed Chu, who were released on bail of NT$100,000 and NT$50,000 respectively. Chief Telecom said in a statement it would fully comply with the investigation and reserved the right to sue media for false reporting.

Prosecutors suspect that the defendants exported the servers to Japan using forged documents before rerouting them to Hong Kong, where they could be sent onward to China. At least one shipment is believed to have already cleared Taiwan customs, according to sources familiar with the matter. The suspects were aware of U.S. export restrictions that bar the sale of advanced AI chips to China, Macau and Hong Kong without a license.

The case marks Taiwan's first public crackdown on AI chip diversion after years of U.S. pressure to take a more active role in curbing China's access to sensitive technology. In March, the U.S. Department of Justice unsealed an indictment charging Super Micro cofounder Wally Liaw Yih-Shyan, Super Micro Taiwan sales manager Chang Ruei-tsang, and contractor Sun Ting-wei with attempting to divert more than US$2.5 billion of Nvidia AI tech to China. Chris McGuire, an expert on China and AI at the Council on Foreign Relations, described chip smuggling as a "really significant problem" in Taiwan and Southeast Asia, adding that it is "really, really important that allies align with the United States" on export controls and legal enforcement.
