US-Canada trade talks collapse as 50% tariffs take effect

International & World desk

Trade negotiations between the United States and Canada collapsed on Friday in Washington, plunging the two countries into a deepening trade war. The US imposed 50% tariffs on $20 billion (£14.6 billion) worth of Canadian goods, which came into force on Saturday, covering products from hockey sticks to tongue depressors. Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney has vowed to match the US tariffs "dollar for dollar," with new levies on steel, dairy, appliances and electronics expected to take effect from 8 September.

UK PM Burnham arrives in Ukraine with plan for long-range missile production

International & World desk · anchored on AP, Reuters

UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham arrived in Kyiv on Monday for his first foreign trip since taking office, marking Ukraine’s 35th Independence Day and announcing a plan to help Ukraine produce its own long-range cruise missiles. Burnham was photographed at Kyiv railway station alongside Ukrainian Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha, who confirmed that European Council President António Costa, Luxembourg Prime Minister Luc Frieden, Moldovan President Maia Sandu, and Estonian President Alar Karis were also visiting. The trip comes as European leaders gather for a meeting of the Coalition of the Willing, which Burnham will co-chair for the first time alongside French President Emmanuel Macron and German Chancellor Friedrich Merz.

Zelensky rejects wartime elections as ex-defense minister questions corruption awareness

International & World desk

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky has rejected calls for wartime elections, saying they would be a “tsunami” that would “split Ukraine,” as his former defense minister publicly questioned the president’s awareness of corruption in his inner circle. In remarks released Sunday, Zelensky said holding a vote under martial law, which has suspended elections since Russia’s full-scale invasion in 2022, would be “a huge risk” and that “if we want to destroy the country, then we can move in the direction of elections during these times of war.” The president’s five-year term ended in May 2024.

Ukraine drone strikes on Russian refineries trigger fuel crisis across Central Asia

International & World desk

Ukrainian drone attacks on Russian oil refineries and fuel depots have triggered a severe fuel shortage that is spreading panic across Central Asia, forcing tens of millions of Russians to endure hours-long queues at petrol stations and driving cross-border "fuel tourism" into Kazakhstan. The strikes, which have hit facilities from annexed Crimea to western Siberia since early this year, have disabled key refineries including Russia's largest plant in Omsk, which stopped operating after a Ukrainian drone attack in early July damaged a crude distillation unit. At least 30 Russian regions are now affected, according to Reuters, with the crisis particularly hitting Moscow and its surrounding area, where chains such as Gazprom Neft and Tatneft have restricted fuel sales.
