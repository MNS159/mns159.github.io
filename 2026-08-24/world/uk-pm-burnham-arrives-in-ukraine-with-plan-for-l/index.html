UK PM Burnham arrives in Ukraine with plan for long-range missile production

International & World desk · anchored on AP, Reuters

UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham arrived in Kyiv on Monday for his first foreign trip since taking office, marking Ukraine’s 35th Independence Day and announcing a plan to help Ukraine produce its own long-range cruise missiles. Burnham was photographed at Kyiv railway station alongside Ukrainian Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha, who confirmed that European Council President António Costa, Luxembourg Prime Minister Luc Frieden, Moldovan President Maia Sandu, and Estonian President Alar Karis were also visiting. The trip comes as European leaders gather for a meeting of the Coalition of the Willing, which Burnham will co-chair for the first time alongside French President Emmanuel Macron and German Chancellor Friedrich Merz.

In a speech in front of St Sophia Cathedral, Burnham compared Ukraine’s resistance against Russia to Britain’s fight against Nazi Germany, saying: “My nation kept the flame of European freedom alive in the 20th century, you hold that flame in the 21st.” He vowed continued support “despite Russia’s outrageous threats to my country,” and praised “the sheer strength” of the Ukrainian people, insisting “this is the moment to keep up the pressure on Russia, the moment to squeeze their ability to fund this illegal war.” The prime minister’s office said Burnham would tell Ukrainians that the UK “is four-square behind Ukraine’s independence, and Russia’s threats only strengthen the UK’s resolve.”

The centerpiece of Burnham’s visit is a plan to allow the European defence company MBDA to declassify information about UK-made components of the Anglo-French Storm Shadow/Scalp long-range missiles, enabling Ukraine to build its own version. The UK will also help France set up assembly lines in Ukraine for long-range missiles, a promise French President Macron made last month. In a statement released Sunday night, Burnham said: “On Ukraine’s 35th anniversary of independence, the Ukrainian people should be in no doubt: the UK is behind you today and for as long as it takes. … Russia should be in no doubt of our resolve. We will not back down until there is a just and lasting peace.”

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy, in a speech marking Independence Day, said that 35 years ago many intelligence agencies doubted Ukraine’s independence would last, but “Ukrainians restored the independence of their state, and we will definitely protect the independence of Ukraine.” He added: “Ukrainians will not be cobblestones under the feet of tsars and rulers. Ukrainians will not submit. Ukrainians will not be bargaining chips in the disputes of capitals.” Zelenskyy acknowledged that “things are really very difficult for all of us,” but said unity “is holding us firmly by the hand.” He also renewed his call for more help from European leaders, telling them “we are not asking you to fight for us, [but] to give Ukraine everything it needs to stop this war.”

The Kremlin responded sharply to Burnham’s plans. Spokesperson Dmitry Peskov said Britain was “methodically and regularly throwing oil on the fire and methodically and regularly plotting schemes to prevent even the slightest progress in the peace process.” He added that Russia would target and “destroy” facilities producing the missiles in Ukraine. Separately, the European Union announced it had approved €6.1 billion ($7.1 billion) in military aid for Ukraine. European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen posted on X: “On Ukraine’s Independence Day, we show our unfaltering support. … Europe stands with you today, tomorrow, and for as long as it takes.”
