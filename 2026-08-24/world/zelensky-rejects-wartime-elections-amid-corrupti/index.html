Zelensky rejects wartime elections as ex-defense minister questions corruption awareness

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts slightly center-left

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky has rejected calls for wartime elections, saying they would be a “tsunami” that would “split Ukraine,” as his former defense minister publicly questioned the president’s awareness of corruption in his inner circle. In remarks released Sunday, Zelensky said holding a vote under martial law, which has suspended elections since Russia’s full-scale invasion in 2022, would be “a huge risk” and that “if we want to destroy the country, then we can move in the direction of elections during these times of war.” The president’s five-year term ended in May 2024.

The call for elections came from Mykhailo Fedorov, who was fired as defense minister last month after reportedly falling out with Ukraine’s top military commander. In a video statement and subsequent interview with the BBC, Fedorov argued that “democracy should not be the hostage of Putin” and that “people cannot endlessly live in a state where they do not understand why certain decisions are made.” He said it was his “moral right” to start the conversation about elections, adding: “If I cannot share my ideas and be open about them with my own society, then what are we fighting for?”

Fedorov also pressed Zelensky to answer questions about a money-laundering investigation announced last week. Ukraine’s anti-corruption bureau said it had uncovered a scheme to misappropriate more than $3 million in Ukrainian currency to pay bail for a former energy minister accused of money laundering. A senior official in Zelensky’s office alleged to be involved was sacked. Fedorov said he had no evidence the president was personally corrupt, telling the BBC: “In all the time that I worked with him, not a single time have I received from him any task or assignment that would go against law or my sense of integrity.” But he added: “Let him answer this question … I believe that he’s ready for such questions. It’s his responsibility to tell us all whether he was aware of all that or not.”

A spokesperson for Zelensky responded by noting that Fedorov “was part of every government since 2019” and that “it’s strange that he phrases it as though these were somehow different governments without him. As everyone remembers, we didn’t hear any strong statements from him back then.” Zelensky himself was critical of Fedorov, saying: “I believe he is going himself — or is being pushed — toward wrong things.” Experts have cited numerous obstacles to holding a wartime vote, including how soldiers, millions of Ukrainian refugees, and people in occupied territories would cast ballots, as well as disinformation threats from Russia. A recent poll showed Zelensky would still be more popular as a presidential candidate.

Fedorov, 35, is credited with modernizing Ukraine’s military and pioneering drone warfare as defense minister and previously as digital transformation minister. He said he had no immediate political ambitions but did not rule out a future run for president, saying it would depend on “how soon we’ll be able to relaunch this democratic election process … how much energy I would have, whether I will preserve my team … and whether people will tell me, yeah, we can trust you handling that position.” He also confirmed a visit to the United States in the coming weeks, where he hopes to meet Elon Musk to discuss extending Starlink satellite communications into Russian airspace to assist Ukrainian strikes.

Separately, Zelensky said Kyiv believes Russia is planning to mobilize 300,000 more troops after its parliamentary elections in September, and possibly hundreds of thousands more in 2027, as it seeks to capture the rest of Ukraine’s eastern Donetsk region. He also announced an agreement with Germany for the supply of 600 Patriot PAC-2 missile interceptors for 2027–28. France has promised additional interceptor missiles, and Norway said it plans to provide another 85 billion Norwegian crowns ($9.2 billion; €7.8 billion) in financial support to Kyiv in the 2027 fiscal year.
