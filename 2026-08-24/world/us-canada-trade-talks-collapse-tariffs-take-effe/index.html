US-Canada trade talks collapse as 50% tariffs take effect

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts left

Trade negotiations between the United States and Canada collapsed on Friday in Washington, plunging the two countries into a deepening trade war. The US imposed 50% tariffs on $20 billion (£14.6 billion) worth of Canadian goods, which came into force on Saturday, covering products from hockey sticks to tongue depressors. Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney has vowed to match the US tariffs "dollar for dollar," with new levies on steel, dairy, appliances and electronics expected to take effect from 8 September.

President Donald Trump lashed out on social media, writing: "Canada wants the benefits of being a State, without being one!!!" He also said Canada had "charged our great farmers, for many years, massive amounts of tariffs" and declared, "No more!!!" Carney told Canadian journalists that the country was "at war" over trade with the US after Trump "miscalculated" by escalating his tariff attack. "We cannot accept what they've offered and we will not give what they've asked," Carney said, adding that he rejected the deal due to last-minute US demands that would have undermined Canada's sovereignty.

Both sides blamed each other for the breakdown. US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer said the US was compelled to act after a year of retaliatory measures by Canada, telling Fox & Friends Weekend: "We've said enough and so we've taken countermeasures. Our interest is in protecting American workers and protecting American supply chains." Dan Kelly, president of the Canadian Federation of Independent Business, estimated that 40% of small Canadian exporters will be directly hit by the US tariffs, and that nearly one-third expect their revenues will drop by 50% or more.

The collapse has called into question the future of the US-Mexico-Canada Agreement (USMCA), the North American trade pact governing about $2 trillion annually in goods and services. Trump refused to renew the USMCA this summer after Canada and Mexico formally requested a 16-year extension. When asked how the breakdown would affect the pact, Carney said it was "certainly not good news." The escalating trade war has also damaged broader US-Canadian relations, with a 21% drop in Canadians entering New York state in 2025 and 3 million fewer visits compared with the previous year.

The breakdown came as a shock after Trump had claimed on Tuesday that a three-day extension would be applied to the tariff deadline and that a "very fair deal for both" sides had been ironed out. However, details of the agreement leaked to Canadian media sparked alarm that Carney and his negotiators were conceding too much. Manitoba Premier Wab Kinew encouraged Canadians to avoid buying US alcohol, saying "you can't get a good deal with a bad person." Sources told the Canadian Broadcasting Corporation that Canada's former chief trade negotiator Steve Verheul had warned in a briefing call that concessions would only lead to further demands from the Americans.

Democratic lawmakers and governors from US states bordering Canada condemned the tariffs. New York Governor Kathy Hochul posted on X: "Needlessly picking fights with our allies and raising prices here at home. That's Trump's economic policy in a nutshell." Minnesota Senator Amy Klobuchar said the tariffs meant "higher costs for small businesses, farmers, and all Minnesota families," adding: "Minnesotans are paying for Trump's chaos." A petition to expel US Ambassador to Canada Pete Hoekstra has gathered about 248,000 signatures since late July, and a Globe and Mail poll found that only 9% of Canadians agreed the US was a "trustworthy ally," with 51% having cancelled American trips in reaction to Trump's comments.
