Politics Foreign Policy / Iran

U.S. launches 'economic D-Day' against Iran, threatens secondary sanctions on China

Politics desk

U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent wrote in the Financial Times that the U.S. would launch what he called "the single greatest financial offensive ever," describing it as an "economic D-Day" meant to "sever every economic lifeline that sustains the tyrannical regime until Tehran stands alone."

The announcement marks an escalation in the Trump administration's economic pressure campaign against Iran, layered on top of an ongoing military conflict with Israel and years of sanctions dating back to the U.S. exit from the 2015 nuclear deal. Because Iran's oil exports flow overwhelmingly to China, any serious enforcement of 'secondary sanctions' — penalties on foreign entities that do business with Iran — would directly implicate Chinese banks, refiners, and shipping firms, raising the stakes beyond the Iran-Israel conflict into U.S.-China economic relations. The BBC's coverage notes that the administration has previously issued sweeping deadlines and threats (such as Trump's April warning about 'a whole civilisation') only to walk them back after diplomatic intervention, which is relevant context for assessing how the current threat may play out. Iran's threat to close oil exports and restrict passage through the Strait of Hormuz — a chokepoint for roughly one-fifth of global oil and gas — underscores that any escalation carries risk of global economic spillover, not just bilateral pressure on Tehran.

Health & Science desk

UK PM Burnham arrives in Ukraine with plan for long-range missile production

UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham arrived in Kyiv on Monday for his first foreign trip since taking office, marking Ukraine’s 35th Independence Day and announcing a plan to help Ukraine produce its own long-range cruise missiles. Burnham was photographed at Kyiv railway station alongside Ukrainian Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha, who confirmed that European Council President António Costa, Luxembourg Prime Minister Luc Frieden, Moldovan President Maia Sandu, and Estonian President Alar Karis were also visiting. The trip comes as European leaders gather for a meeting of the Coalition of the Willing, which Burnham will co-chair for the first time alongside French President Emmanuel Macron and German Chancellor Friedrich Merz.
