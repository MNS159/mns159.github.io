Watkins absent as Villa thrashed 4-0 by Brighton; transfer speculation intensifies

Sports desk

Brighton & Hove Albion began their Premier League season with a dominant 4-0 victory over Aston Villa at the Amex Stadium, a result that was overshadowed by the absence of Villa striker Ollie Watkins from the matchday squad. The 30-year-old England international was left out as speculation over a move to Saudi Pro League club Al-Hilal continues to grow. Villa manager Unai Emery, who had earlier in the week confirmed offers had been received for Watkins, declined to explain the decision, telling Match of the Day: "This question is for him. You can call him and try to get his answer. The players here wanted to play and we had two strikers with a small injury. The question on Watkins is for him."

Brighton scored four first-half goals to put the result beyond doubt, with Villa finishing the match with 10 men after midfielder Joao Gomes was sent off on his Premier League debut. The defeat compounded a difficult start for Villa, who are undergoing a significant squad overhaul. Watkins could become the latest of Villa's European heroes to depart this summer, following the exits of Ezri Konsa (to Arsenal), Youri Tielemans, Morgan Rogers (whose £117m move to Chelsea set a record for an English player), Lucas Digne (to Paris St-Germain), and goalkeeper Emi Martinez, who was also absent on Sunday after a failed move to Juventus. Villa have signed a replacement for Martinez in Zion Suzuki.

Villa captain John McGinn acknowledged the difficulty of the situation, telling Sky Sports: "It's a difficult one. He's been an amazing player and a record goalscorer - my job as captain is to get the best out of all the players that are here today. It's not easy coming here. I'm proud of the players that were here today and fought for the club." McGinn added that the club still has "a structure in place, standards and rules which have made us a success" and that he is "really excited about the new Aston Villa."

Villa's heavy squad turnover is driven by financial necessity. The club must comply with Uefa's squad cost ratio (SCR), which limits spending on wages, transfers, and agent fees to 70% of football-related revenue, as well as the Premier League's Profit and Sustainability Rules (PSR). Villa have been fined twice by Uefa for breaches, including a £19.4m sanction in June. A former Villa executive told BBC Sport: "It's necessity because of the rules. The PSR, the SCR, issues are well documented. It does seem slightly unfair they're having their ambition capped at this level. But it has forced this very big churn of players."

This summer, Villa have raised around £212m through player sales, including Jhon Duran (£71m), Douglas Luiz (£42.5m), Jacob Ramsey (£40m), and Moussa Diaby (£50m) over the past two years. The club's latest accounts showed a post-tax profit of £17m for the year ending 30 June 2025, following losses of £119.6m and £85.4m in the previous two years. Villa's run to the Champions League quarter-finals in 2024-25 and their Europa League victory in May (which guaranteed around £45.6m, including Champions League qualification) helped them turn a profit. Despite the departures, Emery remains the club's biggest asset, with one former decision-maker saying: "There's a lot of hope and ambition resting with the manager. He's the ace card they've got left."

As Villa prepare for life without Watkins, reports have linked them with Manchester United striker Joshua Zirkzee. The 25-year-old Netherlands international has struggled in the Premier League, scoring five goals in 56 league appearances over two seasons, but notched 12 goals in 37 matches in his final season at Bologna. Zirkzee is also reportedly a target for Juventus and Napoli. Emery, who has consistently improved Villa since taking over when they were 14th, now faces the challenge of integrating a number of new signings, including goalkeeper Suzuki, defender Matteo Ruggeri, and striker Brian Madjo (who scored on his debut against Paris St-Germain earlier this month), while also addressing the potential departure of Watkins.
