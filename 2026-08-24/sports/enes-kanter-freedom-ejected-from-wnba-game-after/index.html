Enes Kanter Freedom ejected from WNBA game after verbal confrontation with Natasha Cloud

Sports desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

CHICAGO — Former NBA player Enes Kanter Freedom, wearing a shirt that read “WOMAN noun adult human female,” was ejected from his courtside seat at Wintrust Arena on Sunday after a verbal altercation with Chicago Sky guard Natasha Cloud during the Indiana Fever’s 113-90 victory. The incident occurred late in the third quarter when Cloud scored a driving layup and then pointed and shouted at Kanter Freedom, who stood up and walked toward the court. Security personnel intervened, and an official gestured for Kanter Freedom to be ejected; he was escorted out to a chorus of boos.

Kanter Freedom, 34, had spent the past few weeks agitating to be eligible for the 2027 WNBA Draft, claiming he now identifies as a woman, as part of his vocal opposition to transgender athletes competing in women’s sports. He has no connection to either team. A fan seated next to him, Tricia Smith, said Cloud instigated the confrontation after her bucket. “So he stood up and said, like, ‘What? What do you want?’ He didn’t do a thing,” Smith said. After his removal, Kanter Freedom told arena officials, “I’m supporting women, and that is the reason they’re kicking me out. It’s unacceptable.”

Cloud did not speak to reporters after the game but met with team and league security. She later posted on social media: “To every trans kid/person, You are apart of MY community & tribe. You belong. You're loved. You're seen. You're perfect as you are. & you will always be protected by me.” Sky coach Tyler Marsh said he told Cloud she was not a distraction. “I told her we all embrace her as someone who protects our team and people who need to be protected,” Marsh said. “There's so many good things about this league … if you come to a game, you should probably come to enjoy the basketball.”

Indiana Fever coach Stephanie White and players Caitlin Clark and Lexie Hull said they did not witness the exchange; Clark recorded 37 points and 10 assists. White urged the media not to “give it any gas,” calling the incident “a social media thing.” The two had clashed previously on social media in 2023 after Cloud criticized the U.S. government and Kanter Freedom replied by comparing the U.S. favorably to authoritarian countries.

The confrontation comes amid broader debate about transgender participation in women’s sports, fueled by Fever guard Sophie Cunningham’s July comments in an ESPN profile that she wants to protect young girls from competing against “biological men.” Cunningham later doubled down, saying, “I stand on what I said.” The WNBA has never had a transgender player and has no public policy on transgender participation; its collective bargaining agreement states “only players who are women are eligible to play.” The league recently formed a task force to discuss the issue, denouncing “bad-faith efforts to use these topics to demean or marginalize others.” Rallies both supporting and opposing transgender athletes in women’s sports have appeared outside arenas, including before Sunday’s game in Chicago.
