Guardiola backs City, key questions answered after guilty verdict

Sports desk

Former Manchester City manager Pep Guardiola has publicly backed the club's owners and leadership after an independent commission found City guilty of all charges related to breaches of the Premier League's financial rules. The commission determined that City arranged "sham contracts" with commercial partners to disguise secret funding of more than £830 million between the 2009-10 and 2017-18 seasons. The club also faces all but one charge for failing to cooperate with the investigation. City continues to deny wrongdoing and has until 2 October to submit an appeal.

"I want every single Manchester City fan across the world to know I am here; more than ever," Guardiola wrote in a social media post. "I am, always have been, always will, be behind my club, my owner, my chairman, Ferran [Soriano], the players, staff, [manager] Enzo [Maresca] and you, our unique supporters. Let me be clear: we will get through this together." Guardiola, who led City to six Premier League titles and a Champions League crown during his decade in charge, had said in November 2024 that he would stay even if the club were relegated.

City chairman Khaldoon Al Mubarak said the club remains confident of proving its innocence, while chief executive Ferran Soriano stated in a video message that "irrefutable evidence" had been "ignored" by the commission. The club, owned by the Abu Dhabi United Group, said it was "both disappointed and surprised" by the verdict. Former City defender Micah Richards said he felt "absolutely devastated" after reading the "damning" findings, adding that a lifelong fan who supported the club for 60 years called him in tears. Former Liverpool defender Jamie Carragher said City's hierarchy are "just gaslighting" and argued that titles should be stripped from the club's record.

The appeal process will be heard by a new three-person commission. Premier League regulations state an appeal should conclude no more than 12 weeks after filing, with a decision within 30 days of its end. City cannot appeal to the Court of Arbitration for Sport but could challenge the process at the High Court. Possible sanctions include a points deduction leading to relegation or expulsion from the Premier League, which would require a vote by 15 rival clubs. If expelled, City would need to apply to the Football Association for re-entry, and the EFL's 72 clubs would vote on whether to accept them, potentially sending the club as low as League Two.

The independent commission's findings have also raised questions about potential criminal investigation. The Fraud Act 2006 allows for up to 10 years' imprisonment for dishonestly making false representations. Former City chairman David Bernstein said on BBC Radio 5 Live that the matter might need to be referred to authorities outside the league. However, football finance expert Kieran Maguire said wider investigation may be unlikely, noting that "there is certainly no corporation tax to recover" because City would have lost more money without the activities it undertook. BBC Sport has contacted the National Crime Agency, HMRC, and the Serious Fraud Office, but none have indicated an investigation has begun.
