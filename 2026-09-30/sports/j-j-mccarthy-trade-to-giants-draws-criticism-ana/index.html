Giants acquire J.J. McCarthy from Vikings for fifth-round pick, drawing sharp criticism and analysis

Sports desk

The New York Giants acquired quarterback J.J. McCarthy from the Minnesota Vikings on Tuesday in exchange for a fifth-round pick, a move made after rookie starter Jaxson Dart suffered a season-ending knee injury in Week 2 against the Los Angeles Rams. McCarthy, the 10th overall pick in the 2024 NFL Draft, joins a quarterback room that currently lists veteran Jameis Winston as the starter, with Dart expected to return.

According to multiple reports, the Giants began exploring quarterback options last Wednesday after additional medical opinions confirmed Dart would undergo surgery and miss the remainder of the regular season. General manager Joe Schoen and the front office ultimately targeted McCarthy as their top option. Giants head coach John Harbaugh was consulted on the trade, and he reached out to his brother, Chargers head coach Jim Harbaugh, who coached McCarthy to a national championship at Michigan, for an evaluation before the deal was finalized.

The trade drew immediate criticism from ESPN analysts. Former NFL wide receiver Steve Smith Sr. said of McCarthy: "He's not very good. So, uh, doesn't really say much for me. Fifth-round draft pick; he's a top-10 pick. Interesting, unique, and, uh, good luck to him." ESPN's Dan Graziano called the move "a terrible idea for the Giants," arguing that bringing in a quarterback the same age as Dart who was drafted higher and will earn nearly $4 million next season could cause more problems than it fixes.

The Giants have made clear that the acquisition does not alter their plans for Dart, who was the 25th overall pick in 2025 and had seized the starting job in Week 4 of his rookie season. In his 10 starts last season, McCarthy went 6-4 while averaging 163.2 passing yards per game with 11 touchdowns and 12 interceptions, adding 181 rushing yards and four scores on the ground. Dart, in 11 starts, went 4-7 but posted a more efficient 15-to-5 touchdown-to-interception ratio and rushed for 487 yards and nine touchdowns. Incumbent backup Jameis Winston will retain his role as QB2, with McCarthy initially behind him on the depth chart as he learns the offense.

The Vikings had no bad blood in the trade; McCarthy never requested a move despite being demoted to QB3 behind Kyler Murray and Carson Wentz. Minnesota's new general manager inherited McCarthy and was less committed to his development than the previous regime. The Giants, meanwhile, waived quarterback Jake Haener—who had been elevated from the practice squad two days earlier—and are not expected to add another arm, according to reports. New York holds contractual control over McCarthy for the next two years, including a salary of nearly $4 million next season.

The Giants host the Arizona Cardinals on Sunday, with Winston under center. The organization has stated that Dart remains the long-term starter when healthy, leaving McCarthy's role uncertain. He could eventually see playing time if Winston struggles, or the Giants could flip him for draft assets later. As one commentator noted, McCarthy's youth and injury history make him a project, but the team is betting that his familiarity with the Harbaugh system and two years of team control provide upside.
