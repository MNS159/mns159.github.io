MLB Wild Card Series Day 1: Padres, Yankees, White Sox, Braves take 1-0 leads

Sports desk

The 2026 MLB postseason opened Tuesday with the first games of the best-of-three Wild Card Series, and four teams moved to within one win of advancing to the Division Series. The San Diego Padres, New York Yankees, Chicago White Sox and Atlanta Braves each took 1-0 leads, while the Chicago Cubs, Boston Red Sox, Houston Astros and Philadelphia Phillies face elimination in Wednesday's Game 2s.

The Yankees blanked the Red Sox 9-0 behind a dominant start from Cam Schlittler, who struck out 10 and induced 24 whiffs over 6⅓ innings. Schlittler, known for three variations of his fastball, added a devastating curveball that generated eight swings and misses on its own. The Red Sox also lost young outfielder Roman Anthony in the sixth inning when the same hand and finger injury that cost him 101 games this season flared up on a check swing. Manager Chad Tracy said, "The hand … he took a check swing and then another swing and his hand flared on him. And it got uncomfortable, so we had to make a move there." Boston now faces Yankees starter Max Fried, one of the most effective active pitchers, with a rested bullpen that did not need to use David Bednar, Brent Headrick or Carlos Rodón in Game 1.

The Braves took a 1-0 lead over the Phillies in a game that turned on Austin Riley's go-ahead three-run homer in the eighth inning. Philadelphia had been four outs from stealing a Chris Sale start and used closer Jhoan Duran in the seventh, but Duran threw 27 pitches and may have some fatigue for Game 2. The Phillies will counter with Cy Young candidate Cristopher Sánchez, while Atlanta starts deadline pickup Tyler Mahle, who improved with increased splitter usage but is not the caliber of Sale. The Phillies have combined for 13 runs in their past six games, including a seven-run outburst in Game 162, and face an uphill climb.

The Padres shellacked the Cubs behind Michael King's ace-grade effort, which kept San Diego from needing to dip deeply into its bullpen. The Cubs led the majors in OPS (offensive production score) during the regular season and outscored opponents by 147 runs, the third-best run differential in MLB, while the Padres outscored opponents by 41 runs. Chicago also ranked second in OPS on the road and third against right-handed pitchers, which Game 2 starter Nick Pivetta is. The Cubs' offense, dormant in Game 1, will need to wake up to force a deciding Game 3.

The White Sox took a 1-0 lead over the Astros, who finished the regular season 81-81 and were outscored by 32 runs. Houston will send ace Hunter Brown to the mound in Game 2, while White Sox high-leverage reliever Grant Taylor is unlikely to be available after throwing a season-high 54 pitches in Game 1. The Astros have home-field advantage at Daikin Park, but as the worst of the four teams down 1-0, they need two straight wins to save their season.

Since the Wild Card Series format was adopted in 2022, only once in 16 tries has the Game 1 loser come back to win the series — last year's Yankees over the Red Sox, thanks largely to Schlittler's Game 3 performance. On only four other occasions did the Game 1 loser force a Game 3. The Dodgers, who earned a bye into the Division Series, used the extra time to allow banged-up players to recover. Freddie Freeman, who slashed .184/.259/.224 with one extra-base hit in September, said his knee is in a good spot now and that the five-day break has been huge for working on his swing mechanics. "I'll never blame anything physical-wise of why I didn't play well," Freeman said. "I just didn't play well the last couple months of the season."
