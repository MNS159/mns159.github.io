US ends military mission in Iraq, withdraws all forces

Politics desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts slightly center-right
✓ Facts
  • U.S. Central Command (Centcom) announced Wednesday that all U.S. forces and equipment departed Erbil Air Base in northern Iraq, marking the end of the U.S. military's 12-year mission in Iraq as part of a yearlong wind-down of Operation Inherent Resolve.
  • Centcom said the move is part of a yearlong wind-down transitioning toward a "U.S.-Iraq bilateral defense relationship."
  • The U.S. military presence in Iraq began with the 2003 invasion that toppled Saddam Hussein and evolved into the anti-ISIS coalition Operation Inherent Resolve, established in 2014.
  • About 2,500 U.S. troops had remained in Iraq under Operation Inherent Resolve before this withdrawal.
  • The withdrawal timeline stems from a 2024 agreement between Washington and Baghdad, reached under President Joe Biden, after Iraqi officials concluded their security forces were increasingly capable of handling the fight against ISIS.
  • Army Sgt. Michael Emmanuel Swinton, 30, was killed at Erbil Air Base in July during the controlled detonation of a downed Iranian attack drone.
  • Operation Inherent Resolve's headquarters relocated to Amman, Jordan, this year, allowing U.S. personnel and assets to redeploy while Iraqi forces assumed greater responsibility for counterterrorism operations.
  • CENTCOM Commander Adm. Brad Cooper told lawmakers in May that ISIS attacks in Iraq and Syria had fallen 70% since 2023, the lowest level since 2013; Iraqi Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi has pushed back a separate deadline for disarming Iran-backed militias to June 30, 2027.
○ Analysis

The U.S. exit from Erbil Air Base closes a chapter of American military presence that began with the 2003 invasion and later shifted to fighting ISIS after the group's 2014 rise, under the multinational Operation Inherent Resolve framework. The withdrawal follows a 2024 U.S.-Iraq agreement, negotiated after Iraqi officials concluded their security forces were increasingly capable of handling the ISIS fight, though the Pentagon declined to confirm whether the drawdown remains on its original timeline or provide details about continuing U.S. involvement. The exit coincides with a separate, unresolved issue: powerful Iran-backed militias in Iraq have resisted disarmament, and the DIA reported that Iran-aligned groups launched hundreds of attacks on U.S. forces and diplomatic facilities in Iraq after the war with Iran earlier this year.

Steelman

The core tension in this story is timing: the withdrawal follows a 2024 U.S.-Iraq agreement reached after Iraqi officials concluded their security forces were increasingly capable of handling the ISIS fight, but it arrives while armed Iran-backed militias—who have resisted disarmament and attacked U.S. forces—remain active, leaving unresolved how the two threads intersect for regional security going forward.

Implications

The Pentagon's refusal to confirm whether the withdrawal remains on schedule, combined with Baghdad's extended 2027 timeline for disarming Iran-backed militias, leaves open questions about the future U.S. military presence in Iraq and the stability of the region going forward.
