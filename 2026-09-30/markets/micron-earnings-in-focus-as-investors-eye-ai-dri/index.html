Micron Earnings in Focus as Investors Eye AI-Driven Memory Boom

Financial & Markets desk

Micron Technology (MU) is set to report its fiscal fourth-quarter earnings after the market closes Wednesday, with Wall Street expecting a sharp surge in profit and revenue driven by the global artificial intelligence buildout. Analysts surveyed by Bloomberg anticipate earnings per share of $31.83, up roughly 950% from $3.03 in the year-ago quarter, on revenue of $51.49 billion, a 355% increase from $11.31 billion. The company's stock has risen 279% year to date and more than 560% over the past 12 months, pushing its market capitalization past $1 trillion.

The memory chip maker's results are expected to be powered by demand for high-bandwidth memory (HBM) used in AI data centers. DRAM, which includes HBM, is forecast to generate $38.22 billion in sales, while NAND storage chips are projected to bring in $12.29 billion. Micron's fiscal third-quarter earnings per share jumped 1,214% year over year, and revenue rocketed 345%. The company has beaten Wall Street's sales and EPS estimates for 12 and 10 consecutive quarters, respectively.

Investors are focused on whether Micron can sustain its elevated gross margins amid concerns that pricing may normalize. The company reported a gross margin of 84.9% in the third quarter and guided for 86% in the fourth quarter, with UBS forecasting 87.6%. Creative Strategies CEO Ben Bajarin told Yahoo Finance, "Can gross margins stay up at the rates that they are, or do we need to see some normalization so it doesn't feel like, you know, price gouging in a moment?" He added that demand remains "continually strong" and that DRAM is "structurally constrained in many different ways, and that's not going away."

A key question for analysts is whether the current memory upcycle will prove structural rather than cyclical. Bajarin noted that long-term supply agreements with customers could give investors confidence in a longer cycle. JPMorgan analyst Harlan Sur estimates that Micron's strategic customer agreements now cover 35% or more of its forward bit production, up from roughly 20% of DRAM and a third of NAND volumes disclosed on the last call. The company has secured multiyear take-or-pay contracts for HBM worth $100 billion, with 16 key customer agreements totaling over $22 billion. Most of Micron's HBM capacity is booked through 2027 and 2028.

The options market points to a potential post-earnings swing of about 7.75% to 7.8% in either direction by the end of the week, implying a range near $990 to $1,155 from current levels. Call trading has been active, led by the $1,100 strike with more than 12,400 contracts traded. Downside positioning was concentrated around $1,000 and $1,050 puts. Memory and chip stocks have been volatile recently amid concerns about AI safety following a call by Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei to slow the development of frontier AI models.

Beyond earnings, investors will watch for commentary on memory pricing, HBM demand, margins, and how long tight supply conditions can persist. The company is also making big bets on future growth, announcing in August a plan to invest up to $10 billion over 10 years to build a research institution in Boise, Idaho. The AI-driven memory boom has boosted chip makers but also pushed up costs for consumer electronics; Apple raised prices on its iPhone 18 Pro line by $100 versus the prior generation, partly to offset rising memory costs.
