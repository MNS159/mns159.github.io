Pilot stabs co-pilot in apparent attempt to crash Israel-bound Flydubai flight; passengers intervene

International & World desk · anchored on Reuters

A Flydubai flight from Dubai to Tel Aviv was diverted to Tabuk, Saudi Arabia, on Wednesday after a pilot stabbed the co-pilot in an apparent attempt to crash the plane, according to Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu. The Boeing 737, Flight FZ1073, was carrying mostly Israeli passengers when an altercation occurred in the flight deck, the airline confirmed. Flight tracking data showed the aircraft plunged about 14,000 feet in less than 30 seconds and issued emergency signals, including code 7500 for “unlawful interference,” before landing safely at Prince Sultan bin Abdulaziz International Airport.

Netanyahu said one of the pilots stabbed the other and then tried to crash the plane. Passengers and off-duty crew members broke into the cockpit and subdued the attacker, the prime minister said, citing a passenger he spoke to. Video from onboard showed the captain lying on the cabin floor with stab wounds and a passenger with a bloody head wound. Passenger Motti Cohen Or, 70, told NBC News that someone shouted, “Someone is stabbing someone in the cockpit,” and the plane began shaking and diving toward the ground. Another passenger, Dalia Shpigel, told Israeli newspaper Haaretz that people seated near the front broke into the cockpit after the plane started to descend and they realized something was wrong.

Flydubai said the aircraft was secured by on-duty crew traveling onboard, who diverted and landed the plane. The airline stated that all passengers and crew were safe and accounted for, and that the reasons and motives behind the incident were unknown and subject to a formal investigation. Cluster2, the Saudi company managing the airport, said both the captain and first officer were taken to a hospital with injuries. Netanyahu said Saudi authorities had arrested the suspected attacker and were interrogating him.

Israeli Defense Minister Israel Katz called the incident an “attempted jihadist terrorist attack.” Netanyahu praised the passengers who intervened, saying they “saved many lives and prevented a major disaster.” The Israeli military scrambled at least two jets in response to the emergency signals. A rescue flight was arranged to take the diverted passengers from Saudi Arabia to Israel’s Ben Gurion Airport, where medical teams were on standby. The incident comes days before the anniversary of the October 7, 2023, Hamas-led attack on southern Israel and amid heightened tensions ahead of Israel’s parliamentary election on October 27. Gulf media outlets initially reported cautiously, with some waiting for official statements, while professor Abdulkhaleq Abdulla noted on social media that Israeli sources were not considered credible by some in the region.
