Ukraine War

Russia launches largest attack on Ukraine's energy grid since spring, killing at least seven

International & World desk · anchored on Reuters

Russia launched its largest attack on Ukraine's energy infrastructure since the spring, primarily targeting Kyiv and the surrounding region, Ukraine's Prime Minister said. At least five people were killed in overnight strikes across the country, including a child in the Kyiv region, officials reported. The strikes also disrupted power supplies, with Ukraine's biggest private energy firm DTEK saying power to 8,000 homes had been restored following "a difficult night" for the capital.

Prime Minister Serhiy Koretskyi wrote on Telegram: "Since the summer, there has effectively not been a single day without enemy attacks on our energy infrastructure. However, a mass and combined attack of this scale has occurred for the first time since the end of the last heating season." In total, 11 regions were attacked overnight, with residential buildings, social infrastructure, civilian enterprises and railways also targeted. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky said there had been strikes on critical infrastructure in the Zhytomyr, Mykolaiv, Kirovohrad and Rivne regions.

The Russian defence ministry said one of its targets was the Trypilska thermal power plant, the largest supplier of electricity to Kyiv, located some 40km from the capital. The operator of the plant, Centrenergo, confirmed damage and said it was assessing the scale. In Kyiv, people woke to find the city blanketed by thick, acrid smoke after a night punctuated by explosions; an air raid alert was still on and the distant sound of air defence gunfire echoed through the murky atmosphere. Residents in several districts were advised to close their windows until the smoke cleared.

Among those killed was a child whose body was found under the rubble of a house, Kyiv regional head Timur Tkachenko said. Kyiv Mayor Vitaly Klischko said three people were killed in total in the capital, including a utility company worker killed at a warehouse building. Another person was killed in the southern Mykolaiv region, acting regional head Heorhiy Reshetilov said. Two more people were killed in the north-eastern Sumy region, regional head Oleh Hryhorov said, adding that a school was also hit there in a separate strike.

On Wednesday, some households in the Kyiv, Dnipropetrovsk, Donetsk, Sumy and Kharkiv regions remained without power, Ukraine's energy ministry said. State-owned grid operator Ukrenergo advised residents not to use powerful electrical appliances between 10:00 and 16:00 local time as electricity supplies were lower than usual. Ukraine has been expecting another protracted Russian assault on the country's energy grid, which has happened every winter since Russia's full-scale invasion began in 2022.

Russia's military said it had intercepted more than 380 Ukrainian drones overnight over a number of regions, including Moscow. Meanwhile, Ukraine's military said it had struck a number of Russian military targets, including a drone warehouse in the occupied Donetsk region in eastern Ukraine. On the battlefield, Ukraine's Third Army Corps said it had recaptured several settlements near the city of Lyman in Donetsk this week as part of a counter-offensive called Operation Vivaldi. Zelensky visited troops in the area on Wednesday. Elsewhere, the front line remains largely stagnant, with both sides failing to make significant gains.
