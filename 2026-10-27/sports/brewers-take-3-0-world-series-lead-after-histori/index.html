Brewers take 3-0 World Series lead after historic 8-run inning

Sports desk

The Milwaukee Brewers moved within one victory of their first World Series championship Monday night, routing the Tampa Bay Rays 10-1 at Tropicana Field to take a commanding 3-0 series lead. The game turned decisively in the top of the fifth inning, when the Brewers strung together seven straight two-out hits and scored eight runs, turning a 2-0 lead into a 10-run margin.

The inning began with Jackson Chourio lacing his third straight single off Rays starter Freddy Peralta. After Jake Bauers flied out, Chourio stole second, and Peralta walked Christian Yelich on six pitches. Reliever Cameron Booser then entered and surrendered four runs without recording an out. "They were spitting on good pitches and putting balls in play," Booser said. "Things seem to be going their way right now, so it's unfortunate. Worst feeling in the world. You feel like you let your team down." Peralta, whose changeup was not landing well, said of the Brewers: "They do a good job of putting the ball in play, trying to pull the ball in play and taking a walk."

The Brewers have built a reputation for relentless pressure. Manager Pat Murphy articulated the mindset: "We want to step on your throat." That approach has unraveled the Rays, who committed their third error in two games in the third inning and have looked increasingly overmatched. Outfielder Chandler Simpson acknowledged Milwaukee's execution: "With them, they are executing really well, and that's what the results say."

The game also featured an emotional moment in the ninth inning when Brandon Woodruff, the longest-tenured Brewer and one of the franchise's best starting pitchers, made his first postseason appearance since 2021. Woodruff had been scheduled for season-ending shoulder surgery but recovered enough to be added to the World Series roster. It was potentially his final appearance for Milwaukee, and it came on the same mound at Tropicana Field where he made his MLB debut in 2017. He allowed one run in the ninth. Woodruff had been slated to start Game 4 as an opener, making Monday's outing a possible final chapter.

The Brewers now stand on the brink of a sweep. History is on their side: no team has ever come back from a 3-0 deficit in the World Series. Murphy, however, remained measured: "This is a best-of-seven series. It looks like we're in the driver's seat, but it's baseball. Anything can happen. I like the way we battle. I'm going to choose us when we can." SportsLine expert Micah Roberts backed Milwaukee in Game 4, saying, "One team has everything going right, and they're laughing, partying, and bringing in an eventual World Series Championship. The other team, the Rays, is playing like their grandmother just died." Game 4 is scheduled for Tuesday at Tropicana Field, with the Brewers looking to close out the series and claim their first title.
