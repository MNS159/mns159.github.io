Harry Kane wins Ballon d'Or, first English winner in 25 years

Sports desk

Harry Kane was crowned the winner of the men's Ballon d'Or on Monday night at a ceremony in London, becoming the first English player to claim football's most prestigious individual award in 25 years. The event, held at the London Palladium, marked the first time the Ballon d'Or was staged in London in its 70-year history. Kane, who plays for Bayern Munich, also ended a 30-year drought for the Bundesliga, with the last winner from the German league being Matthias Sammer in 1996.

The night, however, was marked by chaos. The start was delayed by 45 minutes because of London traffic, with the event scheduled near Oxford Street on the same day the shopping area was made traffic-free, and large crowds at the entrance. Once underway, the ceremony lasted nearly two and a half hours, and the audience was rowdy and boisterous, frequently shouting players' names and booing during commercial breaks. Hosts Kate Scott and Thierry Henry had to pause proceedings several times. Outside the Palladium, hundreds of people gathered, and BBC Sport witnessed metal barriers being scaled, broken, and ripped down, with several people evicted by security. Footage on social media appeared to show fans clashing with security and police; the BBC has approached the Metropolitan Police for comment.

Confusion began hours before the ceremony when the official Ballon d'Or website inadvertently published articles declaring Kane the winner, along with similar pieces for other favourites such as Kylian Mbappe and Alexia Putellas, who won the women's award. A spokesperson told BBC Sport the articles were 'inadvertently published online' and prepared for all nominees, insisting they were not an indication of the final results. After the ceremony, journalists interviewed the winners, but only men were selected to ask Kane questions, with several female journalists apparently ignored. The Ballon d'Or team later apologised.

Kane's victory sparked debate in Spain over whether success at major international tournaments receives sufficient recognition in Ballon d'Or voting. Some Spanish commentators argued that World Cup winners such as Lamine Yamal had equally compelling claims, drawing comparisons with the 2010 controversy when Lionel Messi won ahead of Spain's World Cup-winning midfielders Andres Iniesta and Xavi. El Pais captured the tension, writing: 'What would have been a historic double escaped: in London, goals prevailed over titles.' Spain's sole journalist voter, Jose Felix Diaz, editor-in-chief of AS, said he expected Yamal or Mbappe to win. Former Real Madrid midfielder Guti was critical, questioning the timing of the ceremony in London and suggesting Kane himself had not expected to win. However, the majority of Spanish press acknowledged Kane was a deserving winner.

In Germany, Kane's win was celebrated as a major boost for the Bundesliga and Bayern Munich. 'Just as Bayern and the Bundesliga benefit from Kane, the reverse is also true,' said Lothar Matthaus, the 1991 Ballon d'Or winner. 'I don't think Kane would've won the Ballon d'Or at any other club he could've joined.' Bundesliga chief executive Marc Lenz praised Kane as an ambassador and said the award underlines that the Bundesliga is a stage for the best players. Bayern chief executive Jan-Christian Dreesen called it 'a strong signal for the Bundesliga' that a player from a German club won the award for the first time in 30 years. Bild editor-in-chief for sport Henning Feindt wrote: 'He is the biggest star the league could wish for.' German journalist Raphael Honigstein noted Kane's impact on Bayern's international recognition.

Bayern Munich have confirmed they will honor Kane with a special welcome before Wednesday's DFB Cup match against Magdeburg at the Allianz Arena, and again at Saturday's Bundesliga top-of-the-table match against Borussia Dortmund. Club president Herbert Hainer said: 'Harry Kane can do it all – and he scores goals, goals and more goals. He now joins a line with Franz Beckenbauer, Gerd Müller and Karl-Heinz Rummenigge – that says everything about our history.' Kane, who earlier this year explained how his game had evolved to become an even more prolific goalscorer, celebrated his win by partying into the early hours in London with family and friends.
