New poll shows Democrats hold 8-point lead one week before midterms

Politics desk
✓ Facts
  • An NPR/PBS News/Marist poll, conducted Oct. 19-20 among 1,289 registered voters, found Democrats leading the generic congressional ballot 52%-44% among likely voters, an 8-point edge (margin of error +/-3.8 points for likely voters, +/-3.7 for registered voters).
  • Among registered voters, the Democratic lead on the generic ballot was 10 points; among independents, 52% said they favor Democrats.
  • President Trump's approval rating among likely voters was 40% approve/59% disapprove; among registered voters it was 38%/61%. 53% of both registered and likely voters said they 'strongly disapprove' of his job performance, tied with last month for the highest share recorded in the poll.
  • Trump's favorability among likely voters was 37% favorable to 59% unfavorable.
  • 55% of likely voters said it was more important to vote for candidates offering new ideas and change; 44% said their vote this year would be against Trump, compared with 21% who said it would be for him.
  • Democratic likely voters were 10 points more likely than Republicans to describe themselves as 'very enthusiastic' about voting (71% vs. 61%), though enthusiasm was lower among voters under 45 (42%), non-white voters (53%) and college-educated white women (56%).
  • A third of respondents said they experienced financial hardship in the past year, and 4 in 10 said their personal finances had gotten worse; cost of living, preserving democracy, and immigration were cited as top concerns by different voter groups.
  • Three-quarters of likely voters said they are confident their state and local governments will run a fair and accurate election, including 88% of Democrats, 75% of independents and 68% of Republicans.
○ Analysis

Historically, an advantage of this size on the generic congressional ballot has coincided with substantial House gains for the leading party — Democrats held similar leads in 2018 (+7) and 2006 (+12), years in which they flipped 30-plus seats. However, redistricting since the 2020 Census, including a round of mid-decade map changes initiated after Trump urged Republican-led states to redraw districts, has shifted the partisan baseline of many seats toward Republicans, meaning a similar polling lead today may not translate into identical seat gains. The poll also captures warning signs for Democrats beneath the topline number: softer enthusiasm among some traditionally Democratic-leaning groups (younger voters, nonwhite voters, college-educated white women) and persistent doubt among Democratic likely voters themselves about whether their own candidates will actually win, even as nearly all say they intend to vote Democratic.

Steelman

The topline 8-point Democratic lead and Trump's sub-40% favorability point toward a favorable climate for Democrats, but the poll's internal data — redistricting-altered maps, softer enthusiasm among key Democratic-leaning demographics, and Democrats' own uncertainty about winning — suggest the eventual scale of any gains is genuinely uncertain rather than a foregone wave.

Implications

With the House appearing more likely to shift based on current polling and the Senate described as a toss-up, final outcomes may not be clear on election night itself, given expected delays in vote counting in close races; both the durability of Democrats' enthusiasm advantage and the real-world effect of post-redistricting maps will be tested once actual results come in.
