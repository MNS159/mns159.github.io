Big Tech Earnings Test AI Boom as Spending Concerns Mount

Financial & Markets desk · anchored on Reuters

This week brings one of the most consequential earnings cycles for Big Tech, with Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, Intel, and SanDisk all reporting in the coming days. The results will serve as a critical test of whether the massive spending on artificial intelligence infrastructure is generating enough revenue to justify the outlays. The S&P 500 slipped 0.43% last week and the Nasdaq 100 fell about 0.8%, but underlying those moves, AI-related stocks sold off hard, with Core Weave down 40% and Nevius Group down about 31% over the past month amid spreading debt concerns.

Microsoft and Alphabet are expected to report after the close on Wednesday, October 28. For Microsoft, analysts expect earnings of $4.72 per share on revenue of $90.71 billion, compared with $3.72 per share a year earlier. Microsoft’s capital expenditures totaled $116 billion in the fiscal year ended June, including $35.8 billion in the June quarter alone. Last quarter its Azure cloud unit grew 43%, and Microsoft 365 Copilot passed 30 million paid seats. Investors will scrutinize the company’s spending outlook and Azure growth. Alphabet is also under the microscope after its stock fell 7% in July when it raised its AI spending outlook; its Google Cloud revenue surged 82% last quarter, but capital expenditure guidance is the key number to watch.

Amazon, Intel, and SanDisk are slated for Thursday, October 29 after the close. Amazon is expected to report earnings of $1.96 per share on revenue of $202.2 billion, versus $1.95 per share a year earlier. Amazon Web Services grew 37% last quarter, its fastest in 18 quarters. Amazon spent $54.21 billion on capital expenditures in the June quarter, more than any other tech giant. The key questions are whether cloud growth accelerates enough to justify the spending and whether AWS growth continues at a pace satisfactory to Wall Street. Intel guided for revenue of $15.8 billion to $16.8 billion and adjusted earnings of 38 cents per share. SanDisk guided for revenue of $10.3 billion to $10.8 billion and adjusted earnings of $44 to $46 per share, helped by higher flash memory prices.

The broader context is a market increasingly nervous about the gap between AI investment and returns. Elon Musk warned over the weekend that debt markets have shut off for companies without substantial revenue outside AI data centers. The Federal Reserve also meets this week, adding another layer of scrutiny. The results from the five companies will determine whether the recent selloff in AI stocks spreads to the largest names. As noted in the Yahoo Finance analysis, if revenue keeps pace with spending, the selloff could cool; if not, pressure is likely to intensify.

Among the other major tech names, Apple reported a record $109.42 billion in revenue for its most recent quarter, up 16% year over year, with iPhone sales up 22% and earnings per share of $2.02. Despite those results, the stock fell 7.35% after earnings as the company guided for 9%-11% growth, below the 12% analysts had expected. With the stock trading at around 35 times earnings, the gap between valuation and slowing growth has rattled investors. Analysts remain divided on whether Apple, with its steady ecosystem, offers a safer tech investment compared to the AI spending cycle.

This week will provide definitive signals on the trajectory of the AI boom. The key moments: Wednesday night brings Azure growth and capital spending plans from Microsoft and Alphabet; Thursday night brings Amazon’s cloud results, Intel’s data center performance, and SanDisk’s pricing trends. Investors will be watching whether cloud revenue acceleration can match the unprecedented capital outlays, and whether any warning signs emerge from the memory-chip sector as SK Hynix also reports late Tuesday. The outcome will likely set the tone for the rest of the year in technology stocks.
