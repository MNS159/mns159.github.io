Microsoft offers buyouts to 60,000 employees as AI investment intensifies

Tech / AI desk

On Monday, Microsoft announced voluntary buyout offers to approximately 60,000 employees—roughly a quarter of its global workforce—as the company continues to manage costs while pouring billions into artificial intelligence infrastructure. The offers are available to eligible workers in the United States, India, Canada, and the United Kingdom who are at the vice president level or below, according to the company.

“This offer gives eligible employees who may be considering a future outside the company a new choice to make that transition now with support,” Chief People Officer Amy Coleman said in a memo. Eligible employees will be notified by email and have a limited period to decide. The buyout includes financial padding and career-planning resources. Workers in data-center roles and some sales and marketing units are ineligible, according to a company spokesperson cited by Bloomberg.

This is Microsoft’s second voluntary buyout program in 2026. In April/May, the company offered buyouts to about 8,750 U.S. employees, with roughly 30% of eligible workers accepting, according to Chief People Officer Amy Coleman. In July, Microsoft cut 4,800 jobs globally, including 3,200 in its Xbox division, per separate reports from CBS News and Ars Technica.

Microsoft ended June with 223,000 employees globally, down from 228,000 at the end of 2025, according to regulatory filings. The company has said it expects to spend $175 billion on AI infrastructure—including data centers—in 2026 alone, as stated by Chief Financial Officer Amy Hood in a July earnings call.

The buyouts allow Microsoft to reduce labor costs while giving employees an exit path with support. The moves come as the company reorients resources toward AI, which requires massive capital investment. The program is available to mid-level workers for a limited time, and eligible employees were made aware of the offer on Monday.
