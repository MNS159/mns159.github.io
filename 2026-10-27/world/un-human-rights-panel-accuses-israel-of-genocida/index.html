UN Panel Accuses Israel of Genocidal Acts in Gaza, Crimes Against Humanity in West Bank

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts slightly center-left

A United Nations commission of inquiry has accused Israel of committing genocidal acts in Gaza since a US-brokered cease-fire took effect in October 2025, and of crimes against humanity including extermination, torture, and rape in the occupied West Bank. The report, released Monday by the UN Independent International Commission of Inquiry on the Occupied Palestinian Territory, covers the period from October 7, 2023 through September of this year.

The commission found that Israeli forces continued “genocidal acts” with the “intent to destroy the Palestinian people, in whole or in part” despite the cease-fire, citing attacks that killed at least five Palestinians, including a seven-year-old girl, on the day of the report’s release. In the West Bank, the commission said Israeli military operations and settler violence amounted to a “widespread and systematic attack” on civilians. It documented a sharp rise in airstrike deaths: six Palestinians were killed from 2008 to October 6, 2023, compared with 254 from October 7, 2023, to September 30, 2026, according to the report. The commission also alleged that Israeli authorities intended to collectively punish West Bank residents for the Hamas-led attack on southern Israel on October 7, 2023.

The commission, established by the UN Human Rights Council in 2021, first concluded in September 2025 that Israel had committed genocide in Gaza during the war that followed the October 7 attacks. Israel has repeatedly rejected that finding. The new report also examined abuses by Palestinian security forces, accusing the Palestinian Authority of arbitrary arrests, detention, torture, and targeting journalists and human rights defenders. The Gaza Health Ministry reports at least 74,500 people have been killed in the war, which displaced nearly the entire population.

Israel’s mission in Geneva dismissed the report as part of a “misinformation campaign to demonize the State of Israel,” while the Foreign Ministry called it a “propaganda document detached from reality” that legitimizes jihadist terrorism. The commission cited statements by Israeli officials, including Finance Minister Bezalel Smotrich, who referred to “two million Nazis” in the West Bank and called for areas to “look like Jabalia.” It also cited remarks by IDF Central Command chief Maj.-Gen. Avi Bluth, who reportedly said in May 2026 that soldiers were “killing like we haven’t since 1967.” The commission said it verified four additional cases of sexual violence against Palestinian detainees since March 2025, alleging gang rape and genital violence, and concluded that such acts were not isolated but part of a broader attack constituting crimes against humanity.

The report was released as Israeli forces imposed a lockdown on the West Bank city of Hebron following a shooting near the city. UN High Commissioner for Human Rights Volker Turk condemned the operation, saying the forced displacement of more than 2,000 Palestinians and threats to erase neighborhoods raised “serious concerns of ethnic cleansing.” Turk insisted that displaced people must be allowed to return and that collective punishment is a grave violation of international law. The three-member commission was chaired by Indian jurist Srinivasan Muralidhar; its former chair, Navi Pillay, won the Nobel Peace Prize this month.
