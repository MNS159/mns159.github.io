Finland and Norway leaders hold rare call with Putin

International & World desk · anchored on Reuters

The leaders of Finland and Norway held a 90-minute joint phone call with Russian President Vladimir Putin on Tuesday, marking a rare direct contact between a European leader and the Kremlin since the full-scale invasion of Ukraine began in 2022. Finnish President Alexander Stubb and Norwegian Prime Minister Jonas Gahr Støre initiated the call from Helsinki to discuss diplomatic efforts to end the war, according to statements from their offices. The call had originally been scheduled for 20 minutes but was extended, Støre told reporters. The initiative was conducted on behalf of the two Nordic countries only and not Europe as a whole, the leaders emphasized.

Stubb described the call as “frank and substantive,” adding that the outcome of the war “will have a direct impact on our and the broader European security.” Støre acknowledged “big differences in views” and said “much of what he said was already known,” noting that Putin repeated his narrative of the conflict since 2014. The conversation touched on practical steps such as attacks on energy infrastructure and the situation in the Black Sea, Støre said. The Kremlin said Putin told the leaders that “a just and lasting peace” could only be achieved if Russia’s interests are considered, and all three agreed to continue direct phone communication.

After the call, Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky spoke with Stubb and Støre. Zelensky expressed gratitude for their “active support” and said he discussed “various formats for our engagement with partners in Europe” that could aid diplomacy and defense. The call came ahead of trilateral talks provisionally scheduled for 4 November in Abu Dhabi involving Ukraine, Russia, and the United States. Those talks were agreed after last week’s meeting in Oslo between Zelensky, Stubb, Støre, and U.S. special envoy Steve Witkoff, who later traveled to St. Petersburg to meet Putin. Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk said Stubb had described the outlook for the Abu Dhabi talks as “moderately optimistic.”

The call drew skepticism from several European leaders. Danish Prime Minister Mette Frederiksen said she was “very sceptical,” telling Danish broadcaster DR that the dialogue had not been “co-ordinated with any of the rest of us.” In response, Støre pointed out that all allies had been informed in advance and given a detailed account afterward. He argued that if the only voices reaching Moscow are those from the U.S. administration, it is important for other European states to be heard as well. Estonian Foreign Minister Margus Tsahkna said on X that every contact with Russia must convey Europe’s unity and determination to support Ukraine, adding that “there is no indication that Russia has changed its strategic objectives.” Swedish Prime Minister Ulf Kristersson insisted any peace deal must be achieved on Ukraine’s terms and warned against appeasement. German Chancellor Friedrich Merz said no solution could be found without Ukraine at the table, and that the end of the war “depends only on one person” — Putin.

Tuesday’s call was the first such contact between a European leader and Putin since French President Emmanuel Macron spoke with him in July 2025. It came amid a souring of relations between the U.S. and its European allies after President Donald Trump agreed to lift some sanctions on Russia, allowing oil exports to the U.S. Several European leaders have suggested Europe should establish direct contacts with the Kremlin as the U.S. administration’s position softens. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said last weekend that having a European negotiator at the peace table would be “good.” Meanwhile, Romania summoned the Russian ambassador on Tuesday to protest a September 14 arson attack at a state-owned defense plant in Brașov, which Romanian intelligence attributed to a coordinated sabotage network from Russia. President Nicușor Dan called the incident part of a “premeditated and coordinated campaign” aimed at weakening European states.
