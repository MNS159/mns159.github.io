Ukraine War

Localized ceasefire begins in besieged Ukrainian city Oleshky amid dire humanitarian crisis

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts slightly center-left

A localized ceasefire began Tuesday in the Russian-occupied Ukrainian city of Oleshky, where trapped residents have been eating garden weeds to survive, after the White House said U.S. First Lady Melania Trump secured the truce. The limited ceasefire, confirmed by both Ukrainian officials and Russia's defense ministry, is intended to allow humanitarian aid deliveries and a civilian evacuation planned for Friday. Up to 6,000 people, including 200 children, remain in Oleshky and surrounding areas, according to Kyiv, with only 1,700 still inside the city that had a pre-war population of 24,000.

The city, seized by Russia in the first days of its February 2022 full-scale invasion, has had no electricity, gas or water for three years. No shops are open, and the only partially operational hospital opens a few days a week to perform critical amputations. Many residents told the BBC they are suffering extreme hunger, with one woman who stepped on a landmine currently in hospital after two operations. Another resident, Serhii, who fled three weeks ago, described walking 24 km (15 miles) through minefields, saying "you simply cannot trust the Russian Federation." Routes out of Oleshky have been mined and subject to drone attacks, and the Dnipro River separates the city from Ukrainian-controlled territory with destroyed bridges and mined riverbanks.

The truce began at 04:00 Moscow time (01:00 GMT) and is set to last until 23:59 local time on November 1, according to Ukrainian MP Davyd Arakhamia. Russia's defense ministry said its Dnepr group units have been conducting "engineering reconnaissance" and clearing main access roads of explosives. Ukraine's military general staff urged people not to spread rumors about evacuation routes. Ukrainian parliament's human rights commissioner, Dmytro Lubinets, traveled to the area to coordinate the operation, writing on social media that it is "a humanitarian mission being carried out with the mediation and coordination of US First Lady Melania Trump, in co-operation with Yana Lantratova, Russia's Commissioner for Human Rights."

The White House said last week that "non-military, civilian leaders from both countries would work together to visit Oleshky residents in their homes and determine the level of assistance they require." Melania Trump, who in August 2025 wrote a letter to Russian President Vladimir Putin telling him it was "time to protect children and future generations worldwide," has been credited by President Donald Trump with securing the ceasefire. However, the Vatican has offered its own narrative, suggesting Pope Leo XIV was responsible through phone calls with Putin and Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy. The pope had appealed earlier in September to Putin to allow food and medical aid into Oleshky and a safe evacuation, and urged Zelenskyy to cooperate with Moscow over a ceasefire.

Despite the truce, significant concerns remain. One person speaking to family in Oleshky feared shooting could restart during evacuations and Ukraine would be blamed. Zelenskyy warned that routes demined by Russia would lead civilians "towards Russia and stay there," though he said some would reach Ukrainian-controlled territory "one way or another." Residents also fear Russia may examine phones and online activity for signs of Ukrainian support, leading to detention or torture. The Kremlin acknowledged last month that Oleshky faced "a critical situation" and "problems with supplies of food and medicine," but blamed Kyiv. Elsewhere, attacks continue: on Monday evening, strikes on Kyiv hit a university and a bridge, killing one and injuring 14, while a Ukrainian drone strike on Bryansk, Russia, killed one and injured six. Finnish President Alexander Stubb and Norwegian Prime Minister Jonas Gahr Store held a rare call with Putin on Tuesday, pressing for de-escalation ahead of US-mediated technical meetings planned in Abu Dhabi in early November.
