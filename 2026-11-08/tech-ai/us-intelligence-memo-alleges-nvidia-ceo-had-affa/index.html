US intelligence memo alleges Nvidia CEO had affair with suspected Chinese spy; Chinese firms cleared to buy banned chips have empty offices

Tech / AI desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

A classified U.S. intelligence memo circulated among intelligence agencies and senior Trump administration officials alleges that Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang was targeted in a Chinese industrial espionage operation, according to multiple media reports. The memo, first reported by The New York Times and confirmed by the Financial Times, claims that Alice Huang, also known as Huang Le, a former senior executive at Singapore-based data center company Megaspeed, was recruited by China's Ministry of State Security to gather information about Nvidia's AI chips and influence export policy. The assessment further alleges that the relationship between Jensen Huang and Alice Huang, who are not related, extended beyond business and became romantic, and was monitored by U.S. intelligence for more than a year.

The two were photographed together at a bar in Taipei in June 2024, around the same period that Megaspeed began purchasing billions of dollars' worth of advanced Nvidia chips. Intelligence investigations found that Megaspeed established data centers across Southeast Asia and rented their computing power to Chinese tech giant Alibaba, creating an indirect route for Chinese companies to access advanced computing power despite U.S. restrictions on direct sales of sophisticated processors to China. The network of companies and investors associated with Alice Huang also has financial links to China Electronics Technology Group Corporation (CETC), one of China's major military technology conglomerates, through an investment in one of its cybersecurity subsidiaries.

Separately, an investigation by The New York Times found that two Chinese companies approved by the U.S. Commerce Department to purchase Nvidia's H200 AI chips—New Matrix and Maginfra—have empty offices with no evidence of actual operations. At the address listed by New Matrix, reporters found 'a hollow gray shell with wires dangling from the ceiling where lights should be.' A company directory at the building did not include the firm, and property management staff said they had no record of it. New Matrix was created 17 months ago and has scant online presence. Its owners have ties to Wise Road, a Chinese private equity firm blacklisted by the U.S. since 2024. Maginfra is suspected of being linked to Inspur, which Washington blacklisted in 2023 for supporting the People's Liberation Army, with both companies sharing personnel, patents, and a similar government ownership structure.

The approvals raise concerns about due diligence. 'I have a ton of concerns that neither the Commerce Department or Nvidia is doing the due diligence needed to ensure that the licensing terms are being followed,' Liza Tobin, former China director for the White House National Security Council, told the Times. The Commerce Department has also approved licenses for well-known entities such as Baidu, BYD, Tesla Shanghai, ZTE, Alibaba, Tencent, and ByteDance, as well as Zhejiang Yindun Cloud Technology, whose executives are reportedly related to the founder of Range Intelligent Computing Technology Group, a data center operator that has sold compute capacity to Chinese military contractors.

President Donald Trump banned the export of Nvidia AI GPUs in April 2025, but reversed the decision late last year in exchange for a 25% cut of sales. It took more than eight months for the first H200 shipments to arrive in China, partly due to Beijing's initial refusal to allow Chinese tech companies to buy the chips. Around two dozen firms have since received approval. An Nvidia spokesperson told the Times that Jensen Huang's interactions with Alice Huang were 'not unusual,' and that the company does not share classified information with customers. The spokesperson also said Nvidia sells 'the first, best and most of our technology in America.' China has rejected the espionage allegations as baseless smears. The chairs of three House committees have asked Jensen Huang to appear for a closed-door briefing, and President Trump was reportedly briefed on the matter last year and chose not to intervene.
