Trump offers Dana White White House press secretary role; White says he will meet Monday

Politics desk
✓ Facts
  • President Trump posted on Truth Social Saturday that he offered UFC CEO Dana White the job of White House press secretary, calling him "a violent Fighter and Warrior" and writing "Dana will be a Press Secretary like none other. Dana, please take the job!"
  • Trump's post asked White to bring six named UFC fighters along as "assistants," saying "even though some are not from our Country," to help "defend us against the Lunatic Left."
  • White, who was in London Saturday for a boxing event, told reporters he will fly to Washington and meet with Trump at the White House on Monday; he has not publicly accepted the position.
  • White posted an Instagram photo of himself standing at the White House press briefing room podium with the caption "On to the next chapter."
  • CNN reported, citing a source familiar with the matter, that White had declined the offer; a White House aide separately described the offer as a joke.
  • White House Communications Director Steven Cheung posted on X, "Hell yeah! Do it, @DanaWhite!" in response to Trump's offer.
  • The White House press secretary post has been vacant since Karoline Leavitt left at the end of August; her successor, Katie Zacharia, departed after five days on the job in late October/early November.
  • The offer came one day after Trump announced Stephen Miller would replace Susie Wiles as chief of staff, amid a broader White House shake-up following Republican losses in the Nov. midterm elections that gave Democrats control of both chambers of Congress.
○ Analysis

Dana White and Donald Trump have a long-standing personal and political relationship: Trump helped the fledgling UFC by hosting early events at his Atlantic City casino in 2001, White has spoken at three Republican National Conventions (2016, 2020, 2024), and the UFC staged a cage match on the White House South Lawn in June 2025. White also holds several other high-profile roles, including UFC president/CEO, head of the Power Slap league, founder of the newly launched Zuffa Boxing promotion, and a board seat at Meta, making the press secretary offer logistically unusual regardless of its seriousness. The episode unfolded against a backdrop of administration turmoil — a vacant, high-turnover press secretary post and a simultaneous chief-of-staff change — all occurring just days after Republicans suffered significant midterm losses that will hand Democrats control of Congress in January.

Steelman

Whether read as a genuine offer or a publicity gesture, the episode underscores how unsettled and improvised the White House communications operation has become, with the press secretary post sitting vacant through multiple short-lived appointments even as other senior staffing changes proceed.

Implications

The Monday meeting between Trump and White should clarify whether the offer was serious and whether White will take on, decline, or informally advise on the press secretary role, while the White House communications post remains unfilled amid broader staffing changes as the administration adjusts to incoming divided government in January.
