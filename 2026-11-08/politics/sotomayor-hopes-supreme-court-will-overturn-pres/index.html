Sotomayor hopes Supreme Court will overturn presidential immunity decision, says court has done 'crazy things'

Politics desk
✓ Facts
  • Supreme Court Justice Sonia Sotomayor spoke publicly on Nov. 7 at a civics education event at the Washington Library at Mount Vernon.
  • Sotomayor said she hopes the Supreme Court will reverse some of its past rulings, specifically naming the 2024 presidential immunity decision.
  • Sotomayor authored the dissent for the Court's three liberal justices in the 2024 presidential immunity case.
  • The underlying 2024 ruling was decided 6-3, with the majority opinion written by Chief Justice John Roberts, holding that presidents have presumptive immunity from prosecution for official acts.
  • That ruling halted the criminal case against former President Donald Trump over his efforts to subvert the 2020 election results.
  • Sotomayor said the rule of law is in a 'rough patch' and that 'some norms we may have lost forever,' while adding she does not believe the U.S. will 'fail as a democracy.'
  • Her comments came days after Republicans lost control of the House, Senate, and a majority of governorships in recent elections.
  • A Democratic-controlled Senate would make it harder for Trump to confirm any future Supreme Court nominee if a vacancy occurs.
○ Analysis

Sotomayor's remarks drew attention in part because, in her 2024 dissent, she argued that the immunity ruling 'makes a mockery of the principle, foundational to our Constitution and system of Government, that no man is above the law.' Her public comments at the civics forum reiterated that criticism and named the decision specifically as one she hopes will eventually be overturned.

Implications

With Senate control now in Democratic hands, any near-term Supreme Court vacancy would likely be more difficult for Trump to fill, since confirming a nominee would require Democratic support.
