Poland mourns six killed in Krakow shooting; terrorism probe launched

International & World desk

Poland has begun two days of national mourning after a gunman killed six people in central Krakow on Saturday, in what authorities have described as a terrorist attack. The shooting began shortly before 15:00 local time (14:00 GMT) in Krakow's Main Market Square, when a 25-year-old Polish man drove into the square and began firing at random people from his car, police said. Six people — four women and two men — were killed in the square, including four Polish nationals, a British national, and an Austrian woman. Sixteen people, including five foreigners and two police officers, were taken to hospital; authorities later said the injured included nationals from Austria, Britain, Germany, Portugal, and Russia. The suspect was shot by police within three minutes of the attack beginning and remains in critical condition in hospital after surgery for a gunshot wound.

After the attack, police searched the suspect's home in Ostrowiec Świętokrzyski, about 200km (125 miles) northeast of Krakow, and found three additional bodies. Polish police confirmed on Sunday that the victims were the suspect's 53-year-old father, 52-year-old mother, and 21-year-old sister. Poland's National Prosecutor's Office announced on Sunday it had launched an investigation into the attack as a terrorist offence, stating it was probing the killing of six people using a firearm and the attempted murder of many others, including police officers. A spokesperson for the office said it was highly likely that the killings in Ostrowiec Świętokrzyski and Krakow were carried out by the same person, but investigations are ongoing.

Polish Interior Minister Marcin Kierwinski stated that early investigations indicate the suspect was inspired by far-right ideologies, describing him as an ultranationalist. Police said the suspect was wearing military-style attire and used a military-grade weapon; many pieces of weapons and ammunition were found in his vehicle. According to media reports, the suspect had a valid gun licence and no prior criminal record. Polish authorities have not confirmed his name, though some media have identified him as Michal O. The suspect's car was checked for possible explosive devices, but none were found; residents of nearby buildings were evacuated while the operation was under way.

Witnesses described scenes of panic. Jonathan Balshaw from Ipswich, who was in the square with his girlfriend, said they heard more than 20 shots and hid in a church. Caitlin Shasby from Newcastle said she heard shots from her hotel room and was told to stay inside. Two surgeons who were having lunch with their families rushed to help the wounded after the shooting stopped; they said two police officers were conscious with gunshot wounds to the chest, and the perpetrator was conscious with gunshot wounds to the abdomen, thigh, and chest. One police officer was described as being in very critical condition after being shot three times; in an update on Sunday, police said the two injured officers and other hospitalised individuals were in stable condition, and two women had been released.

Prime Minister Donald Tusk, who travelled to Krakow, said: 'Until now in Poland we haven't had such drastic cases of terrorism... that's what we have to call this cruel attack on people's lives here in Krakow.' Krakow Mayor Łukasz Gibała signed an order declaring a period of mourning in the city until Wednesday. UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham said he was shocked and saddened by the appalling shooting, and British foreign secretary Ed Miliband said the UK government deeply mourns those who died. US ambassador to Poland Tom Rose described it as a savage and murderous attack, and EU top diplomat Kaja Kallas said the shooting was a tragedy that will be felt far beyond Poland. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky called the shooting shocking and extended condolences. Krakow's main market square and surrounding streets remained closed on Sunday, with flags at half-mast and mourners placing candles. The traditional hourly trumpet call from St Mary's church on the square was suspended for 12 hours to commemorate the dead, the first time this has happened since 1939.
