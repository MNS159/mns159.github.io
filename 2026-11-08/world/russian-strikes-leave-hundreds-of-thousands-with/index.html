Ukraine War

Russian strikes leave hundreds of thousands without power in Ukraine ahead of talks

International & World desk

Russian overnight drone and ballistic missile attacks on Saturday damaged Ukraine's energy infrastructure, leaving hundreds of thousands without electricity and disrupting water and heating supplies in the capital, Kyiv, and several other regions, Ukrainian authorities said Sunday. President Volodymyr Zelensky said 12 Ukrainian regions came under attack, with the capital hit hardest. The attacks consisted of more than 30 ballistic missiles and over 170 drones, according to Zelensky. Nine people were reported injured across the country, including a 14-year-old girl.

In the central Dnipropetrovsk region, power company DTEK said more than 600,000 residents were without electricity after a night attack damaged important energy facilities, with repairs complicated by repeated attacks. In the northeastern Kharkiv region, nearly 250,000 customers lost power after a Russian drone hit a critical infrastructure facility, according to Zlatopil Mayor Mykola Baksheyev. Zelensky said that in the Zhytomyr region alone, 140 apartment buildings were left without heating as temperatures hovered around freezing. Kyiv Mayor Vitali Klitschko said the city's water supply system had returned to normal operation and about 60% of apartment buildings were connected to heating. Energy Minister Denys Shmyhal reported power cuts in five regions but did not specify how many homes were affected.

Zelensky said the strikes targeted substations in the Vinnytsia, Zhytomyr, Rivne, Kharkiv, and Dnipro regions, including substations connecting the Rivne and Khmelnytsky nuclear power plants to Kyiv and central Ukraine. In Kyiv, the Northern Bridge, a key transport artery, was closed as workers repaired damage to the road's surface and power lines. The attacks come as Ukrainian and Russian delegations are due to take part in US-brokered trilateral talks in Abu Dhabi, United Arab Emirates, scheduled for Tuesday and Wednesday. Zelensky accused Moscow of preparing for the talks by launching attacks on Ukraine's critical infrastructure, saying Russia was trying to plunge Kyiv and the entire country into a blackout.

The attacks cap a dire week for Kyiv residents, who have experienced day-long power cuts and water supply interruptions. Ukrainian officials say the Kremlin's goal is to freeze civilians into submission over the winter months, a war crime. An independent United Nations investigations panel last week concluded that Russia committed war crimes and crimes against humanity by intentionally attacking civilian infrastructure. Russia's Defense Ministry stated that its forces will continue strikes against Ukrainian fuel and energy infrastructure facilities, which it says are used to aid the Ukrainian military.

Zelensky appealed to the US Congress for increased bilateral support, writing on Telegram: 'We are counting on unity between both parties on issues of security, support for Ukraine, condemnation of Russian aggression, and increased sanctions pressure on Putin.' He also said Russia launched a drone at Komyshuvakha, a village in the Zaporizhzhia region where Moscow had hit an aid distribution center earlier in the week in one of the deadliest attacks of the war. The death toll from that strike has risen to 55, Zelensky said, with emergency services previously putting it at 50. 'Unfortunately, more people may still be under the rubble,' he added.

The talks in Abu Dhabi follow a US-brokered six-day ceasefire last week in the southern Kherson region that allowed hundreds of residents of the Russian-occupied town of Oleshky to evacuate. US First Lady Melania Trump hosted Russian and Ukrainian delegations at the White House on Thursday, calling for a 'more enduring' ceasefire and thanking both Zelensky and Russian President Vladimir Putin without referencing the origins of the war, which began when Putin launched a full-scale invasion of Ukraine in February 2022, causing thousands of civilian casualties and the displacement of millions. Western diplomats say agreeing to Russia's longstanding demand that NATO reverse its expansion across Europe would effectively end the alliance. Zelensky described a recent blueprint set out by Jared Kushner, Donald Trump's son-in-law, as 'Yalta 2.0,' referencing the 1945 summit that divided postwar Europe, and emphasized: 'We don't want to be divided by Russia.'
