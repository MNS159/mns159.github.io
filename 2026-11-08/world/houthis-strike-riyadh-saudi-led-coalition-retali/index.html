Yemen Conflict

Houthis strike Riyadh, Saudi-led coalition retaliates

International & World desk · anchored on Reuters
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

Yemen's Houthi movement said at least 33 people, including women and children, were killed in Saudi-led coalition air strikes on the town of al-Shariyah, east of the capital Sanaa, on Saturday. Houthi-run Al Masirah television broadcast footage of rescuers pulling bodies, including those of young children, from the rubble of several buildings, and showed injured people being carried to hospital. The Houthi-controlled Saba news agency said 39 people were wounded in the attack.

Hours before the strike, the Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted a missile heading toward Riyadh. The Saudi Ministry of Energy later reported that the Aramco tower, the headquarters of the state-owned oil company, sustained minor damage from falling shrapnel in what it described as an attempted attack. The coalition separately said it intercepted and destroyed a drone and four missiles — two ballistic and two cruise — over Riyadh and southern regions, according to coalition spokesman Maj. Gen. Turki al-Maliki.

The war in Yemen, dormant for several years, reignited amid the Iran war. A truce began to unravel when Saudi Arabia struck Sanaa airport, controlled by the Houthis, to prevent an Iranian plane from landing. The Tehran-backed Houthis subsequently targeted Abha airport in Saudi Arabia and began striking Saudi ships using the Red Sea off Yemen as an alternative oil export route to the Strait of Hormuz. Major fighting has since resumed between the Houthis and forces loyal to the Saudi-backed internationally recognized government.

On Friday, an attack on a market in Taiz province, a focus of recent fighting, reportedly killed 30 people. The Houthis accused the Saudis of carrying out that strike, while government forces blamed the Houthis for firing a missile at the site. The United Nations says more than 1,600 people have been killed and over 300,000 displaced since fighting resumed.

The Saudi-led coalition, which has backed Yemen's internationally recognized government for more than a decade, has regularly bombed Houthi positions. The Houthis have fired multiple missiles at targets inside Saudi Arabia, including airports and oil facilities. Houthi leader Mahdi al-Mashat has vowed the group will take revenge for the latest attacks in Yemen.
