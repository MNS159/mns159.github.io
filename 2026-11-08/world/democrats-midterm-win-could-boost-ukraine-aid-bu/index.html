Democrats' midterm win could boost Ukraine aid, but uncertainty remains

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

The Democratic Party secured a decisive victory in the November 3 midterm elections, winning majorities in both the US House of Representatives and the Senate, as well as gubernatorial races in former Republican strongholds such as Texas. The result, described by some as a "blue wave" or "blue tsunami," will give Democrats control of Congress when the new session begins on January 3, 2027, providing a check on President Donald Trump, who remains in office with veto power and the ability to ignore congressional decisions.

For Ukraine, the shift raises hopes of renewed US military and financial aid, which has slowed dramatically under Trump. From Russia's full-scale invasion in 2022 to late 2024, Washington provided nearly $200 billion in assistance, but from October 2025 to March 2026, Kyiv received only $8.2 billion in military aid through "legacy pipelines" from the Biden administration, with another $13 billion in deals still being fulfilled. Europe has taken over most of the financial burden, contributing $83 billion in 2025 alone, but the Kiel Institute reports that almost a third of all weaponry still comes from US companies.

Democrats have pledged to scrutinize Trump's ties to Russia and investigate alleged corruption within the Trump family, including ties to Russian politicians, intelligence, and oligarchs such as Umar Kremlev, who reportedly paid hundreds of thousands of dollars for Donald Trump Jr.'s wedding in the Bahamas in May. They have signaled readiness to issue subpoenas and force disclosure of executive branch records related to Ukraine once they take control of Congress. However, several pro-Ukraine Republicans lost their seats, including Brian Fitzpatrick of Pennsylvania, who co-chaired the Congressional Ukraine Caucus.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said of the results, "We have bipartisan support," adding that he was "thankful to the Republicans, but Democrats very clearly state their support of Ukraine and providing aid to it." Volodymyr Fesenko, head of the Kyiv-based Penta think tank, told Al Jazeera, "Ukraine's positions may become stronger. But we need to keep bipartisan support." He also stressed the need for Kyiv to appoint a new ambassador to Washington after Olha Stefanishyna was fired in August during a probe into alleged illegal enrichment.

Analysts warn that the next two months before the new Congress takes office could be difficult for Ukraine. Kyiv-based analyst Igar Tyshkevych told Al Jazeera that Trump, who currently has Republican majorities in both houses, will likely pressure Ukraine to accept a fast-tracked peace deal with Russia. "Because when Russia isn't willing to concede, the fastest way to solve the matter – either through a truce or through freezing the war – is only possible if [Trump] twists Kyiv's arms," he said. The new Congress will also face a dilemma over corruption allegations involving top Ukrainian officials and purported embezzlement of US aid.

In Russia, pro-Kremlin analysts predicted the Democrats would push for renewed military aid and sanctions. Moscow-based analyst Andrey Kortunov wrote in Kommersant that Democrats will "double down on their efforts to prevent Trump from establishing a dialogue with Moscow, demand the resumption of direct US military aid to Kyiv, insist on the restoration of transatlantic unity on the Ukrainian issue and propose new sanctions against Russia." The Russian Ministry of Foreign Affairs dismissed the concerns, saying Moscow's foreign policies "don't depend on the fluctuations of the political barometer in other nations," while Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said, "President Trump continues to exercise his powers. We sincerely hope that he will continue his peacemaking efforts."
