Berkshire Hathaway buybacks surge to $9.6 billion in Q3 as earnings decline

Financial & Markets desk

Berkshire Hathaway said Saturday it accelerated share repurchases during the third quarter, spending $9.6 billion to buy back its own stock — more than double the $4.5 billion repurchased in the second quarter and bringing total buybacks for 2026 to about $14.4 billion. The increase came as both operating and overall profits declined. The conglomerate, led by Greg Abel after the board formalized his succession as Warren Buffett's successor in day-to-day oversight, reported operating earnings of $12.705 billion, down 5.8% from $13.485 billion a year earlier.

The drop in operating profit was driven largely by a pullback in insurance results. Insurance underwriting tumbled 43% year over year to $1.352 billion, while insurance investment income declined 14% to $2.734 billion. The railroad, energy and manufacturing businesses all posted year-over-year earnings growth. Overall net earnings, which include gains from Berkshire's sprawling stock portfolio, fell 28% to $22.112 billion from $30.796 billion a year prior, as investment gains dropped nearly 50% to $9.407 billion.

Berkshire's cash hoard grew 3% in the quarter to $377.435 billion from $365.514 billion in the second quarter. However, excluding rail and utility cash along with T-bill payables, cash totaled $352.3 billion, down 1.9% quarter over quarter. The company's five largest equity holdings remained unchanged: Alphabet, American Express, Apple, Bank of America and Coca-Cola.

Despite the earnings pullback, Berkshire stock has outperformed recently amid a broad rotation out of technology stocks. Class A shares are up more than 12% over the past month, while the S&P 500 has dropped 5.5% in that period. Year to date, Berkshire has gained 13%, compared with a 7.8% rise for the broad market index.

Morningstar, which rates Berkshire with a Narrow Economic Moat and Low Uncertainty, said adjusted third-quarter operating results came in slightly above its expectations. The research firm noted that insurance underwriting remained solid despite declining prices, and that the manufacturing, service and retailing division posted a decent top-line gain aided by the OxyChem acquisition and the Taylor Morrison deal. Morningstar said it is likely to modestly increase its current fair value estimate of $765,000 for Class A shares and $510 for Class B, but still views the shares as modestly overvalued, trading at about a 12% premium to that estimate. Book value per share rose 10.5% year over year to $536,590.
