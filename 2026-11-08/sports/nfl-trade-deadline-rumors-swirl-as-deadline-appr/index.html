NFL trade deadline rumors swirl as Nov. 10 cutoff approaches

Sports desk

With the NFL trade deadline set for 4 p.m. ET on Tuesday, Nov. 10, a flurry of activity has already reshaped rosters across the league. Six trades have been completed during the regular season, including the Oct. 29 blockbuster that sent Tennessee Titans defensive tackle Jeffery Simmons and a 2028 fifth-round pick to the Chicago Bears for two first-round picks and a 2027 fourth-round pick. Other notable deals include the Indianapolis Colts trading defensive tackle DeForest Buckner to the San Francisco 49ers for a fourth-round pick and guard Quenton Nelson to the Seattle Seahawks for third- and fifth-round picks, both on Oct. 27. The Arizona Cardinals traded wide receiver Marvin Harrison Jr. to the Houston Texans for second- and sixth-round picks on Oct. 11, and quarterback Jacoby Brissett along with a fifth-round pick to the Baltimore Ravens for a fourth- and seventh-round pick on Nov. 4. The Philadelphia Eagles sent guard Cam Jurgens and a seventh-round pick to the Ravens on Oct. 7 for a 2028 second-round pick and 2027 fifth-round pick, and the Pittsburgh Steelers traded cornerback Joey Porter Jr. to the Dallas Cowboys on Sept. 30 for a 2028 second-round pick and 2027 sixth-round pick.

The top name on the trade market remains Indianapolis Colts running back Jonathan Taylor, who has told the team he does not want to be traded but may have little choice as the Colts' season falters. According to ESPN's Jeremy Fowler, the Colts are still asking for a first-round pick for Taylor and maintain they do not have to trade him, while suitors believe a second-round pick plus another asset should suffice. Jordan Schultz reports that the Broncos, Vikings, Packers, Bengals and Giants are among teams that have reached out to the Colts about Taylor, and Schultz believes a trade is 'more likely than not' despite the high asking price. However, on Nov. 8, Josina Anderson reported that the Giants have 'cooled' on Taylor talks due to the Colts' high asking price, and SNY's Connor Hughes noted conflicting reports about the Giants' willingness to meet Indianapolis's demands. The Colts have said they plan to wait until after their Week 9 game against the Cowboys before exploring trades for Taylor and others, per insider Jordan Schultz.

Several other players have drawn trade interest. Baltimore Ravens running back Derrick Henry, a free agent after the season, is 'not available' according to Adam Schefter, as the team is turning down trade requests despite a recent losing streak and the absence of quarterback Lamar Jackson for most of the season. The Colts have received multiple calls about quarterback Daniel Jones but have told interested teams they are not trading him, per Tom Pelissero. The Denver Broncos checked in on Arizona Cardinals tight end Trey McBride, a Colorado native, but the Cardinals summarily declined, per Schefter. The Washington Commanders have not completely abandoned the idea of adding San Francisco 49ers wide receiver Brandon Aiyuk, according to Schefter and Ian Rapoport, though any deal would require discussions with multiple people and is not a certainty. Aiyuk has not played since the 2024 season due to a serious knee injury and missed team meetings, leading the 49ers to void roughly $27 million in guarantees on his contract. ESPN's Jeremy Fowler is skeptical Aiyuk will be reinstated to play anytime soon.

Other potential trade candidates include Arizona Cardinals pass-rusher Josh Sweat, who is open to a trade according to Schultz, and Dallas Cowboys wide receiver George Pickens, whose expiring contract and the team's lack of success could make him available, though there is no firm indication Dallas is looking to trade him. Teams are also watching the New York Jets, wondering whether another loss might open the door for players such as Will McDonald IV or running back Breece Hall to become available, per ESPN's Dan Graziano. The Los Angeles Chargers, the last winless team in the league, are not expected to trade players like Khalil Mack, Ladd McConkey or Justin Herbert, as Adam Schefter reports the team does not like the optics of giving up on the season. Insiders have told NFLTradeRumors.co that there could be anywhere from 12 to 20 trades total during the regular season, with six filed so far, and a similar volume to the 10 trades on deadline day in 2022 is possible.

The deadline arrives as contenders look to position themselves for a playoff run and rebuilding teams seek to maximize value. The Bears, coming off the Simmons trade, are considered one of the two or three best teams in the NFL and could add further to their defense, with cornerback Riq Woolen of the Philadelphia Eagles mentioned as a potential target in mock trade proposals. The Commanders, at 2-4-1, remain alive in the NFC East and have a need at wide receiver with Terry McLaurin on injured reserve, making Aiyuk a possible solution if he is reinstated. All trades must be finalized by 4 p.m. ET on Tuesday, Nov. 10.
