Stephen Curry leads Warriors to seventh straight win, makes clutch history

Sports desk

Stephen Curry scored 41 points, including a perfect 4-for-4 on go-ahead shots in the final two minutes of regulation and overtime, as the Golden State Warriors defeated the Orlando Magic 126-119 on Saturday night at Chase Center. The win extended the Warriors' winning streak to seven games after they opened the season 0-3, making them the first team since the 1950-51 Boston Celtics—before the Bill Russell era—to start 0-3 and then win seven straight, according to BasketNews.com.

Curry finished 13-of-19 from the field, 5-of-10 from three-point range, and a perfect 10-of-10 from the free-throw line, adding six rebounds and six assists. The 38-year-old guard broke LeBron James' record for the most 40-point games after turning 35, now with 16 such games. He also became the third point guard in NBA history—joining Oscar Robertson and Russell Westbrook—to reach 25,000 points and 5,000 rebounds, per NBC Sports Bay Area & California. Curry's 4-for-4 mark on go-ahead shots in the final two minutes of regulation and overtime is the first such perfect performance in the tracking era (since 1996-97), according to NBC Sports.

The Warriors' bench contributed 42 points, with Georges Niang scoring 11. Head coach Steve Kerr opted to start overtime with Curry and Draymond Green on the bench to manage minutes, a decision that initially puzzled the veterans. "No, we're good. We're alright, that ain't changing nothing," Green recalled saying, but they ultimately trusted Kerr. Backup center Charles Bassey scored the first four points of overtime before Curry and Green re-entered with 3:48 left. "The conversation and the energy was more frustration for us, not doubt in the (bench) unit that was going out there," Curry said, per The Mercury News.

The Magic, missing starters Paolo Banchero, Desmond Bane, and Jalen Suggs, grabbed 24 offensive rebounds and attempted 33 more shots than the Warriors. "We got totally outplayed that whole second half," Kerr said. "They were amazing, 33 more field goal attempts. It's almost impossible to win a game but I'm proud of our guys because they gutted it out." Orlando tied the game late in regulation and forced overtime, but Curry's clutch layups and free throws sealed the win.

In his postgame interview, Curry addressed the team's early-season doubters: "I just love the resiliency, attitude, and commitment from everybody. When you set foot on the floor, just play hard, and good things will happen. We don't forget where people thought we were at the beginning of the year." He acknowledged the difficulty of ignoring criticism but said the team has learned to block it out. Social media reactions were mixed, with some users noting the Warriors needed overtime against a depleted Magic team and others predicting Curry's comments could "age horribly" if the team falters later in the season.

The Warriors improved to 7-3, standing strong in the Western Conference after their first 10 games. Curry is averaging 31.3 points, 4.6 assists, and 4 rebounds while shooting 50% from the field, 45% from three, and 99% from the free-throw line. The team will look to extend its streak as they continue to build on their remarkable turnaround.
