Anthropic and OpenAI release cheaper, more efficient AI models

Tech / AI desk

Anthropic and OpenAI both released new AI models today that prioritize cost reduction and efficiency over groundbreaking new capabilities, intensifying competition for enterprise customers who are increasingly turning to cheaper, open-weight alternatives. Anthropic launched Opus 5.5, the latest version of its flagship workhorse model, while OpenAI announced GPT-6 Sol and Luna, the newest iterations of its mid-range and smaller models focused on speed and affordability. The releases come just weeks after OpenAI launched the more powerful GPT-6 Astra, which the company said is the 'world’s best model' for computer work and coding.

Anthropic says Opus 5.5 sets a new state-of-the-art in coding and knowledge work performance, outperforming its own larger Fable model in many benchmarks and succeeding in tasks Fable could not complete. The company reports the model also compares favorably to its Mythos model in biology and cybersecurity capabilities. OpenAI claims GPT-6 Sol makes about half as many mistakes on factual accuracy as its predecessor, reaching the reliability of GPT-6 Astra at lower cost. The new GPT-6 Sol and Luna models are available across OpenAI’s ChatGPT Work, Codex, and API, with Luna rolling out to the desktop app, Free, and Go tiers throughout the day.

The primary focus of both releases is cost savings. Anthropic priced Opus 5.5 at $4 per million input tokens and $20 per million output tokens, a 20% reduction from Opus 5. Cache reads, which account for most agentic and coding workload costs, are priced at $0.20 per million tokens, a 60% decrease. The model also generates output more than 30% faster than Opus 5. OpenAI said the GPT-6 series of Sol and Luna will be available at half the cost of the previous 5.6 generation, attributing the price drop to improvements in caching and inference. Opus 5.5 also communicates with less jargon and places important information earlier in responses, Anthropic noted.

These releases reflect broader market dynamics as organizations adopt model routers and other practices to reduce reliance on expensive frontier models. Anthropic and OpenAI both argue the new models push the frontier modestly while substantially reducing costs. Anthropic’s launch of Opus 5.5 came roughly 90 minutes before OpenAI’s announcement, underscoring the intense rivalry between the two companies. OpenAI repeatedly claims its new Sol and Luna models handle tasks substantially better than Anthropic’s top offerings, including Fable and Opus.

Opus 5.5 is Anthropic’s first model release since CEO Dario Amodei publicly embraced a deliberate slowdown in AI capabilities advancement to allow safety measures to keep pace. The company said the model’s safety training was broadly similar to its predecessors, with external evaluations by organizations including METR and Frontier Design. Anthropic noted that more advanced training and evaluation systems, including improved security and monitoring systems, were already being prepared for future models. Anthropic also announced that Sonnet 5.5 and Haiku 5.5 — the middle and fastest tiers of its Claude lineup — will be released in the coming weeks with similar performance improvements.
