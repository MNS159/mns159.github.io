Markets Eye Trump-Xi Meeting Amid Oil, Trade, and AI Talks

Financial & Markets desk

U.S. stocks slipped on Wednesday as investors monitored diplomatic efforts with China and Iran, with the Dow Jones Industrial Average falling 0.2%, the S&P 500 slipping 0.1%, and the Nasdaq Composite dropping 0.3% after back-to-back record highs. The benchmark 10-year Treasury yield edged closer to 5%, adding friction for equities. Oil prices were little changed, with Brent crude futures trading below $100 per barrel and WTI crude below $91, as a potential thaw in U.S.-Iran relations remained uncertain after President Trump said U.S. and Iranian officials met for several hours at the U.N. on Tuesday.

President Trump is expected to greet Chinese President Xi Jinping at Joint Base Andrews, kicking off Xi's first visit to Washington, D.C., in 11 years. Experts do not expect major policy breakthroughs, but the leaders are expected to discuss trade, the war in Iran, rare earths, and artificial intelligence. A dinner with both presidents is planned for Thursday in Washington, with U.S. executives including Nvidia's Jensen Huang, OpenAI's Sam Altman, Google's Sundar Pichai, Tesla and SpaceX's Elon Musk, Citigroup's Jane Fraser, Amazon's Jeff Bezos, Apple's Tim Cook, Dell's Michael Dell, Meta's Mark Zuckerberg, and Microsoft's Satya Nadella expected to attend. On the Chinese side, business representatives under consideration include BYD, Xiaomi, CATL, Gotion, Hisense, Wanxiang, Bank of China, and COFCO Group, though no Chinese companies had been confirmed as invited as of Tuesday.

China's efforts to build self-sufficiency have reduced the threat to its domestic market from global trade developments, but the trade deficit that escalated tensions has yet to shrink significantly. U.S. concerns about artificial intelligence have gained prominence ahead of the meeting. While an escalation in trade tensions last April briefly sent the U.S. trade deficit with China to its lowest level since 2017, surging demand for AI-related parts has helped send it higher again so far this year, according to China Customs data accessed through Wind Information. However, think tank CF40 estimated that for the first time this year, AI-related exports fell significantly in August from a year ago. Macquarie's chief China economist Larry Hu noted that the recent performance of the PHLX Semiconductor Index "does not bode well for China's export outlook over the next year."

Asia still accounts for more than 60% of U.S. imports, the same as before 'Liberation Day,' according to Jens Eskelund, president of the European Chamber of Commerce in China. He estimated between half and three-quarters of container traffic from China to Southeast Asia subsequently heads to other destinations. Eskelund said he previously expected China to account for 40% of global container exports in 2030, but that milestone was reached this summer. The economic slowdown has pushed companies in China to compete more fiercely with each other and foreign rivals. The American Chamber of Commerce in Shanghai said three-quarters of members responding to a survey saw Chinese rivals as more advanced, and domestic competition surpassed geopolitical tensions as the top challenge for members for the first time since 2022.

Goldman Sachs' chief China economist Hui Shan said in a Sept. 20 report that because there are always high-flying subsectors for the government to point to, policymakers do not appear to feel much urgency to introduce additional easing measures absent a sharp deterioration in the labor market. She noted house prices have already seen a 30% decline over a six-year timeline, typical of historical large-scale property downturns elsewhere, and that weak labor markets and still-falling rents are likely to prolong the downturn. The number of loss-making companies accounts for 24% of industrial firms in China in 2025. On the earnings front, shares of Cracker Barrel Old Country Store rose after the restaurant chain topped Wall Street's expectations.
