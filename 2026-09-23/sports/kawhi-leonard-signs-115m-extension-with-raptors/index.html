Kawhi Leonard signs two-year, $115M extension with Raptors

Sports desk

Kawhi Leonard has agreed to a two-year, $115 million contract extension with the Toronto Raptors, according to ESPN. The deal comes on the heels of the NBA's lengthy investigation into Leonard's dealings with Aspiration and several other sponsors, which resulted in the Los Angeles Clippers being stripped of five first-round picks and fined $30 million. Leonard himself was fined $700,000 with no suspension or voided contract, and his trade to Toronto, initially agreed to in June, was completed after the investigation concluded.

Leonard's new deal comes in slightly below his maximum salary, the second straight contract for him in which that has been the case. Because Leonard, 35, was just traded, he was limited to a 20% raise in the first year of his new deal. Had he waited six months to extend, he would have been eligible for a true maximum closer to $128 million. In exchange for that slight discount, Leonard received a player option for the 2028-29 season, allowing him to become a free agent in the summer of 2028 when he is 37. The extension was limited to two years because of the over-38 rule, but Leonard will be able to sign a multi-year deal in 2028 if he still commands one.

The Raptors returned to the playoffs last season after a three-year drought and pushed the Cleveland Cavaliers to seven games in the first round despite several serious injuries. By swapping out Brandon Ingram and Gradey Dick for Leonard, they hope to take the next step from plucky playoff team to genuine championship contender. Toronto's last trade for Leonard yielded a championship, and with a young and deep roster around him, the hope is this deal will as well. Financially, the Raptors were looking at around $87 million in luxury tax room going into the 2027-28 season; Leonard will eat up the bulk of that space. They also have internal free agents RJ Barrett and Jamal Shead after this season, and Ja'Kobe Walter becomes extension eligible later, so retaining those players could push Toronto into the tax and aprons.

Leonard's player option could help Toronto manage its roster, as older players routinely lower their annual salary in exchange for extra years of security. The Raptors may solve fitting supporting players beyond the next two seasons by having Leonard opt out and extend again in 2028 for multiple years at a slightly lower figure. Leonard's deal also places him in the $500 million career earnings club, joining Kevin Durant, LeBron James, Stephen Curry, James Harden, Paul George, Joel Embiid, and Donovan Mitchell as the eighth player in NBA history to reach that threshold. Nikola Jokić and Giannis Antetokounmpo are near locks to join soon, while Shai Gilgeous-Alexander ($472 million), Jayson Tatum ($469 million), and Jaylen Brown ($420 million) are close.

The extension drew a reaction from Leonard's former teammate Patrick Beverley, who posted a lengthy Instagram video. Beverley, known as "Mr. 94 Feet" for his defensive pressure, referenced his own history with the Clippers, including claims that owner Steve Ballmer promised to build 94 basketball courts around Los Angeles during contract negotiations but only four were built. Beverley also previously said he took less money to stay with the Clippers before becoming frustrated when Leonard was involved in his contract discussions. In the video, Beverley said, "Yeah man, he cold. Yeah, K, you cold. You cold man. For real man. Real Ninja award. Belongs to you man. For real. You cold." He never directly accused Leonard of taking advantage of the Clippers, but contrasted Leonard's new $115 million deal with the franchise's loss of draft capital, money, and key executives.

The Clippers initially rejected the league's findings before later accepting the penalties, including a one-year suspension for owner Steve Ballmer. The Raptors, meanwhile, face tight financial decisions ahead, with Immanuel Quickley still owed $97.5 million over three years and Jakob Poeltl making $28 million per year, while Collin Murray-Boyles is likely to close games as the team's big man. The deal also represents a market reset for players in their late 30s, as Durant and Leonard both signed for less than their maximum.
