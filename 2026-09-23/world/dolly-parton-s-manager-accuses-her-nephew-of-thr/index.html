Dolly Parton manager seeks restraining order against nephew, citing threats

International & World desk

Dolly Parton's management company has accused her nephew Bryan Seaver of mounting an 'obscene, deliberate and escalating campaign of threats' and is seeking a temporary civil restraining order against him, according to US legal documents filed this week in Nashville.

The company that manages Parton's businesses and properties, She's Alive, which is run by her longtime manager Danny Nozell, alleged in court filings that Seaver threatened to ruin the late singer's empire if the estate did not pay him. The documents quote a series of exchanges in which someone identified by the estate as Seaver said he would become 'the hand of retribution for my entire family' and would set up a podcast dedicated to ruining Dolly's brand partnerships, adding: 'Pay me.'

Seaver, the son of Parton's sister Cassie, owned a company that provided Parton's security for the last 20 years of her life and was handpicked by her to announce her death last month. He was fired last week as head of security by She's Alive, which he called 'unexpected' and 'unexplained.' In his response, published by TMZ, Seaver denied making threats and said text messages and emails quoted in the court application were taken out of context, describing some exchanges as 'two guys having rage talk and crying on the phone together while we were grieving.'

The court papers also claim Seaver used his experience as a military contractor to intimidate employees. In an exchange with one of Parton's lawyers, he allegedly described himself as 'a killer' and said that on the day his aunt died he sold $27m (£20m) in arms and ammunition to the Haitian police, according to an email attached to the complaint. Seaver said he is 'a career soldier and military contractor and do conduct arms deals for foreign governments' but denied making threats, saying he was 'talking frankly in a private way with Danny who I thought was my friend.'

The alleged threats have prompted some employees and lawyers for Parton's trusts and estates to resign, according to the court documents. She's Alive has hired private security for its employees, including at their homes. In a statement last month, Nozell said Parton worked until the day she died on multiple projects and had built 'a project roadmap that will guide us for decades to come.' The estate now faces a public dispute over that legacy.
