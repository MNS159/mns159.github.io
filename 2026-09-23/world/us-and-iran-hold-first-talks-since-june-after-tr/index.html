US and Iran hold first talks since June after Trump threatens 'annihilation'

International & World desk

Top US and Iranian officials held talks at the United Nations in New York on Tuesday, marking the first direct engagement between the two countries since a ceasefire collapsed in June. The meeting, mediated by Qatar, involved Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi and US President Donald Trump's envoy Steve Witkoff. No breakthrough was reported, but Witkoff described the discussions as 'constructive and promising' in a post on X, adding that mediators would continue their work.

The talks come hours after Trump, in an address to the UN General Assembly, threatened to 'annihilate' Iran if the conflict remained unresolved. 'Will a deal be made with Iran that lets them rebuild and create a far greater country than it ever was before?' Trump said. 'Or do I annihilate the Islamic Republic and do it quickly, never giving them a chance to kill and destroy people and countries again? Do I drive them into hell?' Trump framed the war with Iran as a success in preventing Tehran from obtaining a nuclear weapon and defended his strategy of 'peace through strength'—ultimatums rather than compromise.

Iranian state media confirmed the meeting. Foreign ministry spokesman Esmail Baghaei, quoted by state broadcaster IRNA, said negotiations were conducted through a Qatari mediator who conveyed Tehran's conditions, including an end to the war on all fronts and an end to US 'acts of aggression'. Iran has demanded that the US and Israel halt their military campaigns, including attacks against the Iran-backed Shia group Hezbollah in Lebanon.

The conflict escalated sharply on 28 February, when the US and Israel launched air strikes on Iran. Iran responded by attacking Israel and US bases in the Gulf and Arab Gulf states, as well as blocking the Strait of Hormuz—a chokepoint through which about 20% of the world's oil and liquefied natural gas previously flowed. The closure has caused wild price fluctuations and increased pressure on Trump to end the war, particularly with US congressional elections in November.

Since the ceasefire collapsed, both sides have exchanged intermittent fire. Iran has attacked vessels attempting to cross the Strait of Hormuz without authorization, while the US has struck Iranian sites in the strait and Iranian tankers off the country's coast. The US has also maintained a blockade of Iranian ports, severely disrupting Iran's economy. Trump has threatened sanctions against any country continuing to trade with Iran, but there has been no indication of a change in Tehran's position. Meanwhile, Yemen's Houthis—also allied with Iran—have intensified attacks aimed at disrupting oil shipping from Saudi Arabia, widening the conflict.
