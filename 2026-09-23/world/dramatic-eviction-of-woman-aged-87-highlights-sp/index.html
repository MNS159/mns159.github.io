Eviction of 87-year-old tenant in Madrid highlights Spain's housing crisis

International & World desk

Police in Madrid forcibly evicted an 87-year-old woman from the apartment she had lived in for seven decades, in a case that has drawn widespread attention to Spain's acute housing shortage. The tenant, identified only as Maricarmen, had been unable to afford the rent set by Urbagestión, the investment firm that bought the property in 2018. Her father first rented the flat in the Retiro district in 1956, and she continued as a tenant under a rent cap. After purchasing the building, Urbagestión exploited a legal loophole to raise the monthly rent from €500 to €2,650, later lowering it to €1,650, still above her monthly pension of €1,350.

Three previous attempts to evict her had been blocked by the courts, but this time police cordoned off the street, removed protesters and journalists, and used batons against some demonstrators. Several hundred supporters, including celebrities such as actor Carlos Bardem and singer Ana Belén, had gathered outside overnight, chanting “Maricarmen stays” and blocking the building's stairs. In a tearful video posted on social media, Maricarmen thanked supporters for backing her against what she called “this cruel struggle by someone who wants to earn money.” Her lawyer, Beatriz Duro, described the police deployment as “completely disproportionate,” saying it was the first time officers had come to the door of the property.

The case has highlighted the depth of Spain's housing crisis, where a shortage of available homes has driven rental costs sharply higher. In Madrid, rents have more than doubled over the last decade, according to property site Idealista, with a 10% rise in the past year alone. The government of Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez has frequently targeted investment companies that push up housing costs, with Sánchez vowing in January to “protect those who are most exposed to that speculation and that greed.” However, critics argue the government has not built enough new homes and that its rhetoric discourages landlords from putting properties on the rental market.

In June, the UN Committee on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights called for the eviction to be halted and for affordable alternative housing to be offered. The local government said Maricarmen had declined a place in an elderly care home. The central government said Urbagestión had repeatedly refused to negotiate, and the BBC stated it had attempted to contact the company. A last-minute offer by an anonymous donor to cover most of her rent was reportedly rejected by Urbagestión, and the eviction proceeded.

Housing Minister Teresa Rodríguez called the eviction “a very sad day, a very dramatic day” that showed the effects of “unbridled speculation.” She accused the market of putting out “the red carpet for the vultures and the speculators.”
