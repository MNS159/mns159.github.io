Federal Reserve Raises Interest Rates for First Time Since 2023; Markets Rally

Financial & Markets desk · anchored on Reuters

The Federal Reserve on Wednesday raised its benchmark interest rate by a quarter of a percentage point, the first increase since July 2023, setting the federal funds rate at a range of 3.75% to 4.00%. The unanimous vote by the Federal Open Market Committee was driven by persistent inflation, which Chair Kevin Warsh called "too high and has been for too long." The decision marks a reversal from a period of steady rates and signals the possibility of at least one more hike this year, according to the committee's guidance.

Markets initially fell on the news, with the Dow Jones Industrial Average closing down 630 points, or 1.2%, on Wednesday, while the S&P 500 lost 0.4% and the Nasdaq slipped marginally. However, a sharp rebound followed on Thursday: the Dow gained 316 points (0.6%), the S&P 500 rose 1.1%, and the Nasdaq jumped 1.7%. By Friday, futures indicated a mixed open, with S&P 500 futures up about 0.8% and Nasdaq 100 futures gaining nearly 1%, while the Dow was set to open slightly lower. The S&P 500 was on track for a weekly loss of 0.2%, while the Nasdaq remained positive for the week, up 0.7%.

The rate hike was intended to combat a sustained surge in inflation fueled by the Iran war and its disruption of global oil supplies. Prices rose 3.4% year-over-year in August, well above the Fed's 2% target. The average U.S. gasoline price topped $4.30 per gallon, and global crude oil prices stayed above $105 a barrel, about 50% higher than before the war began in February. The near-closure of the Strait of Hormuz and Saudi Arabia's shutdown of a key pipeline further constrained supply. "The committee's unanimous vote shows our resolve to achieve price stability on a timelier basis," Warsh said at a press conference following the decision.

President Donald Trump criticized the move, posting on social media to demand lower rates and later telling ABC News that the Fed acted "only for political reasons." However, he expressed continued confidence in Warsh, his appointee, saying, "I'm relying on Kevin, but he's got a very tough board." Fed Governor Michelle Bowman and Kansas City Fed President Jeffrey Schmid were scheduled to speak on Friday, offering further insight into the committee's thinking.

Despite the rate increase, many investors appeared to look past the prospect of a higher-for-longer rate environment. The rally was led by technology stocks, particularly those tied to artificial intelligence, as Nvidia shares gained 2.5% following CEO Jensen Huang's prediction that chip sales would double next year. "Now that we are past this rate hike, stocks can move on, as uncertainty has faded," said Bob Edwards, chief investment officer at Edwards Asset Management. Brian Levitt, chief global market strategist at Invesco, told CNBC that while the cycle will eventually end, he expects it to continue until "something breaks in the AI trade," which he said is not the current environment. Mark Haefele of UBS Global Wealth Management said his team expects the equity rally to continue over the next six to 12 months, citing strong earnings growth and AI adoption.

The rate hike also affected bond markets: the yield on the 10-year Treasury note, which had climbed above 5% earlier in the week for the first time since 2007, retreated to 4.936% on Thursday before rising again to 4.988% on Friday. Mortgage rates, tied to long-term bond yields, edged closer to 7%, according to mortgage advisor Scott Goodrich. Locally, the Santa Cruz County housing market remained steady in August, with median prices holding around $1.35 million, though analysts warned that higher borrowing costs could weigh on prospective buyers. In corporate news, Generac surged 18.3% on news of an up-to-$8 billion supply deal with Amazon to provide backup generators for data centers through 2033, making it the best-performing stock in the S&P 500 on Thursday.
