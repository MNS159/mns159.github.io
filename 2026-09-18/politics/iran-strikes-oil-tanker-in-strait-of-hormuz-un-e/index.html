Iran strikes oil tanker in Strait of Hormuz; UN experts allege US war crimes in Iran

Politics desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum
✓ Facts
  • Iran's IRGC said it attacked a Togo-flagged oil tanker, the Trend, in the Strait of Hormuz after it attempted to "illegally pass" through the waterway.
  • The US-Iran war began on 28 February when the US and Israel launched joint attacks on Iran, and as of the reporting has continued for roughly seven months.
  • The US State Department confirmed it granted visas for a core Iranian delegation—including President Masoud Pezeshkian and Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi—to attend the UN General Assembly in New York, subject to travel restrictions and bans on purchasing luxury and certain American goods.
  • A UN Independent International Fact-Finding Mission on Iran reported reasonable grounds to believe the US committed war crimes in two 28 February strikes: one on a primary school in Minab (at least 156 killed, including 120 children) and one on a sports complex in Lamerd (at least 21 killed, including 7 children).
  • The US has denied striking Lamerd and disputed the weapon type identified there, said it is investigating the Minab strike, and a State Department official said the US does not give credibility to the fact-finding mission's report.
  • The same UN report found Iranian authorities committed crimes against humanity during a crackdown on anti-government protests in December 2025–January 2026, citing mass killings, torture, and at least 29 executions; Iran has not commented on these specific findings.
  • A Fox News poll found 71% of voters (95% of Democrats, 83% of independents, 43% of Republicans) believe the Trump administration lacks a clear strategy to end the war, 60% say the military action against Iran was the wrong decision, and Trump's overall approval stands at 39%, with 37% approval on foreign policy.
○ Analysis

This story combines three linked threads: an active military conflict now in its seventh month, continuing diplomatic engagement despite that conflict, and a new UN report accusing both sides of serious international law violations. The Strait of Hormuz tanker strike illustrates how the war has expanded beyond direct US-Iran-Israel exchanges into disruption of global shipping and energy markets. Simultaneously, Washington's decision to admit an Iranian delegation to the UN General Assembly—under tight restrictions—shows that formal diplomatic channels remain open even as fighting continues. The UN fact-finding mission's report adds a legal and moral dimension, alleging the US carried out indiscriminate strikes killing civilians in Iran while separately finding Iranian security forces committed crimes against humanity during a domestic protest crackdown; both governments dispute or have not accepted parts of these findings, and independent verification of casualty figures remains incomplete.

Steelman

Both the US and Iran now face credible, UN-documented allegations of causing large-scale civilian harm—Washington through disputed missile strikes, Tehran through a violent crackdown on protesters—meaning neither government's public narrative of restraint or self-defense is uncontested, even as diplomacy and warfare proceed in parallel.

Implications

With the UN General Assembly set to host speeches from Iranian and other leaders this week, expect renewed international scrutiny of the war crimes allegations against both the US and Iran, potential further disruption to Gulf shipping and oil markets from strikes like the Hormuz tanker attack, and continued domestic political pressure on the Trump administration as public support for the war erodes ahead of any diplomatic breakthrough.
