Deadly bombing at Pakistan police mosque kills at least 16

International & World desk

At least 16 people, including five police officers, were killed and more than 56 injured when a vehicle loaded with explosives rammed into a mosque at a police headquarters in Kohat, in Pakistan's northwestern Khyber Pakhtunkhwa province, on Friday. The attack occurred as worshippers gathered for Friday prayers, and was followed by a gun battle between security forces and assailants, according to senior police officials.

Provincial police chief Zulfikar Hameed confirmed the bombing was a suicide attack. After the initial explosion, gunmen attempted to enter the police compound and exchanged fire with officers. One assailant has reportedly been killed, and a rescue operation is under way. The mosque was badly damaged and the police station's outer wall collapsed; residents reported hearing the blast from a distance.

Hospital officials said at least 15 bodies and more than 50 injured were initially taken to hospital, with several in critical condition. Bilal Faizi, a senior rescue official, confirmed that five police officers and 10 civilians were among the dead, according to the AP news agency. Most of the wounded were worshippers.

There was no immediate claim of responsibility for the attack. However, both a regional affiliate of the ISIL (ISIS) armed group and the Pakistan Taliban (TTP) are active in the region and regularly target security forces. The attack came a day after a roadside bomb struck a security forces convoy in the nearby Hangu district, killing six soldiers. Government troops conducted raids on Thursday and killed 10 fighters believed to be TTP members.

Militancy in Pakistan's border areas has risen sharply in recent months, targeting the military and police. Islamabad accuses the Taliban government in Afghanistan of allowing groups that orchestrate attacks to operate from Afghan territory. Afghanistan's Taliban government denies the accusation and says militancy is Pakistan's domestic problem. The tension has led to intermittent cross-border attacks.

Pakistan's President Asif Ali Zardari expressed his best wishes for the swift recovery of those injured. Prime Minister Muhammad Shehbaz Sharif condemned the blast and directed authorities to accelerate rescue operations. The Ministry of Interior said the 'cowardly actions' of the attackers could not weaken the 'nation's firm resolve.'
