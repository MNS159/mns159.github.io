Russia-Ukraine War

Denmark speeds up Ukraine aid after Russian warship fires flares at helicopter; Macron says Russia targeted France with hybrid attacks

International & World desk · anchored on Reuters
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

Denmark is accelerating a 1.8 billion Danish crown ($276 million) aid package for Ukraine, primarily for air defenses, after a Russian frigate fired flares at a Danish military helicopter in international waters, the Danish government announced Friday. The incident occurred Monday when the helicopter was attempting to photograph the Russian ship; Denmark said the flares were fired without warning and narrowly missed the aircraft. Russia accused Denmark of carrying out dangerous maneuvers near its vessel.

“If the Russians think they can rattle us… then our response is to ramp up our support for Ukraine faster than we had intended,” Defense Minister Jeppe Bruus told reporters after a meeting with parliamentarians. Foreign Minister Lars Lokke Rasmussen added, “If anyone thinks they can pressure us to hold back on our support for Ukraine, they are mistaken.” Denmark has been one of Ukraine’s most vocal supporters since Russia’s full-scale invasion in 2022 and is a leading provider of financial aid on a per-capita basis.

Separately, French President Emmanuel Macron said Russia has targeted France with hybrid attacks, warning of a changing threat. The remarks come as Russia adapts a training missile to lead its ballistic onslaught of Ukraine, according to a Reuters report.

The incidents underscore escalating tensions between Russia and NATO as the war in Ukraine continues into its third year. The Danish government’s decision to speed up aid directly links the flare incident to a policy response, framing the package as a rebuttal to perceived Russian intimidation. The new support, worth an estimated 1.8 billion Danish crowns ($276 million), will be used for air defenses, Denmark said.
