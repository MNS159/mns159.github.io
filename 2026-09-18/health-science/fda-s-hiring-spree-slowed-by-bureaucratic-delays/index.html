FDA hiring push slowed by bureaucratic delays stemming from DOGE cuts

Health & Science desk

WASHINGTON — The U.S. Food and Drug Administration is seeking to hire more than 2,000 workers to rebuild after the loss of 3,500 employees in April 2025, when Elon Musk’s U.S. DOGE Service cut the agency’s workforce. However, the hiring effort is running into significant bureaucratic delays, according to STAT reports.

The same DOGE cuts that eliminated thousands of jobs have created bottlenecks and confusion in the hiring process, leaving prospective new employees stuck in pre-onboarding limbo. Routine administrative tasks, such as purchase approvals and travel payments, have also fallen by the wayside, further complicating the agency’s rebuilding effort.

FDA leaders have publicly expressed optimism about the hiring push. Karim Mikhail, director of the FDA’s Center for Biologics Evaluation and Research, told drug manufacturers at a conference Monday, “We’re very actively hiring.” The agency has also posted group photos of new employees on social media with messages such as “Welcome to FDA!”

The hiring delays come just over a year after the DOGE cuts triggered a broader staff exodus. Musk is no longer involved with the agency, and the FDA is now focused on adding workers to address the shortfall. The bureaucratic hurdles, however, threaten to slow the recovery of an agency critical to drug and medical product oversight.

Sources: STAT
