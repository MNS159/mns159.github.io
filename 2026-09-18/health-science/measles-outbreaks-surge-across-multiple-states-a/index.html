Measles outbreaks surge across multiple states as exposures reported

Health & Science desk · anchored on Reuters

Health officials in multiple states are reporting new measles exposures and surging case counts, with the U.S. tallying at least 3,294 confirmed cases this year and Pennsylvania recording its fourth measles-related death. In Philadelphia, the Department of Public Health warned of possible exposure on Sept. 11 at several locations inside Children’s Hospital of Philadelphia, including the NICU West, its family lounge, the Atrium, and the main cafeteria between 9:30 a.m. and 4:15 p.m. In New York, the Monroe County Department of Public Health alerted the public to potential exposures at the Frederick Douglass Greater Rochester International Airport on Sept. 8 from 3:20 to 6 p.m. and Sept. 13–14 from 11:20 p.m. to 2 a.m.

Ohio’s measles cases spiked 327%, according to The Newark Advocate. The exposures in Rochester followed earlier incidents at the city’s train station and Strong Memorial Hospital’s emergency department, where nearly 100 people were potentially exposed. The Philadelphia health department urged anyone present during the exposure times to review vaccination status and monitor for symptoms, noting that measles is a highly contagious airborne virus that can linger for up to two hours after an infected person leaves an area.

The U.S. outbreak comes as Bangladesh faces its deadliest measles outbreak since 2005, with at least 175,198 suspected cases and 1,044 confirmed and suspected deaths between March 15 and Sept. 16, according to the country’s health directorate. Experts cited in Scientific American say the numbers are likely underestimates. Measles vaccination rates in Bangladesh fell to 86% in 2025, down from around 97% for the first dose in 2019, after disruptions from the COVID pandemic, political unrest, and funding cuts. “If there is a break in the regular vaccination process, they will just attack,” said Sakil Faizullah, a health communications researcher at Texas A&M University.

Public health experts emphasize that measles requires population vaccination rates of 95% or higher to achieve herd immunity. The U.S. has seen outbreaks in undervaccinated communities in Texas, South Carolina, and Pennsylvania. “We don’t want hundreds of deaths to get people involved,” said Walter Orenstein, an epidemiologist and measles expert. “Measles vaccines are very safe, very effective, and getting vaccinated not only helps you but helps your community.” In Gaines County, Texas, the kindergarten vaccination rate was 82% compared with about 93% nationally.

Amid the surge, Pennsylvania has requested assistance from the U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, according to an exclusive report by Reuters, set against a dispute involving Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. In the U.S., the reasons for immunity gaps differ from Bangladesh, where supply and health system disruption are key, compared with localized undervaccination and vaccine hesitancy. “I think you have to compare a country to itself,” said Jennifer Nuzzo, an epidemiologist and director of the Pandemic Center at Brown University. “The diagnosis is that the U.S. is very sick in a way that we haven’t been in 30 years.”
