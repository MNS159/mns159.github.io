FBI says accused OpenAI spy had folder labeled 'Saudi Gov Docs'

Tech / AI desk

On Monday, a court granted the US government's request to deny release of a former OpenAI engineer accused of bugging a top-secret conference room to steal the frontier lab's secrets. The engineer, Abdulrahman Alfozan, a Saudi national and US permanent legal resident, must continue to be detained while the US makes its case that he brazenly spied on OpenAI, either for his own personal business gain or to benefit the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia.

In a court filing Sunday, US attorney Craig Missakian argued that evidence showed Alfozan had been chatting with a member of the Saudi royal family on WhatsApp while pursuing opportunities to permanently move back to Saudi Arabia during the country's biggest AI push yet. Shortly before his arrest, the FBI found he accessed a folder called 'Saudi Gov Docs' on his iPhone, where he also allegedly stored photos of a second bug he planted in the offices.

The FBI arrested Alfozan days before he could board a plane, Missakian said. The US attorney noted that Alfozan had already booked a one-way ticket for him, his wife, and their baby to Riyadh, Saudi Arabia's capital, allegedly 'paid for by the Saudi government.' Following his arrest, his wife and son booked a sooner flight and immediately left the US without him. The US found that 'a member of the Saudi military booked and paid for yet another one-way ticket for the defendant's wife and son, this one business class to Doha, Qatar.'

The government argued that Alfozan is a flight risk with seemingly endless support from the Saudi government. After receiving 'significant compensation from a decade of high-tech employment in the Bay Area,' Alfozan has 'ample resources' to flee to Saudi Arabia, the government alleged. He also has 'at least two bank accounts in Saudi Arabia,' the government said.

To support their request to detain Alfozan, the government pointed to a case where former Twitter employees were charged with spying for the Saudi government. Charges are still pending against one of the ex-Twitter staffers, Ali Alzabarah, the US said, partly because 'a Saudi foreign official' helped 'coordinate a next-day flight to Saudi Arabia' shortly after Twitter confronted him.
