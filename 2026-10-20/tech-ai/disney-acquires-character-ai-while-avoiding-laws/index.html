Disney acquires Character.AI’s staff and tech via bankruptcy, leaving legal liabilities behind

Tech / AI desk

Disney has found a way to acquire most of chatbot startup Character.AI without taking on its legal baggage, according to court filings tied to the company’s bankruptcy this week. The deal lets Disney hire most of Character.AI’s staff—including former CEO Karandeep Anand—and potentially acquire most of its technology, while leaving the beleaguered startup’s lawsuits and regulatory obligations with the entity that remains.

Character.AI’s bankruptcy declaration, which some initially read as a warning sign for the chatbot industry, was actually a key part of the arrangement. In a declaration filed in the bankruptcy case, the startup’s newly appointed CEO said the goal was to reach a “consensual resolution” with personal injury claimants and a group of states investigating the company, while maximizing the value of Character.AI’s assets for shareholders.

Disney’s structure appears designed so that whatever is left of Character.AI retains responsibility for the legal battles, some of which are still ongoing. In recent years, the company has faced multiple private lawsuits over the safety of its chatbots, as well as investigations from regulators around the world.

The deal represents a new twist on what some have dubbed a “reverse acquihire,” a process in which a buyer carefully orchestrates a succession of deals with a target company to hire key employees and obtain expansive license to use and build on the target’s unique technology. Stripped of its employees, the target becomes a husk of itself.

Companies often take the position that reverse acquihires are not actual mergers and therefore do not need to be notified to federal antitrust regulators as the law would otherwise require. Such arrangements have become more common in the AI industry in recent years, drawing the ire of Congress.
