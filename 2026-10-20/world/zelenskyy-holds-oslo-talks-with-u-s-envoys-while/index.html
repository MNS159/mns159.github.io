Ukraine Diplomacy

Zelenskyy holds Oslo talks with U.S. envoys while Rubio denies withholding intelligence

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy met Tuesday in Oslo with Norwegian Prime Minister Jonas Gahr Støre and Finnish President Alexander Stubb, ahead of planned talks with U.S. special envoy Steve Witkoff. Trump’s son-in-law Jared Kushner participated via video link because of illness. Støre said Zelenskyy had requested the meeting with the U.S. and European allies “at a critical time for Ukraine,” and that the aim was to steer relations toward “a more constructive path.”

Ahead of the talks, U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio denied accusations that Washington was deliberately withholding intelligence from Kyiv, calling such claims “scandalous” and “outrageous.” Rubio spoke with Zelenskyy, who later described the conversation as “constructive” and said they discussed the need for U.S. early warnings, Patriot missile interceptors and SpaceX’s Starlink satellite internet network. Zelenskyy told U.S. lawmakers Monday he believed “some political decision has been made to slow down … support for us,” and said Ukraine had nearly run out of American-made Patriot interceptors needed to stop Russian ballistic missiles. Rubio, speaking in India, acknowledged that Patriot interceptors may not yet have arrived in Ukraine but said nothing had been denied intentionally.

The talks came as Russia unleashed fresh missile and drone strikes across Ukraine, killing at least seven people Tuesday, according to Ukrainian officials. In the Obukhiv district, three people were killed and six wounded; in the Bucha district, one woman was killed and 21 people wounded, eight of them children. A Russian drone struck the Kyiv-Odesa highway, killing three and injuring four. Ukraine’s largest private energy company, DTEK, said three of its facilities in the Odesa region were hit, leaving more than 32,000 families without electricity. In Russia’s Tambov Oblast, a fire broke out at an oil pumping station after an overnight Ukrainian drone attack, the regional governor said.

Relations between Zelenskyy and President Donald Trump have soured sharply in recent weeks. Trump has said it is time for Ukraine to get a new president and that “somebody else” should negotiate on Kyiv’s behalf. Vice-President JD Vance said in a TV interview that Ukraine was “certainly a partner in some ways” but that the U.S. and Ukraine have “divergent interests,” adding that Kyiv should “stop bombing Russian energy.” Trump recently waived sanctions on Russian diesel exports after announcing a deal with Moscow to supply more than 300,000 tons of diesel to the U.S. and other markets. Zelenskyy called the move “a happy birthday present for Putin” and warned it would embolden him.

European allies have expressed concern over the shift in U.S. policy. German Chancellor Friedrich Merz said Monday there were “mixed pictures and messages” on the war and that he was trying to convince the U.S. administration “that this is something we have to resolve jointly.” He noted that the U.S. had not been paying for Ukraine’s defence since the start of 2025 and that Europe was shouldering the burden. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said he hoped a meeting with Witkoff could be organized soon, adding that “we greatly appreciate the mediation efforts of the American negotiators.” It was not immediately clear whether Witkoff would travel to Russia after Oslo.
