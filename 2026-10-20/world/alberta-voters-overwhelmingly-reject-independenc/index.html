Alberta voters overwhelmingly reject independence referendum, resoundingly choose to remain in Canada

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts slightly center-left

Voters in Alberta have decisively rejected a proposal to begin the legal process toward independence, with preliminary results showing roughly 76 percent support for remaining in Canada. With more than 1.5 million votes counted, unofficial results from Elections Alberta indicated close to 24 percent favored starting the steps toward separation, according to projections by CBC and CTV. The nonbinding referendum asked residents whether they wanted to stay in Canada or begin legal steps for a future binding vote on secession.

The vote, the first independence referendum held outside Quebec, was driven largely by economic grievances in the oil-rich province. Separatists argued that Alberta contributes disproportionately to the federal purse and has been unfairly targeted by energy and environmental policies. The push was also encouraged by U.S. President Donald Trump, who has repeatedly suggested making Canada the 51st state, and by U.S. Republican operatives who coached separatist leaders. Alberta Premier Danielle Smith, who opposes independence, was forced to call the vote by a vocal separatist wing within her United Conservative Party.

Prime Minister Mark Carney, who grew up in Edmonton, welcomed the outcome. 'Albertans made a clear choice. They chose unity, to build together, and to take care of each other,' he said in a statement. 'Above all, Albertans chose Canada.' Smith posted on social media: 'Albertans have voted for a stronger Alberta in a united Canada.' She is scheduled to address the province on Tuesday evening. Separatist leader Keith Wilson noted a silver lining, saying, 'It is fair to say that at least a quarter of Albertans feel that Alberta needs to be treated better.'

Carney spent much of the past week campaigning for unity, warning that a vote to 'send a message' would lead to 'years of deep uncertainty and lost opportunities.' Conservative leader Pierre Poilievre, whose parliamentary seat is in Alberta, told supporters that Albertans were not 'quitters' and that 'Canada is more than a government; it is a people, a flag, a land, a history and a promise.' Delays at roughly 100 polling stations pushed results back more than two hours, and Elections Alberta said results for nine other ballot questions on issues such as health care and immigration could take days to count.

The result is a significant setback for the separatist movement and a win for Carney as he seeks to maintain national unity amid an ongoing trade war with the United States. Smith said she hoped holding the vote would settle the question of breaking away for good, but separatist leaders indicated they would continue their push. Carney said the result would allow Canadians to 'move forward together with renewed purpose and high ambition' and accelerate efforts to diversify trade and develop infrastructure, with Alberta at the centre of Canada's energy ambitions.
