Drone attack near market kills 80 in Sudan; UN condemns deadly wave of strikes

International & World desk

A drone strike near a market in the southern Sudanese town of al-Dibab in West Kordofan state killed at least 80 people on Sunday evening, mostly commuters gathering to travel home after the market day ended, according to witnesses and medical sources. The attack, one of the deadliest single strikes since the war began in April 2023, struck a transport hub twice, 10 minutes apart, said witness Ahmed Wadi, who helped bury 50 bodies including his brother Mohamed. A medical source said 30 of the 47 people transported to the nearest town, al-Mujlad, died from their injuries, and that another drone strike in al-Mujlad injured four people.

The strike came amid an escalation in the use of drones in the conflict between the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF), led by Gen Abdel Fattah al-Burhan, and the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces (RSF), led by Mohamed Hamdan Dagalo. The RSF-declared administration, known as Tasis, which controls all of West Kordofan, accused the army of carrying out the attack. Between January and April, drone strikes accounted for at least 880 civilian deaths, more than 80% of all conflict-related civilian deaths in Sudan. After drone strikes on a busy livestock market in Nyala, the capital of South Darfur state, on Friday, the SAF said they had killed RSF fighters, but the RSF accused the military of killing 30 civilians.

The United Nations condemned the wave of deadly drone strikes across Sudan. UN Spokesperson Stéphane Dujarric told journalists in New York: "We are alarmed by the continued deadly drone strikes across the country that are killing civilians, including children, and that are disrupting essential services." He said that on Monday alone, drone strikes injured at least 19 people in a busy market in Abyei and targeted several locations in Khartoum, including near the airport, where one civilian was reportedly killed and flights were cancelled. Over the weekend, drone attacks reportedly killed at least 19 people, including 10 children, across West Darfur, South Darfur and North Kordofan states.

The UN also expressed grave concern over the targeting of aid workers. Since the conflict began, 25 Sudanese Red Crescent volunteers have reportedly been killed while on duty. Last week, a Red Crescent volunteer providing first aid at a health centre in Um Darfa, Blue Nile State, was killed following a drone attack on the facility. "The targeting of those who put their own lives at risk to help others in humanitarian settings is particularly heinous," Dujarric said, reiterating that civilians and civilian infrastructure must be protected under international humanitarian law.

The conflict, which erupted when former allies the SAF and RSF turned on each other, has killed hundreds of thousands of people and displaced many more, fuelled by foreign powers supporting each side. Last week, UN political affairs chief Rosemary DiCarlo warned the Security Council that the situation was deteriorating "with no breakthrough on the horizon," saying the warring parties "seem to believe that they can make greater gains on the battlefield and that continued external support will allow them to pursue their objectives by force." She urged external actors to stop fuelling the violence and drone usage, and called on the parties to return to negotiations.

The escalating violence comes as 20 million people face acute hunger, with a rising risk of famine in parts of Darfur and Greater Kordofan. Despite immense challenges, the UN and its partners continue to deliver aid, recently rehabilitating a water facility in Zalingei, Central Darfur, restoring safe water for some 2,500 people and providing water, sanitation and hygiene support to more than 2,000 families. Dujarric stressed the need for more funding, noting that Sudan's humanitarian response plan is just over 43% funded.
