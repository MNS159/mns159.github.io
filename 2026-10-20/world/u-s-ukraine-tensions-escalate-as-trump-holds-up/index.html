U.S.-Ukraine Tensions Escalate as Trump Holds Up Missile Aid and Shifts Toward Russia

International & World desk · anchored on Reuters
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts slightly center-left

President Donald Trump escalated his public criticism of Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy this week while his administration held up delivery of Patriot interceptor missiles that Ukraine says it urgently needs to defend against daily Russian strikes. In an interview published Tuesday, Zelenskyy told Italian newspaper Corriere della Sera that the U.S. was delaying the transfer of interceptors already purchased by European nations for Ukraine, and that Ukraine had nearly run out of the U.S.-made Patriots used to take down Russian ballistic missiles. “If you cannot rein Putin in, then give us interceptor missiles so I can protect our energy infrastructure,” Zelenskyy said, referring to Russian President Vladimir Putin. Secretary of State Marco Rubio acknowledged Tuesday that a batch of interceptors ordered for Ukraine may not have been delivered, but denied there was a political motive, saying “every other country in the world” also wants Patriots and that the administration faces a balance of obligations.

The tensions came as Ukraine and Russia exchanged strikes over the weekend. Ukrainian drones struck strategic targets in and around St. Petersburg, including a key port oil terminal and a refinery, causing large fires—the largest attack on the city since Russia launched its full-scale invasion in 2022, according to NPR. Russia's defense ministry said it intercepted 1,000 drones over various regions that night. Russian missile strikes on Ukraine over the same period killed at least 11 people, and at least four people were killed in Kyiv in attacks Tuesday, according to Kyiv officials. Ukraine's air force said it received no early warning of the strikes from U.S. partners, though Rubio denied there had been any change in intelligence sharing. Zelenskyy said the Starshield military satellite system, vital for intercepting Russian drones, had been restricted and needed to be restored; a senior Pentagon official told CBS News there had been “no change to Department of War policy regarding intelligence sharing or any capabilities we are enabling Europeans to provide to Ukraine.”

Trump has made a series of moves that critics say align Washington with Moscow. On Friday, he exempted Russia from a congressionally approved sanctions package that would have placed steep tariffs on Russian energy sales and increased sanctions on senior Kremlin officials, including Putin. Trump justified the waiver by saying the legislation was not in the national interest and that more sanctions would drive Russia away from a peace deal. He also struck a deal with Putin to import Russian diesel, a move aimed at lowering U.S. gas prices ahead of midterm elections. Trump has publicly blamed Zelenskyy for continuing the war, saying on Monday that “Zelenskyy doesn't know what he's talking about” and that “it's time for somebody else to negotiate on behalf of Ukraine.” He has never publicly acknowledged that Russia started the war and has at times blamed Zelenskyy for the full-scale invasion ordered by Putin.

Zelenskyy met Tuesday in Oslo with U.S. envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner, with the latter joining remotely, along with Norwegian Prime Minister Jonas Gahr Store and Finnish President Alexander Stubb. Store said the talks aimed to put “diplomatic efforts on a more constructive path.” Axios reported that Witkoff would travel from Oslo to Russia to meet Putin; the White House did not immediately confirm. Meanwhile, Trump said he was ready to meet Putin and Chinese President Xi Jinping in China next month for talks that could include discussion of the Ukraine war. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov confirmed “the topic of such a hypothetical has indeed been mentioned.” Ukraine's Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha warned that a meeting on Ukraine's fate without Kyiv at the table would “only encourage Putin to continue this war.”

Rubio, speaking from a visit to Uzbekistan on Monday, insisted that Trump has not deliberately harmed Ukraine and that the president is frustrated by accusations to the contrary. “There has not been a deliberate political directive to go out and harm Ukraine,” Rubio told reporters. He urged Zelenskyy to focus on ending the conflict. Zelenskyy, in his newspaper interview, said Ukraine had built its relationship with Washington “on honesty, fairness, and, most importantly, transparency,” and that when “you lie while your people are at war, it is very painful.” He said he wanted to maintain open and pragmatic relations with the U.S., and appealed directly: “Let's believe that America really wants to, but cannot. Then, as a partner, I am willing to meet President Trump halfway: if they strike us and we cannot strike back—that is neither honest nor fair—but if this is what you need, if you cannot rein Putin in, then give us interceptor missiles so I can protect our energy infrastructure.”
