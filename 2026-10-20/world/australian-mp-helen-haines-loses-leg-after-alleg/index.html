Australian MP Helen Haines loses leg after alleged car attack; suspect charged

International & World desk

Australian independent MP Helen Haines lost one of her legs and remains in critical condition after she was struck by a car in an alleged targeted attack outside her electorate office in Wangaratta, Victoria, on Monday. The 65-year-old member for Indi was airlifted to Melbourne's Alfred Hospital for emergency surgery, where surgeons are also fighting to save her other leg. A 43-year-old man, identified by local media as environmental activist Brendan Nugent, was arrested at the scene and charged Tuesday night with dangerous driving causing serious injury, intentionally causing serious injury, and the federal offense of causing harm to a Commonwealth public official. He is expected to appear at Wangaratta Magistrates Court on Wednesday.

Victoria Police said they will allege the attack was targeted. According to witnesses cited by Home Affairs Minister Tony Burke, the driver completed a U-turn before ramming Haines against a vehicle, then reversed and struck her a second time. Haines was loading or unloading items from the trunk of her car at the time, Prime Minister Anthony Albanese said. Police confirmed a highway patrol unit was on scene within eight seconds of the call. They searched a property in Tarrawingee, about 12 kilometers (7.5 miles) from Wangaratta, and stated the incident appears isolated with no other known threats.

Local barber Ricky Jefferies, whose shop is next to Haines' office, ran to intervene after hearing the crash. He pulled the driver from the vehicle and restrained him until police arrived. Jefferies said he removed a knife from the driver's pocket and threw it away, noting the man did not pull it out. 'I had no choice but to take him out of the car because it was the right and safest thing to do,' Jefferies told reporters. He downplayed his role, saying others who helped 'didn't even check for danger' and 'deserve the credit.' Wangaratta Mayor Irene Grant praised Jefferies as 'extraordinarily modest about the very brave action that he took.'

The suspect's father, Rod Nugent, and his partner, Christine Cunningham, issued a statement saying Haines had been their friend 'for more than 20 years' and described the attack as 'devastating and unfathomable.' They promised to assist Victoria Police 'in any way we can.' Haines, a former nurse and midwife, was first elected to federal parliament in 2019 and has been a prominent crossbench member, known for her role in establishing the National Anti-Corruption Commission and campaigning for rural health services, telecommunications access, and against misuse of taxpayer funds.

Prime Minister Anthony Albanese said early investigations suggest the attack was deliberate, adding, 'This wasn't an accident.' He noted a rise in political violence, stating, 'The temperature has increased in recent times.' Home Affairs Minister Tony Burke reported that there were 1,067 reports of harassment or threats against high office holders in Australia in the last financial year, up from 951 the year before. 'There is a direct line between the temperature of debate and things willing people will say, and then when people are willing to take action,' Burke said. A vigil was held Tuesday at Holy Trinity Cathedral in Wangaratta, where locals lit candles and left bouquets on the main street outside Haines' office.
