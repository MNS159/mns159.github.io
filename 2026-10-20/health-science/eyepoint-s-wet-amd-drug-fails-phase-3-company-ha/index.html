EyePoint's wet AMD drug misses Phase 3 goal; company halves workforce, stock sinks 82%

Health & Science desk

EyePoint, Inc. announced Oct. 19, 2026, that DURAVYU (vorolanib intravitreal insert) failed to meet the primary endpoint in LUCIA, the second pivotal Phase 3 clinical trial testing the treatment for wet age-related macular degeneration (wet AMD). The miss sent the company's shares down 82%, according to Barron's, and prompted EyePoint to halve its workforce, as reported by Clinical Trials Arena.

In LUCIA, DURAVYU did not achieve statistical non-inferiority in mean change in best corrected visual acuity (BCVA) versus an on-label 2 mg aflibercept control, despite demonstrating visual improvement. The trial did meet key secondary endpoints, including a superior 38% reduction in treatment burden (nominal p-value <0.0001), with 52% of eyes supplement-free and 71% receiving zero or one supplement up to Week 56. EyePoint also reported strong anatomic control and a continued favorable safety profile with redosing, results consistent with the first Phase 3 trial, LUGANO.

The company said it will complete additional analyses of the LUCIA and pooled Phase 3 datasets to further assess next steps for DURAVYU as a potential wet AMD treatment. 'We are disappointed that the LUCIA trial did not meet the primary endpoint given the promising scientific rationale of DURAVYU and its potential to improve outcomes for patients with wet AMD,' said Jay S. Duker, M.D., President and Chief Executive Officer of EyePoint, who thanked the patients, caregivers, clinicians and study teams involved.

Duker emphasized that both LUCIA and LUGANO demonstrated DURAVYU's ability to control fluid and reduce treatment burden for wet AMD patients, and said those findings support the drug's therapeutic potential in diabetic macular edema (DME). Noting the multifactorial and inflammatory nature of DME and compelling results from the Phase 2 VERONA trial, he said the company believes DURAVYU is well positioned to potentially deliver stable vision with fewer injections than standard of care. EyePoint's DME program remains on track, with pivotal Phase 3 topline data from two identical clinical trials anticipated in Q4 2027.

The wet AMD setback led EyePoint to end the Phase III trial early and halve its workforce, according to Clinical Trials Arena. Barron's, which reported the 82% stock decline, characterized the success of the company's strategy shift as 'pretty debatable.' EyePoint held a conference call to discuss the results at 8:00 a.m. EDT on the day of the announcement.
