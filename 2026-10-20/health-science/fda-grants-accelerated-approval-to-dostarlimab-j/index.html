FDA grants accelerated approval to dostarlimab (Jemperli) for MSI-H/dMMR rectal cancer

Health & Science desk

The FDA on Monday granted accelerated approval to GSK's dostarlimab-gxly (Jemperli) as monotherapy for adults with previously untreated stage II or III locally advanced rectal cancer that is microsatellite instability-high (MSI-H) or mismatch repair deficient (dMMR), making it the first immunotherapy approved for this specific patient population. The approval was announced in an FDA press release dated October 19, 2026, and marks the 11th drug reviewed under the Commissioner's National Priority Voucher pilot program. The FDA completed the review nearly four months ahead of schedule, according to acting FDA Commissioner Kyle Diamantas, JD.

The decision is supported by interim results from the phase 2 AZUR-1 trial (NCT05723562), which enrolled 154 patients with previously untreated dMMR/MSI-H locally advanced rectal cancer. Patients received up to nine doses of dostarlimab every three weeks over six months. The trial met its primary endpoint at an interim analysis: 47% of 116 evaluable patients achieved a clinical complete response at 12 months (CCR12), as measured by tumor imaging and confirmed by independent central review. Per investigator analysis, a key secondary endpoint, 68% of 144 evaluable patients achieved CCR12. In an exploratory analysis, an additional 12 patients (8%) were found to have a pathological complete response, meaning their surgically resected tissue showed no sign of cancer. With a median follow-up of more than two years, almost three-fourths of patients had not needed surgery, chemotherapy, or radiotherapy, and none of the responders have seen their disease return, according to investigators and GSK.

The approval also draws on an earlier phase 2 trial conducted at Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center led by Andrea Cercek, MD, which demonstrated a meaningful and sustained clinical complete response at 12 months, with 93% of patients maintaining that cancer-free status for at least 12 months. That small study initially captured public attention in 2022 when every patient experienced a complete response. MSI-H/dMMR tumors are known as 'hot' tumors that are more visible to the immune system and therefore more amenable to immunotherapies like dostarlimab, which targets PD-1 to block a signal cancer cells use to evade immune attack. Published estimates suggest approximately 3% of locally advanced rectal cancers carry these genomic features, and MSI-H/dMMR tumors constitute about 5% to 10% of all rectal cancers, of which there have been an estimated 50,000 new cases across all stages in the U.S. in 2026.

Cercek noted during a press briefing ahead of the approval that traditional standard of care for locally advanced rectal cancer—often chemotherapy, radiation, and surgery—can affect bowel, urinary, and sexual function, as well as fertility, with about 30% of patients needing a permanent colostomy. 'With just Jemperli alone, we were able to offer the majority of patients complete normal organ preservation, normal pelvic function, which was really unheard of in standard of care in rectal cancer,' she said. She added that so far, five babies have been born to patients from the initial academic study, including four to two women who '100% would not have been able to have children due to the location of their tumor' and the fact that pelvic radiation therapy can induce menopause.

Because the approval is accelerated and based on interim results, GSK is required to confirm dostarlimab's benefit in a confirmatory trial. The phase 3 Azur-2 study is evaluating dostarlimab used before and after surgery against post-surgical chemo or observation in stage 3, MSI-H/dMMR resectable colon cancer, and is expected to read out in 2028, said Hesham Abdullah, MD, GSK's global head of oncology R&D. Detailed data from Azur-1 are slated for presentation at the European Society for Medical Oncology annual meeting. The approval comes days after the FDA cleared Roche's Tecentriq, used alongside chemo, as a post-surgical treatment for patients with stage 3 dMMR colon cancer, after that regimen improved disease-free survival by 50% compared with chemo alone.

Dostarlimab can cause the immune system to attack healthy organs and tissues, and these reactions can be severe or fatal. The most common adverse events, reported in at least 15% of patients treated for this type of rectal cancer, were fatigue, rash, diarrhea, and itching. 'Standard treatment for rectal cancer has major life-changing effects. For certain types of rectal cancer, this approval represents a groundbreaking option that avoids surgery, radiation, and chemotherapy. This represents a significant benefit for patients and their families,' said Angelo de Claro, MD, director of the FDA's Oncology Center of Excellence, in the FDA press release. 'The FDA is committed to closing the gap between scientific progress and patient access, and that commitment has to show up not just in policy, but in the pace of our work,' added Diamantas. 'Completing this review nearly 4 months ahead of schedule means some patients who might otherwise be preparing for surgery, radiation or chemotherapy may now have a different conversation with their doctor.'
