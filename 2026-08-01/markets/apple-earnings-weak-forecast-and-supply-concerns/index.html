Apple earnings: weak forecast and supply concerns overshadow sales beat in Cook's last report

Financial & Markets desk

Apple reported stronger-than-expected earnings and revenue for the fiscal third quarter, driven by a 22% increase in iPhone sales, but the company issued weak guidance for the current period, citing 'supply constraints.' The stock slid more than 6% in extended trading.

For the quarter ended in June, Apple posted earnings per share of $1.91 adjusted, beating the LSEG consensus estimate of $1.89. Revenue came in at $109.42 billion, above the $108.65 billion expected. Net income climbed to $29.79 billion, or $2.02 per share, from $23.43 billion, or $1.57 per share, a year ago. Apple said earnings included 11 cents per share from tariff rebates. Gross margin was 50.1%, which the company noted is not comparable to analyst estimates of 47.9% due to tariff rebates. The company held $146.52 billion in cash.

In key product categories, iPhone revenue reached $54.25 billion, topping the $53.86 billion estimate. Mac revenue surged to $10.35 billion, well above the $8.74 billion forecast. iPad revenue fell short at $6.19 billion versus the $6.92 billion expected. Wearables revenue was $7.88 billion, slightly above the $7.82 billion estimate. Services revenue came in at $30.74 billion, below the $31.22 billion consensus.

Apple is reckoning with a global memory crunch, which CEO Tim Cook recently called a 'hundred-year flood,' and a rush for chip manufacturing capacity that has already forced the company to lift prices on Macs and iPads. Apple has not said it will raise prices on iPhones, but many analysts are expecting hikes as soon as this year. Revenue growth in the quarter topped 15% for a third straight period.

The earnings call was CEO Tim Cook's last before handing leadership over to John Ternus, a 25-year Apple veteran and the current head of hardware. Ternus said little on the call. Meanwhile, Apple is gearing up for a launch of a redesigned Siri using Google technology alongside new iPhone hardware in September, a critical test for the company as investors worry that it has fallen behind in artificial intelligence.
