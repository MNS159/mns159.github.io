Three Fed officials dissent from rate hold, push for hike to combat inflation

Financial & Markets desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

Three Federal Reserve officials dissented this week against the decision to hold interest rates steady, arguing that the central bank should hike now to prevent inflation from becoming entrenched. Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack, Minneapolis Fed President Neel Kashkari, and Dallas Fed President Lorie Logan voted against keeping the Fed's key overnight borrowing rate in a range between 3.5% and 3.75%, where it has remained all year following three cuts in late 2025. The other nine voting members of the Federal Open Market Committee voted to hold rates.

In statements released Friday, the dissenters warned that inaction risks prolonging inflation, which has held above the Fed's 2% target for more than five years and spiked again this year amid the Iran war and the impact of President Donald Trump's tariffs. 'In my view, now is the time for the [FOMC] to act to speed the return of PCE inflation to our 2 percent objective and deliver on our commitment to price stability for the American people,' Hammack said. 'The longer that high inflation persists, the more challenging and costly it can be to bring it back down.'

Kashkari argued that small rate increases now could prevent the need for larger moves later. 'In my view, a potential series of small policy moves would be better than waiting and eventually concluding that even bolder actions were necessary,' he said. His comments echoed lessons from the 1970s high-inflation period and the more recent episode in which Fed officials initially dismissed price spikes as 'transitory' during the Covid pandemic. 'I increasingly believe that monetary policy does have an important role to play in addressing a series of successive supply shocks that might lead to entrenched higher inflation,' Kashkari added.

Logan said the Fed cannot rely on an unexpected economic shock to lower inflation and must be proactive. 'Labor, consumption and financial market conditions indicate that monetary policy is not restraining the economy,' she said. 'Without any policy restraint, inflation will likely continue to trend above target until there's an unanticipated shock.' Hammack noted that supply-side factors such as energy prices have boosted inflation this year, but added, 'I see inflationary pressures coming from the demand side of the economy, as well.' She said constituents in the Cleveland area describe 'pricing pressures as broadening rather than fading, and consumers are expressing despair over persistently higher prices.'

Though price increases eased in June as Middle East tensions briefly softened, energy costs have since risen again, stoking fears that the Fed will have to tighten. Fed Chairman Kevin Warsh, who voted in favor of the hold, said he remains resolute in returning inflation to target. 'We have begun a new chapter, and we understand that the five-plus years of inflation above target cannot be cured in nine weeks — or by a single month of modest price decreases,' he said. Hammack, however, said she is 'not confident it will return to our objective on its own.' Logan is expected to release a further statement explaining her vote later Friday.
