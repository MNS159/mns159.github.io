Novo Nordisk inflammation drug fails heart disease trial, shares plunge

Health & Science desk · anchored on Reuters

Novo Nordisk said Friday that its investigational anti-inflammatory drug ziltivekimab failed to reduce the risk of major heart events in a pivotal Phase 3 trial, a setback to the Danish drugmaker's efforts to expand beyond its blockbuster obesity and diabetes treatments. The company's shares plunged as much as 9.3% to around $46.70, erasing more than $30 billion in market value and marking the stock's largest intraday loss since February. Novo Nordisk's market capitalization fell from $229.2 billion to $198.5 billion, a nearly 69% decline from its all-time high of $635.7 billion in June 2024.

In the late-stage study, which enrolled over 6,300 people with heart disease, kidney disease, and inflammation, ziltivekimab did lower markers of inflammation, Novo said. However, that did not translate into a reduction in the risk of major adverse cardiovascular events, including cardiovascular-related death, heart attack, or stroke. The company said the drug "did not achieve" its goals to reduce such events, and it did not disclose how much the drug reduced risks.

Analysts said the results were "strategically negative" for Novo Nordisk. Jeffries analysts wrote in a note that ziltivekimab would have needed to show at least a 20% risk reduction, and the failure effectively erased a growth opportunity for the company's business beyond its Ozempic and Wegovy market that could have been worth more than $10 billion annually. Goldman Sachs analysts had praised the drug's potential ahead of the results, writing that ziltivekimab could have been the foundation for Novo Nordisk to build a franchise in cardiovascular disease care and relieve potential risks to sales, which are largely reliant on Ozempic and Wegovy.

The trial's failure raises questions about the increasingly popular approach of targeting inflammation to prevent cardiovascular complications. Novo Nordisk, despite the success of its Ozempic and Wegovy drugs in recent years, has worked to expand its other businesses to bolster sales. The firm has moved to increase the GLP-1 market by expanding into pill versions of its medications, and has launched a Wegovy pill in the United Arab Emirates and the U.K., with plans to sell in the U.S. and globally.

In a separate development, Novo Nordisk clashed with Eli Lilly earlier this month, accusing the rival GLP-1 manufacturer of "deceptive" advertising that Novo Nordisk said relied on outdated data to make its products look less effective. The Ozempic maker said it looked to resolve the dispute out of court, but "Lilly refused, calling its advertising 'truthful' and 'transparent,'" according to court documents. The motion also asks the court to require Lilly to issue corrective advertising that indicates its ad campaigns had "planted false beliefs in consumers' minds."
