FDA Expands Pluvicto Approval to PSMA-Positive Metastatic Hormone-Sensitive Prostate Cancer

Health & Science desk

The U.S. Food and Drug Administration has approved lutetium Lu 177 vipivotide tetraxetan (Pluvicto, Novartis) in combination with an androgen receptor pathway inhibitor (ARPI) for adults with PSMA-positive metastatic androgen pathway modulation-naïve or -sensitive (mAPMN/S) prostate cancer, previously referred to as metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer (mHSPC). The decision, announced July 31, 2026, expands the radioligand therapy's use from later-stage metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer (mCRPC) into the first-line setting, roughly doubling the eligible patient population, according to clinicians and the manufacturer.

The approval is based on results from the Phase 3 PSMAddition trial. The primary endpoint of radiographic progression-free survival (rPFS) was met, showing a hazard ratio of 0.67 (95% CI 0.55–0.82) favoring Pluvicto plus standard of care over standard of care alone, representing a 33% risk reduction. Overall survival data are still maturing, with a hazard ratio of 0.80 (95% CI 0.63–1.01) that has not yet reached statistical significance. Additional efficacy measures included a hazard ratio of 0.72 for rPFS and 0.70 for time to metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer, as noted by investigators. Grade 3 or higher adverse events occurred in 50.7% of patients receiving the Pluvicto combination versus 43.0% with standard of care alone.

The prescribing information includes warnings and precautions for risks of radiation exposure, myelosuppression, renal toxicity, embryo-fetal toxicity, and infertility. The expanded indication applies to patients whose tumors are PSMA-positive, as determined by an approved imaging agent.

Clinicians and researchers involved in the PSMAddition trial responded to the approval. Neeraj Agarwal, Presidential Endowed Chair of Cancer Research at the Huntsman Cancer Institute, noted the approval was based on the trial results and congratulated the team led by Scott Tagawa. Shilpa Gupta, Director of Genitourinary Oncology at the Cleveland Clinic, said her site was the highest-enrolling U.S. site and expressed gratitude to patients, families, co-investigators, and research teams. Karine Tawagi, Assistant Professor at the University of Illinois Chicago, highlighted the rPFS and time-to-mCRPC hazard ratios and noted that quality of life was similar between arms. Katy Beckermann, Medical Director of GU Clinical Research at Tennessee Oncology, stated, "Radioligand therapy just entered the first-line setting in prostate cancer" and noted that imaging access, isotope supply, and radiation-safety workflows will need to keep pace with the expanded population.

Michael Hofman, Nuclear Medicine Physician at Peter MacCallum Cancer Centre, who ran early prospective trials of Lu-177 PSMA-617, called the news "terrific" and credited patients, investigators, and industry for building the evidence. Victor Bultó, President of Novartis US, said the approval "enables patients at an earlier stage of the disease to receive it" and that advancing options into earlier settings is critical to improving outcomes. The approval moves Pluvicto into a frontline space that already includes docetaxel-based triplet therapy, PARP inhibition for BRCA2-positive patients, and other mechanisms showing rPFS benefit, according to Beckermann.
