FDA panel backs Replimune's melanoma drug after two rejections

Health & Science desk · anchored on Reuters

A U.S. Food and Drug Administration advisory panel voted 10-to-3 on Friday to recommend approval of Replimune's experimental treatment for advanced melanoma, according to STAT. The panel determined that the company had collected sufficient data to allow the FDA to review RP1, an engineered viral immunotherapy.

The vote comes after the FDA rejected RP1 twice — once last year and again this past April. Investors have interpreted the decision to hold an advisory committee meeting as a sign of new lenience under the FDA's new leadership, STAT reported. The outcome could signal how the agency weighs potential benefits and risks for investigational drugs going forward.

Replimune's stock surged following the advisory panel's backing, Reuters reported. The FDA is not required to follow the panel's recommendation but typically does so.

The panel's support marks a potential turning point for RP1, which is designed to treat advanced melanoma, a serious form of skin cancer. The engineered viral immunotherapy works by infecting and destroying cancer cells while stimulating an immune response.

The FDA is expected to make a final decision on RP1 in the coming months. The advisory committee's vote provides a boost for Replimune, which had faced setbacks with the drug's previous rejections.
