OpenAI Reaches 1 Billion Active Users as AI Becomes Daily Habit

Tech / AI desk

OpenAI has reached 1 billion active users, according to a blog post by the company's chief operating officer, as artificial intelligence becomes an increasingly ingrained daily habit for individuals and businesses. The milestone reflects growing confidence in the technology, with users deploying AI more often and more deeply over time.

Among individuals, after six months of using ChatGPT, people send 50% more messages each day and use the tool for twice as many kinds of work, OpenAI COO said in the post. Among businesses, companies often start with one team or workflow and then expand adoption across operations, she added. 'We are still early,' she wrote. 'More capable systems will complete longer projects, coordinate across tools, and handle more of the work between an idea and a finished result.' She noted that individuals and small businesses will gain capabilities once available only to much larger organizations, and enterprises will apply intelligence more broadly across their operations.

A PYMNTS Intelligence report, 'The AI On-Ramp: Data Shows How Everyday Tasks Build Consumer Habits,' found that generative AI becomes a mass habit through small, repeatable daily tasks. The most common universal usages include finding product links, drafting texts or emails, and symptom lookup. Another PYMNTS Intelligence report, 'Financial Services Pull Ahead in the Enterprise AI Race,' found that while all enterprise sectors have adopted AI, financial services and insurance firms are among those with the highest adoption, with the most common use cases being structured, auditable back-office functions.

The announcement came a day after OpenAI made changes to the price or performance of three of its AI models to improve their performance per dollar across enterprise workloads. The company cut the price of GPT-5.6 Luna by 80%, cut the price of GPT-5.6 Terra by 20%, and provided faster performance of GPT-5.6 Sol in the API while leaving its price unchanged. 'These are not simply changes to a price list,' the COO wrote. 'They expand the range of work that becomes practical and give customers more flexibility to balance intelligence, speed, reliability and cost.'

Sources: PYMNTS.com
