FIFA president Infantino's plans to sell competition stakes unravel

Sports desk

FIFA president Gianni Infantino's plan to sell stakes in the men's World Cup and other competitions to private investors collapsed on Saturday after five days of mounting opposition, with European football's governing body UEFA saying it had 'lost confidence' in his leadership. The proposal, which lacked a formal title, was withdrawn by FIFA after drawing condemnation from confederations, member associations, and senior FIFA staff. The plan would have offered countries an extra $40m for football development if they signed up by 19 September, but critics questioned why private investment was needed given FIFA's financial position: the not-for-profit body holds over $2bn in reserves and is expected to announce record revenues of about $15bn from this summer's World Cup.

UEFA's statement was among the most ferocious ever aimed at a FIFA president, accusing Infantino of failing to deliver on promises of transparency made when he was elected in 2016. 'When Gianni Infantino asked for the trust and the votes of FIFA's member associations to elect him as their president in 2016, he said: "Of course we have to be transparent." He told the assembled stakeholders: "The money of FIFA is your money. It's not the money of the FIFA president. You are the national associations and the money of FIFA has to serve for the development of football and not for anything else." On both these promises, he has failed to deliver,' UEFA said. Asian Football Confederation president Sheikh Salman bin Ebrahim Al Khalifa, a FIFA vice-president, and Football Australia chair Anter Isaac also criticised the lack of consultation and transparency.

The episode drew comparisons to the European Super League debacle of 2021, when 12 top clubs, including six from the Premier League, attempted to form a breakaway competition but abandoned the plan after 72 hours of fierce public backlash. In that case, the plan was leaked before clubs could present their case; here, FIFA attempted to counter criticism in the early hours of Friday but by then the direction of travel was set, with UEFA and others already united against the proposal. FIFA's chief operating officer Kevin Lamour delivered a crushing internal blow, describing the plan as 'the project of one person' and saying FIFA's administration had been 'deceived'. In a statement, he said: 'Our mission - the mission of the hundreds of passionate, dedicated, and exemplary FIFA employees - is to serve football. If that means I lose my job, then so be it. I will understand and respect that decision. At least I'll sleep well tonight.' BBC Sport has approached FIFA for comment.

US President Donald Trump, who has a close relationship with Infantino and helped present the World Cup to Spain less than two weeks ago, distanced himself from the plans when asked on Friday, saying he had not spoken to Infantino about them. A report in the New York Post, which broke the news of the plan's withdrawal, quoted an unnamed source saying the situation had become a 'brand nightmare' for Joshua Kushner, brother of Trump's son-in-law Jared. Kushner's venture capital firm Thrive Eternal would have led the proposed investor group. Infantino now faces the task of clearing up the fallout and building bridges, with UEFA vice-president Laura McAllister telling BBC Radio 4's Today programme: 'All of the confederations will be considering options now. There will be moves, I'm sure, to encourage people to stand, but it's early days yet.' Infantino is up for re-election as FIFA president in March, and the source material indicates support for his re-election may be receding.
