Trump backtracks from promise to let Ukraine build Patriot missiles

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts slightly center-left

President Donald Trump has reversed course on his offer to let Ukraine manufacture its own Patriot air-defense interceptors, telling reporters at a cabinet meeting on Friday that the US has "not agreed to that" and that sharing the underlying technology is "a hard thing to give away." The retreat, which occurred during Trump's first cabinet meeting of his second term at Camp David, contradicted both Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy's readout of their White House meeting earlier this week and Trump's own public pledge at a NATO summit in Turkey in early July, where he told Zelenskyy, "We're going to give a license to you to make Patriots."

"We have not agreed to that," Trump said on Friday. "We're talking about it, but it's a hard thing to give away that kind of technology." He warned that nations given such sensitive technology could one day turn it against the US: "Those people that you give that technology, they can someday turn on you. You know that is possible." Trump also told the Financial Times that he was "not sure" about the license and that "we don't really license equipment."

Zelenskyy, who met with executives at Lockheed Martin — the US defense giant that produces the Patriot — during his Washington visit, said after the White House meeting that Trump "accepted that he will give us licenses." On Friday, as Trump's cabinet meeting was underway, Zelenskyy posted on X that he had a "good phone call" with Vice President JD Vance and reiterated that Patriot interceptors remain a "top priority." Ukraine's air force has said a shortage of interceptors is hampering its ability to shoot down Russian ballistic missiles; the Patriot is one of the few systems capable of intercepting them.

Trump described the Patriot interceptor as traveling at "8,600 miles an hour" and recalled an incident in which five missiles fired "at Jordan" were shot down in sequence — which he rendered as "bing bing bing bing." He said Washington "will be building many plants" to produce Patriots and Tomahawk missiles domestically, but not, for now, in Ukraine. The reversal comes amid a global shortage of Patriot interceptors compounded by US confrontations with Iran. The Center for Strategic and International Studies estimates that two-thirds of the Pentagon's Patriot interceptors have been used, and the army has awarded a $58.6 billion contract to Lockheed Martin to rebuild supplies, while the White House has requested tens of billions of dollars more in emergency funding from Congress.

Trump also addressed the war in Ukraine, saying both sides will have to make concessions: "Nobody wants concessions, but they're both going to have to make some concessions. I mean, you got to make a deal." He said his envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff would visit Ukraine in the coming days. "Very simply put, we want the war in Ukraine with Russia ended," he told the Financial Times. The Camp David meeting came a day after Trump announced what he called a "historic" agreement for Hamas to disarm and for Israeli forces to withdraw from Gaza, a claim that has yet to be confirmed by Israel. Pressed on the sequencing of the deal, Trump declined to specify, saying only that Washington had "an understanding" with Israel and that the Israelis were "very happy about it."
