US-Iran War

Trump threatens more strikes on Iran; Iran warns against 'flames of war'

International & World desk · anchored on AP
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

President Donald Trump threatened further U.S. military action against Iran on Friday, saying the United States will hit Iran “very hard” until it can no longer take it, while Iranian authorities warned that Washington is “fanning the flames of a full-scale regional war.” Speaking to reporters during a Cabinet meeting at Camp David, Trump said “we just want to win” in Iran and signaled that U.S. operations could continue for some time. White House press secretary Karoline Leavitt later accused Tehran of signing a memorandum of understanding on a truce last month, then quickly “broke it, shot at commercial ships, and killed American soldiers,” adding: “Iran will continue to pay until they come to the table in, what President Trump deems, a meaningful way.”

Iran’s wartime military command chief, Major General Ali Abdollahi, responded Saturday by warning regional neighbors that the U.S. is using their “capital, resources and infrastructure as a shield” while strengthening Israel’s war machine. “Any country that makes itself a defensive shield for criminal, aggressor America will burn in the fire of war,” he said. The warning came after major U.S. media outlets reported that the U.S. and Israel may imminently launch an extensive wave of attacks on Iran’s civilian infrastructure, including power plants and petrochemical manufacturers, with an operation that could last up to two weeks and aim to cut electricity across Tehran. Trump has yet to give final authorization, the reports said, citing unnamed sources. Iran’s state-linked Tasnim news agency cited an unnamed source saying armed forces have prepared an extensive retaliation plan to hit “vital infrastructure” linked with the U.S. and Israel.

The threats follow weeks of escalating conflict that began February 28, when the U.S. and Israel launched a war on Iran that has included strikes on military bases, leaders, and civilian infrastructure. The U.S. military attacked at least five Iranian provinces overnight into Thursday, targeting IRGC command centers, missile and drone facilities, coastal surveillance and air-defense sites, and maritime capabilities, according to U.S. statements; casualties included several soldiers and multiple civilians, among them a two-year-old boy killed when his home was hit on the southern island of Qeshm. In response, Iran’s Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps and regular army have launched ballistic missiles and drones at U.S. and Israeli bases across the region and struck energy infrastructure in Israel, Saudi Arabia, the United Arab Emirates, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain, and Oman.

On Saturday, Kuwait’s military said its air defenses destroyed multiple Iranian drones, with some damage reported to government facilities and civilian vehicles. Maj. Gen. Saud Abdulaziz Al-Otaibi, spokesman for Kuwait’s Defense Ministry, said the drones hit “a number of vital facilities,” including a government structure in the country’s north, with no casualties. The British Navy reported that a tanker was struck in the Strait of Hormuz off the coast of Oman, damaging its engine room, and that a separate explosion occurred close to another vessel; no casualties were reported in either incident. Iran has repeatedly attacked vessels attempting to transit the strait without its authorization and has kept the waterway closed to traffic not approved by the IRGC. Iranian state media broadcast footage from near the missile-struck ship, warning that this was “the smoke of mistakenly trusting the US” promises of safe passage, and said four other ships were turned back.

Elsewhere, Israeli strikes on Gaza killed at least two people and injured dozens more Friday night, according to local health officials, after Hamas confirmed it will disarm as part of a U.S.-brokered ceasefire announced last October. The deal calls for Hamas to hand over power to an independent Palestinian administration, an Israeli withdrawal, and deployment of an International Stabilization Force; Israel has not officially commented. Israeli far-right lawmaker Itamar Ben-Gvir called the deal “unacceptable,” saying “Israel must win.” In southern Lebanon, Israeli forces set off explosions to destroy an underground tunnel network under Beaufort Castle that Israel says Hezbollah used as a command. Houthi rebels in Yemen forced eight Saudi ships to reroute from the Bab el-Mandeb strait after announcing a blockade on Saudi shipping, according to Houthi military spokesman Brig. Gen. Yahya Saree.

Iran has signaled it will not give up the Strait of Hormuz as leverage. Mohammad Bagher Zolghadr, secretary of Iran’s Supreme National Security Council, said continued U.S. strikes and a naval blockade only worsen the situation in the strait, warning that “the global economy, and US energy market and voters will pay the price.” Iran’s Foreign Ministry said it has not sent negotiation requests to Washington but that messages have been relayed through mediators. Parliament speaker and chief negotiator Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf said Saturday that “we have been victorious” but that “the victories of this war must be solidified.” In Tehran, public messaging remained focused on vengeance for Supreme Leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei, who was killed on the first day of the war along with dozens of other officials; a banner in Valiasr Square depicted a boy carrying a flag of blood revenge, and the hardline daily Keyhan ran the headline: “Trump, his advisers and allies are frustrated by Iran’s steadfastness.”
