Stock markets decline, bond yields rise after Warsh's hawkish Jackson Hole speech

Financial & Markets desk

U.S. stocks finished lower Friday, erasing some of the week’s earlier gains, after Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh delivered a hawkish speech at the Kansas City Fed’s annual Jackson Hole economic symposium that reinforced expectations of further interest-rate increases. The Dow Jones Industrial Average ended fractionally lower, the S&P 500 fell 0.3%, and the Nasdaq Composite dropped 0.5%. Despite the Friday retreat, all three major indexes posted gains for the week: the Dow rose 0.5% to snap a two-week losing streak, while the S&P 500 gained 0.5% and the Nasdaq also ended higher, marking their fourth advance in five weeks.

In his roughly 30-minute address, Warsh expressed continued concern about inflation, saying, “While this summer’s PCE and CPI readings were better than expected, they do not tell me that underlying trends have meaningfully improved.” He declined to offer explicit forward guidance on the Fed’s next policy move, but traders interpreted his remarks as hawkish. The yield on the 10-year Treasury note climbed nearly six basis points to above 4.73% late Friday, and the U.S. dollar index rose 0.5% to 99.65. According to the CME Group’s FedWatch tool, market pricing for a quarter-point rate hike at the Fed’s next meeting increased significantly after the speech.

Technology shares were mixed, with Nvidia falling 4.6% to lead Dow decliners a day after its strong earnings report, and Marvell Technology sinking 10% after its quarterly results narrowly topped estimates but disappointed investors. The iShares Semiconductor ETF declined more than 3%. Workday jumped nearly 6% to lead the S&P 500 and Nasdaq after posting better-than-expected fiscal second-quarter earnings. Other notable movers included PayPal, which lost 13% after Bloomberg reported that Advent and Stripe were no longer pursuing a deal for the fintech firm, and Gap, which soared 13% after naming a new CEO for its Old Navy brand.

Elsewhere in markets, U.S. benchmark West Texas Intermediate crude oil fell 0.1% to $83.50 a barrel, while Brent crude slipped 0.4% to $89.35. Gold futures dropped 3.2% to $4,515 an ounce, and Bitcoin traded at $77,400, down from overnight highs above $81,000. BioNTech shares slid 7.5% after the German firm halted a Phase 2 trial of an mRNA-based colorectal cancer vaccine.

For the year, the Nasdaq has gained about 14%, while the S&P 500 and Dow are up 13% and 11%, respectively. Warsh’s speech came amid a month in which bond yields had climbed, suggesting wavering faith in the Fed’s commitment to fighting inflation. Yields on 30-year Treasurys held steady after the speech, a sign the Fed chair may have partially succeeded in reasserting the central bank’s credibility.
