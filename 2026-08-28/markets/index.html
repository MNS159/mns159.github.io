Fed Chair Warsh signals rate hikes may be needed as inflation remains elevated

Financial & Markets desk · anchored on AP

Federal Reserve Chairman Kevin Warsh on Friday signaled that interest rates may need to rise if inflation does not show further progress, upending market expectations for a hold in September. Speaking at the Fed’s annual Jackson Hole symposium, Warsh said recent encouraging inflation readings “do not tell me that underlying trends have meaningfully improved,” adding, “We must be confident that underlying inflation is moving to our objective, clearly and at sufficient speed. Otherwise, we have work to do.”

Stock markets decline, bond yields rise after Warsh's hawkish Jackson Hole speech

Financial & Markets desk

U.S. stocks finished lower Friday, erasing some of the week’s earlier gains, after Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh delivered a hawkish speech at the Kansas City Fed’s annual Jackson Hole economic symposium that reinforced expectations of further interest-rate increases. The Dow Jones Industrial Average ended fractionally lower, the S&P 500 fell 0.3%, and the Nasdaq Composite dropped 0.5%. Despite the Friday retreat, all three major indexes posted gains for the week: the Dow rose 0.5% to snap a two-week losing streak, while the S&P 500 gained 0.5% and the Nasdaq also ended higher, marking their fourth advance in five weeks.
