AI industry criticizes Trump's plan to tax chips as counterproductive

Tech / AI desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

The artificial intelligence industry is pushing back against former President Donald Trump's proposal to impose tariffs on imported semiconductors, warning that the policy would increase consumer prices, delay product launches, and slow AI adoption in the United States. In a letter reported by Ars Technica, the industry described the plan as "the single dumbest way imaginable" to approach chip policy, arguing that tariffs would harm the very innovation the US seeks to lead.

The letter warned that tariffs would raise the cost of "everyday tools" such as smartphones, laptops, tablets, smartwatches, connected devices, and vehicles, straining household budgets that are already under pressure. It also cautioned that higher prices could delay the introduction of new devices featuring the latest AI technologies, limiting consumer choice. "Consumer devices are the primary interface through which Americans access AI-powered tools. AI only delivers on its promise when people can actually use it—and tariffs that price consumers out of the device market would slow AI adoption at the very moment the United States is positioned to lead," the letter stated.

To mitigate the blow to AI firms, the Trump administration is reportedly considering some tariff relief, with sources telling Politico that any exemptions would likely be tied to foreign firms investing in US chip manufacturing, such as Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Co. Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick is said to favor that approach. The administration may also phase in tariffs to avoid a sudden shock to consumers, particularly ahead of the holiday season, as Trump has previously exempted goods from tariffs to avoid backlash that could hurt his approval ratings.

The tariff proposal comes at a time when the global data center buildout has already created a scramble for high-end semiconductors, which are forecast to remain in short supply through 2027. Gartner projected yesterday that global semiconductor revenue will reach $1.6 trillion in 2026—much sooner than expected—driven by rising prices. Economists anticipate that tariffs would further increase chip prices, which could "hammer US chip designers such as Nvidia and Advanced Micro Devices" that rely on overseas manufacturing, according to Politico. The policy could also hurt companies like Apple, which competes with foreign rivals not subject to the same tariffs, while potentially benefiting Chinese firms as chip suppliers may shift business to China to avoid the levies.

Sources: Ars Technica
