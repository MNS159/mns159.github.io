Ukraine War

CIA Chief Warns Russia Against NATO Attack as Ukraine War Escalates

International & World desk · anchored on Reuters, AP

CIA Director John Ratcliffe traveled to Moscow this week for a secret meeting with Russian intelligence chiefs, delivering a warning against any attack on NATO territory as Western officials reported that Russia is losing 6,000 more troops in Ukraine each month than it can recruit. Ratcliffe met with FSB head Aleksander Bortnikov and SVR director Sergei Naryshkin, urging the Kremlin to take peace negotiations seriously and to cut off economic and military support for Iran, according to reports from The New York Times and CNN. The visit, conducted aboard a C-17 military transport aircraft, signaled the urgency of U.S. intelligence assessments that Russia may be preparing an act of aggression against a NATO member in the near term, though not a full-scale invasion.

Nepal-Tibet flood death toll surpasses 500; thousands missing as rescue efforts resume after lake threat

International & World desk

The death toll from Wednesday's catastrophic flash flood along the Nepal-Tibet border has risen to at least 579, Nepalese officials said Friday, with more than 2,000 people still missing, including over 500 foreigners from more than 30 countries. The International Red Cross estimates that more than 93,000 people have been directly affected and are in urgent need of humanitarian assistance. Rescue operations resumed Friday after being temporarily halted when a barrier lake formed by debris from the initial disaster began overflowing, raising fears of a secondary flood.

Iran War

Six months into Iran war, regime clings to power, Pentagon depleted, global crises widen

International & World desk

Six months after the United States and Israel launched a war against Iran with the stated goal of toppling its government, the regime in Tehran has not only survived but entrenched itself, even as the conflict has drained U.S. munitions stockpiles, shut the strategic Strait of Hormuz, and cascaded into crises far beyond the Middle East. The initial five-week bombing campaign — from Feb. 28 until an April 8 ceasefire — killed Supreme Leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei and dozens of other top officials on the first day, destroyed Iran’s limited air force and navy, and badly damaged its missile and drone programs. Yet Iran’s leaders have maintained control, and the country has kept the Strait of Hormuz effectively closed, using the vital oil and gas waterway as its biggest strategic weapon.

Norway's King Harald dies at 89; Haakon VIII ascends throne

International & World desk

Norway's King Harald V, Europe's oldest living monarch, died early Friday at Oslo University Hospital at age 89 after being treated for a rare blood condition, hemolytic anemia. The palace announced his death at 06:35 local time (04:35 GMT), saying he passed away peacefully with his wife, Queen Sonja, and son, Crown Prince Haakon, at his bedside. Haakon immediately ascended the throne as King Haakon VIII, and his wife, Mette-Marit, became queen. The new king held an extraordinary council of state meeting with government ministers, adopting the royal family's motto 'All for Norway.'
