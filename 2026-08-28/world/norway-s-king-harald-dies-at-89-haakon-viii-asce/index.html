Norway's King Harald dies at 89; Haakon VIII ascends throne

International & World desk

Norway's King Harald V, Europe's oldest living monarch, died early Friday at Oslo University Hospital at age 89 after being treated for a rare blood condition, hemolytic anemia. The palace announced his death at 06:35 local time (04:35 GMT), saying he passed away peacefully with his wife, Queen Sonja, and son, Crown Prince Haakon, at his bedside. Haakon immediately ascended the throne as King Haakon VIII, and his wife, Mette-Marit, became queen. The new king held an extraordinary council of state meeting with government ministers, adopting the royal family's motto 'All for Norway.'

Thousands of mourners gathered outside the royal palace in Oslo, laying flowers, flags, and cards. An estimated 10,000 people lined the streets as the late king's coffin was transported from the hospital to the palace in a public procession. Oslo Mayor Anne Lindboe postponed her wedding, originally scheduled for Friday, writing on Instagram: 'It's hard to imagine an Oslo and a Norway without you.' The government declared a period of national mourning, with flags flown at half-mast and all non-work-related social events canceled or postponed. The Norwegian Armed Forces honored Harald with a 21-gun salute from Akershus Fortress.

Prime Minister Jonas Gahr Støre, who laid flowers at the palace and signed a book of condolences, said in a televised memorial address that the country was 'united in grief and gratitude' and that Harald's 'legacy lives on.' European royals and world leaders paid tribute. Britain's King Charles III, a second cousin of Harald, called him a 'towering figure' who guided Norway with 'warmth and humility.' Swedish King Carl XVI Gustaf, Danish King Frederik and Queen Mary, European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen, and NATO chief Mark Rutte also offered condolences. Norwegian footballer Erling Haaland thanked Harald for 'everything you meant to Norway.'

Harald, who reigned for 35 years since succeeding his father King Olav V in 1991, was widely praised as a modernizing 'people's king' who broke with tradition by marrying a commoner, Queen Sonja, and served as a unifying figure during national crises, including the 2011 far-right terrorist attacks. He was born in 1937, the first prince born in Norway in over 500 years, and spent World War II in exile in the United States after the Nazi invasion. Despite his popularity, a poll earlier this year showed support for the monarchy had dropped from 72% in 2024 to 54%.

King Haakon VIII, 53, is due to take an oath of allegiance before the Norwegian parliament on Tuesday and will later participate in a consecration service, as Norway no longer holds coronations. His daughter, Ingrid Alexandra, 22, is next in line as crown princess after succession rules changed in 1990. Haakon assumes the throne amid family challenges: Queen Mette-Marit faced scrutiny over a past friendship with convicted sex offender Jeffrey Epstein, for which she apologized; she received a lung transplant earlier this year after suffering from pulmonary fibrosis; and Mette-Marit's son from a previous relationship, Marius Borg Høiby, was convicted in June of rape and other offenses and is appealing. The date of Harald's funeral has not been announced; he will lie in state in the palace chapel for the public to pay respects.
