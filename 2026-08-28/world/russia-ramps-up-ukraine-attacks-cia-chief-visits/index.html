CIA Chief Warns Russia Against NATO Attack as Ukraine War Escalates

International & World desk · anchored on Reuters, AP

CIA Director John Ratcliffe traveled to Moscow this week for a secret meeting with Russian intelligence chiefs, delivering a warning against any attack on NATO territory as Western officials reported that Russia is losing 6,000 more troops in Ukraine each month than it can recruit. Ratcliffe met with FSB head Aleksander Bortnikov and SVR director Sergei Naryshkin, urging the Kremlin to take peace negotiations seriously and to cut off economic and military support for Iran, according to reports from The New York Times and CNN. The visit, conducted aboard a C-17 military transport aircraft, signaled the urgency of U.S. intelligence assessments that Russia may be preparing an act of aggression against a NATO member in the near term, though not a full-scale invasion.

Western officials, speaking on condition of anonymity, said Russia is losing approximately 6,000 more soldiers per month—killed or wounded—than it can recruit, despite acquiring about 1,000 new recruits daily. The officials assessed that Russian President Vladimir Putin has not changed his maximalist objectives in Ukraine but faces increasing domestic pressure, including fuel shortages that are affecting public support for the war. Putin has responded to battlefield losses by escalating strikes on Ukrainian civilian infrastructure, firing over 360 missiles since early July, according to the officials. They said Russia is still making slow gains in eastern Donbas but that the attrition rate is unsustainable without a mass mobilization, which they consider politically and economically risky.

The CIA director's visit came amid heightened rhetoric from Moscow, including threats to strike British military targets inside and outside Ukraine. Russian Foreign Ministry spokesperson Maria Zakharova warned that Britain was "one step away" from becoming legally complicit in what she called "terrorism" and that failure to change course would bring "catastrophic consequences." British Prime Minister Andy Burnham, who visited Kyiv on August 24, announced that the UK would provide Ukraine with previously classified information on British-manufactured components for long-range cruise missiles. Burnham condemned Russia's threats and said UK support for Ukraine would not waver.

Western officials said the hostile rhetoric from Moscow is consistent with longstanding Russian military doctrine aimed at applying pressure on the West and exploiting fears to fracture European support for Ukraine. They assessed that Putin wants to avoid a direct military confrontation with NATO, despite the escalating rhetoric and a string of suspected Russian drone incidents near NATO territory, including a drone loaded with explosives that reached a runway at Leipzig airport in Germany earlier this month. One official highlighted the risk of miscalculation leading to a military confrontation, while another said NATO allies should remain calm but not complacent.

U.S. President Donald Trump has publicly dismissed concerns that Russia would attack a NATO member, telling Axios and CNN this week that he is "not concerned" and that Putin "is not going to be attacking a NATO territory." Trump described Ratcliffe's trip as "semi-routine." However, the Institute for the Study of War noted that a CIA director's personal visit to deliver a warning is unusual and generally reserved for matters of the highest importance, contrasting it with the November 2021 trip by then-CIA Director William Burns to deter Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine. The current threat, analysts said, is significantly different from that of 2021, involving possible hybrid attacks such as cyberattacks, sabotage, or small-scale incursions rather than a conventional invasion.
