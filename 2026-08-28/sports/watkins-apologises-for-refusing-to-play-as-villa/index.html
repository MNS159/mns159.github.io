Watkins apologises for refusing to play as Villa sell him to Al-Hilal; Jackson arrives

Sports desk

Aston Villa manager Unai Emery said Ollie Watkins apologised for refusing to play in Sunday's 4-0 defeat at Brighton as the striker prepares to complete a £51 million move to Saudi Pro League side Al-Hilal. Villa have agreed a deal to sign Chelsea forward Nicolas Jackson for up to £65 million as his replacement, making Jackson the club's record signing. Watkins, 30, is expected in Saudi Arabia on Friday to finalise the transfer, ending a six-year spell at Villa Park in which he scored 108 goals in 278 appearances, including a club-record 91 in the Premier League.

Emery paid an emotional tribute to the England international, who apologised to the manager and squad for making himself unavailable against Brighton. "He told us after the Brighton match he was sorry and that he had made a mistake. He was wrong, but we accepted it. The players as well," Emery said. "His commitment and his behaviour was fantastic. He's a fantastic guy — a fantastic guy even more than a player. I told him 'bye' with a huge hug and with small tears." Emery added that Watkins had been "fantastic" during his Villa career, but that "now it's a new chapter here with Nicolas Jackson."

Jackson, 25, spent last season on loan at Bayern Munich, scoring 11 goals and providing three assists in 1,320 minutes across all competitions. The Senegal forward averaged 1.0 goal involvements per 90 minutes, compared with Watkins' 0.6 per 90 over 3,837 minutes. Jackson also posted higher per-90 numbers for shots (3.8 vs 2.6), shots on target (2.2 vs 1.2), expected goals (0.7 vs 0.5) and touches in the opposition box (8.5 vs 5.6). However, Opta cautions that Jackson's sample was smaller and substitute-heavy; he started only 15 of 34 appearances, while Watkins started 43 matches. In two Premier League seasons with Chelsea, Jackson scored 14 from 18.7 xG in 2023-24 and 10 from 12.3 xG in 2024-25. Emery previously worked with Jackson at Villarreal, and the forward has credited the manager with transforming his understanding of the game. "Unai taught me everything about football," Jackson has said. Villa hope to have Jackson registered in time for Monday's visit of Arsenal.

Watkins is the latest key player to depart in a major squad overhaul. Villa have already sold Morgan Rogers, Ezri Konsa (£50m, aged 28), Youri Tielemans and Lucas Digne, while bringing in Johan Manzambi, Joao Gomes, Zion Suzuki and Matteo Ruggeri. Emery defended the strategy, saying: "We took decisions because it's good for the club. Of course, we don't want to be a club that sells players, but the responsibility is there. It's crazy [money for someone] 30 years old and £50m for Ezri Konsa. How we developed in the last year with some players and how we can sell some players now is completely the right decision. We have to rebuild the team as fast as possible. [Fans] have to accept our way and why we are taking some decisions."

As part of the rebuild, Villa completed the free transfer of midfielder Leon Goretzka on Thursday. The former Bayern Munich player, who won seven Bundesliga titles and the Champions League with the German club, is described by Emery as "a winner" and "a very competitive player." Emery added: "He is versatile and arrives fit and training well. Hopefully on Monday he can be on the field." With Watkins unavailable and both Tammy Abraham and 17-year-old Brian Madjo injured, Villa started the Brighton match without a recognised centre-forward, with Emiliano Buendia leading the line.
