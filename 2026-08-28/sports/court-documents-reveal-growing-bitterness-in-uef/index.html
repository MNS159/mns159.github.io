Uefa’s US court filings accuse Infantino of ‘criminal mismanagement’ in escalating feud

Sports desk

Uefa has filed court documents in the United States that accuse Fifa president Gianni Infantino of “criminal mismanagement” and a “stunning dereliction of duty” over his now-abandoned plan to sell stakes in competitions to private investors. The papers, lodged to obtain evidence for potential criminal proceedings in Switzerland, describe the Fifa Forward Enterprise (FFE) plan as “a fraudulently off-market price promoted by Infantino for his own benefit” and claim the World Cup was assigned an “absurdly low enterprise value” of about £15bn. The filings request subpoenas against Fifa, venture capital firm Thrive Capital, and former Formula 1 chief Greg Maffei, seeking documents and deposition testimony related to the deal.

The FFE scheme, which proposed selling equity in Fifa’s competitions to outside investors, collapsed days after being revealed last month. Infantino defended it as a way to unlock commercial potential but later apologised for “errors”. Uefa’s legal filings, seen by BBC Sport, accuse Infantino of acting under “a veil of secrecy” with “cronies” and “co-conspirators”, and include a chapter on “the enduring history of corruption among Fifa officials”. The documents request access to evidence in the US for use in litigation in Switzerland against Infantino, though it remains unclear whether a criminal complaint will ultimately be lodged.

Infantino has not publicly responded to the latest legal escalation. Instead, he has embarked on a world tour to shore up support, recently visiting Vietnam, the Dominican Republic and Paris. He received a qualified endorsement from the Saudi Arabian Football Federation, which said it “supports the continued efforts of Gianni Infantino to restore unity”. Uefa, meanwhile, has pledged to “secure fundamental reform, restore proper limits on presidential authority and ensure that Fifa is once again governed as an institution—not around one individual”. It is also demanding an independent external review of the FFE plan.

Leading sports lawyer Nick de Marco KC said the first stage—the US disclosure application—has a “high” likelihood of success. “What Uefa have to show is that they have an arguable case for criminal proceedings to be brought against Infantino,” he said. “If they can show that… then they ought to be given disclosure of a lot of the documents.” However, he cautioned that a trial is “a long way off” and that the Swiss prosecutor would then decide whether a case exists. Uefa’s move follows its earlier warning to Fifa to retain all “relevant materials” related to the plan, and a threat by three confederations for Infantino to stand down, which he resisted.

The conflict is the latest chapter in a long-running power struggle. In 2002, a criminal complaint against former Fifa president Sepp Blatter was filed but took no action, and last year a Swiss attempt to prosecute Blatter and former Uefa president Michel Platini ended with both being cleared. Infantino is expected to seek a fourth term in a presidential election scheduled for March 2021 (sic). Uefa has warned that if he runs, it will “join forces with its partner confederations to ensure that member associations are offered a credible alternative”. The next Fifa Council meeting in October could be highly charged, with presidential candidate nominations due in November. Until then, the division shows no sign of healing.
