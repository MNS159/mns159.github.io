England dominate Pakistan on day two of second Test at Lord's

Sports desk

England's four-man pace attack of Ollie Robinson, Jofra Archer, Josh Tongue and Gus Atkinson bowled Pakistan out for 110 on day two of the second Test at Lord's, giving the hosts a first-innings lead of 180. England then reached 81-3 in their second innings by the close, extending their overall lead to 261. The performance puts England in complete control of the match and within sight of a series win.

Resuming after England posted 290 in their first innings, Pakistan's reply unravelled quickly. Archer removed captain Shan Masood for one with the score at 5-1, and the pattern continued as Robinson ran through the lower order. Pakistan lost five wickets for 19 runs in seven overs as Robinson finished with 4-11, his fourth wicket haul of the series. He bowled 13 overs for just 11 runs, the most economical figures by a pace bowler bowling 10 or more overs in England for 13 years, including a stretch of 31 consecutive deliveries without conceding a run.

The pace attack has drawn praise for its variety. Archer touched 95mph in the quickest opening spell of his career, while Robinson operated around 78mph, relying on control of line and length. Across the series, Robinson has hit a good length with 70% of his deliveries; Atkinson is next at 52%. Tongue, whose unusual wide-on-the-crease action sees him deliver from 96.4cm from the stumps compared with Archer's 40.3cm, has taken 30 Test wickets at a strike rate of one every 39.8 balls. Twenty-two per cent of his deliveries in this series would have hit the stumps.

Former England captain Michael Vaughan said the attack "will challenge better batting line-ups" and noted that Robinson has become "the tone-setter we were crying out for in the winter". BBC chief cricket commentator Jonathan Agnew described it as "a well-balanced attack" and said Tongue "is the one who is really developing the best of them". Captain Joe Root said after the first-Test win at Headingley that his pace bowlers 'do not know how good they can be', comparing the group favourably to his earlier bowling stocks that included James Anderson, Stuart Broad, Chris Woakes and Ben Stokes.

With a home Ashes series less than a year away, England's attack is emerging as a potential weapon against Australia. The four-man pace unit was forced upon the side by Ben Stokes' retirement but has proved potent, with Robinson taking 17 wickets at an average of 8.84 across three Tests this summer. England are on course to win the second Test and seal the series against Pakistan, who have struggled against a bowling line-up that has offered pace, accuracy, and variety without the use of a specialist spinner.
