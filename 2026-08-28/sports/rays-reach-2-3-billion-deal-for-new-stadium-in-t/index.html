Rays reach $2.3 billion deal for new stadium in Tampa, targeting 2029 opening

Sports desk

The Tampa Bay Rays have secured a $2.3 billion deal for a new stadium and surrounding development in Tampa, with the goal of opening in time for the 2029 season. The agreement, which keeps the franchise in the Tampa-St. Petersburg area for decades, was finalized Friday when Hillsborough County commissioners voted 5-2 to approve a package of county funding. The Tampa City Council had voted 4-3 the day before to approve its share.

Under the terms, the city of Tampa is contributing roughly $80 million in public funding, and Hillsborough County will contribute nearly $800 million. The remaining $1.4 billion, plus any cost overruns, will be privately financed. The new complex will be located near Hillsborough College's Dale Mabry campus. The county will own the stadium, and the Rays will pay $4 million per year in rent under a lease expected to run 35 years.

The new ballpark will replace Tropicana Field, which has been the Rays' home since the franchise's inception in 1998, except for the 2025 season when the team played at a Yankees minor league affiliate ballpark in Tampa due to damage from Hurricane Milton. The Rays' lease on Tropicana Field runs through the end of the 2028 season.

Major League Baseball commissioner Rob Manfred praised the development in a statement: 'This is a great day for Tampa Bay Rays fans, the entire Tampa Bay region and Major League Baseball.' He thanked the county commissioners, the Tampa City Council, and project leaders, adding that the agreement 'provides a tremendous foundation for the Rays and Major League Baseball to continue thriving in this community for generations to come.'

Rays CEO Ken Babby also issued a statement, saying: 'The Tampa Bay Rays enthusiastically thank and applaud the Hillsborough County Board of County Commissioners for making history.' He noted that the deal secures a 'privately financed and mixed-use neighborhood, a reinvented Hillsborough College campus, and a new ballpark that secures the long-term future of Major League Baseball here in Tampa Bay.' Babby said the team looks forward to celebrating before breaking ground, calling the project the 'Forever Home.'

The vote brings to a close more than a decade of negotiations over a new Rays ballpark, after prior owner Stuart Sternberg worked on several frameworks that never advanced this far. While the majority of the project is privately financed, public funding is involved. Academic research, the source noted, holds that using tax dollars for such projects provides at best negligible economic benefits to the public.
