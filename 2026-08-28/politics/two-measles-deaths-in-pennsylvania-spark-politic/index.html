Two measles deaths in Pennsylvania spark political backlash and criminal probe into RFK Jr.'s vaccine claims

Politics desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts slightly center-left
✓ Facts
  • Pennsylvania officials this week reported two measles-related deaths in Lancaster County, the first measles deaths in the U.S. this year and the first in Pennsylvania in more than three decades.
  • Pennsylvania has recorded 424 measles cases so far this year across more than two dozen counties, including 94 new cases in the most recent week, with 73 people hospitalized.
  • One of the two deaths was a newborn; the Lancaster County Coroner's Office said the infant tested positive for measles but listed the primary cause of death as a laceration of the spleen.
  • HHS Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. publicly questioned whether the deaths were attributable to measles and suggested Gov. Josh Shapiro's office may have fabricated them for political purposes; Shapiro accused Kennedy of spreading vaccine misinformation.
  • A January 2019 letter signed by Kennedy to Samoa's then-prime minister, obtained by The Guardian and AP, proposed a vaccine-focused assessment of Samoa's MMR program and used the words 'vaccine' or 'vaccination' eight times.
  • In 2025 Senate confirmation hearings before the Finance and HELP Committees, Kennedy testified that his 2019 trip to Samoa 'had nothing to do with vaccines.'
  • Samoa's 2019 measles outbreak, which followed a 10-month vaccination halt after two infants died from a bad MMR vaccine batch, killed 83 people—most of them children under age 5—and caused more than 1,860 hospitalizations.
  • Sens. Ron Wyden and Ed Markey and the watchdog group American Oversight have called for a Justice Department criminal investigation into whether Kennedy lied to Congress; Sen. Angela Alsobrooks has called for his resignation or firing.
○ Analysis

The controversy sits at the intersection of a real, escalating public-health emergency and a political fight over accountability at the top of federal health policy. Measles, one of the most contagious known diseases, is spreading in multiple states as childhood vaccination rates have fallen below the 95% threshold needed to prevent outbreaks; nearly 2,800 U.S. cases have been reported this year, already surpassing all of 2024. Robert F. Kennedy Jr., a longtime vaccine skeptic who now runs HHS, has drawn scrutiny both for his handling of the Pennsylvania deaths and for newly surfaced documents about his 2019 Samoa trip, which critics link to a deadly outbreak there after vaccination was paused. Whether Kennedy's 2025 congressional testimony that the trip had 'nothing to do with vaccines' constitutes a prosecutable false statement is now a live legal and political question, one complicated by the fact that any prosecution would require action from a Justice Department under the same administration Kennedy serves.

◐ Debate

Democratic

  • Sens. Ron Wyden and Ed Markey argue newly surfaced letters prove Kennedy lied under oath about his Samoa trip's connection to vaccines, and say this fits a broader pattern of him spreading vaccine disinformation that has cost lives.
  • Gov. Josh Shapiro contends Kennedy's public doubts about the Pennsylvania measles deaths amount to misinformation that scares parents away from vaccinating their children during an active outbreak.
  • Sen. Angela Alsobrooks and watchdog group American Oversight argue Kennedy's alleged false testimony and handling of the outbreak disqualify him from continuing to lead HHS, calling for his resignation, firing, or a DOJ investigation.

Republican

  • Kennedy has publicly disputed that the Pennsylvania deaths were definitively caused by measles, pointing to the coroner's finding that the newborn's primary cause of death was a ruptured spleen, and suggested Shapiro's office may have exaggerated the deaths for political advantage.
  • Kennedy has maintained that his 2019 Samoa trip was intended to introduce a medical informatics system for digitizing health records rather than to focus on vaccines, disputing the interpretation of his letter to Samoa's prime minister.
  • A law professor cited in coverage notes that even if lying-to-Congress allegations are credible, prosecution would require the Trump Justice Department to act, which is considered unlikely—suggesting the criminal probe calls face a steep practical hurdle regardless of merit.
Steelman

Both the Pennsylvania dispute and the Samoa revelations risk becoming proxies for a partisan blame game that, as one infectious-disease specialist warned, distracts from the underlying and worsening reality: measles is spreading fast among unvaccinated populations, vaccination rates have slipped below safe thresholds nationwide, and more deaths are likely regardless of who is blamed for the ones that have already occurred.

Implications

The Justice Department's decision on whether to investigate Kennedy will test how executive-branch accountability functions when the accused official serves within the same administration empowered to prosecute him. Meanwhile, with kindergarten vaccination rates below the herd-immunity threshold in multiple states and school terms beginning, public health officials expect measles cases and hospitalizations to keep rising in Pennsylvania, Florida, Texas, and Utah before the political and legal disputes are resolved.
