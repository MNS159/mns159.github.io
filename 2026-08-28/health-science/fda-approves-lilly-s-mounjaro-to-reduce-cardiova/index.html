FDA approves Lilly's Mounjaro to reduce cardiovascular risk in adults with type 2 diabetes

Health & Science desk · anchored on Reuters

The U.S. Food and Drug Administration approved Eli Lilly's Mounjaro (tirzepatide) to reduce the risk of major cardiovascular events—including heart attack, stroke, and cardiovascular death—in adults with type 2 diabetes who are at high risk for these events. The approval makes Mounjaro the first dual GIP and GLP-1 receptor agonist proven to lower such risks, according to the company. The decision was based on results from the SURPASS-CVOT trial, which enrolled more than 13,000 participants across 30 countries over a median follow-up of about four years. In the head-to-head trial against Trulicity (dulaglutide), a GLP-1 drug with an established cardiovascular benefit, Mounjaro showed an 8 percent lower rate of the composite endpoint of cardiovascular death, heart attack, or stroke (MACE-3). The estimated hazard ratio was 0.92 (95.3% CI: 0.83, 1.01), demonstrating non-inferiority; superiority over Trulicity was not established.

SURPASS-CVOT was the first cardiovascular outcomes trial to compare two incretin medicines directly rather than against a placebo, and it was the largest and longest study of tirzepatide to date. The trial randomized 13,299 participants 1:1 to receive either Mounjaro (15 mg or maximum tolerated dose) or Trulicity (1.5 mg) once weekly. Over a median of 210.1 weeks, Mounjaro met the primary objective of non-inferiority for reducing MACE-3. The safety profile was consistent with Mounjaro's established effects; the most common adverse events were gastrointestinal-related, generally mild to moderate in severity, and occurred primarily during dose escalation. Eli Lilly said in a statement that 'heart health deserves attention throughout the course of treatment, not just after a serious cardiovascular event.'

Mounjaro, a once-weekly injectable, is already the No. 1 prescribed branded type 2 diabetes medicine for adults in the U.S. for glycemic control and weight loss, the company said. The new approval adds a cardiovascular risk reduction indication to its label. An estimated one in three adults with type 2 diabetes in the U.S. has cardiovascular disease, and heart disease is the leading cause of death in this population. The drug works by activating both GIP and GLP-1 receptors, which regulate blood sugar and appetite. Full results from SURPASS-CVOT were published in The Lancet.

The label for Mounjaro includes a boxed warning about the risk of thyroid C-cell tumors, including medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC). It is contraindicated in patients with a personal or family history of MTC or Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia syndrome type 2. Other serious potential side effects include pancreatitis, low blood sugar (especially when used with sulfonylureas or insulin), serious allergic reactions, dehydration leading to kidney problems, severe stomach problems, vision changes, gallbladder problems, and risk of pulmonary aspiration during surgery. The most common side effects are nausea, diarrhea, decreased appetite, vomiting, constipation, indigestion, and abdominal pain. Mounjaro is available in doses from 2.5 mg to 15 mg per 0.5 mL injection.

The approval expands treatment options for physicians managing type 2 diabetes patients at high cardiovascular risk. Mounjaro joins Trulicity, which also carries a cardiovascular risk reduction indication, but as a dual agonist it offers a different mechanism. Eli Lilly noted that the approval provides a new option to help manage cardiovascular event risk in addition to the drug's established benefits on blood sugar and weight. The company markets Mounjaro as a pre-filled single-dose pen, and the drug is administered once weekly via subcutaneous injection.
