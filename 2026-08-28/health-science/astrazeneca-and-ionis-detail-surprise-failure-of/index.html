AstraZeneca and Ionis Detail Surprise Failure of Heart Disease Silencer Drug, Raising Questions for Alnylam

Health & Science desk

AstraZeneca on Friday detailed the surprise failure of a closely watched Phase 3 clinical trial for its experimental heart disease drug, a silencer developed with Ionis Pharmaceuticals. The study found that the drug offered no additional benefit for patients with the progressive condition ATTR-CM (transthyretin amyloidosis with cardiomyopathy) who were already taking a different type of medicine called a stabilizer. The effect was so pronounced that it tanked the entire trial, according to STAT.

Most participants in the study were on a stabilizer at the outset. The trial’s miss has implications beyond AstraZeneca and Ionis, affecting other companies in the multibillion-dollar ATTR-CM market, including Alnylam Pharmaceuticals, which markets another silencer, and BridgeBio, the maker of a new stabilizer. Analysts have said the outcome bolsters the case that stabilizers, which are oral drugs, may be superior to injectable silencers.

The failure represents the latest twist in a competitive landscape for treating ATTR-CM, a condition that causes progressive heart damage. AstraZeneca’s silencer was designed to reduce production of the abnormal protein that drives the disease, but the trial results suggest that for patients already stabilized on an oral stabilizer, adding the silencer provided no additional improvement.

Sources: STAT
