FDA approves updated COVID-19 vaccines targeting XFG variant for fall 2026

Health & Science desk

The US Food and Drug Administration has approved updated COVID-19 vaccines from Moderna, Novavax-Sanofi, and Pfizer-BioNTech for the fall 2026 season, the companies announced Thursday. The vaccines are designed to prevent severe disease caused by the dominant XFG subvariant of the JN.1 coronavirus strain. The FDA approved the shots for adults 65 and older, as well as younger people with one or more chronic conditions that raise their risk of severe illness.

Each vaccine has slightly different approved age and eligibility criteria. Pfizer’s mRNA vaccine, Comirnity XFG, is approved for adults 65 and older and people ages 5 to 64 with an underlying condition. Moderna’s mRNA vaccine, Spikevax, is approved for people 65 and older and those 6 months to 64 years old with an underlying condition; Moderna’s new lower-dose vaccine, mNexspike, is approved for people 65 and older and those 12 to 64 with an underlying condition. Nuvaxovid, the only protein-based COVID-19 vaccine available in the US, originally licensed by Novavax and now produced by Sanofi, is approved for adults 65 and older and people ages 12 to 64 who are at high risk for severe COVID-19.

Health conditions that may increase the risk of severe COVID-19 include asthma, cancer, chronic lung or kidney disease, diabetes, heart disease, liver disease, mental illness, obesity, pregnancy, and smoking, according to the US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention. Under the Trump administration, the federal government has shifted from a universal COVID-19 vaccine recommendation to a more restricted one in which everyone 6 months and over is advised to consult with a healthcare provider before getting the shot.

The CDC has not yet made an official recommendation for this year’s vaccines, but most medical organizations recommend getting one, and insurance companies have indicated they will continue to cover the shot. AHIP, an organization representing most health insurers, has said its members will cover the total cost of vaccines recommended by a CDC committee at least until the end of 2027. However, insurance coverage has been in doubt because that committee has not met, due to a lawsuit claiming its members were appointed illegally. CVS is currently advertising free COVID-19 vaccines with most insurance but notes that appointments may be unavailable in some states.

COVID-19 case numbers are not especially high right now, and the United States is not recording many hospitalizations or deaths. However, CDC data shows the test positivity rate for the week ending August 15 approaching 5% for the first time since early February, with numbers climbing in every state. According to the CDC, cases are growing or likely growing in 48 states. As of early July, XFG.1.1 was the predominant coronavirus strain causing illness in the US. Pfizer said it has already begun distributing vaccines, and experts say the shots should begin arriving at pharmacies and doctors’ offices in September.
