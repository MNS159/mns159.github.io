Nvidia scandal deepens: Huang resigns, Lutnick faces pressure, both summoned to testify

Politics desk · anchored on Reuters
✓ Facts
  • Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia CEO on Thursday; Nvidia named Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress as interim CEO.
  • Huang admitted on Sunday to having been 'unfaithful' to his wife, following reports of an affair with Alice Huang (no relation), a former executive at Singapore-based Nvidia customer Megaspeed whom US intelligence reportedly suspects of ties to Chinese intelligence; China has rejected the allegation.
  • Reports on Wednesday described a second alleged affair between Jensen Huang and Nvidia executive Kimberly Powell, who reported directly to him.
  • Nvidia's stock market value fell by $550bn over five trading days; the company says it has no reason to believe trade secrets or national security were compromised and has launched an independent inquiry.
  • Senator Tim Scott (R-SC), chair of the Senate Banking Committee, summoned both Jensen Huang and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick to testify; Huang was asked to appear voluntarily on December 3 with a subpoena possible, and Lutnick is set to testify before the same committee in December.
  • Three House committees (Intelligence, Foreign Affairs, and the Select Committee on China) summoned Huang for a closed-door interview on December 1; his team told lawmakers he plans to cooperate.
  • The Commerce Department, led by Lutnick, did not add Megaspeed to its export blacklist despite a classified memo on the concerns and despite Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent saying other agencies had favored blacklisting the company for months; the New York Times reported Nvidia's general counsel contacted Commerce investigators probing Megaspeed by invoking a senior Trump appointee, after which the probe was frozen and handed to the Justice Department.
  • Attorney General Todd Blanche said the Justice Department has not yet concluded whether Alice Huang engaged in espionage for Beijing, and the White House has told the New York Post that Trump retains 'complete confidence' in Lutnick.
○ Analysis

Nvidia makes the advanced chips that power the global AI boom, and Washington has restricted their export to China since 2022 over fears they could aid Beijing's military and technological ambitions. Jensen Huang had unusually close access to President Trump — traveling with him and advocating against the export restrictions he saw as hurting Nvidia's business — making him an influential voice in one of the administration's biggest internal policy fights. The scandal over his personal relationships, including with a woman US intelligence suspects of Chinese intelligence ties, has intersected with a separate and consequential policy dispute: whether the Commerce Department, under Lutnick, moved too slowly to restrict a company (Megaspeed) suspected of helping divert advanced chips to China, and whether Nvidia's own intervention helped stall that investigation. What began as a personal scandal has therefore widened into a test of how AI export-control policy is made and enforced at the highest levels of government.

Steelman

Across party lines, lawmakers and officials agree the core unresolved question is not the affair itself but whether export-control enforcement failed — and who in the administration knew what, and when — making the coming congressional testimony the real test of accountability.

Implications

The scheduled December testimony from both Huang and Lutnick, alongside ongoing Justice Department and Nvidia internal investigations, will determine whether this becomes primarily a personal scandal or triggers broader scrutiny of US export-control enforcement and decision-making inside the Trump administration's China policy.
