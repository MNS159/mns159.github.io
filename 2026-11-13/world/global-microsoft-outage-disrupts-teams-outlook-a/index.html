Global Microsoft outage disrupts Teams, Outlook and other services

International & World desk · anchored on Reuters

A major Microsoft outage disrupted services including Teams, Outlook, and AI software Copilot across 74 countries for roughly 18 to 20 hours, beginning late Thursday and continuing into Friday. Microsoft said the issue was a technical problem, not a cyber-security incident, and that it had "addressed the underlying issue" and was gradually restoring services. The company apologized, saying it was "deeply sorry for the disruption this incident has caused our customers and partners."

The outage affected Microsoft 365 apps used by more than 430 million people, with nearly 130,000 reports of problems logged on the outage website Down Detector. Users experienced varying levels of access: many could not send or receive emails, view files, or use calendar apps, while some could still use Teams without issues. Microsoft attributed the problem to "elevated load" on its infrastructure stemming from an issue during an earlier network capacity upgrade. The company warned of continued "intermittent access issues" and delayed email delivery, extending the email timeout window to 48 hours to protect delayed messages.

Experts said the outage's duration was unusually long. Professor James Ferryman of the University of Reading explained that the part of Microsoft's servers that sets up secure connections became swamped, causing a cascading failure: "One server falls over, its work gets passed to the next one, and meanwhile huge numbers of phones and laptops keep trying to get back in." He said the fix required bringing services back bit by bit to manage the surge of reconnecting devices. Gergely Orosz, a former software engineer at Skype and Uber, called it potentially the "most disruptive" business email outage "of all time."

The disruption had widespread effects. In the UK, GP surgeries, pharmacies, police, and schools were affected; some police forces urged the public to call rather than report crimes online. In the US, congressional offices, the City of Tampa, and law enforcement agencies reported problems; a Colorado sheriff's office said it could not process bond payments via email. Courts from Los Angeles to Texas warned of communications delays. In Canada, a Toronto healthcare network experienced email issues but said patient care was not affected. Japan's government reported disruption to its shared IT platform. Indian airlines IndiGo and Akasa Air said email support was affected, and Taiwanese financial firms reported disruptions. Tech companies OpenAI and Anthropic also reported problems with their Microsoft connectors.

Microsoft's executive vice-president of Copilot, Charles Lamanna, said: "We regret that today's outages caused disruptions for our customers, and we are working to address the issue." By Friday afternoon, Microsoft reported "encouraging recovery progress" in Europe and improvement in North America, with email queues "significantly reduced and approaching normal levels." The company warned that some users might still experience intermittent issues. Australian cyber-security expert Troy Hunt noted that the outage "reminds us how critical email still is" and affects not only work but also communication about appointments, purchases, and account access. Mark Gregory, an associate professor at RMIT University, said the outage appeared to involve a misconfiguration in the network layer, causing a cascading overload of servers, and advised businesses reliant on cloud services to have backup options.
