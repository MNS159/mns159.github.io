UN watchdog 'increasingly concerned' over uninspected Iran nuclear site

International & World desk

The United Nations nuclear watchdog has said it is "increasingly concerned" about a nuclear facility in Iran that it has not been allowed to inspect. In a quarterly report to member states, the International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA) said it does not know the exact location of the Isfahan Fuel Enrichment Plant (IFEP), whether it contains nuclear material, or its status for safeguards purposes. The IAEA described the lack of information as "a matter of serious proliferation concern" that "needs to be rectified with the utmost urgency."

Iran informed the IAEA of plans to set up the uranium enrichment plant in the city of Isfahan on 12 June 2025, a day before Israel began attacking its nuclear sites. The United States joined the strikes later that month. Iran initially agreed to allow inspectors to visit the facility, but the inspection was cancelled after the Israeli attacks began. Since the start of the war, Iran has allowed only very limited IAEA inspections, telling the agency that it is not possible to resume inspections amid the current security situation. Iran later passed a law suspending cooperation with the IAEA, and the watchdog withdrew its inspectors for safety reasons.

The nuclear complex at Isfahan suffered extensive damage in the US and Israeli strikes, particularly the parts above ground; the condition of its underground facilities is not clear. The IAEA believes much of Iran's highly enriched uranium was stored there. Before the strikes, Iran had accumulated an estimated 441kg of uranium enriched to 60%, close to weapons grade. Under a 2015 nuclear deal, Iran had agreed to limit its enriched uranium stockpile to 300kg and enrichment to 3.67% purity, limits it subsequently exceeded. The US and Israel said they attacked Iran's nuclear sites to prevent it developing nuclear weapons, something Iran has consistently denied seeking, saying its programme is for peaceful civilian purposes only.

Iranian Foreign Ministry spokesperson Esmaeil Baghaei said Iran remains committed to the Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty but blamed US and Israeli attacks for limiting cooperation with the IAEA. Iran told the IAEA this month that discussion on resuming inspections cannot resume amid "persistent, explicit, and public threats by the President of the United States of America … to commit further acts of armed aggression." Iran said the issue would not be resolved "until the root causes of the present situation are addressed and the essential requirements for normal safeguards implementation are restored." The IAEA said it is "fully prepared to resume its safeguards activities in Iran at short notice" and urged Iran to consider limited technical measures that could make inspection visits possible.
