Europe pushes for seat at Ukraine peace talks amid US-Russia diplomacy

International & World desk · anchored on AP

European leaders are scrambling to secure a seat at the table in peace talks to end Russia's war in Ukraine, after US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner held separate meetings with Russian President Vladimir Putin in Moscow and with Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy in Dublin. The US envoys indicated that European representatives would be included in future rounds of negotiations, but the Kremlin has since cast doubt on that, saying no final decision on European participation has been made. The discussions come as Moscow revives its maximalist demands from December 2021, calling for a new European security architecture that would halt NATO's eastward expansion and roll back alliance infrastructure from central and eastern Europe — demands Western leaders previously rejected as an ultimatum.

At an EU summit in Dublin, leaders debated who should represent Europe in the talks, with no consensus yet. EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas has put herself forward, arguing she is the natural institutional voice for the bloc, but she faces resistance from some who view her as uncompromising on Russia. European Council President Antonio Costa is also a contender, while many national leaders want to attend themselves. Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni warned that the absence of a united European voice has allowed freelance diplomacy, and German Chancellor Friedrich Merz pleaded with the US to avoid surprises, referencing the 'Anchorage moment' when European allies were stunned by US overtures to Russia without Kyiv or Europe present.

Moscow has shown little interest in including Europe, criticizing European leaders for their military support for Ukraine. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said that once Russia understands European 'true intentions and their readiness to facilitate the peace process, then it will be possible to take a decision about dialogue on Ukraine in a wider format.' Russia's foreign policy aide Yuri Ushakov has suggested Moscow will add new demands to its list, citing Western efforts to inflict a strategic defeat on Russia. The EU's top diplomat Kallas called Russia's push for a new security architecture a 'trap' designed to shift attention from a ceasefire, insisting that the priority should be a ceasefire first, then a peace agreement, and only then discussions on wider European security.

Putin appeared uncompromising and confident during the talks, buoyed by US engagement and recent outreach from European leaders. Russian media have even compared a potential meeting between Putin, US President Donald Trump, and Chinese President Xi Jinping on the sidelines of the APEC summit in Shenzhen to the 'Big Three' of Stalin, Roosevelt, and Churchill in 1945. Despite suffering huge battlefield losses and facing economic pressure from Ukrainian drone strikes on its oil refineries, Putin has shown no willingness to end the fighting. Zelenskyy has insisted on European involvement, telling leaders in Dublin that while Europeans are not yet at the table with Russia, they are already involved and must become a regular part of the negotiations.
