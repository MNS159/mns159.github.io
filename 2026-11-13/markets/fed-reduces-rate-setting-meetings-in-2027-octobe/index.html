Fed reduces rate-setting meetings in 2027, October session becomes non-voting

Financial & Markets desk · anchored on Reuters

The Federal Reserve announced Thursday that its October 2027 meeting will not include a vote on interest rates, reducing the number of rate decisions from eight to seven that year. The Federal Open Market Committee will instead dedicate the Oct. 26-27 session to "the discussion of broad monetary policy strategy issues," with no post-meeting statement or formal vote. The final meeting of 2027 has been moved from December to Nov. 16-17.

Fed Chairman Kevin Warsh, who telegraphed the change at his October press conference, said the shift is part of a broader effort to streamline communication and reduce the central bank's market footprint. "Getting policy right involves assessing near-term developments at timely, regular, and information-rich intervals," Warsh said. "But it's equally important we reserve time and space, as an intellectually diverse group, for strategic deliberations—for thinking anew about big questions, looking out over longer time horizons." He indicated that in 2028, two of the eight FOMC meetings would become non-voting strategy sessions.

Warsh has been critical of the Fed's prior communications strategy, arguing that excessive guidance on policy moves handcuffed policymakers. Since he took over, the post-meeting statement has been shortened, and press question-and-answer sessions have been cut to roughly half an hour from the customary 45 minutes. "I believe the setting of the meeting schedule and agenda is more than some administrative detail," Warsh said. "It's an institutional choice, affecting the nature and the quality of our deliberations and, ultimately, the wisdom of our decisions."

The Federal Reserve Act of 1913 mandates the FOMC meet at least four times per year. The meeting cadence varied from four to 19 over the years and was set at eight in 1981. Bloomberg reported in September that several regional Fed presidents—including Austan Goolsbee, Beth Hammack, Anna Paulson, and Jeffrey Schmid—had expressed openness to scaling back the rate-setting schedule. The October meeting was chosen for the strategy session in part because it historically preceded federal election cycles, and the Fed has typically avoided rate adjustments at that meeting to avoid the appearance of political motivation—though there have been exceptions, such as the half-point cut in 2024.

The move comes as President Donald Trump continues to call on the Fed to slash interest rates to zero. Warsh, a Trump appointee, has been cagey about the future direction of rates. The Fed raised rates by 25 basis points at its September 2026 meeting, its first hike since 2023, as inflation remains high. U.S. inflation in October reached an annualized rate of 4%, double the Fed's 2% target. Financial markets have priced in a 100% chance of another 25-basis-point hike at the Dec. 8-9 meeting. The tentative 2027 FOMC schedule includes meetings on Jan. 26-27, March 16-17, April 27-28, June 8-9, July 27-28, Sept. 14-15, Oct. 26-27 (strategy only), and Nov. 16-17.
