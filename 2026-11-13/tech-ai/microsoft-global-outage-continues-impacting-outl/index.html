Microsoft Outage

Microsoft global outage continues, impacting Outlook and cloud services

Tech / AI desk

Microsoft’s massive outage, which began Thursday and disrupted Outlook email, Teams, and a wide range of cloud services worldwide, is slowly recovering as the company works to restore full functionality. The incident, initially reported in the U.S. and the Americas, quickly spread globally, affecting millions of users and knocking out not only email and video conferencing but also Microsoft’s Copilot AI tools, Defender security software, SharePoint Online, and system administrator dashboards. Some users also experienced issues accessing cloud-based Windows computers.

By Friday morning U.S. East Coast time, Microsoft reported that service availability and email delivery performance were improving across a growing portion of affected infrastructure. Senior Verge correspondent Tom Warren, citing sources, linked the outage to Azure VNet configuration changes. Microsoft has stated there is no evidence of malicious activity, describing the event as a technical issue unrelated to cyberattacks, AI agents, or DDoS. The company said it continues to investigate the root cause and will share a full report after mitigation.

Charles Lamanna, Microsoft’s Copilot, agents, and platform leader, said in a statement: “We’re seeing encouraging recovery progress, with service availability and email delivery performance improving across a growing portion of affected infrastructure. We know our customers rely on our services to get important work done every day, and regret the impact these outages had on those who depend on us. Our teams are reviewing what happened and how we can prevent similar issues going forward.”

Despite the recovery, some users may still experience intermittent connectivity issues or delayed email delivery, Microsoft said in a status update. Doug Madory, director of internet analysis at Kentik, noted on Bluesky that traffic was returning to Microsoft’s services after about 18 hours of downtime. The outage affected a broad range of industries, including TechCrunch’s own email, and raised questions about Microsoft’s ability to swiftly recover from an incident of its own making.

This is the second major Microsoft cloud outage in recent months. In September, a days-long disruption was attributed to a “core authentication configuration” issue that prevented users from accessing services. Microsoft has not yet provided a detailed explanation for the current outage, and a spokesperson did not respond to questions about whether the company has logs or other means to detect malicious activity. The company has promised a full post-incident review.
