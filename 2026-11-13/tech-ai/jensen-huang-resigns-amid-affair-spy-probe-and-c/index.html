Nvidia Scandal

Jensen Huang resigns amid affair, spy probe, and congressional investigations

Tech / AI desk

Jensen Huang resigned as CEO of Nvidia on Thursday, ending his 33-year tenure at the helm of the AI chip giant amid a widening scandal involving extramarital affairs, a federal espionage investigation, and congressional inquiries. The resignation came after reports that Huang had affairs with a Chinese executive suspected of being a spy and with at least two Nvidia employees.

According to a TechCrunch timeline, U.S. officials believe that one of Huang's romantic interests, Megaspeed executive Alice Huang (no relation), is a Chinese spy who has been feeding information to Beijing. Megaspeed, a Singapore-based AI infrastructure company, purchased $4.8 billion worth of Nvidia chips over the past two years and has been investigated by the U.S. and Singapore for allegedly illegally sharing chips with China. The Justice Department has taken over the federal investigation. Separately, The New York Times reported that Huang also had an extramarital relationship with Kimberly Powell, Nvidia's vice president of healthcare, who reports directly to him. Reuters later reported a third affair with another unnamed Nvidia employee.

Beyond the personal and national security dimensions, Huang's leadership left a significant environmental footprint. Under his watch, Nvidia's supply-chain emissions grew 725% since 2020, reaching about 10.7 million metric tons of CO2 in fiscal year 2026, according to the company's sustainability report. An analysis by researcher Ketan Joshi and environmental groups estimated that emissions from the use of Nvidia chips could have reached 21 million metric tons in 2025, comparable to Russia's state-owned coal company. Huang had defended AI's environmental cost, comparing it to a surgeon who "has to cut you open to save you," and argued that AI is the best opportunity to address climate change.

Environmental groups have long criticized Nvidia's lack of accountability. Greenpeace East Asia's Avex Li said in a press release that Huang "failed to put climate responsibility at the heart of the company’s growth strategy" and urged interim CEO Colette Kress to commit Nvidia to decarbonizing through renewable energy and ensuring its technology respects human rights. Greenpeace activists disrupted Huang's speech at a Berlin conference last month, and the organization says Nvidia has not met with them despite two years of attempts. Katrin Wu of Greenpeace International stated, "Huang may be gone, but the ‘tech colonialism’ Nvidia perpetuates continues."

The scandal has thrust Nvidia into an unprecedented spotlight just as data centers face growing backlash from communities. Reports indicate that Huang was not confident in his successor, Colette Kress, and described him as "broken." The company's influence on global AI infrastructure and its dealings in China are now under intense scrutiny, with implications for the stability of the AI industry and U.S.-China tech competition. Nvidia did not immediately respond to requests for comment from The Verge.
