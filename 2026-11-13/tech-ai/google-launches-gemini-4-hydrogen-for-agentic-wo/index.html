Google launches Gemini 4 Hydrogen for agentic workflows, replacing 3.8 Flash across products

Tech / AI desk

Google has released Gemini 4 Hydrogen, a new AI model designed for fast, efficient execution across agentic workflows, replacing the previous Gemini 3.8 Flash across the company's products. The model is rolling out immediately to all tiers of Google AI Studio, the Gemini API, the Gemini app, Google Search, and paid Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers in Google Antigravity, with access beginning globally for all Search users. Google claims Hydrogen completes tasks 2.5 times faster than 3.8 Flash at its default high thinking level, reduces fabricated claims by more than five times, and more than triples 3.8 Flash's score on Terminal-Bench 4.1 (57% vs. 18.2%).

Hydrogen offers four thinking levels — low, medium, high (default), and a new extra-high tier called xhigh — allowing users to balance quality, latency, and cost. At its high default setting, Hydrogen scored 51 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, surpassing the more powerful Gemini 4 Argon (50), and reached 54 at the xhigh setting. The model is priced at $1.50 per million input tokens and $7.50 per million output tokens across its entire 1 million-token context window. On multi-step tasks like deep research and data analysis, Hydrogen ranks No. 5 on Agent Arena's live benchmark.

Google positions Hydrogen as a complement to its frontier models, Gemini 4 Carbon and Gemini 4 Argon, which are designed for deep, long-horizon reasoning on hard problems. Hydrogen is built for rapid execution in everyday coding, agentic workflows, and multi-model collaboration. To facilitate this, Google introduced Orchestrator in Google Antigravity, which dynamically switches between models like Carbon and Hydrogen within the same session. On Terminal-Bench 4.0, Orchestrator reached near-Carbon task success rates while consuming 24% less quota and completing tasks 19% faster on average.

In Google Search, Hydrogen brings several improvements: better understanding of user intent, including handling multi-part queries, highly specific requests, and hard-to-understand typos; more visual responses such as images, structured responses, comparison tables, and custom tools in AI Mode; and an upgraded query fan-out technique that breaks single prompts into multiple subqueries to surface a wider range of relevant links. Google called Hydrogen its 'biggest model improvement to Search yet.'

For developers and enterprise users, Hydrogen excels in iterative repository coding and debugging, open-ended agentic execution, multimodal knowledge work, and web development. It leads its class on HLE-Diamond (58.8%), ranks No. 1 overall on Vals Tax Agent Bench (52.3%), ties for No. 1 on Vals Legal Research Bench (55.8%), and leads its class on AutomationBench-AA. On the new Multimodal Knowledge Agent benchmark, Hydrogen scored 53.4% for end-to-end agentic knowledge work over complex visual files, and on Agents' Last Exam v1.1 it scored 71.6% for professional workflows. In early testing with partners, Hydrogen ran 2x to 2.8x faster than 3.8 Flash while reducing token usage by 12% to 23%.

In the Gemini app, Hydrogen improves multi-turn conversations by better following user guidance, calling the right tools, and producing reliable outputs with less back-and-forth. It can pull context from uploads, Google apps like Gmail and Google Drive, and connected apps like Airtable and Dropbox. Google says Hydrogen is designed to stand on its own for everyday work or to pair with Carbon and Argon as a high-efficiency execution engine in multi-model workflows.
