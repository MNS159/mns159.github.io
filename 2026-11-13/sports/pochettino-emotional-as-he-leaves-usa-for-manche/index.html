Pochettino emotional as he leaves USA for Manchester United job

Sports desk

Mauricio Pochettino has been appointed Manchester United head coach, signing a two-and-a-half-year contract after United paid a release clause of approximately £7.4 million to extract him from his role as USA national team boss, with an additional payment for his backroom staff. The 54-year-old Argentine broke down in tears during his first news conference after the move was confirmed, describing the decision to leave the US job as 'one of the most difficult' of his career.

'It's difficult to talk now,' Pochettino said. 'I'm a very emotional guy. It's a very sad decision. But we are professionals and we are trying to make our dreams come true.' Pochettino had signed a new four-year deal with the USA in the summer but will depart after leading the national team in matches against Haiti on 14 and 18 November. Asked why he signed a new deal only to accept United's offer, he said: 'I never said I wanted to go back but they know there aren't too many opportunities that happen in your career like Manchester United. When it arrives, it's difficult to turn down.'

Pochettino replaces Michael Carrick, who was sacked on Thursday after United's worst start to a domestic campaign in 40 years, leaving the club 18th in the Premier League with just one win in 10 league matches, one point above bottom-placed Sunderland. Carrick released a statement thanking fans for their support, writing: 'I've had the immense privilege of leading Manchester United, the football club that I love. There are very few who get to live that and feel it, and for that I'm incredibly grateful.' Pochettino's first match in charge will be an away match against Liverpool on 22 November.

Pochettino's arrival at Old Trafford marks the end of a decade-long courtship by United. He first came to prominence during an 18-month stint at Southampton, leading them to eighth place in his only full season, before moving to Tottenham in 2014. At Spurs, his reputation rocketed as he led the club to top-four finishes four times in five full seasons — a club that had finished in the top four just twice in the 22 years before his appointment. United first considered a move for Pochettino two years into his time in north London, when he dined with legendary former United boss Sir Alex Ferguson in Mayfair weeks before Louis van Gaal was sacked. The club ultimately appointed Jose Mourinho, then Ole Gunnar Solskjaer, and later Erik ten Hag, before finally hiring Pochettino.

Pochettino has described himself as a 'disciple' of Argentine coach Marcelo Bielsa and has said his sides need 'to be protagonists, always press high, work hard and be dynamic.' He has also expressed deep admiration for Ferguson, naming him as the three Premier League managers he would have loved to have played for. 'When I was young, when I started my career as a manager, he was always my inspiration, my reference,' Pochettino said. United officials have stressed unanimity in the hierarchy regarding the appointment, stating privately that any claim of a split between the Glazer family and Sir Jim Ratcliffe is 'categorically false.'
