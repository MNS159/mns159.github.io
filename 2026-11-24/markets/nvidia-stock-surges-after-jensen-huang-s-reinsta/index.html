Nvidia stock surges 30% after Huang reinstatement, adding nearly $1 trillion in market cap; analysts question durability of rally

Financial & Markets desk

Nvidia shares rocketed more than 30% on Monday, posting their best single-day gain since May 2003 and adding nearly $1 trillion in market capitalization, after the company's board reinstated Jensen Huang as CEO and president. The surge lifted major U.S. stock indexes, which had closed Friday in correction territory — the S&P 500 alone tumbled more than 9% in the weeks following Huang's Nov. 12 resignation over an alleged affair with a woman reported to be a Chinese intelligence asset.

Nvidia's board said it had held discussions with Huang and was satisfied with his candor and ability to lead. "The board believes Jensen is the right person to lead Nvidia, and that his reinstatement is in the best interests of our stockholders, employees, partners and customers," chair Harvey Jones said in a statement. Jones, who will remain chair, thanked interim CEO Colette Kress. Huang said he was "honored to return." The board said it had received input from senior U.S. government officials and concluded that Huang's reinstatement poses no national security risk.

The relief rally also boosted AI-related stocks: shares of neoclouds CoreWeave and Nebius each climbed more than 30%. But Wall Street strategists cautioned that the bounce may not last. "I think the rally will probably fizzle out because now people will pull back and still have the same questions we had before he was replaced," said Jay Woods, chief market strategist at Freedom Capital Markets. Mark Gibbens of Gibbens Capital Management called the reinstatement "a bottom and a floor in the AI trade," while Art Hogan of B. Riley Wealth Management noted that Friday's indiscriminate selling had already signaled a possible bottom before the Nvidia news.

The sell-off that preceded Huang's return stemmed from broader concerns: worries about data center approvals after the midterms, fears that hyperscaler capital expenditure may have peaked, and the national security implications of Huang's relationship. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent initially urged the board to find an outside CEO, but reversed course after President Donald Trump intervened. Trump told CNBC on Monday that he felt compelled to act because "I watched our country lose $5 trillion because of a bad election" and that if he had not gotten involved, "you'd probably have a very sad Nvidia." Bessent downplayed market risks, saying Nvidia "continues to be fantastically profitable."

Despite the rally, lingering questions remain. Peter Boockvar, chief investment officer at One Point BFG Wealth Partners, said the "picks and shovels" trade, including Nvidia, depends on the rate of capex spending. Goldman Sachs traders summed up the uncertainty: "Not sure yet whether good or bad that the fate of the market is almost equal to the fate of one person." On Capitol Hill, Sen. Tom Cotton told The Wall Street Journal that "we need to catalog the damage that's been done" and accused Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick of not responding to letters about China threats. Congress is expected to investigate the episode, including whether China used the affair to divert advanced chips for military use.
