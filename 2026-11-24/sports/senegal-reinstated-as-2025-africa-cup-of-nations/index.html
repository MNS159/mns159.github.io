Senegal reinstated as Africa Cup of Nations 2025 champions by CAS ruling

Sports desk

Senegal have been reinstated as winners of the 2025 Africa Cup of Nations after the Court of Arbitration for Sport overturned a previous decision that had stripped the West Africans of the title they won on the pitch during a chaotic final against host nation Morocco in January. The CAS panel ruled unanimously that the referee's decision to resume the match after Senegal's walk-off fell within his sole authority, upholding the original result of a 1-0 victory for Senegal after extra time. The ruling comes 10 months after the tournament and more than six weeks after a closed-door hearing in Lausanne on 8 October.

In its verdict, CAS stated that 'the referee's decision to resume the match or to stop it fell definitively within his sole authority' and that 'by blowing his whistle to restart play, the referee considered that the conditions for resuming the match had been met'. Senegal's football federation (FSF) said it 'welcomes this ruling with deep satisfaction', adding that it 'affirms the triumph of sporting fairness and reaffirms a fundamental principle: the true outcome of a match is determined exclusively on the field of play'. The federation posted a picture of the team lifting the trophy with the caption 'again and always champions of Africa'.

The drama in the final erupted in second-half stoppage time when referee Jean-Jacques Ndala awarded Morocco a penalty after consulting his pitchside monitor. Senegal coach Pape Thiaw led several players off the field in protest, causing a delay of over 15 minutes before Brahim Diaz's Panenka-style spot-kick was saved by Edouard Mendy. Pape Gueye scored the winning goal in extra time. In March, a Confederation of African Football appeals board ruled that Senegal had forfeited the match by breaching competition regulations when they left the field, awarding Morocco a 3-0 victory. Senegal swiftly appealed to CAS. The three-man panel was chaired by French lawyer Gerald Simon, with Italian professor Luigi Fumagalli nominated by Morocco and German professor Ulrich Haas nominated by Senegal.

Morocco's football federation (FRMF) expressed 'surprise' at the ruling and said it would 'pursue all available legal avenues to challenge the decision'. It has raised a grievance alleging that Haas held a telephone call with FSF president Abdoulaye Fall on Sunday, which Fall 'categorically' denies. The FRMF could appeal to the Swiss Federal Tribunal, which has the power to set aside a CAS award; a precedent exists in the case of Chinese swimmer Sun Yang, whose doping ban was later reduced after a new CAS hearing. Meanwhile, CAF said it respects the award and 'will take all the necessary measures to implement it'.

The walk-off during the final has already prompted a change in football's rules. In April, the International Football Association Board (Ifab) introduced a rule allowing referees to issue red cards to players who leave the field in protest, stating that 'a team that causes a match to be abandoned will, in principle, forfeit the match'. Both Morocco and Senegal have since changed coaches and secured qualification for the 2027 Afcon with two rounds of qualifying remaining. The full grounds for the CAS decision will be published unless the parties request confidentiality, and the debate over the incident's impact on African football's reputation is expected to persist.
