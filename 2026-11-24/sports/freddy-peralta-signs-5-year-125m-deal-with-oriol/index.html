Freddy Peralta signs 5-year, $125M deal with Orioles

Sports desk

The Baltimore Orioles signed right-handed starter Freddy Peralta to a five-year, $125 million contract, the team announced Tuesday. The deal includes a $10 million signing bonus and equal annual salaries of $23 million, with no deferred money or opt-outs, per Noah Trister of the Associated Press. Peralta, who turns 31 in the 2027 season, was ineligible for a qualifying offer after being traded during the 2026 season, so Baltimore will not forfeit a draft pick.

Peralta arrives after a turbulent 2026 campaign. He struggled with the New York Mets, posting a nearly 5.00 ERA over 22 starts, but was traded to the Tampa Bay Rays at the deadline and rebounded, throwing 55 1/3 innings of 3.25 ERA ball in the regular season and earning ALCS MVP honors in the postseason. His turnaround stemmed from adjustments made by the Rays, including raising his arm angle and pitching exclusively from the stretch, which boosted his fastball velocity and command, though his strikeout rate did not fully return to Milwaukee levels.

The signing comes a day after the Orioles lost left-hander Trevor Rogers to the Chicago Cubs on a five-year, $120 million contract. Rogers' departure netted Baltimore a compensatory draft pick after the first round. Orioles president of baseball operations Mike Elias said in a statement, "Freddy has proven to be a winning player who understands what it takes to compete and succeed on the biggest stage. His professionalism, preparation, and commitment to winning will set a strong example for our organization."

Peralta's $125 million guarantee places him between Rogers and left-hander Ranger Suarez, who signed for five years and $130 million. Peralta was considered a potential $200 million-plus free agent before his Mets struggles, but his late-season rebound allowed him to secure a deal reflecting a No. 2 starter's value. He has not been on the injured list since 2022 and is one of two pitchers—along with Kevin Gausman—to make at least 30 starts in each of the past four seasons.

Peralta joins a Baltimore rotation fronted by Kyle Bradish and Shane Baz, with Brandon Young slotting in as the No. 4 starter. The team will hold a competition for the final spot among Trey Gibson, Cade Povich, and several upper-minors prospects. The Orioles entered the day with an estimated 2027 payroll around $127 million, giving them room to add another mid-level salary after adding Peralta's $25 million average annual value.

The Orioles' fourth-place finish in the AL East in 2026 raised questions about the state of their rebuild, particularly amid stalled development from young position players like Jackson Holliday, Gunnar Henderson, and Colton Cowser, and the trade of catcher Adley Rutschman. Peralta is expected to be a frontline starter for a club that may still seek a true No. 1 arm via trade, with candidates such as Reid Detmers or Logan Webb mentioned in reports.
