FDA Approvals

Roche breast cancer pill wins second FDA nod, expanding use to early-stage disease

Health & Science desk

Roche’s oral breast cancer drug giredestrant (Wilserdly) received its second U.S. Food and Drug Administration approval in a week on Monday, November 23, 2026, clearing it for use as a monotherapy in adults with early-stage, estrogen receptor (ER)-positive, HER2-negative breast cancer following surgery. The approval covers patients with stage I, II, or III disease who have an intermediate to high risk of recurrence, making giredestrant the first new endocrine therapy for early-stage breast cancer in more than 20 years.

The decision follows a November 18 approval of giredestrant in combination with everolimus for ER-positive, HER2-negative, ESR1-mutated locally advanced or metastatic breast cancer. With the two approvals, giredestrant becomes the first selective estrogen receptor degrader (SERD) indicated for both early- and advanced-stage breast cancer. ER antagonists like giredestrant block estrogen from binding to receptors on cancer cells and promote degradation of the receptors, cutting off a key driver of tumor growth.

The early-stage approval was supported by the Phase 3 lidERA trial, the first Phase 3 study to show a benefit with an oral ER antagonist in early-stage breast cancer. Compared with standard hormone therapy, giredestrant reduced the risk of invasive breast cancer recurrence or death by 30% (hazard ratio 0.70; 95% confidence interval 0.57–0.87; p=0.0014). At three years, 92.4% of patients receiving giredestrant were alive and free of invasive breast cancer versus 89.6% on standard therapy, corresponding to about 28 additional patients per 1,000 remaining free of invasive disease. Patients on giredestrant also had a lower risk of distant recurrence. Overall survival data were immature but showed a positive trend. Fewer patients discontinued giredestrant due to adverse events than those on standard therapy (5.3% vs. 8.2%). Common side effects include muscle and joint pain, hot flashes, fatigue, headache, and nausea; laboratory abnormalities include elevated liver enzymes, decreased blood cell counts, and high blood lipid levels. Women who have not completed menopause are instructed to take an additional hormone-suppressing medicine with giredestrant.

“By addressing a critical need in early-stage disease, where the chance for cure is greatest, [giredestrant] has the potential to establish an important new standard of care for patients and physicians,” said Levi Garraway, MD, PhD, chief medical officer and head of global product development at Genentech, in a news release. Teresa Graham, chief executive officer of Roche Pharmaceuticals, said the company will continue to invest in the broad development of giredestrant, exploring its potential across different stages of the disease and in new combinations. Participants in the lidERA trial will be followed to assess overall survival, and Genentech agreed to submit those data to the FDA as a postmarketing commitment.
