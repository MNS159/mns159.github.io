Ukraine sinks Russian warship, strikes cargo ships in widening sea campaign

Tech / AI desk

Ukrainian explosive naval drones sank a Russian missile corvette docked in the Libyan port city of Benghazi and struck a Russian cargo ship it was escorting, according to reports from The Associated Press and statements from Ukrainian officials. The attack, which occurred on November 22, used at least two drone strikes to sink the Russian Navy’s Merkury, with Ukraine’s Security Service describing a total of seven naval drones launched from a mother ship. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy released video footage showing the drone boats approaching and exploding against both the burning warship and the dry cargo vessel MV Angara.

Zelenskyy noted that a Ka-27 combat helicopter aboard the Merkury was also destroyed in the strikes. He described the MV Angara as transporting weapons. Casualties included at least 10 people, among them Libyan and Russian nationals, according to Libyan National Army officials who also reported that some wounded crew members from the warship were transferred to local hospitals.

The Merkury was a Steregushchiy-class missile corvette, designated by Russia as Project 20380. This class carries both anti-ship and air-defense missiles, is armed with torpedoes for counter-submarine operations, and mounts a naval cannon on deck. Although corvettes are typically smaller than destroyers or frigates, NATO classifies Russian missile corvettes like the Merkury as equivalent to frigates.

Originally intended to join Russia’s Black Sea Fleet, the Merkury was unable to enter the Black Sea after Russia’s full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022 because Turkey invoked the 1936 Montreux Convention to restrict warship passage. Instead, the vessel mainly operated from Russia’s Northern Fleet bases in Arctic waters, according to naval analyst HI Sutton.
