Jensen Huang reinstated as Nvidia CEO amid scandal after Trump, tech allies pressure board

Tech / AI desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts slightly center-left

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang returned to his post on Monday, less than two weeks after resigning over a scandal involving an affair with a customer and suspected Chinese spy, as well as a second reported affair with a subordinate. The board's abrupt reversal was spurred by President Donald Trump, who applied pressure over the weekend after the company and the wider tech sector lost value. Huang received a hero’s welcome at Nvidia’s Santa Clara headquarters, with employees lining walkways to applaud him.

The reinstatement followed frenetic lobbying by a war room of Huang's Silicon Valley allies, who argued to Trump and Vice President JD Vance that the founder was integral to the health of the U.S. AI industry and the broader economy. "Great men make great mistakes. But nobody has ever been like this, not Elon Musk, not anyone. All the success we’re enjoying, all the tech gains, that’s all down to one guy," said an ally of Huang. Nvidia gained nearly $1 trillion in market value on the news, though it remains about a fifth less valuable than a month ago.

During his first all-hands meeting since returning, Huang called claims that Trump was directly involved in his reinstatement — a narrative promoted by Trump himself — "completely untrue." He added, "Don’t think for a second politics played a role in the company. We are the most apolitical company," according to attendees. Huang said he did not return because he was a tech bro "above the law" or the code of conduct. He apologized, saying, "I made some terrible mistakes. I was unfaithful to Lori, and I hurt my kids and of course, I hurt all of you." He also said he does not believe in succession planning and is attending therapy daily.

Huang admitted to more affairs beyond the two publicly reported — with Megaspeed executive Alice Huang (an alleged Chinese spy) and Nvidia VP of health Kimberly Powell. During a meeting with he board before his reinstatement, he acknowledged additional affairs, according to The Wall Street Journal. Nvidia faces probes by the FBI and SEC, congressional investigations, and questions about his personal connections to the suspected spy involved in chip smuggling. The Commerce Department has added Megaspeed and its affiliates to its Entity List, citing national security risks and suspicions of smuggling advanced AI chips to China. The DOJ has taken over the investigation but says it is ongoing.

Tech leaders celebrated Huang’s return. Elon Musk posted on X, "The mistake has been corrected," after earlier saying it was like kicking Michael Jordan off a team on a winning streak. Sam Altman simply posted "Jensen!" Binance co-founder Changpeng Zhao expressed support, as did Epic Games CEO Tim Sweeney, who called it "a demonstration of American resiliency." Former Uber CEO Travis Kalanick also voiced support. Hedge fund manager Bill Ackman wrote, "The wisdom of the crowd prevails."

Political skepticism remains. Senators John Cornyn (R-TX) asked, "No national security risk? Based on what?" and Pete Ricketts (R-NB) said the decision doesn't excuse Huang from testifying before Congress. Huang is still expected to testify publicly before the Senate Banking Committee on December 3 and privately before Congress on December 1. The Trump administration backed Huang's return while restricting trade with Megaspeed, after facing accusations that Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick was too close to Huang and failed to police chip sales to China.
