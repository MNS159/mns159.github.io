Trump's role in Nvidia CEO reinstatement sparks political backlash

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts slightly center-left

US Attorney General Todd Blanche on Tuesday declined to comment on whether Nvidia or its reinstated CEO Jensen Huang is under federal investigation, calling the company a 'good corporate citizen' while saying he had 'no information' about Huang's security clearance status. The remarks came as lawmakers from both parties demanded answers about President Donald Trump's involvement in Huang's return to the helm of the AI chipmaker less than two weeks after he was forced to resign over revelations of extramarital affairs, including one with an alleged Chinese spy.

Nvidia's board unanimously voted to reinstate Huang as CEO and president on Monday, reversing its earlier position. Board chairman Harvey Jones said the decision was in the 'best interests of our stockholders, employees, partners and customers'. The board added that it had received input from senior levels of the US government and was confident Huang's reinstatement posed no national security risk. Trump said Monday he spoke to the company's directors and convinced them to bring back Huang. The White House has emphasized that Trump has placed his businesses in a trust managed by his sons, though the president has personally invested in Nvidia.

The controversy centers on Huang's relationship with Alice Huang (no blood relation), a former executive for Singapore-based tech company Megaspeed, which is suspected of helping divert advanced Nvidia AI chips to China in violation of US export restrictions. The US commerce department has announced it is preparing to blacklist Megaspeed. In a joint statement last week, the commerce and justice departments said they began investigating Megaspeed in 2024 'for potential export control violations.' Blanche said the justice department has 'hundreds, if not more' export investigations at any one time, adding: 'I have no reason to stand up here and say Nvidia is anything but a good corporate citizen.'

The reinstatement has drawn bipartisan political anger. Republican Representative Don Bacon said Nvidia should have 'ignored the Administration's inexplicable desire to save Huang.' Republican Senator Pete Ricketts said Huang's behavior 'doesn't absolve Nvidia of answering for national security concerns and unaccounted-for chips.' Democratic Representative Robert Garcia, incoming chairman of the House oversight committee, said Trump's 'direct involvement' raises 'serious national security and corruption questions.' Democratic Senator Ruben Gallego called for Congress to 'investigate what stock positions took place before the announcement.' Huang is set to testify before Congress, where he will be questioned over his relationship with the Megaspeed executive due to national security concerns.

In China, Huang's reinstatement became the top trending post on Weibo, with many users pleased to see the return of the influential billionaire who declared he loved China. Beijing has dismissed claims that Alice Huang was recruited by China's Ministry of State Security as a spy, with state media framing the saga as a smear campaign. The episode has reinforced views among Chinese officials that Washington is divided between those favoring restricting Chinese access to cutting-edge tech and those like Huang who argue such restrictions will force Chinese firms to build independent alternatives. New US rules are tightening restrictions on advanced chip exports and targeting Chinese firms' ability to use computing power in overseas data centers, while China has yet to retaliate.

Former CIA officer Julia Curlee, who worked on the White House national security council during Trump's first term, said the president was warned about the risks posed by Huang's relationship but failed to act, calling it 'the latest episode of the president ignoring or not believing the intelligence that's being provided to him.' Arkansas Senator Tom Cotton wrote on X that the Nvidia controversy 'is just the tip of the iceberg and it's not resolved because Jensen Huang has his job back.' Nvidia's stock market value surged on the announcement of Huang's reappointment but reversed some gains in volatile Tuesday trading, with shares down more than 7% in early afternoon trading, though still above Friday's close before the return was announced.
