Trump signals possible resumption of Iran strikes as Iran rebuilds and Israel faces growing threats

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts right

President Donald Trump has signaled his willingness to resume military strikes against Iran next year following recent drone attacks, Fox News reported, as tensions in the Middle East escalate. The development comes as Iran rebuilds its military capabilities and Israel faces a growing strategic dilemma, according to analyses by the Jerusalem Post and Al Jazeera.

The Jerusalem Post reported that the Trump administration's stalling tactics have allowed Iranian hardliners to gain the upper hand. Iran now holds three strategic cards: control over the Strait of Hormuz, the vulnerability of some 50,000 US troops deployed across the Middle East, and the ability to threaten Gulf state oil facilities with its arsenal of missiles, rockets, drones, and UAVs. The analysis described Iran as "a wounded, desperate, and dangerous animal that has laid a carefully planned ambush" for the US.

The war with Iran, now in its 10th month, has cost more than $47 billion, according to Pentagon figures cited by Al Jazeera. Repairing damaged US bases could cost $9.4 billion, according to the Center for Strategic and International Studies. Hundreds of structures have been damaged since February 28. Naval Support Activity Bahrain, home to the Fifth Naval Fleet, sustained serious damage with repairs estimated at more than $400 million. Dozens of aircraft, including F-35A and F-15E fighter jets, have been lost.

Experts told Al Jazeera that the US may reduce its military footprint in the region after the war. Michael O'Hanlon of the Brookings Institution said, "I think we will go smaller, more dispersed, more hardened, and generally further away from Iran." Mark Cancian of CSIS said rebuilding is unlikely until a ceasefire, and some bases may not be rebuilt. Kelly Grieco of the Stimson Center argued that access to a network of airstrips and temporary bases may matter more than permanent operating facilities.

New alliances could further reduce reliance on the US. In August, Saudi Arabia, Turkiye, and Pakistan signed the Mecca Joint Defence Agreement, committing to mutual defense. Their combined active military personnel exceeds 1.4 million, and their defense budgets total more than $124 billion. The pact is expected to expand to include Egypt and Syria.

Israel faces a critical decision as Washington is perceived to be walking into Iran's ambush, according to the Jerusalem Post. Jerusalem must choose between waiting for US action, risking a painful Iranian surprise, or launching its own preemptive move to reshuffle the strategic landscape across the Middle East.
