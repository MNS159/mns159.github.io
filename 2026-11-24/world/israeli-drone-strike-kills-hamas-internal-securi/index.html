Israeli drone strike kills Hamas internal security chief in Gaza

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

An Israeli drone strike in western Gaza City has killed Naeem Abu Nahel, the head of Hamas's internal security service, according to Gaza's Hamas-run interior ministry and the Israeli military. The 50-year-old had held the position since September, when his predecessor was captured by Israeli special forces. Witnesses reported that Abu Nahel was targeted as he walked on a side street near a tent camp for displaced people.

At least seven Palestinians were killed in the series of Israeli air strikes across Gaza, health officials and first responders said. In addition to Abu Nahel, three people including a teenage boy were killed in the Wadi Gaza area south of Gaza City, and three others died at a police station in the al-Mawasi area west of Khan Younis. Gaza's police force said two of its officers were among those killed in the second attack and accused Israel of an ongoing campaign targeting the police apparatus to sow chaos. The Israeli military said it struck military operatives in both areas.

The Israeli military said Abu Nahel possessed extensive institutional knowledge about the internal security service, which is responsible for enforcing discipline, protecting infrastructure, and rooting out informants. It alleged he had advanced attacks on Israeli troops and civilians during the war and that his death constituted a blow to the apparatus's management capabilities. Gaza's interior ministry said the killing followed what it described as important achievements by the internal security service in combating gangs collaborating with Israel.

Violence has continued in Gaza since a US-brokered ceasefire was agreed in October last year. Israeli strikes occur almost daily in areas under Hamas's control, home to 2.1 million people, most of them displaced. Over the past 13 months, at least 1,679 people have been killed in Gaza, according to the territory's Hamas-run health ministry, whose figures are considered reliable by the UN. Israel says six of its soldiers have been killed in the same period.

The humanitarian situation remains dire, with heavy rainfall earlier this month causing flooding and damage to thousands of shelters. The UN and its partners have called on Israel, which controls Gaza's crossings, to allow in more supplies for winter, including tents, heaters, water pumps, vaccines, and medicines. The health ministry warned of a rapid rise in meningitis cases, with 272 reported in October, about eight times the usual monthly average, and 15 deaths attributed to the infection so far this year. The Israeli military body Cogat said it was working to provide a dedicated humanitarian response and had presented a newly approved package of equipment to UN agencies.

The Gaza war was triggered by the Hamas-led attack on southern Israel on 7 October 2023, in which about 1,200 people were killed and 251 taken hostage. Since then, more than 74,740 people have been killed in Israeli attacks in Gaza, the health ministry says.
