Knicks, Karl-Anthony Towns far apart on extension as second-apron constraints loom

Sports desk

The New York Knicks and star center Karl-Anthony Towns are in a contract standoff as the sides remain far apart on a four-year extension, with Towns publicly acknowledging Monday that the chances of a deal before the season "don't look good." Towns, who is entering the final guaranteed year of his contract, is eligible for a four-year extension worth up to $276 million, but multiple reports indicate the Knicks have offered roughly $200 million. "No (expletive), Sherlock. I just won a championship. You'd think it would be pretty easy," Towns said when asked if he believed he deserved a new deal.

The Knicks' reluctance to offer a max extension stems not from owner James Dolan being cheap, according to multiple reports, but from the punishing financial restrictions of the NBA's second apron — a team salary threshold set at $221 million this season. Dolan stated after the Knicks' 2023 championship that the team would not cross the second apron, calling such a move "suicidal." Only one team, the Cleveland Cavaliers, operated above the second apron last season. Teams that cross it face severe roster-building restrictions, including limitations on signings and trades.

The Knicks' cap situation is tight even without a Towns max deal. New York currently has roughly $93 million in room below the second apron. If the Knicks exercise Josh Hart's $22.3 million team option for next season and Towns picks up his $61 million player option for 2027-28, the team would have only about $10 million in space below the apron with four roster spots to fill and Miles McBride still unsigned. Further down the line, Jalen Brunson and OG Anunoby will be due for new contracts in 2028, and Mikal Bridges the following year. Paying Towns the full max would likely force the Knicks to short or discard another core player.

Some around the league have speculated that the Knicks are setting up Towns as a scapegoat. One prominent agent told Heavy Sports that the team may be positioning itself to blame Towns if it later trades a popular player like Hart to avoid the second apron. "If you have to trade a popular guy like Josh, the Knicks want to say, 'Well, we had to do that because KAT would not take $50 million less than the max,'" the agent said. "They're just throwing him under the bus in advance."

Despite the impasse, most observers expect a deal to eventually get done. Towns has become an irreplaceable player for the Knicks, with his stretch shooting and high-hub playmaking unlocking New York's postseason offense during the championship run. He was arguably the team's best player in the playoffs. The Knicks have also brought in Ochai Agbaji, Bruce Brown, John Konchar, James Wiseman and Drew Eubanks for training camp, with one of those wings potentially serving as a replacement for McBride if the team trades him before the deadline to manage future cap constraints. The Knicks' situation mirrors that of the Oklahoma City Thunder, which parted with Lu Dort, Aaron Wiggins and Isaiah Joe this summer to keep its championship core intact.

The standoff reflects the broader reality of the second-apron era, where even championship production does not guarantee a max contract. If Towns waives his 2027-28 player option and begins a four-year extension next season, he will be 35 by the end of the deal and making north of $50 million per year. As one analysis noted, "An NBA contract is supposed to be about future performance. As crass as it sounds, Towns may have just played the best basketball of his life." The Knicks face a deadline of Oct. 19 to reach an extension before the season begins.
