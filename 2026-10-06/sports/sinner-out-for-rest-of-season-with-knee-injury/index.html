World number one Sinner out for rest of season with knee injury

Sports desk

World number one Jannik Sinner will not play again in 2026 because of an ongoing right knee injury. The Italian has not played competitively since defending his Wimbledon title in July, and the five-time Grand Slam champion said he "needed to think about my long-term career" when deciding to end his season early.

Announcing the decision in an Instagram video, the 25-year-old said: "Me together with the doctors have decided that I need a course of treatment for my knee." He added: "The last couple of years gave me so many beautiful feelings and emotions, which this year is going to be way different. I will do everything possible to get back 100%."

Sinner was the dominant player for much of the year, winning 44 and losing just three of his singles matches. He won five Masters 1000 titles in a row between March and May, becoming the youngest man to complete the career Golden Masters, and finished 2026 with six titles on all three surfaces. He also posted a 10-1 record against top-10 players, with his sole defeat coming against Novak Djokovic in Melbourne at the start of the year.

His season included a five-set Australian Open semi-final loss to Djokovic and a shock second-round exit at the French Open in May, where Juan Manuel Cerundolo beat him in hot and humid Paris conditions to end Sinner's 30-match winning streak. He bounced back superbly at Wimbledon, surviving a five-set first round in which he bled through his shoes at one point before eventually beating Zverev in the final to defend his title. Sinner's injury means second-ranked Alexander Zverev is all but guaranteed to replace him as world number one by the end of the year. Zverev, who has won the French Open and US Open this year, trails Sinner by 1,280 points in the live rankings. Sinner, however, will be unable to defend the points he claimed at the end of last year with title triumphs in China, Vienna, Paris and the ATP Finals.
