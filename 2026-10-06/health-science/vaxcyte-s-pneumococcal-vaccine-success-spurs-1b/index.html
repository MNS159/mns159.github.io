Vaxcyte's pneumococcal vaccine succeeds in Phase 3 trial; company plans $1B fundraising

Health & Science desk

Vaxcyte said Monday that its experimental pneumococcal vaccine, VAX-31, met the primary endpoints in a Phase 3 clinical trial, positioning the California-based biotech to compete with Pfizer and Merck in the lucrative vaccine market.

VAX-31 is designed to protect against 31 serotypes, or strains, of bacteria that can cause pneumococcal disease, including pneumonia. By comparison, Pfizer's Prevnar covers 20 strains and Merck's Capvaxive covers 21 strains. In the trial, just over 3,500 adults age 50 and older were randomized to receive VAX-31, Prevnar, or Capvaxive. Investigators then measured the strength of their antibody responses against different strains.

Vaxcyte needs two more trials to deliver results before it can file for approval: a study looking at VAX-31 administered alongside an influenza vaccine and a study in adults who previously received a different pneumococcal shot.

The successful Phase 3 readout sets up Vaxcyte to challenge Pfizer and Merck in the pneumococcal vaccine market.
