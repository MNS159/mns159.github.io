Media Merger

Paramount closes $110 billion acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery to form Skydance

Tech / AI desk

Paramount announced Tuesday the completion of its $110 billion acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery, creating a combined media company called Skydance. The megamerger brings together two of the world's largest film studios, major television networks including CBS, CNN, HBO, MTV, TBS, Comedy Central, and Food Network, and the streaming platforms Paramount+ and HBO Max. Skydance will also control franchises such as 'The Lord of the Rings,' 'Game of Thrones,' the DC Universe, and 'Yellowstone.' The new company says its annual revenue will be nearly $70 billion.

David Ellison will lead Skydance alongside Ynon Kreiz, the former chairman and CEO of Mattel. Ellison’s Skydance Media merged with Paramount last year, and his family—backed by the billions of his father, Oracle co-founder Larry Ellison—is the combined company’s largest shareholder. Skydance Class B shares began trading Tuesday on the New York Stock Exchange under the ticker symbol 'SKYD.'

The deal followed a months-long bidding battle with Netflix, which had previously reached an agreement to purchase Warner Bros.’ film and television studios and streaming business, excluding its cable networks. Paramount sweetened its February bid by promising shareholders additional cash if the deal failed to close by a specified deadline and by agreeing to cover the breakup fee Warner Bros. would owe Netflix for ending its existing agreement.

Paramount cleared its last major legal hurdle last month after settling with 12 U.S. states that had sued to block the merger over antitrust concerns. Under the settlement, Skydance must release a minimum number of theatrical films over the next five years, starting with 30 in years one and two, and must spend at least $300 million more on production in the U.S. compared to what the two companies spent last year combined.

David Ellison said in a statement: 'Today is a historic day, not just for Skydance but for our entire industry. From the start, our ambition was to bring these two storied studios together and create a stronger competitor, with the talent, resources, and reach to tell great stories in every genre, on every platform, for audiences everywhere. Our focus now turns to the future: building a company that empowers creatives, entertains audiences and rewards shareholders. We couldn’t be more excited to get to work.' The merger has faced criticism from lawmakers and Hollywood talent about the negative impact of media consolidation.
