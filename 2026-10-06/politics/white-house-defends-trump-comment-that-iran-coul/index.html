White House defends Trump comment that Iran could 'take out' Los Angeles and San Diego

Politics desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum
✓ Facts
  • President Trump made the comments at a campaign rally in Grand Island, Nebraska on Monday evening.
  • Trump said: 'It's a small price to pay for keeping the world safe, for keeping our country safe... Let 'em take out Los Angeles, let 'em take out San Diego... This is a very small price to pay, and it's going to be over with very soon.'
  • White House Communications Director Steven Cheung posted on X Tuesday saying the 'media freakout' was 'not rooted in reality' and that Trump meant U.S. cities would have been threatened if a nuclear Iran had not been stopped.
  • The White House's Rapid Response account on X called Los Angeles Times reporters 'buffoons' and said Trump was describing U.S. efforts to prevent Iran from being able to 'take out' LA and San Diego with a nuclear weapon.
  • California Gov. Gavin Newsom called the remarks 'deranged and dangerous' and, in a later post, 'disgusting and disqualifying.'
  • Los Angeles Mayor Karen Bass said the comments were 'dangerous and could potentially compromise our safety.'
  • San Diego Mayor Todd Gloria said the city is 'not collateral damage' and asked Trump to stop 'reckless war talk.'
  • Los Angeles and San Diego both host major U.S. military installations, including Naval Base San Diego, Naval Amphibious Base Coronado, Marine Corps Base Camp Pendleton, the Space Systems Command headquarters, and Los Angeles Air Force Base.
○ Analysis

The controversy stems from ambiguous phrasing in a campaign speech about the administration's earlier military action against Iran's nuclear program. Trump's line—'let 'em take out Los Angeles, let 'em take out San Diego'—was interpreted by critics as either a literal invitation for attack or, as the White House insists, a rhetorical device describing a hypothetical threat that U.S. action had already neutralized. The dispute matters partly because both cities are home to significant military infrastructure, and partly because it unfolds against the backdrop of a tense Trump-Newsom relationship ahead of a possible 2028 presidential matchup, with Newsom positioning himself as a vocal Trump critic.

Steelman

The entire dispute hinges on a single ambiguous sentence from a live rally speech, and both sides are arguing past each other about intent rather than contesting any factual claim about Iran's current capabilities.

Implications

The episode is likely to become a recurring talking point in the emerging 2028 political rivalry between Trump-aligned Republicans and Newsom, while also fueling broader scrutiny of how administration officials characterize national-security rhetoric from campaign stages.
