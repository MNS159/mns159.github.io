Former German spy chief arrested on suspicion of treason and espionage

International & World desk

Germany’s former foreign intelligence chief, August Hanning, has been arrested on suspicion of treason, espionage, and obtaining classified information for a foreign power, the federal prosecutor’s office said on Tuesday. Hanning, 80, who led the Federal Intelligence Service (BND) from 1998 to 2005, was arrested in Bad Toelz-Wolfratshausen in southern Germany. His former chief of staff, identified only as Manfred D. under German privacy rules, was also arrested in Steinfurt in western Germany on charges of aiding and abetting attempted treason and treasonous espionage.

Prosecutors allege that Manfred D., who served as Hanning’s chief of staff and later as chief of staff for several subsequent BND presidents from 2012 to 2026, used his access to confidential information to pass Hanning approximately 2,000 documents, many of them classified and intended only for internal BND use, including executive intelligence briefings. The two allegedly agreed in 2010 to the arrangement, in which Manfred D. provided the documents "in exchange for remuneration." Hanning then used the material in his private consulting work, prosecutors said.

On one occasion, Hanning allegedly used classified intelligence provided by Manfred D. to draft an analysis for an officer of a foreign intelligence service. He is also accused of maintaining close contact with a representative of another foreign intelligence service for several years, passing on political information partly based on material from Manfred D. Investigators have not disclosed which foreign powers were involved and said it has yet to be determined whether the material reached its intended recipient.

The arrests stemmed from evidence found during a search of Hanning’s residence in connection with a separate case involving Hamburg businesswoman Christina Block, who has been accused of orchestrating the kidnapping of her two children during a custody dispute. Investigators reportedly discovered an electronic data storage device containing sensitive BND documents. The case is not the first involving the BND: in 2023, a high-ranking German intelligence officer, Carsten L, was charged with treason for allegedly passing state secrets to Russia, including intelligence about the war in Ukraine and the Wagner mercenary group.

German lawmakers reacted with shock. Marc Henrichmann, who chairs the parliamentary intelligence oversight committee, said, "Whoever allies themselves with the enemies of our liberal democracy will be found out." Konstantin von Notz, a Green party lawmaker on the same committee, called for a swift and thorough investigation and renewed criticism of former top officials with security clearances doing private consulting work. Hanning is due in court on Wednesday, while Manfred D. was scheduled to appear on Tuesday. Premises belonging to both men were searched in several locations, including Berlin.
