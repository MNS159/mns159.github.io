AI competition

US-China AI race intensifies as American lead erodes, analysis warns

Tech / AI desk

The United States' lead over China in artificial intelligence has all but vanished, according to a new analysis published by CNBC, which argues that Washington is increasingly reacting to Chinese breakthroughs rather than shaping the competitive environment. The analysis, written by Dewardric McNeal, managing director and senior policy analyst at Longview Global, contends that the defining question is no longer whether China can compete at the technological frontier, but whether the U.S. can adapt quickly enough to compete against a Chinese innovation ecosystem advancing on model performance, cost, deployment, customization, financing, standards, developer adoption, and global reach.

Google DeepMind unveils Gemini Robotics 2 with full body control

Tech / AI desk

Google DeepMind last week unveiled Gemini Robotics 2, the latest version of its vision-language-action (VLA) model, introducing intelligent whole-body control, advanced dexterity, and multi-robot collaboration. The updated model enables robots to reason through every movement, allowing a humanoid to walk, crouch, stretch, and manipulate objects to clean a cluttered room, and to team up with other robots to finish tasks faster. DeepMind also released Gemini Robotics ER 2, an embodied reasoning model that acts as an agent for communication and multi-step planning, and Gemini Robotics On-Device 2, a VLA optimized to run locally on robotic devices with fast adaptation to new robot embodiments using a few hours of data.

Australian booksellers condemn 'horrific' destruction of rare titles to feed AI training data

Tech / AI desk

Booksellers at Melbourne's Rare Book Fair have spoken out against what they call the 'horrific' destruction of rare titles by generative AI companies, after a US copyright lawsuit revealed Anthropic engaged in 'destructive scanning' — buying physical books, slicing off spines for efficient scanning, then pulping the remains. The practice, ruled lawful under US copyright law as 'transformative use', is now understood to be widespread across the AI industry.
