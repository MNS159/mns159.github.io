Chinese AI models gain ground in US amid policy debate over competitiveness and security

Tech / AI desk

Chinese artificial intelligence models are gaining popularity in the United States, driven by their affordability and efficiency, even as U.S. policymakers and tech companies debate potential restrictions. The latest catalyst was the July launch of Kimi K3 by Chinese startup Moonshot AI, which prompted San Francisco-based Mozilla CTO Raffi Krikorian to switch from Anthropic's Claude Fable, calling K3 "snappier." According to Sensor Tower, Kimi had more than 930,000 downloads globally in the week after K3's release, a 200% increase from the prior week, with U.S. downloads surging 387% to about 86,000. Moonshot temporarily suspended new subscriptions after demand pushed capacity near its limits.

US charges American citizen for wiping phone at border

Tech / AI desk

The US government is prosecuting American citizen Sam Tunick for allegedly wiping his phone when federal agents tried to seize it at Atlanta’s Hartsfield-Jackson airport on January 24, 2025. Tunick is charged under a little-known and rarely used statute that makes it illegal to destroy or damage property to prevent authorities from seizing it. According to prosecutors, Tunick gave agents a “duress password” that triggered the phone’s factory reset instead of the actual passcode, using a feature of the privacy-focused operating system GrapheneOS.

Space

SpaceX eyes tower catch for next Starship after 13th flight

Tech / AI desk

SpaceX completed its 13th full-scale Starship test flight on Friday, sending the vehicle on a suborbital trajectory from South Texas to an intact splashdown in the Indian Ocean west of Australia. The 408-foot-tall (124-meter) Starship Version 3 rocket lifted off from Starbase, Texas, at 5:51 pm CDT (6:51 pm EDT; 22:51 UTC), powered by 33 methane-fueled Raptor engines on the Super Heavy booster. Unlike previous water landings that ended in fire, the ship gently tipped over and floated, allowing engineers to inspect its heat shield via drone for the first time. The more than 18,000 ceramic tiles withstood temperatures up to 2,600° Fahrenheit (1,430° Celsius) during reentry.
