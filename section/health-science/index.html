Sharp Acceleration in Global Warming Detected by Scientists

Health & Science desk

Global warming has shifted into a faster gear, with the planet warming at about 0.35°C per decade over the past ten years compared to just under 0.2°C per decade from 1970 to 2015, according to a new analysis from the Potsdam Institute for Climate Impact Research (PIK). The acceleration, visible around 2013–2015, appears in all five major global temperature datasets with a statistical certainty exceeding 98%, the researchers report in Geophysical Research Letters.

Cyclospora Outbreak: How Parasite Infects Gut and Nurses Trace Sources

Health & Science desk

This summer, the United States is experiencing its largest recorded outbreak of cyclosporiasis, the disease caused by the Cyclospora parasite. As of a July 28 update from the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, 45 states have reported cases, with Michigan serving as the epicenter. More than 10,000 people in Michigan have been sickened, including over 900 in Washtenaw County alone, where public health nurses have conducted hundreds of interviews with patients. The outbreak has been linked to lettuce from Taylor Farms in central Mexico, which was voluntarily recalled on July 17, and health officials are also investigating parsley and cilantro as potential sources in North Carolina.

Study Links Cancer's Intense Gene Activity to Self-Induced DNA Damage

Health & Science desk

New research from the Hebrew University of Jerusalem suggests that cancer cells may damage their own DNA by forcing critical genes to operate at extreme levels. In a study released August 2, 2026, in Science Advances, investigators led by PhD student Osama Hidmi under Prof. Rami Aqeilan found that powerful DNA control regions called super-enhancers drive unusually intense activity in growth-related genes, placing physical strain on the DNA and leading to breaks. The damage is repeatedly repaired, but each repair cycle can introduce small errors, allowing mutations to accumulate and potentially helping tumors evolve and become more aggressive.
