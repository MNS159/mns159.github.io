Physics

Dark matter detector LUX-ZEPLIN detects unexplained signal, scientists urge caution

Health & Science desk

Scientists hunting dark matter have detected a rare particle interaction that could become their most promising clue yet, but they caution that the finding is not statistically strong enough to qualify as a discovery. The LUX-ZEPLIN (LZ) experiment, operating nearly a mile underground at the Sanford Underground Research Facility in South Dakota, recorded a single event in a region where dark matter is expected and where background signals are very low. The finding, presented at the 2026 TeV Particle Astrophysics conference in Japan, has a statistical significance of 2.6 sigma, corresponding to roughly a 0.5% chance that the event could be produced by known background sources. Particle physics generally requires a 5-sigma result to claim a discovery.

Injectable scaffold helps stroke-damaged brains rebuild in mice

Health & Science desk

Biomedical engineers at Duke University have developed an injectable biomaterial that helped repair brain damage from ischemic stroke in mice, according to a study published September 3, 2026, in Cell Biomaterials. The treatment, a microporous annealed particle scaffold (MAPS) loaded with signaling molecules, recruited the body's own immune cells, stimulated new blood vessel growth, supported neural changes, and improved motor function. Mice treated with the optimized scaffold performed better on a grid-walking test, and by eight weeks their performance could not be statistically distinguished from that of healthy controls, with the improvement persisting for the remainder of the study.

MIT engineers bacteria into living transistors for computing

Health & Science desk

MIT researchers have engineered bacteria to act as living transistors, creating modular circuit components that can be printed onto growth material and wired together into biological computers. The team demonstrated circuits capable of adding two or three inputs and routing a single input to a selected destination, according to a study published September 4, 2026, in Nature Chemical Biology.
