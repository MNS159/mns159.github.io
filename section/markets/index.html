S&P 500, Dow hit record highs on Mideast deal hopes and AI earnings

Financial & Markets desk · anchored on AP, Reuters

U.S. stocks surged Tuesday, pushing the S&P 500 and Dow Jones Industrial Average to record highs, as optimism over a potential deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz and a wave of strong corporate earnings, particularly in artificial intelligence-related tech stocks, lifted markets. The S&P 500 rose 1.5% to close at 7,600.50, just shy of its all-time closing high of 7,609.78, while the Dow climbed 693 points, or 1.3%, to a record 53,178.41. The tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite jumped 2.1% to 25,913.90, still about 4.3% below its own record set June 2.

Palantir surges after 'otherworldly' earnings, US commercial revenue soars

Financial & Markets desk

Palantir Technologies (PLTR) shares surged more than 27% on Tuesday after the company reported fiscal second-quarter earnings that beat analyst expectations and raised its full-year guidance, with CEO Alex Karp describing the results as 'otherworldly' in a call with analysts. The company posted adjusted earnings of $0.41 per share, exceeding the $0.35 consensus estimate, and revenue of $1.94 billion, compared with expectations of $1.8 billion. Revenue climbed 93% from about $1 billion a year ago, and net income rose to $1.07 billion, or 41 cents per share, from roughly $329 million, or 13 cents per share, in the same period last year.

SpaceX Reports First Quarterly Earnings Since IPO; Shares Down 24%

Financial & Markets desk · anchored on Reuters

SpaceX (SPCX) reported its first-ever quarterly earnings as a public company after the closing bell Tuesday, giving investors their first detailed look at Elon Musk’s rocket, satellite and AI business since the record IPO in June. Analysts surveyed by LSEG expected second-quarter revenue of $6.93 billion and a loss of 26 cents per share, according to CNBC. Bloomberg consensus pegged revenue at $6.81 billion and an adjusted loss of 24 cents per share, with adjusted EBITDA of $2.0 billion, Yahoo Finance UK reported. The company’s reported figures may not be directly comparable to consensus estimates because initial reports often contain unpredictable adjustments, CNBC noted.
