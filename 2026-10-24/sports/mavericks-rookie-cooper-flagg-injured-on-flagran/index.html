Mavericks' Cooper Flagg exits win with shoulder injury after flagrant foul by Pacers' Aaron Nesmith

Sports desk

Dallas Mavericks guard Cooper Flagg left Friday's 121-105 victory over the Indiana Pacers with a left shoulder injury after being fouled on a breakaway by Pacers forward Aaron Nesmith. The reigning Kia NBA Rookie of the Year did not return for the second half. Flagg finished with 15 points, five rebounds and six assists in 17 minutes before the injury, according to the NBA.

The play occurred with one minute left in the first half. Flagg stole the ball and raced toward the basket for an apparent dunk when Nesmith ran in from behind and sent him hard to the floor. Flagg immediately grabbed his left shoulder. Mavericks forward Naji Marshall confronted Nesmith, sparking a brief shoving match that resulted in double technicals on Marshall and Nesmith. After review, the officials upgraded the foul to a Flagrant 1. Flagg made both ensuing free throws before exiting.

Mavericks coach Dusty May, who grew up in Indiana, called the play unfortunate. "Nesmith's a great guy. Heck of a player. I've known him since he was in high school. An unfortunate play. Hopefully Coop's fine, but obviously it's something we wish wouldn't have happened," May said, as recorded by The Athletic's Mike Curtis. May added that Flagg was in "good spirits" in the locker room and will be evaluated over the next couple of days.

Veteran guard Kyrie Irving, who led Dallas with 24 points on 5-of-10 3-point shooting, emphasized the team's protective response. "We've got to set an example for every single one of us, especially Coop, our youngest guy on the team. We gotta protect him, and when we see him go down like that, it doesn't make us feel good," Irving said, per CBS Sports. He called the locker room a brotherhood and said the team wants to create an atmosphere of backing each other.

Flagg averaged 21 points, 6.7 rebounds, 4.5 assists and 1.2 steals last season and entered the year ranked No. 25 on CBS Sports' Top 100 NBA players list. He had 32 points in the season-opening loss to Houston on Wednesday. On Friday, despite the abbreviated outing, he shot 4-for-6 from the field with three steals. The Mavericks (1-1) recorded their first win under new coach May. P.J. Washington added 20 points and 13 rebounds. Indiana's Tyrese Haliburton played his first home game since the 2025 NBA Finals. Dallas hosts the San Antonio Spurs on Saturday before visiting the Miami Heat on Monday, with Flagg's status uncertain for both games.

Sources: CBS Sports·NBA.com·ESPN
