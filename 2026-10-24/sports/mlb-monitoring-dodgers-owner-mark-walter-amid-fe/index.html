MLB monitoring Dodgers owner Mark Walter amid federal probe, Sparks sale plans

Sports desk

Major League Baseball is monitoring the federal investigation involving Los Angeles Dodgers owner Mark Walter, Commissioner Rob Manfred confirmed, as Walter reportedly plans to sell the WNBA's Los Angeles Sparks for a record $700 million. The price, which includes the team's yet-unopened practice facility, would shatter the previous WNBA sale record of $300 million set earlier this year when Tilman Fertitta bought the Connecticut Sun.

The investigation, opened after an internal whistleblower complaint, involves the U.S. Attorney's Office in Manhattan and the Securities and Exchange Commission. Authorities are examining whether Walter and his investment firm Guggenheim Partners, as well as his conglomerate TWG Global, properly disclosed billions of dollars in loans involving two of his insurance companies, Delaware Life Insurance Co. and Clear Spring Life and Annuity Co. The probe seeks to determine if related-party transactions that placed loans on the insurers' books after passing through a third entity constituted fraud, according to the Wall Street Journal. A TWG spokesperson said Walter and the company "have always acted in good faith" and that "nothing about these transactions was any different."

Walter has been divesting sports assets amid the investigation. Earlier this year, he sold the Los Angeles Lakers to Josh Kushner and Bob Iger for a record $12.5 billion, barely a year after buying them for $10 billion. He also sold his stake in Chelsea FC to Clearlake Capital. Bloomberg reported in August that TWG has been trying to raise cash to pay down affiliated loans on the books of its insurers. Walter's remaining holdings include the Dodgers, the Cadillac Formula 1 team, and the entire Professional Women's Hockey League.

Dodgers president Stan Kasten has emphatically denied any sale of the team. "I wanted to make sure everyone understood one thing very clearly: The Dodgers are not being sold," Kasten told reporters this summer. "They're not gonna be sold. They're not for sale. There's no process that has been started to sell it, period." Manfred said the league is monitoring the external law enforcement investigation, adding, "As always when we have an investigation, external law enforcement, whatever, we monitor that and that's where we are right now."

The Sparks, which Walter bought for around $10 million in 2014, have undergone massive changes after missing the playoffs for a sixth consecutive season — the longest active drought in the league. The team has hired a new president, fired its general manager and head coach, traded star guard Kelsey Plum, and lost forward Nneka Ogwumike to retirement. New president Stacy Johns and general manager Ariana Andonian now face an ownership change as they attempt to reshape the roster and coaching staff. Josh Kushner and Bob Iger, the new Lakers owners, are reportedly not interested in buying the Sparks.
