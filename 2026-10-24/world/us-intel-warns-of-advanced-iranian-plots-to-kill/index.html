US intelligence warns of advanced Iranian plots to kill American officials abroad, prompting diplomatic pullback

International & World desk

The United States intelligence community has warned that Iranian plots to target US personnel abroad have reached “advanced stages,” prompting the State Department to reduce diplomatic staff and evacuate family members from multiple posts across the Middle East, according to a classified memo and US officials. The warning was detailed in an unclassified memo sent Friday by Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton to Secretary of State Marco Rubio, first reported by Axios and confirmed by a US official to NBC News. Clayton wrote that Iran has “substantially increased efforts to target US personnel” and that lethal plotting efforts “have reached advanced stages in both the Middle East and certain other countries.”

The memo warned that the risk is “relevant to more individuals than are currently cleared to receive this information,” and Clayton said the intelligence community would work to downgrade as much information as possible so Rubio could share it with State Department personnel. The alert follows weeks of escalating attacks by Iran-backed Houthis on Saudi infrastructure and comes as President Donald Trump has signaled possible new military strikes against Iran after the midterm elections, including options targeting nuclear facilities and strategic islands, according to US officials cited by NBC News.

The State Department on Wednesday announced an ordered departure of non-emergency personnel and all family members from the US embassy in Saudi Arabia, citing Houthi threats. Separately, the department has quietly reduced staffing caps at US diplomatic posts in Jordan, Bahrain, Kuwait, Qatar, the United Arab Emirates, and Lebanon, according to CNN, with some embassies already on “Restricted Operations” status. Families who had only recently returned to their posts after being evacuated during the initial phase of the US-Israel war on Iran that began in February are now being told to leave again, multiple sources told CNN. The American Foreign Service Association said families are “understandably upset, confused, and angry,” with some reporting confusion over financial support for relocation.

The US military has also been quietly scaling back troop presence at bases across the region, NBC News reported. The moves echo drawdowns at the start of the war, which began on February 28 and has killed 21 US military personnel in the Middle East, according to the Pentagon. Trump said Friday at a rally in Florida, “We may do another little Epic Fury,” using the codename for the initial wave of US-Israel strikes against Iran. Senior US military officials have presented Trump with options including major strikes against tunnels or entrances of Iran’s nuclear facilities or against Kharg Island, which hosts most of Iran’s oil production, and Qeshm in the Strait of Hormuz, NBC reported.

Iran has not formally responded to the intelligence memo. But the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps warned Wednesday that Tehran would adopt a new “offensive” strategy if the US renews attacks, and Iranian security officials have said Tehran has plans to target undersea communications cables in the Gulf in the event of new hostilities, according to Reuters. US authorities have long accused Iran of plotting attacks against Americans, including assassination attempts against Trump on US soil and a 2022 plot against former National Security Advisor John Bolton. Earlier this month, the US withdrew bombers from an airbase in England over concerns about a potential attack linked to Iranian intelligence.

The State Department said it is providing per diem allowances and support for lodging and schools for affected families. A State Department official said the department “constantly evaluate[s] new information about the security environment” and that “the threat of Houthi attacks and of Iranian terror plots targeting U.S. officials are informing our decisions on staffing adjustments.” The official added, “We are committed to using every tool available to help families navigate this difficult time.”
