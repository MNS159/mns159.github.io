Gunmen kill seven at church funeral in Jordan, authorities launch manhunt

International & World desk

Gunmen opened fire during a funeral at a Greek Orthodox church in Karak, Jordan, on Friday, killing seven people and wounding at least 34 others, officials said. The attack took place in the Karak governorate. Jordan's Interior Minister Mazen al-Faraya described the incident as a 'painful and horrific' act carried out by a 'group of extremists' and said authorities were working to apprehend the perpetrators.

According to officials, the unidentified gunmen arrived in a car, entered the church, and opened fire before fleeing. CCTV footage verified by the BBC appears to show three gunmen moving from a road toward the church. Witnesses inside the church reported intense gunfire erupting about 15 minutes after the funeral began. Mourner Khalid al-Mdanat told BBC Arabic the attack lasted around 15 minutes, with people throwing themselves under pews, cradling the dead, and fleeing. One witness said the scene was 'catastrophic,' with bodies in the courtyards and broken glass and blood on the floor.

At least 34 others were wounded, according to officials, with Jordan's Health Minister Ibrahim al-Budour stating that two of the wounded are in critical condition. No group has claimed responsibility for the attack. Jordan declared three days of national mourning, the state-run Petra News Agency reported.

Jordan's King Abdullah II said on X that the kingdom 'will confront the terrorist act that targeted Karak with the harshest penalties' and offered condolences to the families of the victims. Prime Minister Jafar Hassan said during a security meeting in Karak that the attack was the result of 'terrorist ideology and blind extremism' and pledged that the assailants would be punished. Security forces were deployed to the area, and roads around a hospital treating the wounded were closed amid an atmosphere of anger and grief.

Such violence is rare in Jordan, which has a sizable Christian minority in the majority-Muslim country. In 2016, gunmen ambushed police in a series of attacks in Karak, including at a Crusader castle popular with tourists, killing 10 people, including seven officers. Friday's attack drew condemnation from across the region. Lebanon's President Joseph Aoun called it a 'heinous terrorist attack' that 'violated the sanctity of death and funeral mourners.' The grand imam of Al-Azhar, Ahmed al-Tayeb, also condemned it, and Iraq's foreign minister expressed solidarity. U.N. Secretary-General Antonio Guterres described the attack during a funeral as 'especially heinous' and called for all those responsible to be held accountable.
