Israeli forces launch major West Bank operation after Hebron attack kills two soldiers

International & World desk · anchored on AP
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

Israeli forces have launched a major military operation in the occupied West Bank after two Israeli soldiers were killed and three others wounded in a shooting attack at a checkpoint near Hebron on Friday. The two Palestinian gunmen were shot dead at the scene. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said the military would "destroy" the Hebron neighborhood where the attackers came from and ordered a full lockdown of the West Bank, with Israeli forces going house-to-house in the area. The military described the campaign as a "counterterrorism" operation lasting several days, involving soldiers, police, and other security agents, with operations also in "dozens" of other areas across the West Bank.

By Saturday morning, Israeli forces had raided hundreds of homes in the Hebron area and turned dozens of houses into military interrogation centers, according to the Palestinian Prisoners' Society. Around 300 Palestinian families were being displaced in the city's al-Jalda neighborhood, Al Jazeera reported, and bulldozers wrecked a three-story building in Hebron. In total, Israeli forces arrested around 70 people between Friday and early Saturday while interrogating hundreds more. The Israeli military said it found a large quantity of ammunition in the attackers' vehicle and believes the gunmen intended to carry out a much larger attack on a nearby highway but instead opened fire at the checkpoint.

Netanyahu likened the operation to previous campaigns in West Bank refugee camps where residential buildings were demolished and thousands were displaced. He said he had reviewed plans for the largest military operation in the West Bank in decades, though he did not say when it could be carried out. Israeli rights group B'Tselem warned the offensive amounts to "collective punishment and acts that could constitute war crimes." The Palestinian Authority called for international pressure on Israel to protect Palestinians, warning of the dangers of collective punishment including forced displacement of entire neighborhoods, demolition of homes, and the closure and siege of Palestinian cities.

The attack and the military response come just days before Israel's general election on Tuesday, with Netanyahu—who has built his campaign on the promise of security—canceling campaign events scheduled for Friday morning. Far-right ministers in Netanyahu's government called for even more aggressive action: Finance Minister Bezalel Smotrich urged a campaign in the West Bank "exactly like in Gaza or Lebanon," while National Security Minister Itamar Ben-Gvir called for Israel to dismantle the Palestinian Authority. Opposition leaders offered condolences to the families of the killed troops and praised those who killed the attackers.

The operation also takes place amid heightened tensions in the West Bank, where at least 96 Palestinians have been killed by Israeli forces or settlers since the start of the year, according to the UN, and four Israelis had been killed by Palestinians there over the same period before Friday's attack. Israeli settler violence against Palestinians has been particularly rife during the annual olive harvest, with the Palestinian Colonization and Wall Resistance Commission recording 409 attacks by Israeli forces or settlers against Palestinian farmers and their land so far in October. The Palestinian Authority said more than 40,000 people have been forcibly displaced from their homes by Israeli settler violence and raids in the West Bank since the beginning of 2025.
