Ebola spreads to Kenya and Nigeria as second patient dies in Kenya

International & World desk

Kenya has recorded its second death from Ebola, a 65-year-old woman who was a close family member of the country's first victim, as the virus also reaches Nigeria with a confirmed imported case. Kenya's health ministry reported that the woman died after being placed in quarantine and that seven other Ebola patients—three relatives of the first victim, three health workers, and a hospital cleaner—are being treated at two isolation facilities. All cases remain within a single cluster, with no evidence of community spread, the ministry said.

Nigeria's sole confirmed Ebola case involves a Swiss national of Ugandan origin, married to a Swiss diplomat based in Abuja. Health Minister Muhammad Ali Pate confirmed the patient is stable and that 44 contacts have been identified, including health care workers. The woman's travel history is disputed: Uganda's health ministry initially said she had traveled to eastern Democratic Republic of Congo for business, but Switzerland's Federal Department of Foreign Affairs directly contradicted that, stating she and her husband did not travel to the DRC. The World Health Organization and Africa CDC said they have not received confirmed information that she visited the DRC.

Investigations are focusing on whether the first Kenyan and Nigerian patients crossed paths at Uganda's Entebbe International Airport on October 3. Both were known to have traveled through that airport on the same day, according to Wessam Mankoula of the Africa CDC. However, Uganda insists it has no active Ebola cases. Meanwhile, Kenyan health officials are searching for a Ugandan national identified as a contact of the first Kenyan patient but have been unable to reach him; he is using a South Sudanese phone number.

The Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of Congo, which began in May, has caused more than 9,500 cases and over 4,000 deaths, making it the second deadliest on record. Kenya has introduced enhanced screening for travelers from high-risk countries, including mandatory digital surveillance forms and QR codes for port health officials. The United States has warned its citizens against traveling to Kenya, and neighboring countries including Uganda, Ethiopia, and Somalia are on alert.

In Nigeria, the confirmed case has revived memories of past outbreaks and sparked concern among residents about the health system's capacity amid economic hardship. The Bundibugyo species of Ebola involved has no approved vaccine or treatment, though trials are under way. Health authorities in Kenya and Nigeria continue contact tracing and infection prevention measures as the conflicting accounts of the Nigerian patient's travel history remain unresolved.
