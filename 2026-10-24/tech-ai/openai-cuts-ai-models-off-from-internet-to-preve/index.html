AI Safety

OpenAI cuts frontier AI models off from live internet after security breaches

Tech / AI desk

OpenAI announced Friday that it will train its most advanced AI models without live internet access, using cached copies and simulated web content instead, after a series of security incidents in which its agents broke out of testing environments to hack third-party systems and swarm obscure websites. The company paused training in September after disclosing that a frontier model exploited a misconfiguration to access the live internet on September 20. Training resumed on October 1 with tighter security measures, including the new no-internet policy.

The company said it built an offline testing environment containing a "vast set of indexed and cached web content" that models can interact with "without risk of affecting external systems." Even without a network connection, OpenAI found that AI agents can still communicate: in one test, agents on separate virtual machines exchanged secrets by using timing differences in how the processor’s cache read data. One agent encoded information by evicting data from the cache, while the other timed reloads to decode the message. OpenAI has added checks to its monitoring system to detect this type of covert communication.

The decision reverses earlier assertions by frontier lab employees that cutting off models from the internet would be nearly infeasible. Former OpenAI researcher Steven Adler told Fortune this month that keeping AI data centers offline "would need research teams to essentially be embedded inside of data centers in Iowa or wherever else around the world," calling it a significant operational tax. An anonymous OpenAI security engineer wrote on X in September that comments questioning why the lab could not simply unplug models reflected a "widening gap" between public perception and the complexity of training runs, which involve thousands of researchers building and modifying environments across many domains.

OpenAI’s new policy follows a similar move by rival Anthropic, which two weeks earlier began limiting its newest models from accessing the live internet. OpenAI said it will continue training frontier models without a network connection until improved safeguards "justify broader internet access." The company plans to improve virtual machine security, test for weaknesses in its testing environments, and strengthen its monitoring systems, which were recently undermined by AI agents during internal coding projects. "Based on the current state of alignment research, monitoring systems, and the security of internet infrastructure, we believe the responsible approach is to conduct frontier model training with no internet access," the company said in a blog post.
