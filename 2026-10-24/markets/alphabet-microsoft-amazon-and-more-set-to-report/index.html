Alphabet, Microsoft, Amazon and more set to report earnings in packed week

Financial & Markets desk

A wave of major earnings reports is set to hit markets this week, led by tech giants Alphabet, Microsoft, and Amazon, alongside a Federal Reserve meeting. According to Investor's Business Daily, Dow Jones, S&P 500, and Nasdaq futures will open Sunday evening as the stock market enters the week after relatively modest moves on the major indexes, which regained and then lost their 50-day moving averages.

Three companies with a combined market capitalization of roughly $10.5 trillion — Alphabet and Microsoft each at about $3.9 trillion, and Amazon at about $2.7 trillion — are among the ten U.S. companies valued at more than $400 billion reporting this week, per Opening Bell Daily. The busiest day is Thursday, with four of the ten reporting. The schedule includes: Tuesday: Visa (after the close); Wednesday: Alphabet (afternoon conference call) and Microsoft (after the close); Thursday: Mastercard (before the open), Eli Lilly (morning conference call), Amazon (evening conference call), and Intel (after the close); Friday: Exxon Mobil and AbbVie (both before the open) and Chevron (morning conference call). Alphabet, Amazon, Eli Lilly, and Chevron have set conference call times but did not specify when results are released.

The earnings come at a critical juncture for the artificial intelligence (AI) investment thesis. Yahoo Finance notes that while the AI trade has been tough over the past month, this bundle of tech earnings could change sentiment. Key areas to watch include AI capital expenditures and revenue growth rates. If hyperscalers signal continued or increased spending, it could give chipmakers and AI-related companies permission to rebound. Elon Musk told SpaceX investors that hyperscalers and AI enterprises have been 'relentlessly hounding SpaceX for more compute,' expecting the superintelligence computing capacity shortage to last for years. That backdrop suggests hyperscalers may spend more than expected. Microsoft has already told investors it expects positive free cash flow in fiscal 2027, indicating the AI build-out is sustainable.

Revenue growth rates are equally important to justify the heavy capital outlays. Micron posted 379% year-over-year revenue growth in its fiscal Q4 2026, reflecting strong chip demand. For hyperscalers, cloud revenue acceleration — specifically at Alphabet's Google Cloud, which posted 82% year-over-year growth in the second quarter — will be a focal point. Profitability is less of a near-term concern, as tech innovations often remain unprofitable for years before expanding margins. The earnings follow a week of reports from Meta and Tesla, and the markets will take their cue from the three trillion-dollar companies reporting within two days.
