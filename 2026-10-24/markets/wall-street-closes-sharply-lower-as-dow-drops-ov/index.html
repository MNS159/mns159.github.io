Wall Street ends week sharply lower; Dow drops 652 points as oil surges above $117

Financial & Markets desk · anchored on Reuters

U.S. stocks closed sharply lower Friday, capping a volatile week, as a sell-off in technology shares driven by artificial-intelligence spending fears spread to other sectors and oil prices climbed to fresh highs on Middle East tensions. The Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 652.82 points, or 1.23%, to 52,215.16. The S&P 500 lost 39.75 points, or 0.52%, to 7,623.68, and the Nasdaq Composite shed 53.52 points, or 0.21%, to 25,955.34. The Cboe Volatility Index, known as the market’s fear gauge, rose 2.21% to 19.45.

The tech rout was fueled by renewed anxiety about corporate investment in artificial intelligence, ignited by earnings reports from Tesla and Meta Platforms. Tesla cut its full-year capital-expenditure guidance by 20% and Meta left its capex guidance unchanged without providing details on future plans. “The news stoked fears, long held by market participants, that the AI investment boom may have peaked, with the pessimism leading to a broad-based sell-off across the AI ecosystem,” said Kyle Rodda, senior financial market analyst at Capital.com. He added that the perceived risk had shifted from companies spending too much on AI to “that future capex could be lower than previously thought and the rate of growth in investment could be slowing down.”

Telecommunications stocks were hit hard after reports that SpaceX is in talks with cellular infrastructure companies to build a nationwide wireless network. Shares of AT&T fell 7.96%, T-Mobile dropped 7.71%, and Verizon declined 1.18%. SpaceX shares rose 1.89%. American Express fell 3.62% after announcing a roughly 5% global workforce reduction, despite posting higher third-quarter revenue and profit. Shares of luxury goods holding company Kering tumbled 12.4% in premarket trading after disappointing quarterly results.

Oil prices extended their rally, with Brent crude climbing above $117 a barrel, as geopolitical turmoil in the Middle East fanned supply concerns. The rise in crude added to worries about inflation ahead of the Federal Reserve’s Oct. 27–28 policy meeting. Market pricing still reflects an expectation that the Fed will hold its benchmark interest rate unchanged. In the bond market, the yield on the 10-year U.S. Treasury note hovered near 4.99%, after dipping below 5% on Thursday for the first time in about a month. On the economic data front, the University of Michigan consumer sentiment index for October was revised upward to 48, but remained at its lowest level in five months.

European stock markets mostly closed lower. The pan-European Stoxx 600 index fell 0.31% to 635.77 points. Germany’s DAX 40 declined 0.75%, France’s CAC 40 dropped 0.36%, and the U.K.’s FTSE 100 slipped 0.32%. Italy’s FTSE MIB 30 edged down 0.36%, while Spain’s IBEX 35 bucked the trend, rising 0.4%.

Investors are now focused on the upcoming earnings reports from major U.S. technology companies, including Microsoft, Alphabet, and Amazon, scheduled for the following week.
