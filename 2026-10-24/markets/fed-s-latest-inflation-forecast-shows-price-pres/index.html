Fed's October inflation forecast shows CPI accelerating to 3.77%, core PCE rising, dimming rate-cut hopes

Financial & Markets desk

The Federal Reserve’s latest inflation forecast for October shows price pressures persisting, with the Consumer Price Index (CPI) expected to accelerate to a 3.77% annualized rate, up from 3.7% in September, according to the Federal Reserve Bank of Cleveland’s Inflation Nowcasting tool as of Oct. 20. The Core Personal Consumption Expenditures (PCE) index, the Fed’s preferred long-term inflation gauge, is projected to reaccelerate to 3.05% in September and 3.13% in October after three consecutive months at 3%, the tool indicated.

The projected reacceleration reflects more than just an energy supply shock. Rapidly rising fuel costs, driven by the ongoing closure of the Strait of Hormuz amid the Iran war, are pushing headline inflation higher. Businesses are also altering shipping routes and shifting suppliers, adding costs that are feeding into core inflation. In September, fuel oil prices surged 74.0% year-over-year and gasoline rose 34.2%, according to data from economist Charlie Bilello.

Fed Chair Kevin Warsh and the Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) already raised the federal funds rate by a quarter point to 3.75%-4.00% on Sept. 16 in a unanimous vote. Futures markets now price a 72% probability that rates will rise by at least 75 basis points to 4.50%-4.75% by Sept. 15, 2027, according to the CME FedWatch Tool. The FOMC’s September Summary of Economic Projections also revealed a hawkish shift: only one of 17 policymakers sees the long-run federal funds rate below 3%, while 11 see it above 3% and six see it at 3%. More than half project rates staying at or above 3.6% through 2029 to bring inflation down to the Fed’s 2% target.

The persistent inflation and rate-hike outlook pose risks to stock markets that have rallied to record highs in 2026, largely on AI-driven earnings growth. History shows mixed outcomes: the S&P 500 produced positive returns during the 1994 and 2004–2006 tightening cycles, but fell sharply in 2022 when the Fed acted aggressively and late. Analysts caution that higher-for-longer rates could eventually dampen corporate profits as borrowing costs rise, and if the AI infrastructure build-out slows, especially with headline inflation near 4% and consumer confidence low.

With the Fed’s inflation forecast deteriorating and the likelihood of further rate increases climbing, investors face a challenging environment. The market’s fate now hinges on whether corporate earnings can continue to outpace the headwinds from rising rates, rising energy costs, and geopolitical disruptions.
