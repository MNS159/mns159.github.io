Alnylam Reports Positive Initial Phase 1b Data for Huntington's Drug Brezosiran

Health & Science desk

Alnylam Pharmaceuticals announced positive initial results from its Phase 1b clinical trial for brezosiran (ALN-HTT02), an investigational RNAi therapeutic for Huntington's disease. The data, presented at the European Huntington’s Disease Network Clinical Research Congress 2026 in Krakow, Poland, showed that a single intrathecal dose of brezosiran produced robust, dose-dependent reductions of mutant huntingtin protein (mHTT) in cerebrospinal fluid. Across the first five cohorts, the highest dose evaluated achieved a mean reduction of 60–64% at month 3, with a maximum individual reduction of 83%. The company said the durability of mHTT lowering supports the potential for twice-yearly dosing in future development.

The ongoing Phase 1b study is a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, single-ascending-dose trial in adults aged 25 to 70 with genetically confirmed Huntington's disease at HD-ISS Stage 2 or early Stage 3. A total of 48 participants have been enrolled across five dose cohorts, with a sixth cohort currently enrolling. The initial data include individuals followed for up to 12 months, during which an encouraging safety and tolerability profile was observed, with no dose-dependent increases in CSF inflammatory markers, neurofilament light chain, or brain ventricular volume. All eligible participants may transition into an open-label extension study receiving brezosiran every six months for up to 36 months.

Brezosiran targets exon 1 of the huntingtin (HTT) mRNA transcript, a strategy designed to reduce production of all forms of the huntingtin protein, including full-length mutant HTT and the shorter mutant HTT1a, which may contribute to disease progression. The drug uses Alnylam’s proprietary C16 conjugate platform for delivery to the central nervous system and is being developed in collaboration with Regeneron Pharmaceuticals. Huntington's disease is an autosomal dominant neurodegenerative disorder caused by an expanded CAG repeat in the HTT gene, with no approved disease-modifying therapies currently available.

Dr. Blair R. Leavitt, a principal investigator on the study, said the results represent a promising advance for a program aimed at addressing a disease with no approved disease-modifying treatments. Pushkal Garg, Alnylam’s Chief Research and Development Officer, said the company plans to advance brezosiran into a placebo-controlled pivotal study in 2027, pending regulatory alignment, to evaluate its ability to slow disease progression. The data will also be featured during Alnylam’s RNAi Roundtable webcast on October 26, 2026.
