House Republicans force impeachment vote on Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth over Iran War

Politics desk · anchored on AP
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum
✓ Facts
  • Rep. Thomas Massie (R-Ky.) introduced a privileged resolution on the House floor Monday containing eight articles of impeachment against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, a mechanism that requires a House vote within two legislative days, setting a deadline of Wednesday at the latest.
  • The articles accuse Hegseth of violating congressional war powers by continuing hostilities in Iran for more than 90 days without authorization, of unlawful strikes tied to the Iran war, Yemen Houthi operations, Caribbean drug-boat strikes and the extradition of former Venezuelan leader Nicolás Maduro, and of retaliating against Sen. Mark Kelly (D-Ariz.) over a video urging troops to resist 'unlawful orders.'
  • This marks Massie's second impeachment push against Hegseth; he first introduced articles of impeachment in September.
  • House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-La.) said impeaching a Cabinet secretary 'in the middle of hostilities abroad' would be 'a terrible, terrible look' and argued secretaries should not be impeached for 'political gain,' while House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries (D-N.Y.) said he is a 'hard yes' on impeachment.
  • Only two Cabinet secretaries have ever been impeached by the House in U.S. history, and the Senate did not convict either; conviction requires a two-thirds Senate vote, and the House currently has 218 Republicans and 214 Democrats.
  • Hegseth held a news conference Tuesday — his first since May — to address the impeachment threat, and President Trump publicly defended him, saying 'he's guilty of two things, basically winning two wars.'
  • An AP VoteCast survey of more than 100,000 voters found about 6 in 10 said the U.S. made the 'wrong decision' in taking military action against Iran, amid broader Republican losses in the midterm elections.
○ Analysis

The impeachment push is unusual because it originates from within the president's own party: Massie, a frequent Trump critic who lost his re-election bid, is using a rarely invoked procedural tool — a privileged resolution — to force a floor vote that House GOP leadership would otherwise have ignored. Impeaching a sitting Cabinet secretary is historically rare and has never resulted in a Senate conviction, so even if the House votes to impeach Hegseth, his removal from office would still require a two-thirds Senate vote that is not currently plausible given the chamber's partisan makeup. The vote comes as Congress returns from a midterm election in which Republicans lost control of both chambers, with multiple GOP figures, including Vice President JD Vance, pointing to backlash over the Iran war and its economic effects — including higher gas prices — as a contributing factor.

◐ Debate

Democratic

  • House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries (D-N.Y.) said he is a 'hard yes' on impeaching Hegseth.
  • Rep.-elect Brad Lander said the House should impeach Hegseth, along with FBI Director Kash Patel and HHS Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr., arguing voters' midterm message reflected anger over the war and other policies.

Republican

  • House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-La.) called the impeachment effort a 'terrible, terrible look' during ongoing hostilities and said Massie's push amounted to 'political gain' or a 'cheap trick.'
  • Rep. Warren Davidson (R-Ohio) said the effort 'scapegoats' Hegseth for decisions made by the president and Congress's own inaction, calling the underlying military actions 'not unprecedented.'
  • Rep. Don Bacon (R-Neb.) said he is 'torn' over whether to back the impeachment, while President Trump and Secretary of State Marco Rubio publicly defended Hegseth's performance.
Steelman

Beyond the partisan framing, this is fundamentally an intra-Republican reckoning over accountability for the Iran war's political fallout — with the ultimate outcome likely hinging less on the impeachment vote itself (which cannot realistically produce a Senate conviction) than on whether President Trump chooses to remove Hegseth before Democrats gain the power to pursue their own, more extensive investigation in January.

Implications

The House vote's outcome is uncertain given the chamber's narrow 218-214 Republican margin and the possibility that GOP members who lost re-election may be absent, but even a successful impeachment vote would face a steep two-thirds conviction threshold in the Senate that neither party currently has the votes to clear. The more immediate pressure point may be President Trump's own decision on whether to retain or remove Hegseth before Democrats take control of Congress in January and can pursue more extensive hearings.
