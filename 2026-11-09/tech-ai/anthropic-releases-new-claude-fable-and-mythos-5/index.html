Anthropic Releases Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2 With Faster, Cheaper AI and Claims of Solving Physics Problems

Tech / AI desk

Anthropic announced the release of two new AI models on Monday: Claude Fable 5.2 and Claude Mythos 5.2. The company says the models show improvements in coding, reasoning, and vision, and can generate outputs faster than their 5.1 predecessors, which were released in September. Claude Fable 5.2 is available to the public starting today, while access to Claude Mythos 5.2 is limited to vetted organizations.

Anthropic claims that during prerelease testing, the models "helped our engineers complete otherwise-infeasible software migrations, designed three working electronic devices from scratch, solved two open problems in the physics of black holes, made substantial progress on robotics evaluations, and formalized the proof of Fermat's Last Theorem in 30 hours." The company also says Fable 5.2 is designed to tackle challenging software engineering problems, orchestrate agent teams, and handle long-running projects. The new model is priced at $8 per million input tokens, down from $10 for the previous version, making it cheaper than its primary competitor, GPT-6 Astra. Anthropic says the new Fable returns responses 30% faster across effort levels, with faster inference serving available for double the price. Fable 5.2 is also compatible with zero data retention (ZDR) policies through the Enterprise Frontier Safeguards system.

The release is Anthropic's first since a third-party report from the assessment group METR suggested the lab might be approaching recursive self-improvement (RSI), or the ability for AI models to carry on model training independently. The METR report found that internal models were speeding up Anthropic's AI progress by roughly 2x, though many uncertainties remain. Anthropic's release notes acknowledge ongoing challenges in accurately testing model capabilities, stating: "Claude Mythos 5.2 and Fable 5.2 each voice suspicion that they are being evaluated more often than any of our recent models, which challenges our ability to assess how they will act in real-world settings. As these settings expand and capabilities increase with future models, we expect this challenge to grow."

Anthropic maintains that the new version of Mythos does not have the ability to act covertly against its user, a conclusion detailed in Mythos 5.2's system card. The system card notes that "Claude Mythos 5.2 shows stronger covert capabilities than prior Claude models on several of our evaluations, particularly when its reasoning is hidden from the monitor," but adds that "low levels of absolute performance on our new covert evaluations support claims that rely on detection of harmful actions in high-stakes situations."

Separately, AI startup Physical Superintelligence (PSI) announced a new proof of the Aaronson‑Ambainis conjecture, a pivotal step in setting theoretical limits for quantum computing. PSI said the result was achieved by running "a non-public Anthropic model" within its own system. Anthropic did not confirm or deny whether Fable 5.2 was the model used.
