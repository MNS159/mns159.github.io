FDA orders class-wide label update for GLP-1 drugs over rare eye condition risk

Health & Science desk

The Food and Drug Administration on Monday ordered a labeling update for all approved GLP-1 receptor agonists to warn of a potential increased risk of a rare but serious eye condition called non-arteritic anterior ischemic optic neuropathy, or NAION. The condition, sometimes described as an "eye stroke," is caused by insufficient blood flow to the optic nerve, typically leads to sudden vision loss, and can result in blindness; there are currently no proven treatments for it. The class-wide requirement covers 12 marketed drugs, including Novo Nordisk's semaglutide products Ozempic and Wegovy, as well as Eli Lilly's tirzepatide franchise Mounjaro, Zepbound, and the oral formulation Foundayo.

The FDA's action is based on an assessment launched after a 2024 study published in JAMA Ophthalmology found an association between Novo's semaglutide and a higher risk of NAION. The agency conducted a meta-analysis of 126 clinical trials across development programs. The analysis identified 15 probable NAION events: 10 in patients treated with GLP-1s, five in patients receiving placebo, and none in patients who received other non-GLP-1 glucose-lowering medication. This translated to a NAION incidence rate of 6.4 events per 100,000 patient-years for those treated with GLP-1s, compared with 3.8 events per 100,000 patient-years for comparators.

The FDA also examined real-world evidence through its data network, looking at NAION incidence among adults with type 2 diabetes. That study found no higher risk for NAION tied to GLP-1s compared with SGLT-2 inhibitors such as Boehringer Ingelheim and Eli Lilly's Jardiance, but did identify an increased risk when GLP-1s were compared with DPP-4 inhibitors such as Merck's Januvia. The regulator additionally reviewed reports of NAION among GLP-1 users from its adverse event monitoring system, but said those reports were difficult to interpret because risk factors for the disease often overlap with conditions commonly reported in patients treated with GLP-1s. An analysis of animal data failed to identify a mechanism for how GLP-1s could raise the potential risk of the eye condition.

The safety update comes as GLP-1 drugs have exploded in popularity for their potent weight loss effects, becoming some of the industry's top-selling products. Eli Lilly's tirzepatide brands Mounjaro and Zepbound generated a combined $36.51 billion in 2025, while Novo Nordisk's semaglutide franchise—Ozempic, Wegovy, and Rybelsus—brought in $36.19 billion over the same period. By comparison, Merck's PD-1 blockbuster Keytruda, which has amassed more than 40 indications, earned nearly $32 billion in 2025.

Analysts at BMO described the risk as limited for obesity drug coverage given the rarity of NAION incidence, noting that the FDA's action formalizes a previously recognized safety signal with low absolute event rates and amounts to a Warnings and Precautions update rather than a restriction on approved indications. The FDA's order requires pharmaceutical companies to inform patients and doctors of the potential risk through updated labeling.
