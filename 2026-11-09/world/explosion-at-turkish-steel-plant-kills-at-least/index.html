Explosion at Turkish steel plant kills at least 17

International & World desk

At least 17 people have died and 12 others have been injured in an explosion at an iron and steel factory near the port city of Izmir in western Turkey, authorities said. The blast occurred around midday on Monday at the Habas Demir Celik plant in the Aliaga district, a major port and one of Turkey's main shipbreaking hubs. All those killed and injured were workers at the facility, one of the country's leading steel producers.

The local governor said the explosion happened after a crane carrying a replacement boiler collapsed, causing materials to fall into a large industrial furnace below. Eyewitnesses said the blast shook the ground, with one person initially believing an earthquake had struck. Footage from the scene showed a large fire inside the facility and a towering column of smoke rising into the air. The company that runs the factory said the accident occurred during scheduled maintenance work on one of the plant's electric arc furnaces.

President Recep Tayyip Erdogan originally said the death toll was 16, but later Health Minister Kemal Memişoğlu announced that one of those who had been critically injured had died of their injuries, taking the toll to 17. Izmir Governor Süleyman Elban told media that some people were believed to still be under the rubble. Izmir Mayor Cemil Tugay said the full number of dead and injured would only become clear once the rubble had been cleared. At least five people have been arrested, according to local media reports cited by BBC News's Turkish service.

Workplace accidents remain a chronic problem in Turkey, the Turkish Health and Safety Labor Watch, an independent civil society organisation, said. Workers' groups said there is a long record of safety issues at the factory. The Izmir chapter of Turkey's Labour Party said it was the third fatal incident at the factory in three weeks, and warned that seven employees had died in accidents at the plant in the past 10 years, adding that maintenance requirements and safety standards were not being met. Speaking after a cabinet meeting, Erdogan confirmed the deaths and said investigations were under way, while search and rescue operations continued. The European Union and Azerbaijan were among those to send condolences.
