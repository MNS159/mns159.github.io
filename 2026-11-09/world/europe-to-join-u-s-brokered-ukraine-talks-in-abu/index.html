Europe to join U.S.-brokered Ukraine talks in Abu Dhabi, defying Kremlin

International & World desk

European officials will join U.S.-mediated peace talks between Russia and Ukraine in Abu Dhabi this week, France's foreign minister said Sunday, pushing back against the Kremlin's insistence that there could be no role for European powers at the meeting. "France will be there and will ensure that Europe's voice is heard," Jean-Noël Barrot told French radio, without specifying whether French or other European officials would take part directly in negotiations or simply observe. The meetings, set for Tuesday and Wednesday, had previously been presented as a three-way session between Washington, Moscow and Kyiv.

The Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov had earlier ruled out a European role, saying the trilateral format had already been agreed. Speaking to a Russian news channel Friday, he accused European governments of failing to decide who speaks for them, while acknowledging that security talks would at some point require Europe's involvement. On Thursday, President Trump met Kremlin envoy Kirill Dmitriev and said a coming agreement was "going to involve more than Ukraine," adding it "might involve all of Europe."

This will be the fourth round of talks between the two warring nations brokered by the Trump administration, and the first since those in Geneva in February. Earlier U.S.-brokered meetings have made little progress. Moscow confirmed it would return to the negotiating table only after a visit by U.S. envoy Steve Witkoff to meet Russian President Vladimir Putin in St. Petersburg in October. Those talks saw Putin announce the release of an American prisoner from a Russian jail — an apparent Kremlin gesture to Trump's earlier lifting of sanctions on Russian diesel exports, a move Ukraine vehemently opposed. Witkoff has said the agenda for these latest talks will include postwar security arrangements, de-escalation of the conflict and humanitarian issues, shortly after Melania Trump helped broker a limited ceasefire that allowed humanitarian aid to enter a town inside Russian-occupied Ukraine.

Russia's delegation will again be led by hardline Putin aide Vladimir Medinsky, who has participated in most talks with Ukraine since March 2022. For the first time, the talks will also include Deputy Foreign Ministers Sergei Ryabkov and Mikhail Galuzin, as well as Yury Zhigarlovsky, a deputy head of the General Staff's operations directorate, alongside military intelligence chief Igor Kostyukov. The U.S. think tank Institute for the Study of War suggested some of those same Russian officials have previously sought to slow down previous talks or push harder on Moscow's demands. The gaps between the two countries remain substantial: Russia has demanded that Ukraine give up large parts of its territory and renounce NATO membership, while Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy has repeatedly insisted any security commitments should be clearly spelled out.

Zelenskyy told CNN on Friday that he had not been given details of the agreement Trump referenced, and warned: "For me, it's not simple, and it can be dangerous." He invoked the 1945 Yalta conference, where Allied leaders settled Europe's division without the involvement of several affected countries. The meetings in the United Arab Emirates also follow Democrats' midterm victories, which Zelenskyy said could improve Ukraine's chances of more U.S. aid. Russia, too, may be looking to push for the Trump administration's outlines of a Ukraine deal before a Democratic-controlled Congress arrives in January. Also hovering over the talks is the prospect of Trump, Xi and Putin gathering for a trilateral meeting at the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit in Shenzhen, China later this month; while details remain murky, all sides have confirmed discussions are underway.

As officials travel to Abu Dhabi to talk peace, the war has raged on regardless. Zelenskyy said Russia fired 36 ballistic missiles at the Kyiv area overnight Saturday, with only four intercepted, accusing Moscow of treating diplomacy "as a form of aggression." In southern Ukraine, the death toll from a guided-bomb strike Monday on an aid distribution site in Komyshuvakha rose to at least 55.
