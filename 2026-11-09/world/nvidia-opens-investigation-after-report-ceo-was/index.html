Nvidia opens investigation after report CEO was target of Chinese spy

International & World desk

Nvidia has announced it is opening an independent investigation after reports that its CEO, Jensen Huang, was the target of a foreign intelligence effort. The New York Times reported that US officials suspect Alice Huang, the managing director of Singapore-based data centre company Megaspeed, of working for China's spy agency to collect intelligence. Nvidia said on Sunday that while it has no reason to believe that US national security or Nvidia secrets were compromised, the inquiry will examine all available information. The company's board stated that it prioritises US national and economic security and has confidence in Jensen Huang and his leadership team.

The New York Times reported that Donald Trump was briefed last year on intelligence that Huang had a close relationship with Alice Huang, who is of no relation to the Nvidia CEO. She had previously been suspected of smuggling Nvidia chips to China, prompting American officials to surveil and monitor her. Those officials have determined that she was acting as a Chinese intelligence asset, passing commercial information to Beijing, the Times reported, citing unnamed sources. China has denied the allegations, calling them 'entirely groundless, speculative and intended to smear and malign China.'

Speaking to reporters on Sunday, Trump downplayed the revelations, saying of Jensen Huang: 'I think he's an honorable guy. I think he probably has an excuse for it.' Trump brushed off a question about whether he regrets not acting on the intelligence when briefed, and suggested he would be happy to take Huang with him on a trip to Asia next week. 'I haven't heard he's done anything wrong,' Trump said. 'He dealt with somebody. But, you know, so does everybody else.' Republican lawmakers have expressed more alarm, asking Huang to give the House foreign affairs and intelligence committees a closed-door briefing next week on Nvidia's security protocols and his relationship with Alice Huang.

Axios reported on Sunday, citing a draft email by Huang to employees, that Huang admitted to having a romantic relationship with a suspected Chinese spy and took full responsibility for his actions. 'I was unfaithful to my wife. My actions hurt her and our children. There is no excuse for what I did,' Huang said in the draft email, also seen by Reuters. Nvidia did not immediately respond to a request for comment on the Axios report. Huang fully supports the review and has committed to cooperating with it, the company added.

The Times reported that a memo drafted by US Secretary of State Marco Rubio circulated among top policymakers in the Trump administration last spring, citing intelligence that the businesswoman who had a relationship with Nvidia's chief executive was a spy for China's ministry of state security. Despite those concerns, Huang was invited to join Trump's delegation when he traveled to China in May. Huang and his wife, Lori Huang, were also invited to a state dinner at the White House in September for Xi Jinping and seated at the top table with Trump and China's president.

Nvidia's powerful chips serve as a cornerstone of the AI boom, placing Jensen Huang in one of the most influential leadership positions in the industry. He has developed a close partnership with Trump as the US government attempts to defend its position as a leader in AI development. National security concerns have prompted strict US export controls, effectively barring the company from selling its top-tier processors to customers in China. The investigation also follows a rough period on Wall Street for AI tech firms; a sell-off of tech stocks erased about $2tn in a month, with Nvidia's drop of 3.1% being the heaviest force pulling the S&P 500 downward.
