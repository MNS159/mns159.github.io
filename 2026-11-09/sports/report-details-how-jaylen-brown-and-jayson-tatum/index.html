Report details how Jaylen Brown and Jayson Tatum's Celtics partnership unraveled

Sports desk

The Boston Celtics stunned the NBA world this summer by trading Jaylen Brown to the Philadelphia 76ers in a deal that brought back Paul George, two first-round picks, and two second-round selections. Recent reporting, however, indicates that the trade was preceded by a quietly deteriorating relationship between Brown and the organization, as well as with fellow star Jayson Tatum.

Brown had arguably the best season of his career in 2025-26, finishing sixth in MVP voting, earning Second Team All-NBA honors, and averaging career highs of 28.7 points, 5.1 assists, and 6.9 rebounds per game. With Tatum limited to just 16 regular-season games while recovering from right Achilles tendon surgery, Brown stepped into the role of the Celtics' primary option. He believed his performance had elevated him to the same level as Tatum, but the front office apparently did not share that view.

According to King, Brown made it clear to the front office that he wanted to be treated more like a franchise player, on and off the court. After averaging 28.7 points per game, he felt he had reached a place in his career where he deserved the preferential treatment reserved for the game's premier players, including Tatum. The Celtics had previously asked Brown to sacrifice parts of his game for the team's benefit, a formula that helped Boston win the 2024 NBA title, with Brown earning Finals MVP after averaging 23.9 points, 5.9 rebounds, 3.3 assists, and 1.2 steals per game in the playoffs.

NBA Hall of Famer Tracy McGrady, a friend of Brown, commented on the situation, saying, 'I think his frustration lies deeply within the organization and other things that we don't really have the details to.' The reporting suggests that despite Brown's standout 2025-26 campaign, Boston continued to view Tatum as being on a level above Brown.

The 76ers went all-in this summer, adding Brown and LeBron James to a roster that already included Tyrese Maxey, VJ Edgecombe, and Joel Embiid. Philadelphia currently sits atop the Eastern Conference with a 9-2 record. Brown is averaging 22.3 points, 6.7 rebounds, 4.0 assists, and 1.2 steals per game this season, with a usage rate of 25.9%—his lowest since the 2019-2020 campaign. For now, Brown appears content with his role, but the long-term dynamics of the star-laden 76ers lineup remain to be seen.
