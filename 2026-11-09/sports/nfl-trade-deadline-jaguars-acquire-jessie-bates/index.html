Jaguars acquire All-Pro safety Jessie Bates III from Falcons in multi-pick trade; Vikings urged to explore QB market

Sports desk

The Jacksonville Jaguars acquired All-Pro safety Jessie Bates III from the Atlanta Falcons on Monday, sending a 2027 second-round pick and a 2028 fifth-round pick to Atlanta in exchange. Bates, 29, is in the final year of his contract and is expected to become a free agent at the end of the season, but the Jaguars have a history of extending players acquired in midseason trades, as they did with wide receiver Jakobi Meyers last year. The move bolsters a Jacksonville secondary that lost safety Eric Murray for the season to an elbow injury and comes as the Jaguars sit at 6-2 and eye a postseason push.

Bates entered the NFL as a second-round pick of the Cincinnati Bengals in 2018 out of Wake Forest. After five seasons in Cincinnati, he signed a four-year, $64.02 million contract with the Falcons in 2023. This season, Bates recorded one interception, three forced fumbles and 60 total tackles in nine games, while allowing a 78.3% completion percentage and a 115.2 passer rating in coverage, per TruMedia. He is tied for the second-most interceptions (28) in the NFL since entering the league and has been named a second-team All-Pro three times, including in 2025. He is also the only player in the past 40 years with at least 800 tackles and 25 interceptions before age 30.

Falcons head coach Kevin Stefanski said Monday that the trade was not a signal of a sell-off, despite Atlanta falling to 3-6 after a loss to the Bengals in Madrid. “Obviously, we’re not where we want to be, we want to play better, but we’re very much in the thick of this thing and we trust in the guys that we have,” Stefanski said. “I understand the question, and I understand the question in light of the trade, but in no way, shape or form.” Stefanski named DeMarcco Hellams as Bates’s replacement at safety. Hellams, a seventh-round pick in 2023 out of Alabama, appeared in 15 games as a rookie but missed all of his second season after breaking his ankle during the preseason. Defensive back Sydney Brown is also expected to see increased reps.

CBS Sports graded the trade a B for the Jaguars, noting that two picks — especially a second-rounder in 2027 — is a steep price for a potential rental, but that Bates is one of the NFL’s best safeties and deepens a position group hit by injury. The Falcons received a B+, with analysts pointing out that Atlanta was likely to lose Bates in free agency anyway and that collecting a second-round pick is better than any compensatory pick they would have received. The trade is one of several moves reshaping rosters ahead of Tuesday’s 4 p.m. ET deadline. Other deals reported include defensive players Jeffery Simmons (Tennessee to Chicago), DeForest Buckner (Indianapolis to San Francisco) and Joey Porter Jr. (Pittsburgh to Dallas), as well as offensive skill players Jacoby Brissett (Arizona to Baltimore) and Marvin Harrison Jr. (Arizona to Houston).

Meanwhile, the Minnesota Vikings (6-1) are facing questions at quarterback. Carson Wentz will start Monday night against the Buffalo Bills after the team opted to bench Kyler Murray. ESPN’s Dan Orlovsky urged the Vikings to make “outrageous” trade calls if the offense continues to struggle. “If this offense looks pedestrian again, I just do not believe Minnesota can stand pat. I think anything is on the table at quarterback,” Orlovsky said on “The Pat McAfee Show.” “Even if it sounds outrageous to the NFL community, I think you have to start at, like, the most outrageous call you could make [for a] quarterback, and just work your way down the line.” Wentz has made two appearances this season, totaling 276 passing yards and three touchdowns while completing 59.0% of his passes; all three touchdowns came in a Week 1 win over the Packers.
