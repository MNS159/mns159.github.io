NBA rookie extensions: Thompson twins make history; Keyonte George, Ausar Thompson sign; Cavs GM promotion

Sports desk

The NBA offseason's rookie-extension wave reached a historic milestone this week as Detroit Pistons forward Ausar Thompson agreed to a five-year, $155 million rookie-scale contract extension, according to ESPN's Shams Charania. The deal, first reported by Charania on Sept. 18, is fully guaranteed and keeps the All-Defensive First Team forward in Detroit. With the agreement, Ausar and his twin brother, Amen Thompson, became the first pair of brothers in NBA history to each sign a contract worth at least $100 million. Amen, selected No. 4 overall by the Houston Rockets in the 2023 draft one pick before Ausar went No. 5 to Detroit, agreed to a five-year, $208 million rookie-scale extension earlier this month. The brothers' new deals are worth a combined $363 million this offseason.

Ausar Thompson's extension follows a breakout season in which he averaged 9.9 points, 5.7 rebounds, 3.1 assists and a league-leading 2.0 steals per game on 52.5% shooting, helping the Pistons secure the Eastern Conference's top seed. He finished third in Defensive Player of the Year voting and carried that impact into the 2026 NBA Playoffs, averaging 2.0 steals and 1.8 blocks over 14 games. He became the first player since Anthony Davis in 2020 to record at least 25 steals and 25 blocks in a single postseason. The extension comes amid a reported standoff between the Pistons and starting center Jalen Duren, with the team valuing Thompson even over Duren.

The Thompson twins headline a broader wave of 2023 draft-class extensions. Utah Jazz guard Keyonte George recently agreed to a five-year, $157.5 million rookie-scale deal. According to Charania, Charlotte Hornets forward Brandon Miller — who averaged 20.2 points, 4.9 rebounds and 3.3 assists in 65 games last season and is expected to take on a bigger role after the departure of LaMelo Ball — is in active conversations with the Hornets and could be in line for a $200+ million deal. Other players from the 2023 class, including Thunder guard Cason Wallace, Warriors sharpshooter Brandin Podziemski, Heat forward Jaime Jaquez Jr. and Magic guard Anthony Black, are moving toward smaller extensions, with negotiations likely to depend on their performance this upcoming season. Players have until mid-April to reach new deals.

In a separate development, the Cleveland Cavaliers have promoted a general manager, according to multiple reports. The promotion was not detailed in the source material beyond the headline, but it marks a front-office leadership change for the franchise. No further specifics were provided.

In other NBA-related news, the league's summer lull has been punctuated by off-court items. A viral post pairing today's NBA stars with 1980s throwback jerseys and era-appropriate hairstyles drew attention, with Nikola Jokic's Nuggets mullet and mustache, Anthony Davis in a Washington Bullets uniform, and James Harden's high socks among the highlights. Separately, Nigerian-Canadian NBA executive Masai Ujiri, who led the Toronto Raptors to their first NBA championship in 2019, is backing a $294 million sports and entertainment hub in Nairobi, Kenya. The project, led by Pan-African developer Zaria Group and supported by Helios Sports & Entertainment Group, will feature a 10,000-seat indoor arena, serviced apartments, a hotel and supporting infrastructure as part of the Nairobi Railway City master plan. It is expected to create about 3,500 direct construction jobs, 1,500 permanent operational positions and up to 25,000 temporary event-related roles annually, and is part of Kenya's broader push to host the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations alongside Uganda and Tanzania.
