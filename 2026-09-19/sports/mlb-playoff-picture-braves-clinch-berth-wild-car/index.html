MLB playoff picture: Braves clinch berth, wild-card races tight, final week scenarios

Sports desk

With eight days remaining in the 2026 MLB regular season, five teams have clinched postseason berths, and the Atlanta Braves became the latest to secure a spot. The Braves, who hold a 90-64 record and a 99.8% chance to win the NL East, join the Brewers, Rays, Yankees, and Dodgers in the 12-team field. The Milwaukee Brewers (96-58) have clinched the NL Central and hold the inside track to the No. 1 seed in the National League, while the Los Angeles Dodgers (94-60) have clinched the NL West. In the American League, the Tampa Bay Rays (93-60) have opened a four-game lead over the Yankees in the AL East and are closing in on their first division title since 2021, while the Yankees (89-64) and Boston Red Sox (84-70) appear headed for a Wild Card Series meeting.

The Red Sox are the next team expected to clinch, with a magic number of two. Boston can secure a playoff spot on Saturday with a win and a loss by either the Texas Rangers or Houston Astros. The AL wild-card race is tight in the AL West, where the Astros and Rangers both enter Saturday with 77-77 records and are tied for the division lead. The Cleveland Guardians (79-75) have pulled ahead of the Chicago White Sox (78-76) by one game in the AL Central, though the White Sox hold a slight edge in postseason probability (82.7% to 82.1%). The Rays and Yankees have already clinched AL playoff spots, while the Guardians, Astros, Rangers, and White Sox are still competing for the remaining AL berths.

In the National League, the Braves' clinch leaves the division and wild-card races in focus. The Cubs, Phillies, and Padres all enter Saturday with identical 85-69 records and are in strong position for wild-card spots. Chicago holds the tiebreaker over Philadelphia and San Diego thanks to a strong head-to-head record, giving the Cubs the No. 4 seed and home-field advantage in the first round. The Padres have been on an impressive run since Labor Day Weekend. The Arizona Diamondbacks (80-74) are fading, with just a 2.1% postseason probability. The Braves, meanwhile, are closing in on the NL East title with a 99.8% division probability, while the Phillies (99.6%) and Padres (98.5%) are near-locks for the postseason.

If the season ended today, the AL bracket would feature the Rays and Guardians as the top two seeds with byes, while the Astros would host the White Sox and the Yankees would host the Red Sox in the Wild Card Series. In the NL, the Brewers and Dodgers would have byes, with the Braves hosting the Padres and the Cubs hosting the Phillies. The Yankees placed Aaron Judge on the injured list again this weekend, a development that could affect their postseason outlook.

Beyond the playoff race, the St. Louis Cardinals and Washington Nationals, both recently eliminated and below .500, have emerged as pleasant surprises after being projected for roughly 100 losses each. The Nationals have a positive run differential and one of the best offenses in MLB, ranking second in expected weighted on-base average (xwOBA) at .324, while the Cardinals rank sixth at .321. Both teams have young lineups — the Nationals have the youngest in the league, and the Cardinals have the second-youngest — but their bullpens have been glaring weaknesses. The Nationals rank 29th in bullpen ERA (5.04) and have the most blown saves (39) and meltdowns (110) in MLB, while the Cardinals rank 24th in bullpen ERA (4.55) with 30 blown saves and 106 meltdowns. Both teams have rotation depth and prospects on the way, and the possibility of an expanded 14-team postseason in a future collective bargaining agreement could lower the bar for contention in 2027.
