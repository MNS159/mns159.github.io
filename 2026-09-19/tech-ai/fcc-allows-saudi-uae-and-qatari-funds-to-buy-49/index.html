FCC allows Saudi, UAE, and Qatari funds to buy 49.5% stake in Paramount

Tech / AI desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

The Federal Communications Commission (FCC) has approved a plan allowing sovereign wealth funds from Saudi Arabia, the United Arab Emirates, and Qatar to acquire a 49.5 percent indirect equity stake in Paramount, the owner of CBS and 28 local CBS stations. Under U.S. law, companies holding FCC broadcast licenses must obtain approval for foreign ownership exceeding 25 percent. Paramount filed a petition seeking a waiver of that limit, and the FCC granted it yesterday.

The investment is tied to Paramount’s $111 billion acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery, a deal that would combine two major movie studios, merge streaming services Paramount+ and HBO Max, and give Paramount ownership of CNN and other television channels. According to the Los Angeles Times, the three sovereign funds plan to invest a total of $24 billion in the combined entity: Saudi Arabia’s Public Investment Fund will contribute $10 billion, while the Qatar Investment Authority and Abu Dhabi’s L’imad Holding Co. will each add $7 billion. The merger has not yet closed because several U.S. states filed a lawsuit to block it, though the Trump administration’s Department of Justice approved the deal.

In its ruling, the FCC defended the waiver by noting that the purchased shares carry no voting rights, stating that the foreign investors “will not be able to wield any influence, let alone control, over decisions involving the Licensees.” However, FCC Commissioner Anna Gomez, the sole Democrat on the commission, strongly dissented. “The FCC just let some of the most repressive governments in the world indirectly control nearly all of a combined Paramount-Warner Bros.,” Gomez wrote on X. “An investment this large in one of America’s biggest media companies doesn’t just buy equity, it secures influence over what gets said and what gets made.”

The advocacy group Free Press also voiced opposition, with a representative telling Variety that “control over for-profit, commercial domestic news media by any government is an extraordinary situation that would surely strike most Americans as unseemly, precisely because of the utility of the news media as a propaganda tool for those governments.” Critics have noted the contrast between the FCC’s willingness to allow significant foreign government ownership and its recent actions under Chairman Brendan Carr, which have included threatening ABC, attempting to block stations from airing interviews with Democrats, censoring late-night TV hosts, and pressuring journalists.
