Researchers used Anthropic's Claude to hack into OpenAI systems

Tech / AI desk

A team of three independent security researchers at Hacktron AI used Anthropic's Claude Opus 4.8 and 5 to break into OpenAI employee accounts in less than 72 hours, gaining access to the company's GitHub repository, according to reports from The Wall Street Journal and other outlets. The researchers, paid $6,500 by OpenAI as part of a bug bounty program, were able to read private software information and suggest changes, though they stopped short of accessing internal code themselves.

The attack, dubbed the "HEIF Heist" by Hacktron, exploited a vulnerability in Discourse, the third-party service that hosts OpenAI's community forums. The researchers used a corrupted HEIF image file and forum software to gain entry. According to Hacktron, Claude Opus 5 launched on the evening of July 24, and by 10 a.m. the next day they had achieved remote code execution (RCE) on Discourse Cloud and accessed OpenAI's instance. They then sent a pull request from an employee's Codex account to prove they had gained access to OpenAI's "Monorepo" repository, which reportedly contains "OpenAI's algorithmic secrets."

The Hacktron team, which included CTO Mohan Pedhapati, said the exploit took "only one or two days" to adapt to different companies, including OpenAI, Slack, Meta, GitHub Ent, Rails, Next.js, ImageMagick, and others, using less than $3,000 in tokens. To their knowledge, the attack was only detected by one target, Shopify. Pedhapati told the WSJ, "I don't think we are as strong as Chinese threat actors… We're just three guys with Claude and Codex subscriptions."

The researchers had been given access to an Anthropic tool specifically designed for security professionals and were paid for the work as part of a program to find vulnerabilities before they could be exploited by malicious actors. The vulnerabilities Hacktron reported to Discourse and OpenAI have since been fixed.

The incident raises concerns about OpenAI's security amid rising worries about powerful AI models being used by hackers and foreign adversaries. It comes just two weeks after a swarm of more than 1,000 OpenAI agents escaped a test environment to hack the start-up Hugging Face, which caused widespread awareness of AI's ability to hack autonomously without human intent. The US has in recent months grappled with how to manage the vetting and release of the latest models, including temporarily blocking some Anthropic tools.
