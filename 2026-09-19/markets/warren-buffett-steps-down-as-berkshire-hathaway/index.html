Warren Buffett steps down as Berkshire Hathaway chairman; son Howard named successor

Financial & Markets desk

Warren Buffett, the 96-year-old legendary investor who has led Berkshire Hathaway since 1965, stepped down as chairman of the conglomerate on Friday, effective immediately. Buffett will become chairman emeritus while remaining on the board of directors, and his son Howard Buffett will succeed him as chairman, the company announced. Susan Decker will continue as lead independent director. The move follows a long-standing succession plan and comes just over nine months after Greg Abel replaced Buffett as CEO in May 2025.

In a letter to shareholders, Buffett wrote, "Father Time always wins. He has, however, been generous with me. He has given me the opportunity to see Berkshire reach a point where I am more confident than ever about what lies ahead." He also said of Howard, "Greg runs the company; Howard will guard its culture and values — both worth more than anything on our balance sheet. Think of Howard as a policy the shareholders own and hope never to claim against." Abel stated in the company release, "The culture Warren built and the values he championed will remain at the heart of Berkshire, and Howard will be their guardian."

Buffett's legacy at Berkshire is unparalleled in corporate America. He took over a failed New England textiles mill at age 34 and transformed it over six decades into a financial and industrial juggernaut with $44.5 billion in operating earnings last year and nearly 400,000 employees. Under his tenure, Berkshire posted a 19.7% compounded annual return to shareholders, nearly double that of the S&P 500. Even after stepping down as CEO, Buffett remained active as chairman, coming into the Omaha office daily and consulting frequently with Abel, as Abel told CNBC in March. In July, Buffett revealed to CNBC that he was the driving force behind Berkshire's recent $10 billion private stock purchase in Alphabet, now Berkshire's third-biggest stock holding.

Buffett acknowledged his age-related limitations in the letter, writing, "Recently, I celebrated my 96th birthday with family and friends, including one of my great-grandchildren, who had just turned one. He's moving a bit faster than I am these days." In a Thanksgiving letter to shareholders last year, he had written, "To my surprise, I generally feel good. Though I move slowly and read with increasing difficulty, I am at the office five days a week."

Berkshire shares have struggled in 2026, up just 1% while the S&P 500 has rallied more than 11%, partly due to rising oil prices and investor preference for higher-growth sectors. Buffett's exit as chairman raises the stakes for Abel to perform. Investors are watching whether Abel can deploy Berkshire's $365.5 billion cash hoard as adeptly as Buffett; Abel has stepped up share repurchases to $4.5 billion in the second quarter. In his letter, Buffett praised Abel: "My expectations for him were sky high from the start, and he has exceeded them." Abel told CNBC, "Warren described in his letter today how his role at Berkshire has been 'the best job in the world.' He gave me an extraordinary responsibility — the best job in American business — and then the latitude to lead in a manner consistent with Berkshire's culture and values." Buffett closed, "The company is in excellent hands, and I look forward to remaining a shareholder alongside you."
