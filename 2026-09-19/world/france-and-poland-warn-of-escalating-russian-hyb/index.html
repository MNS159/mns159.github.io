France and Poland warn of escalating Russian hybrid attacks

International & World desk

French President Emmanuel Macron warned on Friday that Russia has intensified hybrid attacks against Europe and France in recent weeks, and announced a plan to protect critical infrastructure and defense industry sites. Speaking at a news conference at the Elysee Palace, Macron said the attacks—which include cyberattacks, sabotage, and misinformation—are intended to intimidate European nations and undermine their support for Ukraine. 'The result will be the exact opposite. We are not intimidated because we know that our security today and tomorrow is at stake in Ukraine,' he stated, adding that the attacks would not be left 'without a response.'

Macron cited last month's drone attack on Leipzig airport in Germany, which has been blamed on Russia, and remarks by Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk suggesting that Russia is planning drone and missile strikes against Poland and other NATO countries. Tusk told Polish MPs on Thursday that there is a 'real risk' of such strikes on NATO territory, with Moscow then claiming they were accidental in an attempt to split the alliance. The warnings follow a Russian drone strike on a passenger train near the Ukraine-Poland border last Sunday, shortly after former UK Prime Minister Boris Johnson and top European security officials had passed along the same line; Ukraine said all passengers were evacuated minutes before the engine was hit.

Hybrid attacks, a term encompassing non-military methods such as cyberattacks, sabotage, and disinformation, are often carried out through proxies, and Russia routinely denies involvement despite mounting evidence. Macron noted that a series of suspicious fires at defense facilities from Italy to Estonia have also been blamed on Russia. Russian President Vladimir Putin has repeatedly said Russia does not plan to attack Europe, but Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov warned that if Europe attacks Russia, 'it will be a completely different war, and it will be very short.' On Friday, Russia also reiterated that any British military facilities in Ukraine used to strike Russian territory are 'legitimate targets.'

Macron said the French government has been tasked with protecting critical infrastructure and the most sensitive defense industry and technology sites against drone and cyberattacks. He announced that France will convene a meeting of the Group of Seven (G7) to discuss energy issues, including cooperation and potential release of strategic reserves. In a domestic context, Macron called on candidates in next year's elections not to exploit public unease over fuel price inflation, which he attributed to uncontrollable international circumstances. The hard-right opposition National Rally, led by Marine Le Pen, currently leads polls for the April-May presidential vote.
