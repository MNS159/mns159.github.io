UN report documents widespread sexual violence by Russian forces in Ukraine

International & World desk · anchored on Reuters

A new report from the UN Office of the High Commissioner for Human Rights (OHCHR) has documented a 'shocking pattern' of sexual violence by Russian troops in Ukraine, attributing 89% of 1,004 verified cases to Russian forces since the full-scale invasion began in February 2022. The report, published on Sept. 18, covers the period through July 2026 and includes victims ranging in age from 4 to 82. 'I am deeply alarmed by the prevalence of these abhorrent acts documented in our report, and fear that they are almost certainly continuing amid the unceasing violence we are witnessing every day in Ukraine,' said UN High Commissioner for Human Rights Volker Türk.

The 20-page report, based on confidential interviews with hundreds of survivors, court documents, and official records, details crimes including rape, gang rape, genital mutilation, electric shocks, and other acts of sexual humiliation. The most common single method recorded was the use of a Soviet-era military field telephone known as a 'tapik' to administer electric shocks to the genitals, reported in 155 cases (27% of the total). Individual testimonies describe Ukrainian prisoners of war being sexually assaulted with batons, cell door keys, or shovel handles. A woman in Zaporizhzhia was raped several times a week for a month by a Russian soldier who promised better treatment for her detained husband; a woman in Kherson was assaulted nightly and repeatedly threatened with gang rape; and in the Kyiv region, two Russian soldiers shot and killed a woman's husband before gang-raping her while threatening to shoot her in front of her four-year-old son.

While the majority of documented victims are men, particularly those held in detention, women and children have been most severely affected in occupied Ukrainian territory. The youngest documented rape survivor was a four-year-old girl, and the oldest an 82-year-old woman, both cases perpetrated by Russian forces. Of the 11% of cases attributed to Ukrainian forces, nearly half involved threats of sexual violence against Russian prisoners of war and prisoners of war from third countries; the remainder included forced nudity, electric shocks and beatings to the genitals, sexually degrading treatment, and two cases each of sexual assault and attempted rape. The report notes that the Ukrainian government has taken 'concrete steps to prevent and address such violations,' while Russia has failed to do the same.

Russia was added in May to the UN Secretary-General's list of parties credibly suspected of committing sexual violence in conflict, which includes Sudan, Somalia, and Haiti. The Kremlin rejected the findings as politically motivated. Russia formally withdrew from the European Convention for the Prevention of Torture in September 2025 following its expulsion from the Council of Europe, ending visits by European monitors to Russian detention facilities, though it remains a party to the UN Convention Against Torture. The Ukrainian foreign ministry welcomed the report, saying it 'once again demonstrated the scale of systematic human rights violations committed by the Russian Federation.' Türk stated: 'Sexual violence is a grave feature of the full-scale armed attack against Ukraine. It must stop immediately, irrespective of the perpetrators. Given the high prevalence of cases attributed to its forces, however, the Russian Federation in particular must take concrete and discernible steps to protect civilians and prisoners of war from all forms of torture, including sexual violence.'
