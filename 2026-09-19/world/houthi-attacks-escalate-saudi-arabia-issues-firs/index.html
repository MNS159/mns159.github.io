Houthi attacks escalate; Saudi Arabia issues first air raid alerts for Riyadh

International & World desk

Saudi Arabia issued its first air raid alerts for the capital Riyadh since July, when the Iran-backed Houthi group declared a maritime embargo and escalated its attacks, authorities said. The Saudi Civil Defence sent alerts for Riyadh and several other regions late Friday and early Saturday local time, and journalists reported hearing explosions in the capital. Officials later said the danger had passed and urged residents to avoid crowds.

The alerts follow a series of Houthi territorial gains and strikes on Saudi infrastructure. The group recently seized the strategic port city of Mokha and Perim Island, a gateway to a key Red Sea shipping route used by Saudi Arabia for oil exports to Asia and Europe. Houthi military spokesman Yahya Sarea said the naval blockade was retaliation for a Saudi blockade of ports and airports in Houthi-controlled northwestern Yemen. Last week, Houthi drones and missiles hit several Saudi oil facilities, causing fires and temporarily halting operations.

Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman personally asked US President Donald Trump for military action against the Houthis, two sources familiar with the matter told CBS News, the BBC’s US media partner. Trump declined direct US military involvement but offered intelligence and targeting support, the sources said. A senior Trump administration official told the BBC: 'We are in continuous dialogue with Saudi Arabia and the Republic of Yemen government regarding regional stability.' The United Nations Security Council held an emergency meeting Tuesday, during which both Yemen’s government and Saudi Arabia called for stronger action against the Houthis, accusing them of choking the Bab al-Mandab Strait to apply economic pressure. Council members agreed freedom of navigation in the Red Sea must be maintained.

The attacks on Saudi oil assets have directly affected global energy markets, with petrol and diesel prices rising sharply in recent weeks, compounding elevated costs from blockades of the Strait of Hormuz. Broader disruption to Red Sea shipping prompted a Saudi-led multinational naval coalition meeting this week aimed at protecting waterways including the Bab al-Mandeb Strait.

The Houthis’ growing military capability has elevated their role in Iran’s 'axis of resistance.' The group recently shot down a Saudi F-15 fighter jet using a missile it said it modified, a feat Hezbollah never achieved. Analysts quoted in German outlet DW described the Houthis as more autonomous than Hezbollah, with their own leadership and ideology, but noted their alignment with Tehran appears to have tightened. The group’s zero-sum approach, heavy taxation, and focus on fighting—rather than public services—have made them difficult to rein in through sanctions or military campaigns.

Yemen has been devastated by a civil war that began in 2014, when the Houthis ousted the government from Sanaa. A Saudi-led coalition intervened in 2015. The UN reports more than 150,000 people have been killed and over 22 million need humanitarian aid, making it one of the world’s worst crises. The Houthis’ recent advances, including the seizure of a strategically important island, have widened the regional conflict and frustrated international efforts to contain it.
