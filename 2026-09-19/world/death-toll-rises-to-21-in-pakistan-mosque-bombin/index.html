Death toll rises to 21 in Pakistan mosque bombing

International & World desk

A suicide car-bomb attack on a mosque at a police compound in northwestern Pakistan has killed at least 21 people and injured more than 100, according to officials. The attack occurred at approximately 13:15 local time on Friday at a mosque inside a police facility in the city of Kohat, in the province of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa. Among the dead are 15 police officers and a four-year-old child, according to Pakistan's APP news agency.

Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Police said a car loaded with explosives rammed the mosque entrance while Friday prayers were underway. Gunmen also fired on the police compound. At least six of the attackers were killed in the explosion and ensuing gun battle, a police spokesperson reported. Police suggested a faction of the Tehrik-e-Taliban Pakistan (TTP), also known as the Pakistani Taliban, carried out the attack. In a post on X, the force referred to the attackers using the designation Fitna-al-Khawarij, the official term for the Pakistani Taliban.

The TTP was formed in 2007 with the aim of enforcing its strict version of Islamic law and is allied with al-Qaeda and the Afghan Taliban. Militancy in Pakistan's border areas has risen sharply in recent months, with armed groups targeting the military and police. Islamabad has accused militants of using Afghanistan as a safe haven to train and plan attacks; Afghanistan's Taliban government denies the allegation, stating militant groups are a domestic problem for Pakistan.

Pakistan's President Asif Ali Zardari expressed his best wishes for the recovery of the injured and called for the complete elimination of foreign-backed terrorists. A UN spokesman said Secretary-General António Guterres strongly condemns the bombing, adding that attacks targeting civilians and places of worship are unacceptable. Faisal Karim Kundi, governor of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, said the province has endured immense sacrifices in the fight against terrorism.

In February, officials accused the Pakistani Taliban of being behind a series of attacks, including the suicide bombing of a Shia mosque in Islamabad in which more than 30 people were killed. Funerals were being held in Kohat following Friday's attack.
