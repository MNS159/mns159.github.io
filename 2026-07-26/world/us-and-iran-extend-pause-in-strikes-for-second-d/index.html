US and Iran pause strikes for second day amid diplomatic push

International & World desk · anchored on Reuters
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts slightly center-left

The United States and Iran have paused their military strikes for a second consecutive day, raising hopes of a return to diplomacy after nearly two weeks of escalating conflict. The US halted its bombing campaign against Iran on Saturday, and Tehran subsequently suspended its retaliatory attacks across the region. The relative calm comes as mediators, including Oman and Qatar, work to revive negotiations on an interim ceasefire deal that collapsed amid a dispute over the Strait of Hormuz.

US Ambassador to the United Nations Mike Waltz told Fox News on Sunday that President Donald Trump was 'giving [the potential for] talks … a little bit of room,' while noting that negotiators have been engaged at every level over the past few days. CBS News, citing two regional sources, reported that the US decision to pause the bombing was linked to the arrival of an Omani delegation in Tehran. Iranian Foreign Ministry spokesperson Esmaeil Baghaei told Austrian broadcaster ORF that talks with Oman about managing traffic in the Strait of Hormuz were 'useful, and progress was made,' adding that 'technical and political consultations' are ongoing.

Despite the pause, both sides maintained their hard-line rhetoric. Iranian army spokesman Mohammad Akraminia said Iran had halted its retaliatory operations but warned the conflict would widen if US attacks resume. Trump shared AI-generated images on social media depicting US fighters bombing Iran and a US flag planted on an Iranian tanker. The US military's Central Command said its naval blockade of Iran 'remains in full effect,' reporting that as of Saturday it had redirected 12 commercial vessels, disabled two, and boarded two to ensure compliance. Iranian media reported that the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps stopped six vessels by firing warning shots, and that an oil tanker exploded after hitting a mine in the strait.

The pause comes amid growing domestic pressure on Trump to end the war. An Ipsos/Washington Post poll found that only 29 percent of Americans approve of the president's handling of the conflict. Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, set to meet Trump in Washington on Tuesday, warned that a resumption of attacks on Israel by Iran or its allies would be a 'terrible mistake' and would elicit a 'very, very forceful response.' Iran's supreme leader, Mojtaba Khamenei, said on Sunday that any peace deal with the US is contingent on an 'absolute, unconditional termination' of Israel's attacks on Lebanon, reiterating Tehran's support for Hezbollah.

The conflict has disrupted international maritime corridors beyond the Strait of Hormuz. The Houthis in Yemen have declared a blockade against Saudi Arabia, turning back or hitting vessels near the Bab al-Mandeb strait and bombing Saudi oil facilities. Ukraine confirmed it struck an Iranian commercial vessel in the Caspian Sea, killing one sailor and wounding three, prompting Iran to summon Kyiv's charge d'affaires and warn that 'the act will not go unanswered.' The war has also deepened Iran's economic crisis: the US naval blockade has severely curtailed oil exports, the country faces a daily petrol deficit of more than 20 million litres, and authorities are considering doubling the price of a third tier of monthly petrol quotas. Rolling electricity cuts are affecting Tehran and other cities, and nonoil trade with China has fallen by 75 percent since the war began.
