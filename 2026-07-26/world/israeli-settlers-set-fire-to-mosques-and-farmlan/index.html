Israeli settlers set fire to mosques and farmland in West Bank

International & World desk

Israeli settlers set fire to two mosques, cars and agricultural land in the occupied West Bank overnight, Palestinian officials said, as tensions in the territory continue to escalate. The attacks targeted the villages of Qusra and Kour, with settlers spray-painting graffiti that included the word 'revenge' on the walls of one mosque, according to the mayor of Qusra, Abdul Azim Wadi. The Israeli military said troops were sent to Qusra and that police would investigate and collect evidence.

The latest incidents come two days after a clash between settlers and Palestinians near the village of Tal, which left four Palestinians and two Israelis dead. Both sides accused the other of provoking the violence. Since that deadly confrontation, Palestinians say there have been multiple settler attacks on villages across the West Bank. In the village of Sarra, residents said six buildings were torched and more than a dozen others attacked with stones. Hanan Turabi, a resident, described seeing crowds of settlers running toward her house and fleeing with her four children at the last second. 'They came from everywhere,' she said. 'We tried to escape but they followed us and threw stones.' Kaied Ahmad Turabi, visiting from Miami, was treated for injuries including a head wound that required 12 stitches. 'They came ready,' he said of the attackers, who he said had covered faces and carried sticks.

The violence has drawn international condemnation but shown little sign of being reversed. The United Nations says 1,114 Palestinians have been killed in the West Bank since the start of the Gaza war in October 2023, the vast majority by the Israel Defense Forces (IDF), and at least 35 of those deaths have involved attacks by settlers. It says 41 Israelis, including civilians and security personnel, have been killed in Palestinian attacks or during Israeli military operations in the West Bank in the same period. The IDF has carried out raids and arrested more than 130 individuals over the weekend in what it called a 'counter-terrorism' operation.

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu responded to the Tal incident by vowing to take 'powerful action,' including the possible demolition of the homes of two Palestinians allegedly involved. He also said he would speed up the establishment and legalisation of farm outposts in the West Bank. New Israeli outposts have already appeared in the area since then, including one near Sarra visible from the main road. Israel's military said it was working to prevent 'incidents of nationalist crime'—its term for attacks by extremist settlers. The settlements, which house about 700,000 Jews alongside an estimated 3.3 million Palestinians, are illegal under international law. Palestinian officials say at least 45 mosques have been vandalised or burned by settlers since 2025.
