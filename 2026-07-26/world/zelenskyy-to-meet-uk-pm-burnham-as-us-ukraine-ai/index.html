Zelenskyy to meet UK PM Burnham as US-Ukraine air ceasefire talks reported

International & World desk · anchored on Reuters
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts slightly center-left

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy is set to meet UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham on Monday in what will be Burnham's first international visit since taking office a week ago, Downing Street said. The meeting comes as Reuters reported that Ukrainian and US officials have discussed a proposal to offer Russia an air ceasefire, to be presented as part of a new initiative to revive peace talks. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said on Sunday that it was premature to comment, stating: "We do not know how credible these reports are or where they come from. These are newspaper reports and nothing more."

During the visit, Burnham will announce the sharing of the intellectual property behind the UK's new "Stone Cloak" electronic warfare system, enabling Ukraine to produce the technology at scale. The electronic jammers, the size of a tablet computer, are designed to block the detection of any drone they are fitted to. The Ministry of Defence has already given thousands of the devices to Ukrainian forces. Downing Street said that following its deployment in Ukraine, the technology will be built into the UK's next generation of long-range weapons, including Project Brakestop, a government programme to develop low-cost cruise missiles.

Burnham said: "Britain stands with Ukraine, shoulder to shoulder, and our support remains unwavering. Russia should be in no doubt of our resolve, and we will not back down until we achieve long lasting and just peace for Ukraine." He added: "Stone Cloak is the best of homegrown British innovation and proven on the frontline, and it will be vital to protecting our security in both our countries."

The two leaders will also meet more than 200 Ukrainian military personnel who have spent the last three weeks taking part in Exercise Sea Breeze, a maritime exercise in the UK aimed at strengthening warfighting capabilities and counter-mine measures in the Black Sea. Since Russia's full-scale invasion in February 2022, total UK support for Ukraine has reached £25 billion, including £16 billion in military assistance.

Separately, Romania said it was summoning Russia's ambassador after a Romanian F-16 fighter jet shot down a drone over the Black Sea, five minutes after it entered the country's airspace. Romania's defence minister, Radu Miruță, confirmed the incident, and the foreign minister, Oana Țoiu, condemned "repeated violations". The Romanian president, Nicușor Dan, said an investigation found that a drone shot down on Friday was a "Shahed model used by the Russian Federation in its war of aggression against Ukraine."

In overnight attacks, Russia launched seven ballistic missiles and 136 drones at Ukraine, the Ukrainian air force said. In Chernihiv, a Russian drone hit a supermarket, killing two people including a nine-year-old child and wounding 25. In Kharkiv, one person was killed and 16 wounded in a daytime drone attack. A glide bomb attack in Zaporizhzhia killed one and wounded six. In Kyiv, a missile attack sparked a fire in a multi-storey apartment block, wounding three. In Sloviansk, two Naftogaz employees died in a Russian attack while trying to help residents. Russia said four people were killed in a Ukrainian drone strike in Russian-occupied Horlivka, and 12 people including two children were injured in a drone attack on the Russian city of Belgorod. Ukraine denies targeting civilians.
