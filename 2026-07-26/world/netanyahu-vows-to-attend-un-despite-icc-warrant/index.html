Netanyahu Vows to Attend UN Despite ICC Warrant, Slams NYC Mayor Mamdani

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said Sunday he plans to attend the United Nations General Assembly in New York City this autumn, defying an International Criminal Court (ICC) arrest warrant and criticism from New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani, whom he accused of 'fomenting hate.' In an interview on Fox News's 'Sunday Morning Futures,' Netanyahu stated, 'I intend to come and speak the truth – speak for Israel and for the Israeli-American alliance from the podium of the United Nations.' He dismissed concerns about Mamdani's previous pledge to have him arrested, saying he was 'not concerned.'

Netanyahu accused Mamdani of trying to 'turn one group against the other,' claiming Jewish New Yorkers 'are afraid right now.' He referenced a pair of stabbings in New York on Thursday, in which a suspect allegedly shouted 'Allahu akbar' while stabbing a Jewish man and an Asian man blocks apart; both men survived. Netanyahu also falsely claimed that Mamdani and his wife had 'celebrated' the October 7, 2023, Hamas-led attack on Israel. Mamdani has repeatedly referred to the October 7 attacks as a 'horrific war crime.' The mayor's office did not immediately respond to a request for comment.

Mamdani, a staunch supporter of Palestinian rights, made opposition to the Israeli military operation in Gaza a central plank of his campaign. He had promised to enforce the November 2024 ICC arrest warrant for Netanyahu, which accuses the prime minister of war crimes and crimes against humanity in Gaza. Last week, however, Mamdani walked back that threat, acknowledging his administration lacked the independent legal authority to enforce the warrant. He urged the federal government to arrest Netanyahu and stated, 'Benjamin Netanyahu is not welcome in New York City.' A YouGov poll published this week found that 46 percent of people in the US believe Netanyahu should be arrested, while 28 percent said he should not.

The ICC issued arrest warrants for Netanyahu and former Israeli Defense Minister Yoav Gallant in November 2024 for alleged crimes against humanity and war crimes. Israel has denied the charges, which Netanyahu called 'bogus.' The Israeli military entered Gaza after the Hamas-led attack on October 7, 2023, which killed about 1,200 people and took 251 hostages. Over 73,000 people have been killed in Israeli attacks in Gaza since then, including more than 21,000 children, according to the territory's Hamas-run health ministry, whose figures are considered reliable by the UN.

A June survey by the Associated Press-NORC Center for Public Affairs Research of over 1,000 Jewish adults across the US found that about 44 percent viewed Mamdani favorably, while about 39 percent viewed him unfavorably. In contrast, only about one-third of Jewish adults had a favorable opinion of Netanyahu, while about six in 10 had an unfavorable view, including 42 percent with a 'very' unfavorable opinion. The survey also indicated that 30 percent of Jewish adults believe Israel has committed genocide in Gaza.
