Wildfires force mass evacuations in France and Spain, disrupt Tour de France

International & World desk

Wildfires raging across southwestern France and parts of Spain have forced more than 300,000 people to evacuate their homes, authorities said Sunday, as the blazes disrupted the final stage of the Tour de France and caused widespread damage. In France, some 55,000 people were evacuated overnight into Sunday in the Gironde region near Bordeaux, bringing the total number of evacuees in the country’s southwest to more than 220,000, according to Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez. In Spain, more than 90,000 people have been evacuated from regions around Madrid and Ávila, with over 60,000 evacuated near the capital alone, the Interior Ministry reported.

The fires have burned vast areas: about 42,000 hectares in southwestern France, making it one of the largest forest fires since World War II, and more than 77,000 hectares in Spain, where the wildfire in Ávila is the largest in the country’s recent history, according to Ecological Transition Minister Sara Aagesen. In France, a total of about 98,000 hectares have burned across the country since the start of the year, a historic record, Nuñez said. In Spain, more than 300,000 acres have burned this year, Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez said, up from an annual average of 250,000 acres over the past decade.

French officials described the fire near Bordeaux as erratic and unmanageable. Captain Nicolas Braz, one of the firefighting leaders, said the fire had created “its own winds — complete with whirlwinds,” adding that it was “erratic and unmanageable.” Nuñez said the situation remained “very unfavourable” and the fire was “moving erratically towards” Bordeaux, though officials downplayed the possibility of evacuating the city. In the coastal town of Le Porge, more than 150 houses have been destroyed. Volunteer Nathalie Augonnet said, “I’m a little devastated for this beautiful town, for its forests, its wildlife and for the people who have lost so much.”

The fires have caused at least three deaths: two firefighters were killed earlier in the week near Bordeaux, and one civilian died in a smaller fire in the Valencia region of Spain, local officials said. In Spain, a new fire front opened near the coastal city of Valencia, where regional President Juanfran Pérez Llorca said, “The fire is beyond our capacity to extinguish.” Sunday brought high temperatures and low humidity, raising concerns that the blazes could spread further. Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez, visiting an area west of Madrid, said “difficult hours lie ahead” but noted “positive” overnight indications toward containment. He called for “a major public pact on the climate emergency.” Madrid’s regional president, Isabel Díaz Ayuso, described the situation as the “worst fire in the history of the region.”

Firefighting efforts in France’s Gironde region involve 2,500 firefighters, including a Swiss team, 1,500 military personnel, and nearly 1,200 police and gendarmes. Dry vegetation and brisk winds are driving the spread, and 60 kilometers of the A63 motorway to Spain have been closed. Bordeaux Airport imposed air traffic control restrictions “to facilitate the work of the emergency services,” delaying all commercial flights. The final stage of the Tour de France was shortened from 133 km to 89 km, and its start time was delayed, because security forces were reassigned to assist with the fires. Tadej Pogačar of Slovenia won his fifth Tour de France on Sunday, tying the record for most victories. The final stage was won by Dutch rider Mathieu van der Poel.

The World Health Organization has warned that climate change is contributing to the frequency and intensity of such fires, as Europe has experienced record temperatures and successive heatwaves since May. French President Emmanuel Macron said the country would “rebuild” and his government would “be there for as long as is needed.” Spain’s Interior Minister Fernando Grande-Marlaska declared a national state of emergency because of the wildfires. Sánchez said the government is working to provide shelter for those affected.
