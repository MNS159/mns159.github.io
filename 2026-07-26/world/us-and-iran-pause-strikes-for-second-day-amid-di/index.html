US and Iran pause strikes for second day amid diplomatic push

International & World desk · anchored on Reuters

The United States and Iran have paused military strikes for a second consecutive day, marking the first sustained break in hostilities after nearly two weeks of nightly attacks. The relative calm comes as diplomatic efforts intensify to revive an interim ceasefire deal signed in mid-June, which has been largely undermined by the recent exchanges of fire. US Ambassador to the United Nations Mike Waltz told Fox News on Sunday that President Donald Trump was 'giving [the potential for] talks … a little bit of room,' while noting that negotiators have been engaged at every level over the past few days.

US broadcaster CBS, citing two regional sources, reported that the decision to pause the bombing was linked to the arrival of an Omani delegation in Tehran. Iran’s Foreign Ministry spokesperson Esmaeil Baghaei told Austrian broadcaster ORF that mediators including Qatar and Pakistan are exchanging messages between the two countries, and that talks with Oman about managing traffic in the Strait of Hormuz were 'useful, and progress was made.' However, Baghaei added that Iran’s past efforts at diplomacy have been 'betrayed,' reflecting lingering mistrust. A senior Iranian official, speaking anonymously to Reuters, said Iran’s position remains 'attack for attack' and that the pause is viewed as 'tactical rather than genuine.'

The New York Times reported that Trump paused plans to escalate attacks due to concerns about draining the Pentagon’s diminishing stockpile of missile interceptors and other air defense munitions in the Middle East. White House communications director Steven Cheung said Trump 'has always been consistent in saying he prefers a diplomatic solution, but he continues to retain all options if Iran continues terrorist activities in the Strait of Hormuz or against allies.' Iran’s army spokesman Mohammad Akraminia said Iran had halted its retaliatory operations, but warned that the conflict would widen further if the US continues its attacks.

The pause follows two weeks of escalating conflict that began with Iranian attacks on shipping in the Strait of Hormuz, a key global energy route. The US responded with nightly strikes on Iranian coastal areas and infrastructure, while Iran counterattacked against countries hosting US military forces, killing at least three at a base in Jordan. The US military’s Central Command said its naval blockade of Iran 'remains in full effect,' reporting that as of Saturday it had redirected 12 commercial vessels, disabled two, and boarded two. Iranian media reported that the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps stopped six vessels by firing warning shots, and that an oil tanker exploded after hitting a mine in the strait.

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, set to meet with Trump in Washington on Tuesday, told Fox News he 'fully' backs Trump’s efforts to weaken Iran and press it to end its nuclear program, adding that if Iran again attacks Israel, 'it will make a terrible mistake.' A regional official involved in mediation said the pause is a 'positive signal' and that a compromise being negotiated centers on having Iran manage vessel transit through the Strait of Hormuz with fewer restrictions. A poll by Ipsos and The Washington Post found that only 29 percent of Americans surveyed approve of Trump’s handling of the war with Iran, and 28 percent say the war was worth it, underscoring growing domestic pressure on the president to find an exit strategy.
