Berlin Pride attack suspect shot dead by police

International & World desk

German police shot and killed Abdul Ballout, the 21-year-old German citizen of Lebanese heritage suspected of carrying out a deadly attack on Berlin's Christopher Street Day Pride event, ending a massive manhunt that had lasted less than 24 hours. Officers opened fire in the Spandau district of western Berlin on Sunday evening after Ballout rushed at them with a knife, police said. Despite immediate resuscitation efforts, he died at the scene.

The attack occurred around 10 p.m. local time Saturday in Tiergarten park near the Brandenburg Gate, where hundreds of thousands had gathered for one of Europe's largest Pride celebrations. A white van was driven into a crowd, killing one woman and injuring at least 29 others. Officials said the suspect then left the vehicle and attacked additional people with a bladed weapon, believed to be a machete. The Pride event was immediately cancelled, with organizers projecting an evacuation notice on the main stage screen.

German Interior Minister Alexander Dobrindt told reporters Sunday that evidence pointed to the attack being an "Islamist terror attack." He described Ballout as a suspected perpetrator who had drawn attention through "a high volume of criminal offences, radicalisation and affiliation with the Islamist scene." According to the Berlin public prosecutor's office, Ballout traveled to Lebanon in May 2025 intending to join the Islamic State group in Syria, was arrested there in July 2025, and sentenced to three months for incitement. After returning to Germany in November 2025, he was held in pre-trial detention and in May 2026 received a suspended sentence of one year and 10 months for "preparing a serious act of violence endangering the state," with a six-month deferral and supervision by a parole officer. He had prior convictions for assault and robbery in 2022 and for robbing a victim of headphones in 2020.

German Chancellor Friedrich Merz visited the scene Sunday and later spoke outside a church service for victims, pledging to "defend the freedom and openness of our lives." "These extremists have no place in our society," he said, adding that Germany would not allow the "poison of terrorism" to affect its society. Dobrindt said Germany's threat level would be raised following the attack, and other states were being advised to review security measures for major events. European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen expressed sympathy, saying the Pride event stands for "freedom, dignity, and equality — the fundamental values of the EU."

Berlin police confirmed that those injured in the attack were no longer in life-threatening condition. A clean-up event and breakfast market scheduled for Sunday were cancelled. Later in the day, members of the LGBT community gathered at the Brandenburg Gate for an impromptu vigil, where speakers condemned extremism but also criticized recent budget cuts for queer organizations. Armin Duenhoelter, a drag artist from Wolfsburg who was backstage at the main stage when the attack occurred, told Agence France-Presse he was still shocked but vowed to return for next year's Pride: "We won't let it beat us down." Berlin Mayor Kai Wegner called the capture of the suspect within 24 hours "a major success for the investigating authorities."
