Tens of thousands evacuated as wildfires rage across Europe and North Africa

International & World desk

Tens of thousands of people have been evacuated from their homes as wildfires continue to rage across parts of Europe and North Africa, with France and Spain among the hardest-hit countries. In France, approximately 55,000 people were evacuated overnight into Sunday from villages south of Bordeaux in the Gironde region, bringing the total number of evacuees in the country's southwest to more than 220,000, according to Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez. In Spain, more than 90,000 people have been evacuated from regions around Madrid and Ávila, with additional evacuations near Valencia. Across both countries, more than 330,000 people have been forced to flee.

The fires in France's Gironde region, famed for its vineyards, have burned about 42,000 hectares, making it one of the largest forest fires since World War Two, officials said. Nationwide, approximately 98,000 hectares have burned since the start of the year, a historic record, Nuñez said Saturday. In Spain, around 77,000 hectares have been affected, according to Minister for Ecological Transition Sara Aagesen, who described the wildfire in the Ávila region as the largest in the country's recent history. More than 150 houses have been destroyed in the French coastal town of Le Porge alone.

Firefighting efforts in France involve 2,500 firefighters, including a Swiss team, 1,500 military personnel, and nearly 1,200 police and gendarmes. Captain Nicolas Braz, a leader of the firefighting operation, said the fire had created "its own winds - complete with whirlwinds," adding it was "erratic and unmanageable." In Spain, Interior Minister Fernando Grande-Marlaska declared a national state of emergency. Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez said "difficult hours lie ahead" but noted "positive" indications overnight toward containing the blaze near Madrid, and called for "a major public pact on the climate emergency."

The fires have also disrupted transportation and events. Bordeaux Airport reported delays to every flight from Sunday afternoon onward, with air traffic control restrictions put in place to facilitate emergency services. The final stage of the Tour de France was shortened from 133 km to 89 km to allow security forces to be redeployed to the firefighting effort. In North Africa, extreme heat and strong winds are fueling wildfires in Tunisia, Algeria, and Morocco, with Tunisia calling in international firefighting aircraft to battle fires threatening forests, farmland, and communities.

At least one civilian has been killed in a smaller fire in Spain's Valencia region, and two firefighters were killed earlier in the week fighting the blaze near Bordeaux. Residents in affected areas have organized volunteer networks to support firefighters. "I think many of them have never experienced a fire of this magnitude before," said Nathalie Augonnet, a volunteer in Le Porge. French President Emmanuel Macron said France would "rebuild" and his government would "be there for as long as is needed." The World Health Organization has warned that climate change is contributing to the frequency and intensity of such fires, as record temperatures and successive heatwaves have been seen across Europe since May.
