Israel approves international stabilisation force for Gaza

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

Israel’s government has approved the entry of the International Stabilization Force (ISF) into Gaza, part of US President Donald Trump’s 20-point “peace plan,” an Israeli official reported on Sunday. The security cabinet approved key legal measures for the force, which will operate “in full coordination with” the Israeli military, according to the Times of Israel. No timeframe was given for deployment.

The ISF will include 200 personnel from “friendly countries such as Uganda and Morocco,” according to Reuters. The cabinet determined that Israel will have the authority to approve which countries can send forces. The force will operate in areas of the Palestinian enclave that are outside Israel’s military control. Israel currently occupies close to 70 percent of Gaza, and its population of almost two million has been forced to live in a diminishing fraction of the strip as Israel continues to move the “Yellow Line” demarcating areas it withdrew to under the first phase of the ceasefire deal.

The plan also calls for an international “Board of Peace” to oversee the administration and reconstruction of the strip, the creation of a National Committee for the Administration of Gaza (NCAG) technocratic governing body, and for Hamas to disarm and Israel to withdraw from the strip. Nickolay Mladenov, director-general of the Gaza Board of Peace – which the vast majority of Western countries refused to join – welcomed the news. He said Israel enabling the deployment was “a critical part of the agreed framework for stabilising Gaza, supporting demilitarisation, and enabling the transition to effective Palestinian transitional administration” under the NCAG.

Though a ceasefire between Hamas and Israel was agreed in October, reports of killings of Palestinians by the Israeli military occur almost daily. The Palestinian Ministry of Health reports that 1,200 Palestinians have been killed since the deal was implemented, with 3,888 wounded. Following implementation of Trump’s deal, Israel dictates who can and cannot enter the Gaza Strip, along with how much aid and food can enter, in the wake of Israel’s two-year, genocidal war on Gaza.

Displaced Palestinians in Gaza cautiously welcomed the approval, hoping the force can help rebuild Gaza and allow them to return home after months of bombardment and displacement. The cabinet’s approval comes a day before Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu is due to travel to Washington, DC.
