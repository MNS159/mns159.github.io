US-Iran Conflict

US and Iran pause strikes for second day as diplomatic efforts continue

International & World desk · anchored on Reuters

The United States and Iran have paused military strikes for a second consecutive day, marking the first sustained halt in hostilities since the US began a nightly bombing campaign against Iran nearly two weeks ago. No US attacks on Iran were reported on Saturday or Sunday, and Iran’s army spokesman, Mohammad Akraminia, confirmed that Tehran had also halted its retaliatory operations. “Our strategy has essentially been retaliatory. We have also halted our retaliatory operations,” Akraminia said, according to the AFP news agency. The pause follows 13 nights of US airstrikes on Iranian coastal areas and infrastructure, which Iran had met with counterstrikes on countries in the region hosting US military forces.

US Ambassador to the United Nations Mike Waltz told Fox News on Sunday that President Donald Trump was “giving [the potential for] talks … a little bit of room.” White House communications director Steven Cheung said Trump “has always been consistent in saying he prefers a diplomatic solution, but he continues to retain all options if Iran continues terrorist activities in the Strait of Hormuz or against allies.” A regional official involved in mediation efforts, speaking on condition of anonymity, called the pause a “positive signal that helps their efforts to de-escalate.” The New York Times reported that Trump paused plans to escalate attacks partly due to concerns about the Pentagon’s diminishing stockpile of missile interceptors and other air defense munitions in the Middle East. CNN and the Times identified Pentagon Chief of Staff Dan Caine as having raised such concerns; CNN also said Vice President JD Vance had expressed reservations.

The pause comes amid renewed diplomatic efforts. US broadcaster CBS, citing two regional sources, reported that the US decision to pause bombing was made due to the arrival of an Omani delegation in Tehran. Iran’s Foreign Ministry spokesperson, Esmaeil Baghaei, told Austrian broadcaster ORF that mediators including Qatar and Pakistan are exchanging messages between Iran and the US, but added that Iran’s past efforts at diplomacy have been “betrayed.” Baghaei said talks with Oman about managing traffic in the Strait of Hormuz were “useful, and progress was made,” with “technical and political consultations” ongoing. A regional official said a compromise being negotiated centers on having Iran run vessel transit through the strait with fewer restrictions on ships.

Despite the pause, tensions remain high in the Strait of Hormuz, a key global energy shipping route. The US military’s Central Command said its naval blockade of Iran “remains in full effect,” noting that as of Saturday it had redirected 12 commercial vessels, disabled two, and boarded two. Iranian media reported that the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps stopped six vessels by firing warning shots, and that an oil tanker exploded after hitting a mine in the strait. Iran asserts it controls passage through the waterway under a June memorandum of understanding with the US, which established a 60-day period for final terms to be negotiated. The US says Iran does not control the strait.

The conflict has widened to other fronts. Iran’s Houthi allies in Yemen have declared a naval blockade on Saudi shipping in the Red Sea, and the Houthis on Saturday claimed responsibility for attacks on Saudi oil facilities. On Sunday, the Houthis said they shot down a Saudi reconnaissance drone. In Yemen, the Saudi-backed internationally recognized government struck Houthi sites in Marib and al-Jawf provinces, and both sides were mobilizing forces, officials said. Meanwhile, Iran’s supreme leader, Mojtaba Khamenei, said on Sunday that peace between Tehran and Washington is contingent on Israel ending its attacks on Lebanon. “The Islamic Republic of Iran considers the defense of these oppressed yet powerful mujahideen [of Hezbollah] to be its strategic mandate,” Khamenei wrote on X. Hezbollah launched strikes on northern Israel in March, drawing Lebanon into the conflict.

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, set to meet with Trump in Washington on Tuesday, told Fox News he “fully” backs Trump’s efforts to weaken Iran and press it to end its nuclear program, adding: “If he can do so without returning to intense military fighting, that's fine. Why not?” Netanyahu warned that if Iran again attacks Israel, “it will make a terrible mistake.” A senior Iranian source told Reuters that Iran’s position remains “attack for attack”: if the US stops attacks, Iran will halt its operations, but the source added that “there is more scepticism than optimism about the halt in attacks. The prevailing view is that the pause is tactical rather than genuine.” The source said Iran is prepared to mount a broad response if the US launches another attack. A poll by Ipsos and The Washington Post indicated that just 29 percent of Americans surveyed approve of Trump’s handling of the war with Iran.
