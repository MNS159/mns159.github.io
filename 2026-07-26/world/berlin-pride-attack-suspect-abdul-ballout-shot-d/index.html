Berlin Pride attack suspect Abdul Ballout shot dead by police

International & World desk

Berlin police shot and killed Abdul Ballout, the 21-year-old German citizen of Lebanese heritage suspected of carrying out a van-ramming and stabbing attack at a Berlin Pride event on Saturday evening, ending a 24-hour manhunt. Officers opened fire after Ballout rushed at them with a knife at an allotment garden complex in the western borough of Spandau on Sunday evening, police said. Despite immediate resuscitation efforts by the Berlin Fire Department, he died at the scene.

The attack occurred at about 22:00 local time (20:00 GMT) on Saturday in Tiergarten park, near the Brandenburg Gate, where hundreds of thousands had gathered for Christopher Street Day, one of Europe's largest Pride events. A white van was driven into a crowd on Ahornsteig, a long path through a forested area of the park, killing one woman and injuring at least 29 others. Officials said Ballout then left the vehicle and used a bladed weapon—believed to be a machete—to stab additional people before fleeing. The van was later found abandoned, having crashed into a tree. German media reported that those injured are no longer in life-threatening condition.

German Interior Minister Alexander Dobrindt said evidence pointed to the attack being an "Islamist terror attack," noting that Ballout was known to authorities for a "high volume of criminal offences, radicalisation and affiliation with the Islamist scene." According to the Berlin public prosecutor's office, Ballout traveled to Lebanon in May 2025 with the intention of joining the Islamic State group (IS) in Syria, and while there made contact with individuals he believed were IS members. He was arrested in Lebanon in July 2025 and sentenced to three months in prison for incitement to religious and sectarian conflict. After returning to Germany in November 2025, he was arrested at Berlin Brandenburg Airport and held in pre-trial detention. In May 2026, he was sentenced to one year and 10 months in prison for "preparing a serious act of violence endangering the state," but the sentence was deferred for six months and he was placed under the supervision of a parole officer. He had prior convictions for assault and robbery in 2022, and for robbing a victim of headphones in 2020.

German Chancellor Friedrich Merz visited the scene of the attack on Sunday and later spoke outside a church service for victims, pledging to "defend the freedom and openness of our lives." He said "these extremists have no place in our society" and that Germany would not allow the "poison of terrorism" to affect its society. Dobrindt said Germany's threat level would be raised following the attack, and other states were being briefed and advised to review security measures for major events. Berlin Mayor Kai Wegner called the arrest within 24 hours "a major success for the investigating authorities." European leaders, including European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen, condemned the attack, with von der Leyen saying the Pride event stands for "freedom, dignity, and equality—the fundamental values of the EU."

The attack shattered the joy of the Christopher Street Day celebration, which commemorates the 1969 Stonewall uprising in New York City. The Pride event was cancelled, and a clean-up event and breakfast market scheduled for Sunday were also called off. Later on Sunday, members of the LGBT community gathered at the Brandenburg Gate for an impromptu vigil, where speakers condemned extremism but also criticized the government for recent budget cuts to queer organizations. Armin Duenhoelter, a drag artist from Wolfsburg who was backstage at the main stage when the attack happened, told AFP he was still shocked but vowed to return for next year's pride, saying, "We won't let it beat us down."
