Berlin Pride ramming suspect shot dead by police after manhunt

International & World desk

German police shot and killed Abdul Ballout, the 21-year-old suspect in a deadly vehicle-ramming and stabbing attack at Berlin's Christopher Street Day Pride event, ending a 24-hour manhunt on Sunday evening. Ballout, a German citizen of Lebanese heritage, was located at an allotment garden complex in the Spandau district and rushed at officers with a bladed weapon, prompting police to open fire, authorities said. He died at the scene despite resuscitation efforts.

The attack occurred around 10:00 p.m. local time Saturday in Tiergarten park near the Brandenburg Gate, where hundreds of thousands had gathered for the annual Pride celebration. A white van drove into a crowd, killing one woman and injuring at least 29 people, several severely. After the vehicle crashed into a tree, the driver fled on foot, and officials said he then attacked additional people with a blade, possibly a machete. By Sunday afternoon, police confirmed none of the injured remained in life-threatening condition.

German Interior Minister Alexander Dobrindt said evidence indicated the attack was likely an "Islamist terror attack." Ballout was known to authorities for a "high number" of prior criminal offenses, radicalization, and affiliation with Islamist circles. According to the Berlin public prosecutor's office, Ballout traveled to Lebanon in May 2025 intending to join the Islamic State group in Syria, was arrested there, and sentenced to three months for incitement. After returning to Germany in November 2025, he was detained and in May 2026 received a suspended sentence of one year and 10 months for "preparing a serious act of violence endangering the state," with supervision by a parole officer. He had prior convictions for assault and robbery.

German Chancellor Friedrich Merz visited the attack scene and later spoke at a church service, pledging to "defend the freedom and openness of our lives" and stating that "these extremists have no place in our society." Berlin Mayor Kai Wegner called the attack "an attack on our free and cosmopolitan society" and praised police for locating the suspect within 24 hours. European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen expressed sympathy, saying the Pride event stands for "freedom, dignity, and equality."

Dobrindt said Germany's threat level would be raised following the attack, and other states were advised to review security measures for major events. The Federal Public Prosecutor General will take over the investigation. Hundreds of mourners gathered at the Brandenburg Gate for an impromptu vigil, where speakers condemned extremism but also criticized recent budget cuts for queer organizations. Saturday's attack is the latest in a string of deadly vehicle-ramming incidents in Germany and worldwide in recent years, including attacks in Leipzig, Mannheim, Munich, and the 2016 Berlin Christmas market attack.
