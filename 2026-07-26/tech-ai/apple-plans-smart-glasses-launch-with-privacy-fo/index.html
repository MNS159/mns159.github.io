Apple plans smart glasses reveal at WWDC 2027, launch by year-end with privacy focus

Tech / AI desk

Apple plans to unveil its first smart glasses at the Worldwide Developers Conference in June 2027, with a retail launch by the end of that year, according to Bloomberg’s Mark Gurman. The company has pushed back an earlier target of early 2027 to refine both the product and its messaging around privacy.

The glasses are expected to rely on on-device processing and will omit features such as facial recognition or an always-on recording mode similar to Meta’s “super sensing.” Apple also will likely avoid using customer recordings to train its AI models, and it is not expected to adopt Meta’s practice of having contractors review user footage. Gurman reports that Apple may eventually release a version without a camera, or with a camera restricted to sensing only—similar to a rumored approach for Apple’s AI-enabled AirPods.

The privacy emphasis comes as Meta’s smart glasses have drawn criticism for enabling non-consensual video recording, with some calling them “pervert glasses.” Apple has spent more than a decade making privacy a defining product message, and entering the same category as Meta risks undermining that reputation regardless of how its approach differs. The company is reportedly working on additional hardware and software privacy features to address these concerns.

The delay to late 2027 gives Apple time to finalize its privacy features and the accompanying marketing narrative. The decision to steer clear of controversial capabilities—such as always-on recording or using customer data for AI training—is intended to differentiate Apple’s offering in a market that has become a flashpoint for consumer privacy debates.

Sources: The Verge·TechCrunch
