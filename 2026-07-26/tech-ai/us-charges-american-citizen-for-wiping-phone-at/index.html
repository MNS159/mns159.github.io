US charges American citizen for wiping phone at border

Tech / AI desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

The US government is prosecuting American citizen Sam Tunick for allegedly wiping his phone when federal agents tried to seize it at Atlanta’s Hartsfield-Jackson airport on January 24, 2025. Tunick is charged under a little-known and rarely used statute that makes it illegal to destroy or damage property to prevent authorities from seizing it. According to prosecutors, Tunick gave agents a “duress password” that triggered the phone’s factory reset instead of the actual passcode, using a feature of the privacy-focused operating system GrapheneOS.

Tunick’s attorneys argue that the detention and seizure were unlawful, and that agents questioned him about child exploitation images as “a pretext for a fishing expedition into Mr Tunick’s connections” to the Stop Cop City movement in Atlanta. The defense says agents refused to let Tunick speak to a lawyer, did not provide a warrant, and did not inform him of his legal rights. The government counters that a warrant was not required because Tunick had not yet been granted permission to enter the United States.

The case comes amid heightened scrutiny of travelers at US borders under the Trump administration, where even citizens can face hours-long detention and examination of social media accounts. Marlon Kautz, a member of the Atlanta Solidarity Fund, told the Guardian earlier this week that “we all have a right to secure our private data against unconstitutional searches. And we should — especially in a time of rising authoritarianism.” Most privacy advocates agree that the only way to protect data at the border is to delete it.

Tunick’s attorneys have filed a motion to toss all evidence, arguing that the detention and search were unlawful. The outcome of the case could set a precedent for the limits of border search authority and the rights of US citizens to protect their digital data from government seizure.
