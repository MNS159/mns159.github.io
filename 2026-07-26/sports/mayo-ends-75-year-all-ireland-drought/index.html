Mayo ends 75-year All-Ireland drought with victory over Kerry

Sports desk

Mayo won the All-Ireland Senior Football Championship for the first time in 75 years on Sunday, defeating Kerry by three points at Croke Park in Dublin. The victory, witnessed by over 80,000 spectators, ended the so-called 'Mayo Curse' that had hung over the county since 1951 and gave Mayo its fourth All-Ireland title.

Kerry took an early lead when David Clifford scored a goal, but Mayo fought back to trail by just one point at half-time. Four minutes into the second half, Darragh Beirne finished a flowing move to put Mayo ahead, a lead they held to the final whistle. Mayo wore a white jersey for the first time in an All-Ireland final since 1951 due to a clash with Kerry's green home kit.

The origins of the 'curse' date to 1951, when Mayo last won the All-Ireland. According to legend, after the victory celebration, the team passed a funeral in Foxford and a local priest reportedly cursed them, saying they would never win again while any of those players were alive. The story was never confirmed, but Mayo lost 11 All-Ireland finals in the intervening decades. The last surviving member of the 1951 squad, Dr Mick Loftus, died in 2023 at age 93; Paddy Prendergast, the last player to feature in that final, died in 2021.

Emotion was high at the final whistle. Former Mayo defender Padraig O'Hora, working as a BBC pundit, said with tears in his eyes: 'Thousands upon thousands of people have dreamed of this day, I can't believe it's here.' Captain Jack Coyne, after delivering a speech, said: 'To every man, women, boy and girl in Mayo, wherever you are, dream big things. Anything is possible and we've shown it today.' Manager Andy Moran, in his first year in charge, went to his family in the stands.

Among the players was 18-year-old Kobe McDonald, who had 'one chance' written on his wrist. His father Ciaran played in previous All-Ireland final defeats. McDonald, who just finished his leaving cert exams, will move to Australia to join St Kilda in the Australian Football League. 'It's surreal, we don't know how to feel,' he said on the GAA Social. 'To look around and see the people, they have given us so much over the years and to give them something proper to shout about is special.'

Mayo will return to Castlebar in their home county for celebrations. The victory ends 75 years of hurt and heartbreak, and the county is expected to greet the team with a sea of celebration on Ireland's west coast.
