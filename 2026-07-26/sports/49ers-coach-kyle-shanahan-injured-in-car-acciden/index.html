49ers coach Kyle Shanahan injured in car accident, will begin training camp in limited role

Sports desk

San Francisco 49ers head coach Kyle Shanahan was involved in a car accident last week and suffered non-life-threatening injuries, the team announced Saturday. Shanahan, 46, will participate in team activities on a limited basis at the start of training camp while he recovers. Assistant head coach Chris Foerster and the team's coordinators will handle the bulk of head coach duties during this period.

The accident occurred July 14 when Shanahan collided with an SUV near his Northern California home, according to ESPN. Shanahan broke his nose, three ribs and his hand, suffered a severe concussion and needed more than 40 stitches to his face. He was taken by paramedics to a local hospital and released later that day. The other driver was not injured, according to Palo Alto Lt. Nicholas Martinez, who also told ESPN that drugs and alcohol were not involved and both drivers cooperated with law enforcement.

Shanahan has been recovering at home since the crash and informed the coaching staff of the incident on Tuesday. He has not yet been cleared by doctors for full coaching duties and will not be until his concussion symptoms subside. In a press conference Saturday, 49ers general manager John Lynch told reporters he does not expect Shanahan to miss Week 1 of the regular season.

The 49ers released a statement thanking all first responders for their efforts and care. Los Angeles Rams coach Sean McVay, a fellow NFC West coach and former colleague of Shanahan, said he has spoken with Shanahan since the accident. "I have talked to him. My thoughts and prayers are with him and his family, and I know he's got an incredible perspective on it all," McVay said. "Most importantly, thank God he and the other person involved are okay."

Shanahan is entering his 10th season as the 49ers' head coach. Since being hired in 2017, he has compiled an 82-67 regular-season record and a 9-5 playoff mark, leading San Francisco to two Super Bowl appearances. The 49ers are coming off a 12-5 regular-season campaign in 2025 that ended with a divisional-round loss to the eventual Super Bowl champion Seattle Seahawks.

Sources: CBS Sports
