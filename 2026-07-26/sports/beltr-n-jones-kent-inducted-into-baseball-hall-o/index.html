Beltrán, Jones, Kent inducted into Baseball Hall of Fame

Sports desk

The National Baseball Hall of Fame and Museum welcomed three new members on Sunday, July 26, 2026, in Cooperstown, New York. The induction ceremony honored outfielders Carlos Beltrán and Andruw Jones, who were voted in by the Baseball Writers Association of America, and second baseman Jeff Kent, who was selected by the Hall of Fame's Contemporary Baseball Era Committee. The three-person class is the smallest since 2022, when Fred McGriff and Scott Rolen were inducted.

Beltrán, a nine-time All-Star and 1999 American League Rookie of the Year, entered the Hall in his fourth year on the ballot, receiving 84.2% of the vote. He retired with 2,725 hits, 435 home runs, and 312 stolen bases, joining Barry Bonds, Andre Dawson, Willie Mays, and Alex Rodriguez as the only players with 400 home runs and 300 stolen bases. Beltrán played for seven teams from 1998-2017, most notably with the Mets, whose cap he will wear on his plaque. He also won a World Series ring in 2017 with the Astros, though that legacy was tainted by the sign-stealing scandal. In his induction speech, Beltrán addressed Mets fans: 'Thank you for believing in me. You challenged me. You let me know when I needed to be better, and that was a lot of times. I got to hear those boos, but I also appreciate your support along the way.'

Andruw Jones, widely regarded as the best defensive center fielder of his generation, was inducted in his ninth year on the ballot, with his support rising from 7.3% in his first year to 78.4% this year. He won 10 straight Gold Gloves from 1998-2007 and slugged at least 26 home runs every year from 1998-2007, including a 51-homer season in 2005. Jones debuted with a historic playoff performance: at 19 years and 180 days, he became the youngest player ever to hit two home runs in a World Series game, doing so in Game 1 in 1996. He played for five teams from 1996-2012 and retired with 1,933 hits and 434 home runs. During his speech, Jones said, 'I never got to see Willie Mays play, so Ken Griffey was my guy. I wanted to be like him. For me and Carlos to join Griffey, Willie, and these other great center fielders in the Hall of Fame is hard to put into words for me.' He also acknowledged Braves legend Chipper Jones: 'Chipper pushed for my induction for many years. The Jones boys are in Cooperstown now.'

Jeff Kent, the 2000 National League MVP with the Giants, was voted in by the Contemporary Baseball Era Committee in December 2025, receiving 14 of 16 votes (12 required). He had exhausted his 10 years on the BBWAA ballot, never receiving more than 46.5% of the vote. Kent is one of the best-hitting second basemen in history, holding the record for most home runs by a primary second baseman with 377, and his 1,158 RBI rank third at the position. He played for six teams from 1992-2002, was a five-time All-Star, and won four Silver Sluggers. In his speech, Kent reflected on a bet with his father: 'If I were to earn a college scholarship, that he'd buy me a pickup truck... I quit racing and focused on school and baseball, and won that baseball scholarship. I got that truck. That truck is still with me.' He added, 'This day isn't about me. It's about baseball. It's a celebration of baseball and I just happen to be a part of it.'

The induction ceremony, held on the lawn outside the Clark Sports Center in Cooperstown, drew thousands of baseball fans. The Hall of Fame class of 2026 marks the culmination of years of voting for each inductee: Beltrán's support climbed from 46.5% in his first year to 84.2%, Jones rose from 7.3% to 78.4%, and Kent finally gained entry after falling short with the writers. All three are now enshrined in Cooperstown, joining the sport's all-time greats.
