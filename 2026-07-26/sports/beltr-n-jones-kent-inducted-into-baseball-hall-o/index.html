Beltrán, Jones, Kent inducted into Baseball Hall of Fame

Sports desk

The National Baseball Hall of Fame and Museum officially welcomed three new members on Sunday, July 26, 2026, in Cooperstown, New York. The induction ceremony honored the 2026 Hall of Fame class, led by 10-time Gold Glove outfielder Andruw Jones. The class is the smallest since 2022, when just two players — Fred McGriff and Scott Rolen — were inducted.

Outfielder Carlos Beltrán was voted in by the Baseball Writers Association of America in his fourth year on the ballot, receiving 84.2% of the vote after rising from 46.5% in his first year. A nine-time All-Star and the 1999 American League Rookie of the Year, Beltrán played for seven teams from 1998-2017 and will wear a Mets cap on his plaque. He retired with 2,725 hits, 435 home runs, and 312 stolen bases, making him one of only five players with 400 home runs and 300 stolen bases, joining Barry Bonds, Andre Dawson, Willie Mays, and Alex Rodriguez. Beltrán was a standout postseason performer, hitting .307/.412/.609 in 65 games, including eight homers in 12 playoff games with the 2004 Astros. He won his only World Series ring with the 2017 Astros, though that championship was tainted by the team's sign-stealing scandal. During his speech, Beltrán thanked Mets fans, saying, "You challenged me. You let me know when I needed to be better, and that was a lot of times. I got to hear those boos, but I also appreciate your support along the way." He also addressed his children: "Everywhere you go, I have to let you know one thing: You're always going to have a dad cheering for you guys."

Andruw Jones was elected in his ninth year on the BBWAA ballot, his support gradually rising from 7.3% in his first year to 78.4% this year. At 19 years and 180 days, Jones became the youngest player ever to hit two home runs in a World Series game when he did so in Game 1 in 1996, surpassing Mickey Mantle. From 1997 to 2007, Jones won 10 consecutive Gold Gloves from 1998-2007, widely regarded as one of the best defensive center fielders of his generation. He also slugged at least 26 home runs every year from 1998-2007, including a 51-homer season in 2005. Jones played for five teams from 1996 to 2012, finishing with 1,933 hits and 434 home runs. In his speech, Jones said, "I never got to see Willie Mays play, so Ken Griffey was my guy. I wanted to be like him. For me and Carlos to join Griffey, Willie, and these other great center fielders in the Hall of Fame is hard to put into words for me." He also thanked Chipper Jones: "The Jones boys are in Cooperstown now. Thank you."

Second baseman Jeff Kent was elected by the Hall of Fame's Contemporary Baseball Era Committee, receiving 14 of 16 votes (12 required) in December 2025. He had exhausted his 10 years on the BBWAA ballot from 2014-23, never receiving more than 46.5% of the vote. The 2000 National League MVP with the Giants, Kent is one of the best-hitting second basemen ever, with 377 home runs — the most by a primary second baseman — and 1,158 RBI, third most at the position. He hit double-digit home runs every year of his career, including his partial rookie season in 1992. Kent played for six teams from 1992 to 2002, earning five All-Star selections, four Silver Sluggers, and three other top-10 MVP finishes. During his speech, Kent shared a story about his father's bet that led him to earn a college scholarship and a pickup truck, which he still owns: "That truck is still with me. It sits in my garage as a simple reminder that some of the simplest things can change the entire direction of your life." He added, "This day isn't about me. It's about baseball. It's a celebration of baseball and I just happen to be a part of it."

The induction ceremony took place on the lawn outside the Clark Sports Center in Cooperstown, with thousands of baseball fans in attendance. The three-person class is the smallest since 2022, and each inductee's journey to Cooperstown reflected different paths: Beltrán and Jones via the BBWAA, and Kent via the Contemporary Baseball Era Committee after falling short with the writers.
