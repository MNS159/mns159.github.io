Luke Littler defends World Matchplay title with record-shattering performance; Beau Greaves wins Women's Matchplay in last-leg thriller

Sports desk

Luke Littler successfully defended his World Matchplay title in sensational fashion on Sunday, defeating seventh seed Gerwyn Price 18-9 in the final at Blackpool's Winter Gardens. The 19-year-old became just the fourth player to win back-to-back Matchplay titles and shattered tournament records set by Phil Taylor.

Littler finished the week with an overall tournament average of 111.04, eclipsing Taylor's previous tournament record of 106.31 set in 2010. In the final itself, he averaged 111.53, breaking Taylor's single-match final record of 111.23 from 2013. Littler also hit 12 180s in the final, setting a new tournament record of 66 maximums, surpassing the total he achieved last year. The victory extended his winning streak in matches of 20 or more legs to 29 consecutive wins.

Price averaged 104.97 in defeat and climbed to fourth in the PDC Order of Merit, reclaiming the Welsh number-one spot from Jonny Clayton. Littler, the world number one, now leads the two-year Order of Merit with £3,143,500, a gap of £2.13 million over second-placed Luke Humphries. The victory was worth £225,000 and marked Littler's fourth ranking title of the calendar year.

Littler dominated from the outset, winning the first mini-session 4-1 with the help of a 167 checkout in the fourth leg. He built a 8-2 lead while averaging 114.74, threatening Taylor's record of 114.99 in any Matchplay game. He took the next two sessions 3-2 each, moving to within two legs with a 148 checkout—his sixth ton-plus finish in the final—before closing out the match. "It's been a crazy week, one of the best weeks of my life with the darts I've played," Littler told Sky Sports. "It's been tough. The stats may look like it's easy, but when you're on this stage with how hot it is, it's not easy." Price added: "Luke is one in a million, I threw everything at him. He's fantastic for the sport. I wish he wasn't as good as he is—I would have won a couple more tournaments."

Littler said he now intends to target a calendar-year clean sweep of PDC ranking titles, a feat never achieved. That would require him to play in enough Pro Tour events to qualify for the Players Championship Finals in November. He has skipped 24 of the first 32 Pro Tour events and has already withdrawn from the two in Hildesheim this week, leaving eight remaining. "I don't think my dad's too happy because we were going to go to Spain and I think we've got to go to Leicester now and play some Pro Tours, but it is what it is," Littler said on Sky Sports. "If I don't qualify then I'm going to get grilled and if I do, I'll get grilled anyway. Who knows what's going to happen?"

In the women's event, top seed Beau Greaves won the Women's World Matchplay for the third time in four years, defeating Fallon Sherrock 6-5 in a last-leg decider. Greaves, 22, led 5-3 but Sherrock forced a deciding leg. Sherrock hit a 180 in the final leg, but Greaves responded with a maximum of her own, set up 145, and took out 56 to seal the win. "I'm not one to celebrate but I just felt the relief after winning," Greaves told Sky Sports. "To hit that last dart was just an outpouring of relief. I am just so happy to win." Sherrock, who dropped only one leg en route to the final, defeated defending champion Lisa Ashton 5-1 in the semifinals but fell short in the final for the third consecutive year. Greaves had earlier survived a deciding-leg semifinal against Gemma Hayter, checking out 104 to win. The victory adds to a landmark year for Greaves, who in April became the first woman to win a PDC ranking title, beating Michael Smith, and in February became the first woman to hit a nine-dart finish on the PDC ProTour.
