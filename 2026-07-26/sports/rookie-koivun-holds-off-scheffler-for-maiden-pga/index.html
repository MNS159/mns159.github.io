Rookie Koivun holds off Scheffler for maiden PGA win

Sports desk

American rookie Jackson Koivun held his nerve to beat world number one Scottie Scheffler by three strokes and claim his first PGA Tour victory at the 3M Open on Sunday. Playing in just his third tournament since turning professional, the 21-year-old former world amateur number one closed with a bogey-free five-under 66 to finish at 25-under 259, a tournament record. Scheffler fired an eight-under 63 but settled for second at 22 under.

Koivun, who won the Jack Nicklaus Award as collegiate player of the year twice while leading Auburn to two national championships, surged into a three-shot lead Saturday with a round of 10-under 61. He stretched his advantage to five in the final round at TPC Twin Cities in Minnesota before Scheffler applied pressure with three straight birdies to start the back nine and two more in the final three holes. Koivun responded with a birdie at the 16th and sealed the win with a 14-foot par save on 18.

"I had some nerves coming down 18," Koivun said. "All throughout the crowd I kept hearing 'Scottie's coming, Scottie's coming'. I just tried to tune that out and keep playing the golf I knew I could and was able to find some good putts coming down the stretch." He added that the 15-foot par putt he made at the ninth hole was "the biggest putt I made all week."

Koivun, ranked 206th in the world entering the week, led the field in strokes gained/approach the green (+9.363) and total (+17.031). He hit 13 of 14 fairways and 15 greens in regulation in the final round and was par or better over his last 42 holes. The victory earns him a two-year PGA Tour exemption, $1.584 million, and moves him to 70th in the FedEx Cup standings, which would qualify him for the playoffs.

Scheffler, who opened the season with a win at The American Express but has now finished runner-up five times in 2026, said: "Depending on how Jackson played, I knew I was going to need a really low number. I wanted to get something really low on the front nine and kind of put myself on the leaderboard. I didn't quite have that." The world number one has a tour-leading 11 top-10 finishes this season.

American trio Denny McCarthy, Brian Harman and Chandler Phillips finished five strokes behind Koivun in a tie for third with Japan's Hideki Matsuyama at 20 under. Michael Kim, who shot a 12-under 59 on Friday, closed with rounds of 70 and 69 to tie for 10th. Koivun's victory draws comparisons to Matthew Wolff's win in the inaugural 3M Open in 2019; Wolff also won in his third pro start after winning the Nicklaus Award.
