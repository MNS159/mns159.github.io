Littler and Greaves win World Matchplay titles

Sports desk

Luke Littler defended his World Matchplay title in sensational style, smashing Phil Taylor's tournament and final average records in the process. The 19-year-old world number one beat seventh seed Gerwyn Price 18-9 in Blackpool to become just the fourth person to win back-to-back Matchplay titles. Littler finished the week with an average of 111.04, beating Taylor's record of 106.31 from 2010, and averaged 111.53 in the final, bettering Taylor's 111.23 against Adrian Lewis in 2013. He claimed £225,000 in prize money and now leads the PDC's two-year order of merit with £3,143,500, £2.13m ahead of second-placed Luke Humphries.

Littler went into the final as the overwhelming favourite and a stunning 167 finish in the fourth leg secured an early break to help him take the first mini-session 4-1. He led 8-2 with an average of 114.74, approaching Taylor's record of 114.99 in any Matchplay game. His level dropped slightly in the next two sessions but he still won both 3-2, moving within two legs with a 148 checkout — his sixth ton-plus finish in the final — before a break and hold sealed his fourth ranking title of the calendar year. Littler also hit 12 180s in the final, setting a new tournament record of 66, surpassing the total he set last year. The win extended his streak of successive victories in matches of 20 or more legs to 29; his last defeat in such a match was the 2024 World Championship final against Luke Humphries.

"It's been a crazy week, one of the best weeks of my life with the darts I've played," Littler told Sky Sports. "It's been tough, the stats may look like it's easy but when you're on this stage with how hit it is, it's not easy." Price, who averaged 104.97 in the final, climbed to fourth in the order of merit and reclaimed the Welsh number one spot from Jonny Clayton. "Luke is one in a million, I threw everything at him," Price said. "He's fantastic for the sport. I wish he wasn't as good as he is — I would have won a couple more tournaments. I hope there's no-one else like him coming through because he's ridiculous!"

Littler reiterated his intention to attempt a clean sweep of PDC ranking titles in the calendar year, a feat never achieved. The Players Championship Finals in November are a sticking point, requiring him to play in — and likely win — some Pro Tour events to qualify. He has skipped the first 24 Pro Tour events and pulled out of the two in Hildesheim this week, leaving eight left. A win is worth £15,000 and a runner-up spot £10,000; the final qualifying spot currently has £22,750 in prize money. "I don't think my dad's too happy because we were going to go to Spain and I think we've got to go to Leicester now and play some Pro Tours," Littler added. "I've been checking the Pro Tour rankings and whoever is in 64th just keeps going up and up. If I don't qualify then I'm going to get grilled and if I do, I'll get grilled anyway." Former BDO world champion Mark Webster said he "half expects" the teenager to achieve the clean sweep, adding: "Who is going to stop Luke Littler?"

Beau Greaves won the Women's World Matchplay for the third time, coming through a thrilling last-leg decider to beat Fallon Sherrock 6-5 at Blackpool's Winter Gardens. The top seed was not at her best but, having been pushed all the way by Sherrock, held her nerve. Greaves let a 5-3 lead slip, and Sherrock hit a 180 in the deciding leg, but the 22-year-old responded with a maximum of her own, followed by a 145 set-up, before taking out 56 to seal the win. "I'm not one to celebrate but I just felt the relief after winning," Greaves told Sky Sports. "To hit that last dart was just an outpouring of relief. I am just so happy to win. I was nervous. That kind of pressure is a privilege."

Greaves came in as the clear favourite and breezed through her quarter-final, but found herself in trouble in the semis against Gemma Hayter, who took her to a deciding leg before Greaves produced a 104 checkout to reach the final. By contrast, Sherrock dropped only one leg en route to the trophy match and took out defending champion Lisa Ashton 5-1 in the semi-final. However, for the third year running she came up just short in the final; a first Women's Matchplay title since the inaugural tournament in 2022 continues to elude her. For three-time women's world champion Greaves, it is another major triumph in a year that already saw her become the first woman to win a PDC ranking title (beating Michael Smith in April) and the first woman to hit a nine-dart finish on the PDC ProTour (against Mensur Suljovic in February). "I knew before that playing women is just a little bit different for me now — I just try and concentrate on the men's game — but when I come back and play at this tournament it is hard and I knew it would be tough," Greaves added. "After last year's disappointment, I am so chuffed to get the win. The relief is so good for my game going forward."
