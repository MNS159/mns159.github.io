Formula 1

Norris wins Hungarian Grand Prix as Ferrari flounders

Sports desk

Lando Norris won the Hungarian Grand Prix on Sunday, securing his first victory of the Formula 1 season in a drive he called 'one of my best performances.' The McLaren driver started from pole position but lost the lead to teammate Oscar Piastri on the first lap after sliding wide at Turn Two. Norris stayed calm, kept pace with Piastri in dirty air, and regained the lead after McLaren pitted Piastri to cover Lewis Hamilton's Ferrari, giving Norris clean air and a blistering pace. Piastri later retired with a gearbox failure, denying McLaren a one-two finish. Norris crossed the line ahead of Red Bull's Max Verstappen and championship leader Kimi Antonelli of Mercedes.

Norris described the performance as a product of hard work with his engineers to understand a car he called 'not an easy car to understand and figure out how to drive on the limit consistently.' He said, 'The pace advantage I had was huge today. But I don't know why. The cars are identical, and I've just been feeling very strong lately.' McLaren team principal Andrea Stella noted that Norris is 'much more confident' and 'more in control of his emotions,' adding, 'We've seen a great trajectory of development for Lando.' The win moves Norris to 98 points behind Antonelli in the drivers' standings, but Norris cautioned against expectations of a sustained title charge, saying, 'It doesn't mean if I'm quick here, we're going to be quick everywhere.'

Piastri, who appeared to direct anger at McLaren over team radio during the race, was actually furious with Williams driver Carlos Sainz after a collision while Piastri was lapping him. 'The timing of my stop was fine,' Piastri said. 'Without getting crashed into, I would have been ahead of Lando still.' He added, 'I don't really care if he didn't see me. The fact that he didn't and no-one told him, or there was a lack of complete awareness, is unacceptable.' Sainz said he had 'no idea I was trying to get lapped' and that it was 'impossible to avoid it.' Piastri's gearbox failure ended his race shortly after the incident.

Ferrari, pre-race favorites after topping Friday practice, endured a difficult Sunday. Charles Leclerc started alongside Norris on the front row but lost positions to Piastri, Verstappen, and Hamilton. Hamilton, who dropped from second to fifth on the grid with a penalty for impeding Piastri in qualifying, overtook Verstappen in the first pit stops only to be overtaken again when Verstappen made a 'brilliant move at Turn One that caught Hamilton napping.' A five-second penalty for pit-lane speeding left Hamilton behind Leclerc at the finish, the Ferraris fourth and fifth. Team boss Frederic Vasseur said, 'We made too many mistakes today, starting with the penalty and the fact that Max overtook us, that each time that we were doing a pit stop, we were losing a position for 0.2 or 0.3 seconds.' Hamilton, who remains second in the championship 50 points behind Antonelli, said, 'You can't make these kind of mistakes if you want to challenge for a title.'

Mercedes' George Russell had another difficult race, dropping to the back at the start due to an engine problem the team took blame for. He recovered to seventh but now trails Antonelli by 59 points. 'I'm sort of past the point of disappointment now,' Russell said. 'It's just unbelievable, the list of things that's happened this season.' Antonelli, who leads the championship, said, 'I did not expect to find myself in this position. But the car has been extremely good, the team has done an incredible job and, on my side, I try to maximise every opportunity I have.' The Hungarian Grand Prix marked the end of the first half of the season; the next race is the Dutch Grand Prix after F1's four-week summer break.
