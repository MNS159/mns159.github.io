Pogacar wins fifth Tour de France title amid Paris finish drama

Sports desk

Slovenian Tadej Pogacar won the Tour de France for a record-equalling fifth time on Sunday, crossing the line on Paris' Champs-Élysées in the peloton to join Jacques Anquetil, Eddy Merckx, Bernard Hinault and Miguel Indurain as the endurance event's most successful male riders. The 27-year-old UAE Team Emirates-XRG rider described his achievement as "something you wouldn't even maybe write in a story book."

The final stage was won by the Netherlands' Mathieu van der Poel of Alpecin-Premier Tech after a frantic charge to the line on the famous cobbles, in which he pipped team-mate Jasper Philipsen of Belgium by inches. Van der Poel, a classics legend with multiple victories in Paris-Roubaix and the Tour of Flanders, added this prestigious victory to his collection, saying: "We have a saying in Dutch that it has to come for your toes. I don't know where this came from, but not from my toes." The stage was reduced from 133km to 89km after the redirection of emergency services to help tackle wildfires in south-west France.

Pogacar's victory marks his third Tour win in succession and includes a record-breaking ascent of the iconic Alpe d'Huez on stage 19, when he sealed another dominant victory. It was the last of five stage wins in a race in which Pogacar used his explosive launch and unmatchable metronomic pace to see off his rivals. He now has 127 wins in an eight-year elite professional career and has won almost every major race in the sport, including the Tour of Flanders, Giro d'Italia, Milan-San Remo, Road World Championships, Giro di Lombardia and Liège-Bastogne-Liège.

Pogacar's only real rival in this year's race was the much-improved Belgian Remco Evenepoel of Red Bull-Bora-Hansgrohe, who finished six minutes 26 seconds behind overall. Third place went to Pogacar's Mexican team-mate Isaac del Toro, who won stage two thanks largely to Pogacar's babysitting pace. The one rider who has broken Pogacar's stranglehold of cycling's biggest race in the past was Denmark's Jonas Vingegaard, who won in 2022 and 2023, but this year the 29-year-old Visma-Lease a Bike rider abandoned after breaking his collarbone on a right-hand bend on stage 15. The mistake was very unlike Vingegaard's near-perfect technique, and attention soon turned to scrutiny of the International Testing Agency, which had awoken Vingegaard at 2am to carry out an anti-doping test. Other riders had the same treatment, including Pogacar at 5am, but the ITA said there were "exceptional circumstances" to take the night tests, which are rare and require additional permission. It is not known when the test results will be published.

Pogacar was booed by some fans during the Tour, as his performances are often so far ahead of any of his peers that some feel frustrated the sport is losing its competitive edge. "Victory is really special," said Pogacar. "Thank you to all the fans and my team. But the biggest thanks goes to all my family who support me all my life. My parents - without them I would not be standing here five times already. Seven Tours and seven times on the podium, and five wins, is something you wouldn't even maybe write in a story book, and it's unbelievable for me. It's a magnificent podium with some incredible champions to fight with. It's a shame Jonas [Vingegaard] crashed out this time and many others. I hope they have a quick recovery and next year we fight again."

In the year when Britain's four-time Tour winner Chris Froome retired, his former team - now known as Netcompany Ineos Cycling - failed to record a stage victory in 21 attempts. Ex-Ineos rider Tom Pidcock of Pinarello-Q36.5 did not manage to win a stage, but he and his team will be satisfied with his consistent battling riding, which earned him ninth in the general classification. Fellow Briton Adam Yates, 33, showed great tenacity after falling ill on stage 16 and spending two days riding at the back of the field before helping pace Pogacar up a climb for victory on stage 19. The green points jersey was awarded to Denmark's Mads Pedersen of Lidl-Trek, and the king of the mountains' polka dot jersey went to Ecuador's Richard Carapaz of EF Education-Easypost.
