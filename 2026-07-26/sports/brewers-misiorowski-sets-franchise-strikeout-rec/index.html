Brewers' Misiorowski sets franchise strikeout record

Sports desk

Milwaukee Brewers right-hander Jacob Misiorowski set a franchise record for strikeouts in a game Sunday, becoming the first pitcher since 1920 to record 11 strikeouts and allow no hits through four innings. Misiorowski finished with 12 strikeouts and allowed just one run on a solo home run by Cole Carrigg in the fifth inning, after not allowing a hit through the first four innings.

Misiorowski was removed after throwing 83 pitches as the Brewers manage his workload for the stretch run. He has thrown 120 innings this season, approaching his career-high of 141⅔ innings set last year, with August, September and a potential postseason run still ahead.

“It's a big discussion. It's a big topic,” manager Pat Murphy said about Misiorowski's workload prior to his last start, via MLB.com. “There's been all sorts of concern, planning, discussion, sidebar discussions about biomechanics, strength testing, all of that. Thus, the reason for the layoff (after the All-Star Game). Now, how do we ramp it back up?”

Sunday's outing lowered Misiorowski's ERA to 1.58 through 20 starts, the only qualified pitcher with a sub-2.00 ERA. He leads Major League Baseball with 185 strikeouts, 18 more than any other pitcher, and his 40.6% strikeout rate (percentage of batters faced) is also tops in baseball, well ahead of second-place Dylan Cease at 36.5%.

Sources: CBS Sports
