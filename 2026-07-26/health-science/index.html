Food Safety

Cyclosporiasis outbreak sickens thousands; federal cuts and delayed food safety rule cited

Health & Science desk

A cyclosporiasis outbreak has sickened at least 4,100 Americans across multiple states, according to NPR, making it the largest recorded outbreak of the parasitic illness in U.S. history. The outbreak has been linked to iceberg lettuce from Taylor Farms, but investigators in states including New York and North Carolina are also examining other potential sources such as parsley, cilantro, and produce items previously tied to cyclospora infections, including snow peas, sugar snap peas, and raspberries.

FDA Grants Full Approval to Selpercatinib for RET Fusion–Positive Advanced Solid Tumors

Health & Science desk

The U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) has granted full approval to selpercatinib, marketed as Retevmo, for the treatment of patients aged 2 years and older with advanced solid tumors that harbor a RET gene fusion. The decision, announced July 14, converts a previously accelerated tumor-agnostic indication into a full regulatory confirmation, based on clinical data showing a 47% objective response rate across more than a dozen cancer types and a median response duration exceeding two years.

THC-CBD Treatment Dramatically Reduces Agitation in Dementia Trial

Health & Science desk

A medically formulated combination of THC and CBD significantly reduced agitation in people with late-stage dementia, according to results from a first-of-its-kind U.S. clinical trial presented July 14, 2026, at the Alzheimer’s Association International Conference in London. The LiBBY trial (Life’s end Benefits of cannaBidiol and tetrahYdrocannabinol) included 120 hospice-eligible participants with Alzheimer’s disease or another form of dementia who were experiencing agitation. After 12 weeks, nearly 90% of those who received the treatment showed overall improvement.
