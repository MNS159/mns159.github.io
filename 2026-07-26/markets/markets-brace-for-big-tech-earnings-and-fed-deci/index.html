Markets Brace for Big Tech Earnings, Fed Decision, and Oil Volatility in Busiest Week of Quarter

Financial & Markets desk

This week marks the busiest stretch of second-quarter earnings season, with the Federal Reserve's rate decision, four of the “Magnificent Seven” tech giants reporting results, and oil prices remaining volatile after briefly topping $100 a barrel. The S&P 500 closed Friday up 0.1% for a weekly loss of 0.6%, the Dow gained 0.6% on the day but fell 0.4% for the week, and the Nasdaq dropped 0.6% on Friday, losing 2.1% on the week. Brent crude retreated 4% to around $96.73 a barrel after crossing the $100 threshold for the first time since May.

The earnings reports from Amazon, Apple, Meta Platforms, and Microsoft will be scrutinized for updates on their artificial-intelligence spending plans. Last week, Alphabet handily beat expectations but its stock sank after the company raised its projected capital expenditures on AI infrastructure. The tech giant already spent more than $78 billion in the first half of the year and expects total capital expenditures between $195 billion and $205 billion in 2026, mostly for data centers and networking. "If you're looking for derivative stock calls, the AI trade is still on," said Mark Mahaney, Evercore ISI head of internet research. "These massive companies with massive amounts of cash and lots of potential leverage are willing to spend a lot on infrastructure, more than anybody thought possible."

The Federal Open Market Committee meets Tuesday and Wednesday and is expected to hold the fed funds rate steady, though some Fed watchers say rate hikes may be coming later in the year. Fed officials have suggested that spending on AI infrastructure could be a more persistent cause of inflation than energy shocks from the war with Iran, and the committee is likely to discuss AI and other inflation factors. The central bank will receive fresh data on Thursday with the Personal Consumption Expenditures price index and second-quarter gross domestic product.

The divergence in the AI trade was evident last week: while Alphabet and other mega-cap tech stocks fell sharply, shares of memory makers Micron Technology, SK Hynix, and Sandisk jumped as memory chip prices rise due to supply shortages. Intel beat expectations, benefiting from demand for CPUs as the tech industry shifts toward AI agents. Interactive Brokers chief strategist Steve Sosnick characterized the dynamic as "makers versus the takers" of hyperscaler spending. The PHLX Semiconductor Index has pulled back from its June record but remains up 66% year to date and 111% over the past year, far outpacing the S&P 500's 8% and 16% gains, respectively. Still, some strategists caution against blanket buys in the sector. "There are two types of semis right now," Gil Luria of D.A. Davidson told Yahoo Finance.

Investors this week will also parse results from PayPal, Visa, Starbucks, Seagate Technology, Qualcomm, and Arm Holdings, as well as the Conference Board's Consumer Confidence Index on Tuesday. The central question across markets is whether the massive investments in AI infrastructure will deliver returns, as tech companies increasingly use debt and stock sales to finance data centers and custom chips.
