Berkshire Hathaway Q2 Operating Earnings Rise 16%, Beat Estimates; CEO Abel Begins Deploying Record Cash Hoard

Financial & Markets desk

Berkshire Hathaway reported second-quarter operating earnings of $12.98 billion, a 16% increase from $11.16 billion a year earlier, as strength across its energy, railroad and manufacturing businesses more than offset weaker insurance results. Revenue rose 10% to $101.8 billion. Both figures beat analyst expectations, according to Investor's Business Daily.

Under new CEO Greg Abel, who succeeded Warren Buffett at the start of 2026, Berkshire began deploying its record cash pile. The conglomerate repurchased approximately $4.5 billion of its own shares during the quarter, a sharp acceleration from $235 million in the first quarter, though still below some expectations. The cash hoard declined to $365.5 billion at the end of June from a record $397.4 billion three months earlier, reflecting buybacks, stock purchases and the closing of the acquisition of homebuilder Taylor Morrison on July 24.

Berkshire also reversed a 14-quarter streak of net stock sales, becoming a net buyer of equities with nearly $20 billion in net purchases during the quarter. New disclosures showed that Alphabet is now among Berkshire's five largest equity holdings by market value, alongside American Express, Apple, Bank of America and Coca-Cola. Buffett told CNBC that he initiated the Alphabet investment after consulting with Abel.

Segment results were mixed. Manufacturing, service and retailing earnings jumped 24% to $4.47 billion, led by aerospace demand at Precision Castparts (up 34.2%) and gains at TTI, NetJets and Brooks Sports. Berkshire Hathaway Energy's after-tax operating profit surged 27% to $891 million, helped by better utility and pipeline results and higher federal tax credits. BNSF railroad posted a 6% increase in earnings to $1.56 billion on 6.5% higher freight volume. Insurance underwriting earnings fell 13% to $1.73 billion, and insurance investment income declined 9% to $3.06 billion, partly due to lower short-term interest rates. GEICO experienced higher claims frequency and severity, along with increased commissions and advertising costs; its combined ratio was 91.2%, meaning 91.2 cents of every premium dollar went to losses and expenses.

Abel cautioned at the annual meeting that the insurance environment is 'becoming a more challenging environment' with more 'capital coming into the industry,' and he expects a softening for the remainder of the year. Despite the earnings beat, Berkshire shares are up only 3% year to date, trailing the S&P 500's 13% gain, though the stock has risen 9% over the last three months. The company's insurance float stood at $177.5 billion, up about $1.1 billion from year-end 2025, providing a low-cost source of investment capital.

With Abel now in charge and the cash hoard beginning to shrink, investors are watching to see whether the new CEO will continue Buffett's cautious approach or pursue more aggressive deployment. The quarter's buybacks and net stock purchases, along with the Taylor Morrison acquisition, signal a shift toward putting Berkshire's fortress-like balance sheet to work.
