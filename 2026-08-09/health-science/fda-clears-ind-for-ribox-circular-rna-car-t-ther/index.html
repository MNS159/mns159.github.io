FDA Clears IND for RiboX's In Vivo Circular RNA CAR-T Therapy in ITP

Health & Science desk

The U.S. Food and Drug Administration has cleared an Investigational New Drug application for RXIM002, an in vivo circular RNA-based CAR-T cell therapy developed by RiboX Therapeutics for relapsed or refractory immune thrombocytopenia (ITP). The clearance marks the second IND for RiboX's circular RNA platform, following the earlier approval of RXRG001 for radiation-induced xerostomia, and positions RXIM002 as the first circular RNA therapy to enter human trials in an autoimmune indication.

RXIM002 is not a conventional CAR-T. It delivers a circular RNA encoding an anti-CD19 chimeric antigen receptor via targeted lipid nanoparticles directly into the patient's body, instructing the patient's own T cells to generate CAR-T cells in vivo. This eliminates the weeks-long, facility-intensive ex vivo manufacturing process—including apheresis, viral transduction, and cryopreservation—that constrains access to standard CAR-T therapies. The FDA also accepted an accelerated dose-titration scheme and a subcutaneous formulation for the Phase 1 trial, based on data from a Chinese investigator-initiated study that the agency deemed sufficiently robust to inform the U.S. trial design without requiring a full safety ramp.

The Phase 1 trial, named POPULUS-1, will enroll patients with relapsed or refractory ITP, a condition characterized by pathogenic autoantibodies driven by aberrant B-cell activation that destroy platelets. The trial's primary endpoint will be the proportion of patients achieving a platelet count at or above 50 × 10⁹/L without rescue therapy at 90 days post-infusion—a durable, treatment-free response that distinguishes immune reset from transient suppression. The FDA's existing CAR-T guidance, finalized in January 2024, was written for ex vivo products and assumes leukapheresis, viral vectors, and release testing of a discrete cellular product. For an in vivo RNA-based therapy, those assumptions do not apply, creating gaps in how the product is defined for release testing, which pharmacodynamic biomarkers serve as proxies for in vivo activity, and how long-term safety monitoring should be structured.

RiboX's regulatory strategy involved arriving at the pre-IND meeting with explicit positions on three definitional questions: how to define the 'product' for release testing, what pharmacodynamic biomarkers (such as CAR-T cell expansion in peripheral blood, B-cell depletion kinetics, and autoantibody titers) would be used as exploratory endpoints, and how the long-term follow-up plan would address cytokine release kinetics, on-target off-tumor B-cell depletion, and the risk of reversion of autoimmune pathology without creating immunodeficiency. The pre-IND request explicitly invoked the FDA's CAR-T guidance while flagging where the in vivo mechanism creates gaps in its applicability—a framing that signals understanding of the regulatory architecture and requests platform-specific guidance rather than forcing a circular RNA product into a CAR-T-shaped box.

The global ITP market was valued at approximately USD 2.5 billion in 2024, and existing immunosuppressive regimens are documented as inadequate, providing commercial rationale for pursuing the indication. However, the regulatory path is demanding. The FDA has not yet issued specific guidance on whether the non-integrating nature of circular RNA reduces the long-term follow-up burden recommended for viral vector-based gene therapies, meaning the agency is expected to apply conservative assumptions until evidence supports otherwise. RiboX has now received IND clearance twice across two disease areas, establishing a precedent that the FDA will engage with genuinely novel platform biology when sponsors have done the mechanistic and manufacturing homework before asking for sign-off.

The POPULUS-1 trial will use follow-up data from the Chinese investigator-initiated study—with some patients now beyond six months of observation—to calibrate dose levels and watch for delayed immune-related signals. If early cohorts show a clean safety profile and the platelet count endpoint is met, the in vivo circular RNA CAR-T platform would move from an elegant concept to a replicable clinical result. The operational lesson for other sponsors is clear: the FDA is willing to walk this path, but each sponsor must draw their own blueprint for their platform before the pre-IND meeting.
