Russia-Ukraine War

Overnight Strikes Kill Seven in Ukraine and Russia; Drone Incident in Bulgaria Raises Tensions

International & World desk · anchored on AP

At least seven people were killed in overnight attacks across Ukraine and Russia, officials reported Saturday. In Ukraine, a Russian missile strike near Kyiv killed three people, including a child, and wounded three others, according to local authorities. Meanwhile, Ukrainian drone strikes hit two Russian oil refineries—the Ilsky and Syzran facilities—causing fires, the Ukrainian general staff said. The attacks come as Russia intensifies its aerial campaign, aware that Ukraine's stock of Western-donated interceptor missiles is running low.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky on Saturday renewed his call for more US-made Patriot interceptor missiles, stating that Ukraine does not have and will never have the financial resources Russia possesses to sustain its war effort. "We have what we need, thanks to European aid and support from other countries. But additional funding specifically for Ukrainian production is what is needed," Zelensky said. His plea follows reports that US President Donald Trump has scaled back direct military aid to Ukraine and appeared to renege on a promise to grant Ukraine a license to manufacture the defensive systems domestically.

In a separate development, a drone carrying explosives crashed near an international gas pipeline in Bulgaria on Saturday, after entering the country's airspace from Romania. Bulgarian Prime Minister Rumen Radev said the drone contained a "significant" amount of explosives but caused no casualties or damage. Bulgaria's defense ministry stated there was no reason to believe the incident was intentional. However, Ukrainian foreign ministry spokesman Heorhii Tykhyi denied any Ukrainian involvement, saying, "We can say with certainty that the Ukrainian Armed Forces did not intentionally direct any assets toward Bulgaria."

The war's human toll was underscored by the death of Oleksiy Yukov, a Ukrainian known as the "collector of souls" for his work recovering the remains of soldiers and civilians from battlefields. Yukov, 40, was killed by a landmine on August 5 while exhuming a body in the eastern Donetsk region. He had lost an eye and sustained a leg injury in a previous mine blast in 2022 but continued his mission. Thousands attended his funeral in Kyiv on Saturday. Yukov had described his work in an award-winning documentary, saying, "If the body is not yet buried, the body remains with the soul. And until it is mourned, until its loved ones let it go, it cannot be reborn – the soul wanders. And the war will not end as long as souls wander."

According to the Institute for the Study of War, Russia is reportedly planning a large-scale strike campaign against Kyiv's energy infrastructure ahead of Ukraine's Independence Day on August 24, aiming to exploit Ukraine's shortage of ballistic missile interceptors. The think tank noted that Russia may launch strikes earlier than last winter to disrupt Ukrainian repair efforts. Ukrainian First Deputy Prime Minister Denys Shmyhal stated on August 3 that previous Russian strikes had damaged or destroyed facilities accounting for about 80 percent of Ukraine's power generation, leaving a six-gigawatt shortfall. Repair costs are estimated at $64 to $70 billion.

Tensions also escalated in Germany, where two drones conducted multiple overflights of a Bundeswehr base in Mechernich that houses US Patriot air defense systems. German media reported the drones may have been Fly-380 surveillance models. Separately, US officials told Western media that a drone containing explosives discovered at Leipzig/Halle Airport on August 5 belonged to a Russian intelligence service. The incidents underscore the widening geographic scope of the conflict, as drone and missile activity increasingly spills into neighboring countries.
