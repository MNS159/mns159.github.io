Iran sets six sweeping conditions for Strait of Hormuz reopening; Houthis claim attack on Saudi Aramco facility

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts slightly center-left

Iran’s Supreme National Security Council has issued six new conditions the United States must meet before the Strait of Hormuz can fully reopen, broadening the terms beyond the June memorandum of understanding, as Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian called for an end to a state of “neither war nor peace” with Washington. The council’s secretary Mohammad Bagher Zolghadr, as reported by state broadcaster IRIB, said the strait would remain closed until the U.S. “corrects its behaviour.” The conditions include an end to U.S. threats and insults to Iran’s national and religious values; a permanent halt to attacks on Iran and its allies in Lebanon, Palestine, Yemen and Iraq; lifting the U.S. naval blockade and withdrawal of U.S. naval and air forces from around Iran; compensation for damage from what Zolghadr called two “imposed wars”; lifting of sanctions; and unconditional release of frozen Iranian assets.

The new demands tie the reopening of the strategic waterway—through which about one-fifth of global oil shipments transited in peacetime—to a wider set of political, military and economic issues than those outlined in the June 17 U.S.-Iran memorandum. That memorandum, according to Reuters, had treated such issues as part of a reciprocal, phased process. The Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) also said on Saturday that reopening Hormuz depends on Washington accepting the conditions and is separate from ongoing negotiations between Iran and Oman, with IRGC spokesperson Hossein Mohebbi stating, “Whenever the United States accepts Iran’s conditions, the Strait of Hormuz will certainly be reopened.”

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said Sunday that discussions with Oman over transit and management of the strait were “approaching the final stages” but added that reopening “is subject to other conditions and compensation for the violation” of the June MoU. He denied direct negotiations with the U.S., saying they were merely “exchanging messages” via intermediaries. Pezeshkian, in remarks to Iranian media, said he believed now was the best time for an agreement, stating, “There is cohesion, strength and unity in the country, and as far as I know, Iran is considered victorious and powerful in this war.” He added that Iran and Washington should have set up a channel to address ceasefire violations, saying, “In any case, violations occur in every memorandum of understanding. In principle, a team should have been formed to address violations back then rather than to fight.”

The Strait of Hormuz has been effectively blockaded by Iran since the U.S. and Israel began a war on Iran on February 28, which has killed more than 3,400 people. Iran has targeted ships it accuses of bypassing its preferred route through Iranian waters. On Saturday, the United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations agency reported a ship was hit by a projectile off Oman in the strait, causing a fire but no casualties; the perpetrator was unclear. An unnamed U.S. official told Reuters on Friday that the blockade of Iranian ports would be lifted once commercial shipping through Hormuz was restored “without impediments,” with U.S. steps tied to Iran carrying out its commitments.

Separately, a fire broke out early Sunday at a Saudi Aramco facility in the coastal city of Jizan, which was extinguished with no casualties, according to Saudi Arabia’s Energy Ministry. The ministry did not specify the cause. Yemen’s Iran-backed Houthi armed group claimed responsibility, with military spokesman Yahya Saree writing on X that the group “succeeded in targeting the Aramco refinery in Jizan with a drone, and the strike was precise.” He said the attack was retaliation for Saudi Arabia’s “breach of the airspace of the provinces of Saada and Hajjah” with drones. Riyadh did not immediately comment. The Houthis have claimed previous attacks on Aramco facilities in Jizan and Yanbu, and last month declared a blockade on Saudi Arabia in the Red Sea. Late Sunday, the Houthis also claimed to have attacked “Saudi enemy” troops and equipment in the Yemeni city of Al-Mokha using ballistic missiles and drones; Saudi forces did not immediately comment. The attacks add strain to a 2022 truce between the Houthis and Yemen’s internationally recognized government, backed by Saudi Arabia.
