International & World Middle East

Iran sets six sweeping conditions for Strait of Hormuz reopening; Houthis claim attack on Saudi Aramco facility

International & World desk

Iran’s Supreme National Security Council has issued six new conditions the United States must meet before the Strait of Hormuz can fully reopen, broadening the terms beyond the June memorandum of understanding, as Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian called for an end to a state of “neither war nor peace” with Washington. The council’s secretary Mohammad Bagher Zolghadr, as reported by state broadcaster IRIB, said the strait would remain closed until the U.S. “corrects its behaviour.” The conditions include an end to U.S. threats and insults to Iran’s national and religious values; a permanent halt to attacks on Iran and its allies in Lebanon, Palestine, Yemen and Iraq; lifting the U.S. naval blockade and withdrawal of U.S. naval and air forces from around Iran; compensation for damage from what Zolghadr called two “imposed wars”; lifting of sanctions; and unconditional release of frozen Iranian assets.

The new demands tie the reopening of the strategic waterway—through which about one-fifth of global oil shipments transited in peacetime—to a wider set of political, military and economic issues than those outlined in the June 17 U.S.-Iran memorandum. That memorandum, according to Reuters, had treated such issues as part of a reciprocal, phased process. The Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) also said on Saturday that reopening Hormuz depends on Washington accepting the conditions and is separate from ongoing negotiations between Iran and Oman, with IRGC spokesperson Hossein Mohebbi stating, “Whenever the United States accepts Iran’s conditions, the Strait of Hormuz will certainly be reopened.”

Overnight Strikes Kill Seven in Ukraine and Russia; Drone Incident in Bulgaria Raises Tensions

International & World desk · anchored on AP

In Ukraine, a Russian missile strike near Kyiv killed three people, including a child, and wounded three others, according to local authorities. Meanwhile, Ukrainian drone strikes hit two Russian oil refineries—the Ilsky and Syzran facilities—causing fires, the Ukrainian general staff said. The attacks come as Russia intensifies its aerial campaign, aware that Ukraine's stock of Western-donated interceptor missiles is running low.

Lionel Messi's father Jorge dies at 68

Lionel Messi missed Inter Miami's Leagues Cup defeat to Monterrey on Saturday night after the death of his father, Jorge Messi, at the age of 68. Jorge, who had been the Argentina forward's agent since Messi was 14, died on Saturday in a hospital in his hometown of Rosario, Argentina, following a long illness. Miami paid tribute before kickoff with a minute's silence, black armbands, and Jorge's name displayed on the big screens at Nu Stadium, while the club added a black ribbon to its social media profiles and said: "Our hearts are with our captain, Leo, and the entire Messi family."
