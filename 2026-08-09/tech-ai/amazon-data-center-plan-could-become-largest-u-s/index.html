Amazon data center plan could become largest U.S. climate polluter

Tech / AI desk

Amazon is backing a new natural gas power plant in Pecos County, Texas, that could be the largest single source of climate pollution in the United States, according to reports from The New York Times. The plant, part of Amazon's planned data center at the GW Ranch site, would be permitted to release up to 33 million tons of carbon dioxide per year — more than any existing coal plant in the country. The facility would house 35 natural-gas turbines with a capacity of 7.65 gigawatts and, at least initially, would not be connected to Texas's power grid, instead delivering electricity directly to the data center.

Amazon has confirmed that it purchased the GW Ranch site and plans to purchase power from the plant. The permit from Texas, as tracked by Cleanview, allows for emissions far exceeding those of the largest coal plants, though plants rarely emit the full permitted amount. Even a fraction of those emissions would represent a significant setback for Amazon's Climate Pledge, which aims to make the company carbon neutral by 2040. Amazon's carbon emissions rose 16% last year, partly due to the energy demands of artificial intelligence.

Amazon spokesperson Margaret Callahan told The New York Times, 'The world looks different now than when we co-founded the climate pledge,' while adding, 'our commitment hasn’t changed.' In a separate statement, an Amazon spokesperson confirmed that the data center 'will be powered by new on-site generation that won’t raise electricity costs for Texas families.' The statement comes amid growing political opposition to data centers over their impact on electricity costs and the environment.

The project is part of a broader trend among tech companies, including Meta and Google, which are building their own power plants — often powered by gas, coal, or other non-renewable sources — to meet the soaring energy demands of AI data centers. The Trump administration has sought to lift restrictions on such polluting power plants. The GW Ranch plant's permitted emissions, even if only partially realized, would mark a major challenge to Amazon's climate goals and underscore the tension between AI expansion and corporate sustainability pledges.
