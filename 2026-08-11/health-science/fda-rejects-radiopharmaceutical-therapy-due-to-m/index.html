FDA rejects radiopharmaceutical therapy due to manufacturing issues, halting competitor to Novartis drug

Health & Science desk

The Food and Drug Administration has rejected a radiopharmaceutical therapy developed by ITM Isotope Technologies Munich SE, issuing a complete response letter (CRL) for 177Lu-edoterotide (ITM-11) as a treatment for gastroenteropancreatic neuroendocrine tumors (GEP-NETs), a rare cancer affecting the pancreas, stomach, small intestine, and other parts of the gastrointestinal system. The agency cited chemistry, manufacturing, and controls (CMC)- and third-party commercial facility-related concerns requiring remediation, according to a news release from ITM. The FDA did not identify any clinical or nonclinical concerns regarding the efficacy or safety profile of the drug.

The decision halts what would have been a competitor to Novartis’s existing radiopharmaceutical therapy. Radiopharmaceuticals are an emerging class of cancer treatments designed to deliver radioactive isotopes directly to tumor cells. ITM’s therapy was evaluated in the phase 3 COMPETE trial, which enrolled 309 patients with inoperable, progressive, grade 1 or grade 2 somatostatin receptor-positive NETs of gastroenteric or pancreatic origin. Patients were randomly assigned 2:1 to receive up to four cycles of 7.5 GBq of 177Lu-edotreotide with a nephroprotective amino acid solution every three months (n = 207) or 10 mg of oral everolimus daily for up to 30 months (n = 102).

Efficacy data presented at the 22nd Annual Neuroendocrine Tumor Society (ENETS) 2025 Conference showed a median progression-free survival (PFS) of 23.9 months with 177Lu-edotreotide compared with 14.1 months with everolimus (HR, 0.67; 95% CI, 0.48-0.95; P = .022). As of the January 2025 data cutoff, interim median overall survival (OS) was 63.4 months versus 58.7 months, respectively, a numerical difference that was not statistically significant (HR, 0.78; 95% CI, 0.5-1.1; P = .206). The primary endpoint of the trial was PFS; secondary endpoints included objective response rate and OS.

Regarding safety, treatment-emergent adverse effects related to each agent occurred in 82.5% of patients who received 177Lu-edotreotide compared with 97.0% of patients who received everolimus. One grade 2 serious treatment-emergent adverse event of myelodysplastic syndrome was reported, and no unexpected adverse events were observed. Developers noted that the agent appeared to be well tolerated.

ITM’s chief executive officer, Andrew Cavey, MD, stated in the release: “Our confidence in [177Lu-edoterotide’s] therapeutic potential has not wavered, and we are committed to working closely with the FDA and our partners to address the items outlined in the CRL.” The developers are reviewing the decision to determine next steps and intend to resubmit the new drug application for FDA review. The FDA had previously accepted the application and set a Prescription Drug User Fee Act (PDUFA) date in November 2025.
