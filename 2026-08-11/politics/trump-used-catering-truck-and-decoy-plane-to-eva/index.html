Trump used catering truck and decoy plane to evade Iran threat at NATO summit

Politics desk
✓ Facts
  • On July 8 at the NATO summit in Ankara, Turkey, Trump boarded the older Air Force One (a legacy Boeing 747) in view of television cameras, then was secretly moved via an airport catering truck raised to cabin height to a smaller Air Force C-32A (a modified Boeing 757), according to Washington Post reporting cited by BBC, Fox News and WTOP.
  • Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth boarded the C-32A separately using external stairs, a move reported as intended to make the flight appear normal.
  • Journalists and some White House staff boarded the legacy Air Force One believing Trump was aboard; they were told to lower window shades, and that jet flew to RAF Mildenhall in England as a decoy while Trump flew separately on the C-32A.
  • Officials told CBS News (BBC's US partner) that intelligence had detected a credible Iranian threat to fire a missile at the president's aircraft, occurring a day after renewed U.S. military strikes on Iran following the breakdown of nuclear negotiations.
  • Trump had arrived in Turkey aboard a new Boeing 747-8 donated by Qatar, said he would fly home partway on the older jet 'for old time's sake,' then after landing at RAF Mildenhall reboarded the legacy Air Force One publicly before transferring to the new Qatari-donated jet for the return flight to Washington.
  • White House communications director Steven Cheung did not confirm or deny the reported plane swap but said the new Air Force One is a 'state-of-the-art aircraft' fitted with security measures.
  • The Justice Department is investigating leaks after New York Times journalists were summoned to testify under oath regarding earlier reporting on security concerns tied to the Qatari-donated jet.
  • The Washington Post's account is based on an official and other reviewed material; the Associated Press said it has not independently confirmed the report.
○ Analysis

The episode sits at the intersection of an active U.S.-Iran military confrontation and lingering controversy over Qatar's gift of a luxury jet intended to serve as a new Air Force One. After the U.S. struck Iranian targets during the NATO summit, intelligence officials reportedly identified a specific threat to Trump's aircraft, prompting security officials to stage an elaborate switch—using a catering truck and a decoy flight—to obscure the president's real location and aircraft. The maneuver adds to previously reported security concerns about the retrofitted Qatari plane, which the Secret Service had reportedly urged Trump to avoid using on this leg of the trip, and which is still slated for further security upgrades this fall. The story also intersects with a separate controversy: a Justice Department leak investigation launched after earlier reporting exposed security deliberations about the Qatari jet, raising questions about press-government relations during sensitive security episodes.

Steelman

Beyond partisan framing, the episode illustrates an inherent tension between the secrecy required for credible presidential security threats and the public's and press's ability to verify what actually happened—both the ruse itself and the subsequent leak investigation stem from that same friction.

Implications

Expect continued scrutiny of the Qatari-donated Air Force One's security systems as it undergoes further upgrades this fall, ongoing questions about the propriety and safety of accepting a foreign-gifted presidential aircraft, and further fallout from the Justice Department's leak investigation as it intersects with press coverage of presidential security operations.
