Progressives face key test in Wisconsin and Minnesota primaries

Politics desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum
✓ Facts
  • Wisconsin and Minnesota hold primary elections on Tuesday that will set the fall matchups for governor (Wisconsin) and governor and U.S. Senate (Minnesota).
  • In Wisconsin's Democratic gubernatorial primary, state lawmaker and chef Francesca Hong, a 37-year-old member of the Democratic Socialists of America, faces Milwaukee County Executive David Crowley, who dropped out of the race in July before reentering days later with Gov. Tony Evers' endorsement.
  • The Republican nominee for Wisconsin governor is expected to be U.S. Rep. Tom Tiffany, who is endorsed by President Trump and faces only nominal primary opposition.
  • In Minnesota's Democratic U.S. Senate primary, U.S. Rep. Angie Craig faces Lt. Gov. Peggy Flanagan for the seat being vacated by Sen. Tina Smith, who announced last year she would not seek reelection.
  • Craig has said she regrets her vote for the GOP-backed Laken Riley Act, which allows detention of people without legal status charged with certain crimes.
  • Earlier this year, a federal immigration enforcement action in the Twin Cities known as Operation Metro Surge resulted in the deaths of two people, Renee Macklin Good and Alex Pretti, who were fatally shot by federal agents.
  • In Minnesota's Republican gubernatorial primary, MyPillow founder Mike Lindell, endorsed by Trump, faces businessman Kendall Qualls, who won the state GOP convention endorsement, and House Speaker Lisa Demuth.
  • Minnesota Gov. Tim Walz announced in January he would not seek a third term; Sen. Amy Klobuchar is the only formidable Democratic candidate running to replace him.
○ Analysis

These primaries arrive a week after progressive Abdul El-Sayed narrowly won a Democratic Senate primary in Michigan, making Wisconsin and Minnesota the next test of whether progressive or moderate candidates can win in Midwestern swing states ahead of the November midterms. Wisconsin is a closely contested presidential battleground where statewide races are typically decided by slim margins, while Minnesota has voted Democratic in presidential races for decades but has become a flashpoint this year over federal immigration enforcement. Both states also feature notable Republican dynamics: in Wisconsin, Tiffany is running as a Trump-endorsed everyman despite Trump's declining approval, while in Minnesota, Lindell's Trump-backed candidacy has divided the state GOP establishment.

◐ Debate

Democratic

  • Progressive-aligned Democrats, exemplified by Hong's campaign and Flanagan's messaging, argue that Democrats have been too cautious and that bold policies like Medicare for All, universal childcare, and forceful resistance to Trump's immigration enforcement are needed to energize voters.
  • Flanagan has argued that Craig's vote for the Laken Riley Act shows Democrats have been 'weak' and 'nibbling around the edges,' and that she would be a more forceful check on the Trump administration.
  • Craig and Crowley-aligned Democrats argue that pragmatic, cross-aisle credentials and fundraising strength position them best to win statewide general elections, with Craig noting she regrets her Laken Riley vote while framing herself as the candidate best able to defeat Republicans in November.

Republican

  • Tiffany and allied Republicans argue that grounding a campaign in relatable, non-Beltway identity ('Wisconsin's Dad') and portraying Democratic primaries as chaotic or driven by 'the craziness of the left' is an effective general-election strategy.
  • Lindell and his supporters argue that his direct alliance with Trump and ability to 'work with the president' gives him a unique advantage as Minnesota's Republican nominee, despite the state's history of rejecting Trump in presidential races.
  • Establishment-aligned Republicans, including those who endorsed Qualls at the state convention and Demuth's campaign, have raised concerns about Lindell's electability, questioning his residency status and resurfacing past personal-conduct allegations, which Lindell denies as a 'coordinated attack.'
Steelman

Both parties are navigating the same underlying tension in these races — an energized insurgent wing (progressive Democrats, Trump-loyalist Republicans) pushing against establishment figures seen as more electable in swing-state general elections — making the primaries a dual test of ideological versus pragmatic strategy heading into the midterms.

Implications

The outcomes will help determine whether Democrats nationally lean into progressive positioning or moderate, electability-focused messaging as a midterm strategy, while the Minnesota GOP primary will test how much influence Trump's endorsement carries against state party establishment preferences; both states' general elections are expected to be closely contested given their competitive histories.
