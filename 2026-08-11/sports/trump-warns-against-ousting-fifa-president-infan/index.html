Trump warns against ousting FIFA President Infantino, calling it 'terrible mistake'

Sports desk

US President Donald Trump has warned that it would be a "terrible mistake" to remove FIFA President Gianni Infantino, marking his first public defense of Infantino since the controversial FIFA Forward Enterprise (FFE) proposal was revealed on July 28. Trump wrote on social media: "FIFA would be making a terrible mistake if, for any reason, they even considered replacing President Gianni Infantino. He is fantastic, having just presided over the most successful World Cup, by four times, ever presented. If he is gone, it will never be as successful or profitable again!"

Trump's comments came after the presidents of UEFA, CONCACAF, and the Asian Football Confederation (AFC) issued an open letter accusing Infantino of breaking trust "through deception" over the FFE plan. The proposal would have created a new company to manage commercial and ticketing rights for all FIFA competitions, including World Cups, with 21% sold to a private investment firm. Thrive Eternal, an American venture capital firm founded by Joshua Kushner—brother of Trump's son-in-law Jared Kushner—was expected to lead the investor group. FIFA later scrapped the plan.

In the letter, UEFA, CONCACAF, and the AFC stated: "When trust is broken through deception, when an individual places himself above the collective that entrusted him with authority, that duty has been abandoned." While the statement did not name Infantino directly, sources told BBC Sport that it should be seen as an opportunity for him to resign with dignity. BBC Sport also reported that preliminary discussions have taken place between the three confederations over breakaway competitions should Infantino remain in office, though substantive talks have not yet begun.

Infantino has a close relationship with Trump, having awarded him the inaugural FIFA Peace Prize in December 2025. The 2026 World Cup, co-hosted by the US, Canada, and Mexico, saw Spain defeat Argentina in the final. During the tournament, Trump faced criticism for various controversies, including his involvement in overturning a red card suspension for US player Folarin Balogun, and was booed by fans. Infantino praised Trump's role, saying the World Cup "exceeded all expectations."

The timing of Trump's intervention has drawn scrutiny. The United States Soccer Federation recently called for "meaningful change" in FIFA leadership. Analysts note that Trump's support may be welcomed privately by Infantino but question its effectiveness in shoring up support within the global football community. Trump's focus on profitability also contrasts with critics who argue the tournament has become too commercialized under Infantino. The crisis was sparked by a plan to raise additional revenue beyond FIFA's record earnings.
