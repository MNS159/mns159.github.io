Trump signs executive order reducing childhood vaccine schedule

Politics desk · anchored on Reuters

President Trump signed an executive order on childhood vaccines Monday afternoon in the Oval Office, with Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr.'s HHS producing the underlying policy memo.

This order is the latest step in a yearslong effort by Kennedy and allied 'Make America Healthy Again' advocates to loosen federal vaccine recommendations, following a January CDC attempt that courts blocked. Because vaccine schedules for school entry are set at the state level, the order's immediate legal force is limited to federal recommendations and Justice Department pressure on states — but its symbolic weight is large, given it reopens the vaccine-autism question that decades of research, including a 2019 Danish study of over 650,000 children, has found no evidence to support. The policy shift arrives as measles cases in the U.S. have reached a 35-year high and two children died of measles in the past year, according to pediatric groups, sharpening the stakes of any change that could delay or reduce vaccine uptake. Mainstream medical bodies (AMA, AAP) and independent vaccine experts warn that splitting combination shots and spacing them out has no proven scientific benefit and could increase missed appointments, while the administration frames the move as restoring parental choice and aligning the U.S. with lighter vaccine schedules used in some other developed nations.

Zuckerberg manifesto outlines vision for open, personalized AI as Meta recalibrates strategy

Tech / AI desk · anchored on AP

Meta CEO Mark Zuckerberg published a 6,500-word manifesto on Monday titled “The Future is for Everyone,” arguing that artificial intelligence should be decentralized and personalized rather than controlled by a single superintelligence. The essay, which echoes a shorter public letter from last year, calls for open-weight AI models that anyone can download and customize, and positions Meta as a steward of “personal superintelligence” for billions of people and small businesses. The post arrives as Meta restructures its AI division—former chief scientist Yann LeCun was replaced last year by former Scale AI CEO Alexandr Wang—and as the company lags behind rivals OpenAI and Anthropic in foundation-model adoption and enterprise revenue, according to Ars Technica and TechCrunch.

US-Iran standoff sends oil higher, stocks lower as Hormuz deal hopes fade

Financial & Markets desk · anchored on Reuters

Oil prices climbed and U.S. stocks slipped on Monday as negotiations between the United States and Iran over a peace deal and the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz hit an impasse, with both sides hardening their stances. Brent crude futures rose 5% over the two days through Monday to settle at $87.72 per barrel, while U.S. West Texas Intermediate crude settled up about 5.1% at $82.13. The S&P 500 dipped 0.06% to 7,753.11, the Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 60.95 points, or 0.11%, to 53,975.98, and the Nasdaq Composite declined 0.32% to 26,605.36. Futures on the Dow, S&P 500, and Nasdaq-100 were roughly flat in premarket trading Tuesday.

Syrian court sentences ex-President Assad to death in absentia

International & World desk

A Syrian criminal court in Damascus has sentenced ousted President Bashar al-Assad to death in absentia after convicting him of premeditated murder, torture, war crimes and crimes against humanity during the country's nearly 14-year civil war. It was the first conviction against the former leader, who fled to Moscow with his family after his regime was overthrown in December 2024, ending the Assad family's five-decade grip on power.
