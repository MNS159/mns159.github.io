US-Iran standoff sends oil higher, stocks lower as Hormuz deal hopes fade

Financial & Markets desk · anchored on Reuters

Oil prices climbed and U.S. stocks slipped on Monday as negotiations between the United States and Iran over a peace deal and the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz hit an impasse, with both sides hardening their stances. Brent crude futures rose 5% over the two days through Monday to settle at $87.72 per barrel, while U.S. West Texas Intermediate crude settled up about 5.1% at $82.13. The S&P 500 dipped 0.06% to 7,753.11, the Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 60.95 points, or 0.11%, to 53,975.98, and the Nasdaq Composite declined 0.32% to 26,605.36. Futures on the Dow, S&P 500, and Nasdaq-100 were roughly flat in premarket trading Tuesday.

The standoff deepened after Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said Sunday there was "no possibility of restarting negotiations" until the U.S. compensates Iran for violations of the June memorandum of understanding. President Donald Trump responded on Monday by rebuking Iran's demand for reparations and telling Axios, "We are only semi-negotiating with them," adding that he would wait for economic pressure to build on the country. The back-and-forth dashed hopes that had been fueled the previous week by Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent's comments to CNBC strongly indicating a deal was imminent.

"We're now in a bit of a Mexican standoff, if you'd like, in terms of who blinks first," said Tony Sycamore, a market analyst at IG. "This is going to be almost a war of attrition now. You probably can see the (oil) market sitting around the $75 to $95 range while we wait to see who blinks first." Iran has said it is closing in on a deal with Oman to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, but Tehran has continued to resist direct negotiations with the U.S. until its conditions are met.

The geopolitical uncertainty weighed on equities, with Intel a key laggard, falling 4% after saying it would issue shares to raise $15 billion. Nvidia dropped 2.9% following a Financial Times report that the AI chip company is working with Apollo Global and Blackstone to create a $500 billion AI infrastructure funding package. Apple declined 1.5%. The three major U.S. indexes had come off their best weeks since April, with the S&P 500 finishing the prior week at an all-time closing record, boosted by a weaker-than-expected July nonfarm payrolls report that raised hopes the Federal Reserve would hold off on rate hikes.

Investors are now awaiting Wednesday's Consumer Price Index (CPI) report for July, which could prove instrumental in setting expectations for the Fed's September meeting. Fed funds futures traders price in about a 52% likelihood of a rate hike in September, down from 67% a week earlier, according to the CME's FedWatch tool. Cleveland Federal Reserve President Beth Hammack told Yahoo Finance on Monday that it may take more than one rate hike to rein in inflation. "We think the risks are skewed towards a hot print, which would probably drive a rebound in rate expectations and, potentially, renewed worries about stagflation," said Jonas Goltermann, chief markets economist at Capital Economics. U.S. Treasury yields rose, with the 2-year yield up 1 basis point at 4.253% and the 10-year yield up 2 basis points at 4.72%.

The earnings calendar this week is lighter, with over 80% of S&P 500 companies having already reported. However, results from AI infrastructure companies including CoreWeave, Nebius, Cerebras Systems, and Super Micro Computer are expected to provide insight into the health of the AI hardware business. Nvidia's new funding measures with six major financial institutions, including BlackRock, Apollo, and Goldman Sachs, are worth more than $500 billion for AI infrastructure, though the company did not disclose financial terms or investment commitments.
