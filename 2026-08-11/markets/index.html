US-Iran standoff sends oil higher, stocks lower as Hormuz deal hopes fade

Financial & Markets desk · anchored on Reuters

Oil prices climbed and U.S. stocks slipped on Monday as negotiations between the United States and Iran over a peace deal and the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz hit an impasse, with both sides hardening their stances. Brent crude futures rose 5% over the two days through Monday to settle at $87.72 per barrel, while U.S. West Texas Intermediate crude settled up about 5.1% at $82.13. The S&P 500 dipped 0.06% to 7,753.11, the Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 60.95 points, or 0.11%, to 53,975.98, and the Nasdaq Composite declined 0.32% to 26,605.36. Futures on the Dow, S&P 500, and Nasdaq-100 were roughly flat in premarket trading Tuesday.

Nvidia and Wall Street giants line up $500 billion in financing for AI compute as new 'investable asset class'

Financial & Markets desk

Nvidia and six of Wall Street’s largest asset managers announced Monday a plan to mobilize at least $500 billion in third-party financing for companies building artificial-intelligence data centers, marking a push to recast Nvidia’s chips as a long-lived, revenue-generating asset that can be borrowed against like real estate or toll roads. The chipmaker said it signed memorandums of understanding with Apollo Global Management, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield Asset Management, Goldman Sachs and KKR to establish financing platforms that will allow Nvidia’s customers — including hyperscalers, frontier AI labs and enterprises — to fund the construction and hardware acquisition without tapping their own balance sheets. Nvidia will have the option of backstopping up to 25% of each loan, a structure that the company said should produce more favorable interest rates for borrowers.
