Syrian court sentences ex-President Assad to death in absentia

International & World desk

A Syrian criminal court in Damascus has sentenced ousted President Bashar al-Assad to death in absentia after convicting him of premeditated murder, torture, war crimes and crimes against humanity during the country's nearly 14-year civil war. It was the first conviction against the former leader, who fled to Moscow with his family after his regime was overthrown in December 2024, ending the Assad family's five-decade grip on power.

Also sentenced to death was Atef Najib, Assad's cousin and former head of political security in Deraa province, who appeared in a striped prison uniform inside a courtroom cage as the judge read his sentence. Najib was convicted of murder, "the intentional killing of children under 15," torture leading to death and responsibility for a 2011 massacre in Deraa that the court ruled amounted to crimes against humanity. The court also sentenced six former military and security officials in absentia to death, including Assad's younger brother Maher, who commanded the army's elite Fourth Division and also fled to Moscow, and former defence minister Fahd al-Freij.

Deraa is widely seen as the birthplace of the uprising against Assad's rule. In March 2011, the detention and alleged torture of teenagers accused of writing anti-government graffiti sparked protests that were met with a violent crackdown by security forces, helping ignite nationwide demonstrations that escalated into a civil war in which an estimated half a million people were killed. Assad, part of the Alawite minority, came to power in 2000 after the death of his father, Hafez, who had ruled since 1970, and much of his rule was defined by the conflict.

Najib, 66, was in charge of political security in Deraa when the uprising began and was removed from the post in April 2011. He fled Syria after Assad's fall but later returned, and was arrested in January 2025 in Latakia. The proceedings against him began on April 26, and 75 plaintiffs had filed cases against him, according to Al Jazeera. He was the only defendant physically present for the verdict; the others were sentenced in absentia.

People gathered outside the court in Damascus were seen celebrating the verdicts, with Al Jazeera's Obaida Hitto calling it a historic moment and noting that Najib was the only codefendant standing trial physically inside the country. Nouha al-Masri, who has spent 15 years waiting for the moment, is both a plaintiff and a member of the five-person prosecution team; her brother was killed in the March 2011 crackdown at Al-Omari Square in Deraa. She said: "The verdict reflects the magnitude of the sacrifices we made, the sacrifices of the martyrs, the suffering and pain of the victims, the missing, and the detainees." She added: "Our demand is that this not be the end of the trials... In the new Syria, there will be no impunity from this day forward."

The trial is being seen as a test of Syria's transitional justice system under President Ahmed al-Sharaa, whose government has faced criticism over delays in launching a promised process. "What is at stake in these proceedings is not confined to the question of whether Najib will be held to account for his actions ... Rather, the true significance of this trial lies in the legal foundation of the judicial record that these proceedings will leave behind," said Fadel Abdulghany, founder of the Syrian Network for Human Rights. The group's 2025 report counted more than 181,000 people forcibly disappeared or arbitrarily detained during the war, 90 percent of them by the Assad regime. It remains unclear whether Syria's new authorities can secure Assad's or Maher's extradition from Moscow. An arrest warrant was issued against Assad in December 2024 on murder and torture charges, and he left as forces under al-Sharaa swept to power in an 11-day campaign. Earlier attempts by Syrian opposition figures to pursue Assad through Western courts did not lead to convictions.
