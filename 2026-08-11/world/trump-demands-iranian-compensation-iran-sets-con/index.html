Trump demands Iran compensation, Tehran sets conditions for Hormuz reopening amid secret flight revelations

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts slightly center-left

President Donald Trump has demanded that Iran pay compensation as part of any peace talks, while Tehran insists the United States must meet its demands before reopening the Strait of Hormuz, as the economic pressure campaign against the Islamic Republic intensifies. The developments come as US media reports revealed that Trump secretly switched planes in Turkey in July due to a credible Iranian assassination threat, with Air Force One used as a decoy while he flew out on a smaller C-32A aircraft, according to The Washington Post and The New York Times.

Trump on Monday said that since Iran is seeking compensation, he now intends to demand the same for the US side. The pivot is part of a broader shift to what the White House calls 'Operation Economic Fury'—a sanctions campaign Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent described as the 'financial equivalent' of a bombing campaign. The administration argues that months of bombing have pushed Iran's economy to a breaking point, with Trump claiming Iran is 'totally broke' and has 'inflation of 300%.' However, the Iranian government recently reported an annual inflation rate of 88.6%, and the International Monetary Fund estimates the economy will shrink by 5.4% this year.

Iran has not been publicly intimidated. Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baqaei said on social media that 'whenever Washington proves itself incapable of pursuing diplomacy, it retreats into sanctions' and warned that 'the real risk is that American politicians... will instead strangle their own remaining chances of a less humiliating exit.' Iran has reiterated that the Strait of Hormuz—a waterway that carried roughly 20% of global oil supplies before the conflict—will not reopen until the US meets its demands to end the war. Tehran also said ships will be expected to pay for transit through the narrow waterway.

The war, which began with US bombing, missiles, and drone attacks on February 28, has largely closed the strait, and attempts to reopen it have been short-lived as Iran uses it as leverage. US Treasury data shows Iranian oil loadings have dropped from 1.8 million barrels per day before the war to less than 500,000 barrels per day. Crude oil prices climbed on Monday as investors interpreted Trump's comments as a sign that fewer ships would traverse the strait, continuing to limit global energy supplies.

The secret flight from Turkey on July 8 was triggered by a 'credible Iran-related threat,' a US official told The Washington Post. Trump publicly stated he would depart the NATO summit in Ankara on an older version of Air Force One 'for old time's sake,' but was actually spirited into a catering container and transferred to a C-32A aircraft that flew him to the United Kingdom. Journalists and some White House staff believed the president was on the decoy plane. White House Communications Director Steve Cheung defended the Qatari-gifted jet used for the return flight, saying the US uses 'every tool' to counter foreign threats.

The shift to sanctions comes as US stockpiles of key weapons have dwindled and stop-start talks have stalled. Experts note that sanctions are a longer-term tool and that Trump has not articulated clear strategic goals for the war. Richard Nephew, a former Obama administration official, said Trump has 'systematically weakened his own hand through his inadequate use of force, inarticulate elucidation of an objective, and vacillation on negotiations.' Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth said the US has 'the most powerful economy in the world' and can put pressure on adversaries. The war and higher gasoline prices remain unpopular in the US, and Trump's approval has suffered.
