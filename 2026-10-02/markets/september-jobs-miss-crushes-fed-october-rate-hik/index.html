September Jobs Miss Crushes Fed October Rate-Hike Odds; Stocks, Bonds Rally

Financial & Markets desk · anchored on Reuters

The U.S. economy added just 29,000 jobs in September, far below the 84,000 expected by economists, while the unemployment rate rose to 4.2%, the Bureau of Labor Statistics reported Friday. The weak reading sent stock and bond markets rallying as traders slashed bets that the Federal Reserve will raise interest rates at its October meeting. The Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 0.6%, the S&P 500 gained 1%, and the Nasdaq Composite surged 1.7%, hitting a record high. The 10-year Treasury yield fell to 5.21%, and the 30-year yield dropped to 5.57%.

Market-implied odds of a quarter-point rate hike at the Fed's Oct. 27-28 meeting collapsed to about 17% on the CME Group's FedWatch tool, down from roughly 36% a week earlier. On the prediction market Kalshi, October hike odds fell to 18% from nearly 70% a week ago. "For the Fed, this number should be the nail in the coffin for an October hike," said Thomas Simons, chief U.S. economist at Jefferies. The August jobs count was revised lower to 133,000, and July was revised from a gain to a loss of 10,000, for a net downward revision of 60,000 jobs.

Average hourly earnings rose just 0.1% in September, bringing the 12-month gain to 3% — the lowest since May 2021 and below the 3.1% expected. The average work week held steady at 34.6 hours. "Americans are frustrated by the lack of opportunities right now," said Heather Long, chief economist at Navy Federal Credit Union. "Wage growth fell to a new 5-year low and is being wiped out entirely by inflation. That stings heading into the holidays." The household survey showed a brighter picture: employment rose by 406,000, the labor force swelled by 485,000, and the participation rate climbed to 61.8%, its highest since May. An alternative measure of unemployment that includes discouraged workers and those working part-time for economic reasons edged down to 7.6%, its lowest since January 2025.

Most job gains came from healthcare, which added 17,000 workers; construction rose by 11,000, and manufacturing added 9,000. Government employment fell by 17,000, temporary help services lost 11,000, information services shed 10,000 amid concerns about artificial intelligence's impact, and financial activities dropped 7,000. Despite the weak payrolls number, broader economic growth has been strong: the Commerce Department revised first-quarter GDP to 2.5% and second-quarter GDP to 2.2%, and the Atlanta Fed is tracking third-quarter GDP at 3.7%.

The report comes as Fed officials weigh the state of the economy and inflation, which has held well above the central bank's 2% target. The Fed's preferred inflation gauge, the core personal consumption expenditures price index, rose 3% in August, lighter than the 3.3% consensus. "This report strengthens the case for the Federal Reserve to remain patient," said Adam Schickling, a senior economist at Vanguard. "The labor market has not deteriorated sharply, but there is also little evidence that it has meaningfully strengthened, giving policymakers reason to wait for additional data." While October hike odds have collapsed, traders still see a greater than 75% chance of a rate increase in December on FedWatch and 65% odds on Kalshi. Brent crude futures fell to $99 per barrel on Friday.
