G7 to release 100M barrels of fuel as Trump threatens diesel export ban

Politics desk
✓ Facts
  • The G7 (Canada, France, Germany, Italy, Japan, UK, US) said Friday it will "begin immediately" releasing 100 million barrels of fuel, including diesel, from stockpiles over the next four months.
  • UK diesel pump prices hit a record high this week, averaging 199.79p per litre according to the RAC, up from 142.38p previously.
  • President Trump said he is considering a ban on US diesel exports and said he "may" ask European countries to release some of their own diesel reserves.
  • The US exports between 1.2 and 1.5 million barrels of diesel per day, with most going to Latin America; European buyers including France, the Netherlands and the UK also import US diesel.
  • US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent posted that Europe should prepare to release diesel supplies so that US farmers, truckers and businesses "should not be left carrying the burden" of rising prices.
  • UK Energy Minister Martin McCluskey held a call with European counterparts on Thursday, and the UK said it had been discussing the US supply threats with the EU, which was due to hold a meeting Friday on the issue.
  • Under IEA rules the UK must hold oil stocks equal to 90 days of net imports, and under EU rules members must hold reserves covering 61 days of consumption; the EU holds roughly 109 million tons of emergency crude and fuel stocks, about a third of it diesel-related.
  • Contributing factors cited for the price spike include the US-Israel war with Iran affecting the Strait of Hormuz, Ukrainian strikes on Russian refineries, a Russian diesel export ban, and China halting some October fuel exports.
○ Analysis

Diesel prices have surged globally due to a combination of disruptions: the war involving the US, Israel and Iran has constrained flows through the Strait of Hormuz, which normally carries about a fifth of the world's oil and gas; Ukrainian strikes on Russian refineries and a Russian export ban have removed another major source of diesel from international markets; and Chinese refiners have reportedly cut back on fuel exports to prioritize domestic supply. Against this backdrop, the G7 said it would release 100 million barrels of fuel, including diesel, from stockpiles over the next four months. Separately, Trump is considering a US diesel export ban, which could lower domestic pump prices ahead of the November midterms by keeping more US-refined diesel at home. But since the US exports 1.2-1.5 million barrels a day and is a key supplier to Europe and Latin America, officials in the UK and EU have been in contact over the issue, including a call between the UK energy minister and European counterparts and a planned EU meeting on the matter.

Steelman

The underlying tension in this story is structural, not partisan: a US export ban could offer relief to American drivers and businesses before the midterms, but experts such as Argus Media's David Fyfe have warned that cutting off US diesel supply would likely cause international prices to skyrocket, which would be felt by countries like the UK that rely heavily on US diesel imports.

Implications

The G7's 100-million-barrel fuel release is scheduled to unfold over the next four months. The UK government has said it continues to engage with international partners and the UK fuel industry, and has stressed there is no cause for concern about diesel shortages, though it expects prices to rise further.
