Man City confirm appeal against guilty financial verdict

Sports desk

Manchester City have lodged an appeal against an independent commission's guilty verdict on all charges relating to breaches of the Premier League's financial regulations between 2009-10 and 2017-18, calling the ruling "unsafe" and containing "clear material errors, of law, principle and fact." The club submitted its appeal on Thursday evening and said "a comprehensive body of irrefutable evidence" supports its positions.

The commission found City "utilised devices" to disguise the true extent of certain club liabilities, including arranging "sham contracts" with commercial partners as part of a scheme to hide more than £830m in secret funding. The Premier League says City "systematically broke Premier League Rules for nearly a decade." It is understood City will argue in their appeal that key sponsorship deals were funded by the Abu Dhabi government rather than the club's owners, Abu Dhabi United Group, which is said to have topped up commercial deals to make the club appear compliant with financial rules.

The Football Association said the verdict has "significant implications for the integrity of the game" and it is "carefully considering the decision and its implications and will take action where appropriate," though it declined further comment while proceedings remain ongoing. Separately, the Treasury Committee has asked HMRC whether it has requested the redacted documents from the commission's report, following a report claiming City failed to pay as much as £12m in unpaid tax because of a "sham" contract with former manager Roberto Mancini, potentially rising to £24m with penalties. City's principal sponsor Etihad Airways, which said it was not named in the published findings, is considering legal action over the "damaging implications" of the release.

The appeal will be heard in private by a new, three-person independent commission panel. Under Premier League regulations introduced this summer, an appeal hearing should conclude within 12 weeks — 24 December if calculated from Thursday — with a decision communicated within 30 days, though the timeframe can be disapplied. Previous precedent suggests sanctions against City will only be imposed after a decision on the appeal, and a second appeal against any sanctions remains possible.

The verdict has created significant uncertainty over City's squad. Sources close to the first team told BBC Sport there is a notable level of concern among players, with some sceptical the appeal will bring a reprieve and others trusting the club's leadership. Most players do not have relegation clauses in their contracts, and their deals are fully guaranteed, meaning they would not take pay cuts if the club lost Premier League status and City would still be entitled to demand transfer fees for any departing players. Agents said players are likely to wait for clarity on sanctions before entering contract negotiations; only Mateo Kovacic, Matheus Nunes, Marcus Bettinelli and Geronimo Rulli have contracts expiring in the next two summers.

The looming punishment also complicates City's transfer plans and player retention, with one agent telling BBC Sport: "Some players want medals, all players want money." City's ability to renew contracts and make signings will depend on the appeal's outcome, and maintaining one of the world's highest wage bills could become harder without Premier League-level income if demotion were to occur. The club maintains its innocence, while the FA and other bodies continue to monitor the case as the appeal process moves forward.
