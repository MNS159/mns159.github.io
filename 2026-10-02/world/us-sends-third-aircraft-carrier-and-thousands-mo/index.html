US sends third aircraft carrier and thousands more troops to Middle East amid Iran war

International & World desk

The United States is sending a third aircraft carrier and thousands more troops to the Middle East, a U.S. official confirmed Thursday, as President Donald Trump vowed that the seven-month war with Iran would end "right after the election" or sooner. The USS Theodore Roosevelt Carrier Strike Group and the USS Makin Island Amphibious Group are heading to the region, adding roughly 10,000 sailors and Marines, according to U.S. officials. The deployment brings total U.S. forces in the region to approximately 20,000, along with hundreds of vessels and aircraft, with the USS George Washington and USS Boxer already operating there.

Speaking at a rally in Denton, Texas, Trump said Iran "is ready to fold up" and that the U.S. would win the war "right after the election—but maybe before the election." He claimed Iran's leaders are "gone," that its navy is at the bottom of the sea, and that its missile manufacturing capacity is "dead." Trump also said Iran was "3 to 4 weeks away" from acquiring a nuclear weapon before the war began, and that energy prices, which have soared during the conflict, would come "pouring down" once the war ends. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent announced that Iran did not load a single barrel of crude oil onto tankers in September, calling the milestone part of "Operation Economic Outcast."

The deployment comes as oil prices surged more than $4 a barrel amid concerns about regional stability. The Saudi-led coalition fighting Yemen's Iran-backed Houthis accused the rebels of attacking an electricity distribution station in the Saudi city of Medina, home to the second holiest site in Islam, drawing condemnation across the Muslim world. Intense fighting between Houthi and Saudi-backed forces near the Red Sea killed 80 combatants over the past day, according to sources on both sides. The United Nations Population Fund said hostilities in Yemen have displaced more than 145,000 people, including 35,000 women of childbearing age and more than 3,200 pregnant women, with some giving birth on the roadside while fleeing.

President Trump suggested Iran could be linked to an alleged attempted hijacking of an Israel-bound FlyDubai flight, in which the co-pilot reportedly stabbed the captain and tried to crash the plane with 174 people on board. Asked if Iran's Revolutionary Guard Corps was involved, Trump said "he could've been, yeah," adding that "they'll be hit, very hard, don't worry." Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said authorities were investigating whether the co-pilot was "dispatched" by a third party. In a separate development, the Justice Department charged Ashton Hamed Ellaboudy, 51, a Department of Energy employee from Richland, Washington, with attempting to provide material support to the Houthis, alleging he helped test and modify solar generators and communications equipment for an undercover FBI source posing as a Houthi representative.

European Union nations have discussed a French proposal to release 50 million barrels of diesel from reserves, a source told Reuters, amid U.S. pressure to ease fuel prices. The discussions included a demand that the U.S. not unilaterally ban diesel exports. Russian President Vladimir Putin said he hopes the Strait of Hormuz remains open to shipping and that Moscow could help remove Iran's enriched uranium. Iran's parliament has completed a committee-level consideration of a proposed bill that would establish what lawmakers frame as the future Iranian management of the Strait of Hormuz, including the imposition of fees on commercial vessels using the vital waterway, according to the semi-official Tasnim news agency. The Trump administration rejects Iran's demands for all commercial vessels to seek permission for transits, and Iran has repeatedly targeted tankers in the waterway since the war began.

Lebanese Prime Minister Nawaf Salam said his country cannot rebuild from the latest Israel-Hezbollah war alone, saying "the scale of the needs exceeds the capacities of the Lebanese state." Lebanese authorities have put the overall death toll from both rounds of fighting at more than 8,900, with hundreds of thousands of people displaced, mainly from the south. Iraqi Ambassador to Tehran Yasser Abdul Zahra Al-Hajjaj said flights between Iran and Iraq will resume soon, as the two countries work to resolve disruptions to air travel. The U.S. Central Command released video of the USS George Washington conducting flight operations in the Middle East, signaling its presence amid the Iranian port blockade.
