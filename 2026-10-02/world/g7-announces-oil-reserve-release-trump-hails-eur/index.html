G7 announces 100-million-barrel oil reserve release; Trump hails Europe diesel deal

International & World desk

The Group of Seven (G7) announced Friday it will release up to 100 million barrels of oil from strategic reserves over four months in a coordinated effort to curb soaring energy prices driven by the U.S.-Israeli war with Iran. The release, to be conducted through the International Energy Agency (IEA), includes a "frontloaded substantial diesel release within the first 20 days by G7 members and partners," according to a statement following a meeting chaired by French President Emmanuel Macron. The group said it will convene under the IEA in the coming days to discuss the possibility of additional diesel releases as necessary.

The announcement came shortly after U.S. President Donald Trump posted on his Truth Social account that Europe "just agreed to release a massive amount of their heavily stocked Diesel Oil." Trump had pressured European countries, notably Germany and France, to release diesel stockpiles to ease soaring prices caused by shipping disruptions in the Strait of Hormuz, a consequence of the war with Iran that began on February 28. The White House official told Al Jazeera that the U.S. is "tight on diesel" due to lost exports from Russia, the Middle East, and China, adding that American refiners are running at record highs.

EU countries, along with the United Kingdom, hold approximately 52 million metric tonnes of gas oil and diesel stocks, including nearly 38 million tonnes of emergency reserves, according to Eurostat data from June 2026. EU rules require member states to maintain emergency oil stocks covering at least 90 days of net imports or 61 days of domestic consumption. Germany holds the largest emergency stock at 5.6 million tonnes, followed by France with 8.2 million tonnes. In contrast, U.S. diesel inventories hit a record low of 107.9 million barrels as of September 11, 2026. The national average for a gallon of diesel in the U.S. was $6.37 on Friday, down from a record $6.52 on September 22.

Trump had previously threatened to ban U.S. diesel exports if European countries did not release their stockpiles. The EU rejected that threat on Friday, with EU trade chief Maros Sefcovic telling reporters after a G-20 trade meeting that he had discussed tight diesel supplies with U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer and expressed Europe's desire for a coordinated approach to lowering prices. An EU government official told Politico that the Trump administration sent a proposal to EU leaders on Thursday asking them to release 120 million barrels of diesel over 180 days. On Friday, the EU's energy task force met to discuss a French proposal under which European countries would release 50 million barrels of diesel and IEA members would release 50 million barrels of crude oil.

Relations between the U.S. and EU have been tense since Trump imposed new tariffs on the bloc last year and demanded a deal to buy Greenland, which led European nations to send troops to the island in a show of defiance. Ties have further soured as EU nations refused to allow the U.S. to use their airbases for attacks on Iran. Frederic Schneider, a nonresident senior fellow at the Middle East Council on Global Affairs, told Al Jazeera that while Europe holds large mandatory product stocks, "the Trumpian fashion of issuing every request as an ultimatum makes it harder for European governments to agree." He predicted Europe would give some ground through an IEA-coordinated release "well short of 120 million barrels" to avoid being perceived as capitulating to Washington.

The issue has been politically damaging for Trump and his Republican Party ahead of the November midterm elections, with Trump's approval on the economy plunging. High global energy prices have been a knock-on effect of the war with Iran, which Trump launched alongside Israel on February 28. In March, the IEA's 32 member countries agreed to unlock 400 million barrels of oil from emergency reserves, though officials said that release has not yet been fully completed. The G7 includes Canada, France, Germany, Italy, the United Kingdom, and the United States, with the European Union as an observer member.
