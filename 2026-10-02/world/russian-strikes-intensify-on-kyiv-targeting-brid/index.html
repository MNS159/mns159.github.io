Ukraine War

Russian strikes paralyze Kyiv bridges, kill one as mayor warns city is being 'torn apart'

International & World desk · anchored on Reuters

Russian drone and missile strikes have intensified on Kyiv, killing at least one person and forcing the closure of three major bridges over the Dnipro River, Kyiv Mayor Vitaliy Klitschko said Friday. The attacks, which included repeated strikes on the Pivdennyi (Southern) Bridge — hit four times in two days — have caused traffic gridlock across the Ukrainian capital. Klitschko warned that the strikes were "tearing Kyiv apart" and described the situation as "very dramatic," with critical infrastructure, logistics, and housing under sustained assault.

The Southern Bridge, a key transport artery connecting western and eastern Kyiv, was struck overnight and again Friday evening, suspending all metro and automobile traffic. The Paton Bridge was closed toward the left bank, and the Metro Bridge was shut in the direction of the right bank, the city administration said. One person was killed when a multi-story residential building in the Darnytskyi district was hit, Ukraine's State Emergency Service reported. At least two others were injured. Kyiv's data centers have also been targeted in recent days, causing internet outages. The city's population is estimated at about three million, many of whom rely on the metro network.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky said the attacks reflect a decision by Russian President Vladimir Putin in early September that "there are no rules now." In an interview with the Financial Times, Zelensky said: "The Russians have failed to occupy us. So now they are carrying out massive attacks and mass killings. They want to destroy our bridges... our data centres, the internet, mobile networks, hospitals, kindergartens, schools, universities — everything. They want people to flee their cities." Zelensky called on allies to tighten sanctions against Russia, arguing that Moscow remains dependent on foreign components for its drones and missiles, and that supply chains run through Europe, the United States, China, and Japan.

Russian President Vladimir Putin, speaking Thursday at the Valdai foreign policy forum, insisted the strikes on Ukrainian economic targets were a direct response to Ukraine's attacks on Russian oil refineries and Black Sea ships. "Now from every direction we're receiving calls 'let's stop'. But they started it," Putin said. He acknowledged that Ukraine had "got some results" from its refinery attacks and that Russia had lost about 1% of its GDP. Ukraine maintains it targets Russian oil infrastructure because it helps finance the war. Russia has ramped up strikes on Ukrainian energy infrastructure every winter since launching its full-scale invasion in 2022.

Elsewhere in Ukraine, Russian strikes hit a kindergarten in Kherson, a grocery store in Kharkiv, and apartment buildings in Kramatorsk and Sumy, Zelensky said. The Dnipro, Odesa, Kharkiv, Kyiv, Poltava, and Rivne regions also came under attack, with at least four people reported wounded. Russia's Defense Ministry said its drones struck a bridge over the Dnipro used for military purposes, a power station in the Kyiv region, the Izmail port near Odesa, and a cargo vessel. Ukraine's military carried out its own overnight attacks on Russia, hitting a Lukoil facility in Volgograd and an oil dispatch station in the Samara region, causing fires. Ukrainian forces also targeted airfields and industrial sites in several Russian regions, including in the Black Sea, Zelensky said. Russian authorities in the Black Sea port town of Tuapse reported damage to an enterprise from drone debris.
