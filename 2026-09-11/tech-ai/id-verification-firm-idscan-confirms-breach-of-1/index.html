ID verification firm IDScan confirms breach exposing 150 million driver's licenses

Tech / AI desk

IDScan, a Louisiana-based identity verification service used by entertainment venues and cannabis dispensaries, has confirmed a data breach that resulted in the theft of driver's licenses from its systems. The company disclosed in a website notice that hackers stole driver's licenses from its cloud, exposing people's full names, driver's license numbers, and identity numbers from other government-issued documents such as passports. The breach reportedly involves more than 150 million driver's license records belonging to individuals in the United States and Canada.

The acknowledgment comes a week after independent cybersecurity journalist Brian Krebs reported that a dark web site allowed anyone to search the driver's license information of over 150 million people, including accessing their photos. Krebs verified the authenticity by examining his own record and reported that the database contained high-profile individuals, including U.S. Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth. IDScan said it received information about a claim of a hack on or around September 1, the same day Krebs broke the story.

IDScan's notice marks the company's first public confirmation of an intrusion. The company said in its statement that, "though full access to the information required payment" — likely referring to a ransom demand from the hackers to access the full cache of stolen data — the company was providing notice to potentially affected individuals. IDScan has not specified how many individuals are affected but notes on its website that it holds over 150 million driver's license records.

The Pentagon told TechCrunch last week that it was aware of the suspected breach, and a spokesperson for the FBI said it was also investigating the incident. IDScan said its investigation is ongoing. The company did not respond to TechCrunch's request for comment about whether the hackers contacted it with a ransom demand not to release the data.

Sources: TechCrunch
