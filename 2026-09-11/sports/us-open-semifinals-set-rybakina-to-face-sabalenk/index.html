US Open semifinals set; Rybakina to face Sabalenka in women's final

Sports desk

The US Open women's final is set for a blockbuster showdown between the tournament's top two seeds, with second seed Elena Rybakina set to face top seed Aryna Sabalenka. Rybakina, who is guaranteed to become world number one after the tournament, fought back to defeat American fourth seed Coco Gauff 3-6, 6-4, 6-4 in the semifinals, silencing a partisan crowd. Sabalenka earlier overpowered home hope Jessica Pegula 7-5, 6-2 to keep alive her bid to become the third woman in the Open era to win the New York singles title three times in a row.

Rybakina's victory ended a remarkable career record for Gauff: the American had won all 18 previous US Open matches in which she took the first set. The Kazakh second seed, who won Wimbledon in 2022 and beat Sabalenka in the Australian Open final in January, will now seek her third Grand Slam title. 'I definitely need to step my game up [in the final], serve better than today, stay aggressive and really go for my shots,' Rybakina said after the match. Pegula said of Sabalenka's performance: 'Probably the best match she's played this year.' Sabalenka said she was 'super desperate' to reach the final and that 'I wanted it badly to go to the finals, and of course, I want to go all the way.'

The women's final will be the first time the top two seeds have contested the US Open title match since Serena Williams beat Victoria Azarenka in 2013. Sabalenka holds a 10-7 edge in head-to-head meetings with Rybakina, including wins in the Miami semifinals and Indian Wells final this year, but Rybakina prevailed in their most recent Grand Slam final encounter in Melbourne.

On the men's side, the semifinals are also set, with the late-night scheduling of matches drawing scrutiny. American eighth seed Ben Shelton, who did not complete his quarterfinal victory over Carlos Alcaraz until 3:34 a.m. on Wednesday, faces 11th seed Frances Tiafoe in Friday's night session. Top seed Alexander Zverev, of whom three of his five matches this fortnight finished past 1 a.m., meets unseeded Karen Khachanov in the day session. 'I've been to bed as late as 6:45am in the past week so I'm kind of a bit jet-lagged,' Zverev said. 'I feel like I'm playing the Australian Open in New York right now.'

The late finishes have sparked debate about fairness and player welfare. Matt Little, Andy Murray's former strength and conditioning coach, noted that 'the advantage of the opponent who has played earlier in the day will be big.' Martina Navratilova called the situation 'not OK and not normal.' While some argue the late-night matches are part of the US Open's identity as a representation of New York, the volume of early-morning matches has increased this fortnight, with three matches finishing after 2 a.m. in the first seven nights and Shelton's win setting a record for the latest finish in US Open history. Solutions such as starting night sessions earlier or reducing men's matches to best-of-three sets have been proposed, but Tiafoe defended the traditional five-set format, saying 'that's the history of the game.'
