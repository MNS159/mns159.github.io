Major Novartis shareholder calls for board shake-up after drug trial setbacks

Health & Science desk · anchored on Reuters

A major Novartis shareholder has called for a shake-up of the drugmaker's board to improve corporate governance after its shares suffered a record fall this week following back-to-back clinical trial setbacks. David Samra, managing director at Artisan Partners and founding partner of International Value Group, said successive chairmen had failed Novartis on acquisitions and that the company needed to change how it oversees deals, according to Reuters.

Novartis reported just before Labor Day that pelacarsen, a cardiac therapy designed to reduce rates of heart attack or stroke by lowering lipoprotein a, or Lp(a), failed in a widely watched, seven-year clinical trial. The company also disclosed that a muscle-wasting disorder drug acquired through its $12 billion takeover of Avidity failed a late-stage study.

Pelacarsen was the first attempt at a new type of cardiovascular treatment, targeting Lp(a), a type of lipid that, at elevated concentrations, increases the likelihood of severe heart problems. An estimated 20% of people have abnormally high levels of Lp(a). Multiple other drug companies are also pursuing their own Lp(a) medications, with billions of dollars invested in the field.

Samra's criticism centered on how Novartis oversees acquisitions, arguing that successive chairmen had failed the company on deal-making. His call for a board shake-up signals investor dissatisfaction with governance at Novartis.

Beyond Novartis, the twin failures could have a chilling effect across the industry, according to STAT, which wrote that the results would likely “pop a hole in the balloon” of the field. The outcomes raise questions about the future of Lp(a) drug development and the broader search for new heart medicines.
