Oracle shares rise 4% after earnings beat as cloud infrastructure revenue more than doubles

Financial & Markets desk

Oracle Corp. reported fiscal first-quarter results that topped Wall Street expectations, driven by cloud infrastructure revenue more than doubling and strong AI-related contract growth, sending shares up 4% in extended trading Thursday. Adjusted earnings per share came in at $1.92, exceeding the $1.74 consensus from LSEG, while revenue of $19.35 billion beat the $19.14 billion estimate. Net income rose to $4.68 billion, or $1.56 per share, from $2.93 billion a year earlier. The results appeared to ease investor concerns about the company's heavy spending on data centers to support the artificial-intelligence boom.

Revenue from Oracle's cloud infrastructure unit more than doubled to $7.4 billion, above the $7.09 billion consensus from StreetAccount, and total cloud revenue (infrastructure plus applications) soared 62% to $11.61 billion. The company's remaining performance obligations (RPO), a measure of future contracted revenue, jumped to a record $664 billion, up $209 billion year over year. "We closed more than $30 billion of additional AI contracts in Q1 without requiring additional capital from Oracle," Chief Executive Clay Magouyrk said on a conference call. The company also delivered more than 300,000 graphics processing units to AI cloud customers during the quarter, nearly triple the capacity delivered in the prior quarter.

The rapid expansion came with a sharp increase in capital spending: first-quarter capital expenditures surged to $28.5 billion from $8.5 billion a year earlier, and free cash flow turned negative $5.4 billion, compared with negative $362 million a year ago. Oracle's total debt stood at $125 billion. The company raised $20 billion during the quarter through an at-the-market equity offering to help fund its data center buildout. Analysts had been watching for any signs of delays in that buildout, particularly after a Bloomberg report that a natural gas pipeline for a New Mexico data center was running behind schedule. "Nothing that we know today would lead us to believe that New Mexico or any of our other sites are delayed relative to the schedules that we included, for example, in our fiscal '27 outlook," Chief Financial Officer Hilary Maxson said in a briefing with reporters. Magouyrk added that Oracle is working to acquire an air permit in the state. Capital spending guidance for the full year remains unchanged, Maxson said.

For the fiscal second quarter, Oracle forecast adjusted earnings per share of $1.85 to $1.93 and revenue growth of 30% to 34%. Analysts surveyed by LSEG had expected $1.89 in adjusted earnings per share and revenue of $21.20 billion, which would represent 32% growth. For the full 2027 fiscal year, the company now projects at least $90 billion in revenue and adjusted earnings per share of $8.10, both slightly ahead of consensus estimates. "Several of Oracle’s bear cases were addressed head on" in the latest report, according to a MarketWatch analyst.

Oracle's legacy software business continued to contract, with software revenue falling 3% to $5.55 billion, slightly below expectations. The company attributed the decline to customers migrating from on-premises licenses to the cloud. During the quarter, Oracle introduced a new AI data platform that automates the creation of enterprise ontologies, and a 100% agentic AI health-care management system for hospitals and clinics. The board declared a quarterly dividend of $0.50 per share. Oracle shares have fallen 22% this year through Thursday's close, compared with an 11% gain in the S&P 500.
