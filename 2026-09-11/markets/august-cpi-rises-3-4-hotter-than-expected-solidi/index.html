August CPI rises 3.4%, core inflation tops forecasts, solidifying case for Fed rate hike

Financial & Markets desk

The consumer price index rose a seasonally adjusted 0.4% in August, putting the 12-month inflation rate at 3.4%, the Bureau of Labor Statistics reported Friday. The annual figure matched July's pace and came in above the 3.3% expected by economists polled by FactSet, though it was in line with the Dow Jones consensus. Stripping out volatile food and energy prices, core CPI rose 0.3% for the month, a tenth of a percentage point above forecasts, while the core annual rate came in at 2.4%. The report is the final major inflation reading before the Federal Reserve's policy meeting next week, and traders responded by ramping up bets on a quarter-point rate hike to about 90%, according to the CME Group's FedWatch tool.

Energy prices propelled the monthly increase, with gasoline jumping 3.9% and accounting for more than one-third of the overall gain. The energy index rose 2.1% for the month and was up 16.3% from a year ago amid escalating tensions in the Middle East. Food prices edged 0.1% higher, as food at home costs were flat and the food index accelerated 2.7% annually. Shelter costs climbed 0.3% after moderating over the prior two months, while transportation services rose 0.5%. Used cars and trucks increased 0.4% and new vehicle prices rose 0.3%. Airline fares rose 2.7% and communications prices increased 2.3%, while apparel was broadly flat and vehicle insurance and medical care declined.

The data locks in market expectations that the Federal Open Market Committee will raise its benchmark interest rate by a quarter percentage point when it concludes its meeting Wednesday. Before Friday's release, traders saw about a 70% probability of a hike; that jumped to about 90% after the CPI. The fed funds rate, a benchmark for multiple consumer loans, is currently pegged in a range of 3.5%-3.75%, where it has been for all of 2026. Fed Chair Kevin Warsh has expressed a commitment to getting inflation back down to the central bank's 2% target and recently said that if the numbers don't improve, "we have work to do." "Chair Warsh and others signaled that interest rates can remain on hold only if disinflation continues and today's August report did not deliver that," said Kathy Bostjancic, chief economist at Nationwide. Nationwide now expects a quarter-point hike next week.

The Fed's preferred inflation gauge, the Commerce Department's personal consumption expenditures price index, showed core inflation at 3.3% in July and headline at 3.7%. Bank of America senior U.S. economist Stephen Juneau estimated that, accounting for Thursday's producer price index report, core PCE is tracking at a 0.26% monthly rate, which would round up to 0.3%. "This could move significantly tomorrow after CPI, but if we are correct, it should greenlight a hike at next week's Fed meeting," Juneau said. The PPI rose 0.4% in August, pushing the annual rate to 5.4%, slightly above forecasts. At the same time, U.S. crude oil climbed above $100 a barrel on Middle East hostilities, and the European Central Bank raised rates by a quarter point on worries that the Iran war would deepen inflation.

"America still has an inflation problem," wrote Heather Long, chief economist at Navy Federal Credit Union. "The upside surprise to core CPI in August means the Fed looks set to hike next week," said Stephen Brown, chief North America economist at Capital Economics. Some economists cautioned that even a soft CPI reading would not reflect the full inflation picture. "Those who just look at consumer prices for their inflation information and interest rate predictions are not looking at the complete picture, and today's PPI is evidence still of an inflation problem throughout the supply chain," said Peter Boockvar, chief investment officer at OnePoint BFG Wealth Partners. Separately, the BLS reported that real average hourly earnings for U.S. workers decreased by 0.1% from July to August and were down 0.3% from a year earlier. Stock futures were little changed Friday, with S&P 500 futures up 0.6% and Nasdaq 100 futures up 0.8%, while bond yields had already surged earlier in the week. Traders also lifted the probability of another rate increase in December to close to 60%.
