Israel destroys Hezbollah tunnel network in southern Lebanon, triggering 4.1-magnitude tremor

International & World desk

The Israeli military said it destroyed a large underground Hezbollah complex beneath the Ali Taher Ridge in southern Lebanon, using more than 1,000 tonnes of explosives to demolish two tunnels spanning over 2km. The blast registered as a magnitude-4.1 seismic event on the US Geological Survey, and tremors were felt as far as the coastal city of Saida, 40km north. A resident in Nabatieh, about 6km from the ridge, told the BBC the house shook 'as if it was literally an earthquake,' causing panic and prompting many to flee toward Beirut.

The Israel Defense Forces (IDF) stated the underground infrastructure included a command centre for Hezbollah's Badr unit and contained dozens of rockets, missiles, unmanned aerial vehicles, anti-tank weapons, mines and explosives. The IDF said it was 'strategic infrastructure that was constructed over two decades, funded and planned by the Iranian terror regime,' adding that all terrorists inside were either eliminated or had fled. The operation completed a mission to destroy eight tunnels and establish a security zone, according to the IDF.

Israeli forces captured the Ali Taher Ridge last week after months of fighting with Hezbollah. The broader conflict escalated on 2 March, when Hezbollah launched rockets into Israel following a strike that killed Iran's supreme leader. Israel responded with a bombing campaign across Lebanon and a ground invasion, occupying roughly 5% of Lebanese territory. A ceasefire deal between Iran and the US in June, alongside a separate Lebanon-Israel framework, reduced fighting, but Israeli strikes and demolitions in southern Lebanon have continued. Under the framework, Hezbollah is expected to disarm, Israeli forces to withdraw gradually, and the Lebanese army to deploy across the area, though Hezbollah has rejected direct negotiations.

At least 4,362 people have been killed in Lebanon during the conflict, including 395 women and 259 children, according to the health ministry, whose figures do not differentiate between combatants and civilians. Israeli authorities report 40 Israeli soldiers and four civilians killed. There was no immediate public response from Hezbollah or the Lebanese government. Lebanese media reported fresh Israeli strikes elsewhere in the south on Thursday, and the Lebanese army has increased deployments in areas under its control to reassure residents.
