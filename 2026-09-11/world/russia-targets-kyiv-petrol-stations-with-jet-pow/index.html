Ukraine War

Russia strikes Kyiv petrol stations with jet-powered drones; Poland warns of intensified provocations

International & World desk

Russia launched 149 drones into Ukraine overnight, including jet-powered Shahed strike drones, hitting petrol stations in the capital Kyiv for the first time. Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk warned that Russia is likely to intensify provocations targeting border crossings with European countries, as NATO aircraft were deployed in Polish airspace in a preventive response.

Ukraine’s air force said it downed 128 of the drones, with impacts reported across 13 locations. In Kyiv, Mayor Vitali Klitschko reported three people injured when a drone struck a gas station in the Holosiivskyi district on Thursday, and on Friday two more petrol stations operated by state-owned Ukrnafta were hit in the Pochaina and Desnianskyi districts. Witnesses described a double-tap attack at one facility. In the northeastern city of Sumy, at least two people were killed and 23 injured in a strike on a shopping mall.

Russia’s Defense Ministry said its overnight strikes targeted Mykolaiv and Chornomorsk, as well as two dry cargo ships heading to the Ukrainian Black Sea port of Odesa. The ministry also claimed its forces intercepted 448 Ukrainian drones overnight across 13 Russian regions, occupied Crimea, the Black Sea, and the Caspian Sea. Russia’s federal air transport agency imposed temporary flight restrictions at multiple airports, including Moscow’s Vnukovo and Domodedovo.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Ukrainian forces struck eight Russian infrastructure targets supporting the war effort over the previous 24 hours, including a refinery in the Yamalo-Nenets region and a maritime trading port in Dagestan, as well as unspecified targets in Moscow, Voronezh, Astrakhan, and Bryansk. A Ukrainian missile strike caused damage in southern Volgograd, and a drone attack caused a fire at a warehouse of Russian e-commerce company Ozon in Saratov.

The Polish Armed Forces Operational Command elevated readiness and deployed military aviation assets for about two hours overnight as a preventive measure against Russian strikes on western Ukraine. Prime Minister Tusk said a direct threat to a border crossing with Ukraine was averted through cooperation with Polish and Ukrainian services. He added that he had received a precise report from the CIA regarding possible events in the coming months and expects similar actions targeting border crossings with Ukraine in other countries. Poland also suspended flights at Lublin and Rzeszów airports during preventive operations.

According to western officials cited by Reuters, NATO forces recently chased away Russian ships in Arctic waters near the Svalbard archipelago that were training to attack undersea cables with a secret sabotage weapon. British and Norwegian officials took their findings to Moscow. Separately, British man Joshua Cammidge was charged in London as an alleged Russian agent in a plot to sabotage a drone factory in England.

Other recent attacks include a Russian strike on a shopping centre in Pavlohrad that killed at least five and injured 76, including children, and a strike on a Bunge sunflower oil factory in Dnipro that killed two people. Ukraine’s State Emergency Service reported impacts on a residential building in Kyiv and attacks in the Dnipropetrovsk region where at least eight were injured. The drone strikes on petrol stations mark an escalation in attacks on civilian infrastructure in the capital as Ukraine faces a difficult winter.
