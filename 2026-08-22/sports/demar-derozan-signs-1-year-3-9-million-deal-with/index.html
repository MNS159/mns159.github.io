DeMar DeRozan signs 1-year, $3.9 million deal with Denver Nuggets

Sports desk

Veteran forward DeMar DeRozan agreed to a one-year, $3.9 million contract with the Denver Nuggets on Friday, according to league sources. The deal, which is for the NBA minimum, brings the six-time All-Star and three-time All-NBA selection to Denver for his 18th season.

DeRozan, 37, spent the past two seasons with the Sacramento Kings. He had one year and $25.74 million remaining on a three-year deal signed in 2024, but the final season was only partially guaranteed. The Kings waived DeRozan on July 6 from a contract guaranteed for $10 million, saving the franchise about $15 million and making him a free agent. The Detroit Pistons were also in the mix for DeRozan, according to a league source.

Last season, DeRozan started all 77 games for the Kings, averaging 18.4 points per game — his first time in 13 years below 20.0 points — while shooting 49.7 percent from the field and 86.8 percent from the free-throw line. He averaged 4.1 assists against just 1.2 turnovers per game, one of the best assist-to-turnover ratios among playmaking wings, and added 80 steals. DeRozan remains an elite midrange shooter, leading the league with 3.0 non-paint two-point field goals per game at a 47.6 percent clip. However, he is a career 30.2 percent three-point shooter and has not grown more comfortable from long range. He averaged only 2.9 rebounds per game, his fewest since his rookie season.

The move comes a day after the Nuggets completed a multi-team trade that sent restricted free-agent forward Peyton Watson to the Cleveland Cavaliers in a sign-and-trade. Watson shot 41.1 percent on three-pointers last season and was assisted on 65.2 percent of his two-point baskets, contrasting sharply with DeRozan, who self-created more than 61 percent of his two-point baskets with the Kings. The Nuggets, coming off a first-round playoff exit, will hope that DeRozan, Cameron Johnson and Aaron Gordon can form a strong wing rotation alongside center Nikola Jokić and guard Jamal Murray.

DeRozan's fit with Denver's offense presents questions. The Nuggets' system relies heavily on Jokić's creation and perimeter spacing from wings, an area where DeRozan's three-point shooting is a weakness. The optimistic view is that DeRozan can help lead the bench unit, a long-standing Achilles' heel for Denver during Jokić's tenure. When Jokić is off the floor, DeRozan's ability to create tough shots becomes more valuable. How coach David Adelman deploys DeRozan in the rotation and how well DeRozan adapts to playing alongside Jokić will determine the level of success Denver can achieve.

DeRozan ranks 16th on the NBA's all-time scoring list with 26,711 points. He was selected ninth overall by the Toronto Raptors in the 2009 draft out of USC and has also played for the San Antonio Spurs and Chicago Bulls. Since the Raptors traded him to the Spurs eight years ago for Kawhi Leonard, DeRozan has played in only 12 playoff games, missing all 10 of his three-point attempts in those contests, and has not been part of a postseason series win since 2018. Nuggets fans have expressed concern about the franchise's roster construction during Jokić's prime years, and DeRozan will bear some of the weight of expectations to help keep Denver in contention in the Western Conference.
