Klay Thompson agrees to 2-year, $13M deal with Heat after Mavericks buyout

Sports desk

Klay Thompson is expected to sign a two-year, $13 million contract with the Miami Heat after reaching a buyout agreement with the Dallas Mavericks, according to multiple reports. The deal includes a player option for the second year. Thompson, 36, will join the Heat once he clears waivers, providing perimeter shooting around new-look Miami stars Giannis Antetokounmpo and Bam Adebayo.

The Mavericks announced the buyout on Friday, releasing Thompson from the final season of his contract, which was scheduled to pay him $17.5 million. Thompson averaged 12.9 points on 40.3/38.7/85.1 shooting splits in 141 games over two seasons with Dallas. The Mavericks are pivoting toward a youth movement led by reigning Rookie of the Year Cooper Flagg and opted to reach a buyout before training camp to allow Thompson to sign with a contender.

"Klay is one of the great players and competitors of his generation, and we are grateful for everything he brought to the Mavericks organization," Mavericks president of basketball operations Masai Ujiri said in a statement. "We have tremendous respect for Klay and all that he has accomplished throughout his career, and we wish him nothing but the best moving forward."

Thompson, a four-time NBA champion and five-time All-Star, spent 13 seasons with the Golden State Warriors before joining the Mavericks via sign-and-trade after the 2023-24 season. He missed back-to-back seasons earlier in his career recovering from ACL and Achilles injuries. Since returning, he has shot 39.3% from three-point range on 8.8 attempts per game over five seasons, though his shooting efficiency has dipped from his peak.

Miami faced hard cap restrictions while building its roster around Antetokounmpo and Adebayo and had hoped to sign LeBron James before he chose the Philadelphia 76ers. The Heat then pivoted to veteran reinforcements and landed Thompson to bolster their pursuit of a fourth title. Thompson will join Andrew Wiggins, Nikola Jovic, Tim Hardaway Jr. and Dru Smith in Miami's wing rotation, giving coach Erik Spoelstra another option for lineups around the All-Star frontcourt.

The Mavericks, who went 26-56 in the 2025-26 season, parted ways with coach Jason Kidd in what the franchise described as a mutual decision. Dallas hired Dusty May in June after he led Michigan to the NCAA Division I men's national championship in April.
