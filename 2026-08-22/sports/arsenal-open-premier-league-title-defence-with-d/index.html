Arsenal open Premier League title defence with dominant 3-0 win over Coventry City

Sports desk

Arsenal began their Premier League title defence with a comfortable 3-0 victory over newly promoted Coventry City at the Emirates Stadium on Friday. Goals from Kai Havertz, Bukayo Saka and Martin Odegaard secured the win, with Arsenal leading 2-0 by half-time. The Gunners became champions last season for the first time in 22 years, and manager Mikel Arteta marked the occasion by becoming the fifth manager to reach 150 Premier League victories in fewer than 250 games, after Pep Guardiola, Jose Mourinho, Jurgen Klopp and Sir Alex Ferguson.

Captain Martin Odegaard set the tone before kick-off, writing in his programme notes that the team's mentality is not to defend the title but to attack it. "We don't feel we have to defend anything," he told Match of the Day. "We want to do it again and that's the mindset. Last year is finished now. We want to do it again and we want to attack it and fight for it again." The crowd serenaded the team with "champions of England" throughout the match, reflecting a new swagger around the club.

Arsenal's attacking fluency was boosted by the rare sight of Havertz, Saka and Odegaard all starting together in the Premier League — something that happened only once last season due to injuries. Odegaard, who also scored in the Community Shield win over Manchester City, already has more goals this season than he managed in 36 league appearances last term. Summer signing Christos Tzolis made his Premier League debut on the left wing after arriving from Club Brugge for £34m, having registered 17 goals and 23 assists in Belgium last season. His cross was deflected into Saka's path for Arsenal's second goal, following two assists in the Community Shield. Odegaard praised Tzolis as "amazing" and "so lively", while Arteta called his performances "phenomenal" and praised his directness and variety of actions in the final third.

Arteta acknowledged the improved atmosphere inside the stadium but cautioned that tougher tests lie ahead. "Let's see when we are 0-0 and when we are maybe losing a game, see how we react," he told BBC Sport. "Today was the start of the game, and the two goals were obviously a good platform for everybody to feel this way. But I think we all learned in the past as well that one really helps the team. And certainly in this kind of atmosphere in the stadium, it's just a joy to witness and to play football." Arsenal did not allow a shot on target until the 86th minute, when Coventry's Jack Rudoni tested goalkeeper David Raya.

The win came despite injuries to Jurrien Timber and William Saliba. According to CBS Sports, Arsenal are expected to further strengthen their squad with the arrivals of Bruno Guimaraes and Ezri Konsa in the coming days. The Gunners face Aston Villa next week in what is anticipated to be a much sterner examination of their title credentials. For now, Arteta's side have made a powerful statement that they intend to attack the season with the same swagger that carried them to the crown.
