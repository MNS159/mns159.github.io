Four-time Pro Bowl running back Nick Chubb retires from NFL after eight seasons

Sports desk

Four-time Pro Bowl running back Nick Chubb announced his retirement from the NFL on Friday via Instagram, ending an eight-year career that included four consecutive 1,000-yard seasons before a devastating knee injury in 2023 altered his trajectory. Chubb, 30, remained a free agent two weeks into the 2026 preseason after spending the 2025 season with the Houston Texans.

"Today's the day I've been avoiding for a while," Chubb wrote. "Twelve-year-old me could never imagine this day, and 30-year-old me is wondering where all the time has gone. I've known for some time now, but I'm finally ready to share that I'm done playing football. I wanted to end my career on a healthy note, along with a winning season, and I was able to do that."

Selected by the Cleveland Browns with the 35th overall pick in the second round of the 2018 NFL Draft out of Georgia, Chubb produced four straight 1,000-yard rushing seasons from 2019 to 2022, earning Pro Bowl honors each year. His best season came in 2022, when he posted career highs of 1,525 rushing yards and 12 touchdowns en route to second-team All-Pro recognition. Over his first five seasons (2018–2022), Chubb amassed 6,341 rushing yards on a 5.2 yards-per-carry average, making him and Hall of Famer Jim Brown the only players in league history with at least 6,000 rushing yards and 5.0 yards per carry in their first five seasons, according to CBS Sports Research.

Chubb's career turned in Week 2 of the 2023 season when he suffered a gruesome left knee injury against the Pittsburgh Steelers, tearing his MCL and damaging his meniscus and medial capsule. He returned in Week 7 of the 2024 season but averaged just 3.3 yards per carry before suffering a season-ending foot fracture in Week 15 against the Kansas City Chiefs. Chubb signed a one-year deal with the Texans in 2025, appearing in 15 games and rushing for 506 yards and three touchdowns on 122 carries (4.1 yards per carry).

"In the NFL, I wanted to lead the league in rushing, win MVP and win a Super Bowl for the city of Cleveland," Chubb said. "I wasn't able to accomplish any of those things. … What's most frustrating but also gives me peace is that I know for a fact I gave it my all."

Chubb's four-year rookie contract was worth $7,383,094, and in 2021 he signed a three-year, $36 million extension with the Browns. He was testing free agency for the first time when he joined the Texans in 2025. NFLTradeRumors.co listed him among its Top 100 Available 2026 Free Agents before his retirement announcement.
