Apple lays off staff from Vision Pro and Siri teams

Tech / AI desk

Apple is cutting more than 200 jobs, with roughly 100 positions eliminated from the team behind the Vision Pro headset and another 100 from the Siri team and the Intelligent Systems Experience group, which focuses on integrating AI into devices, according to a Bloomberg report confirmed by the company. The cuts include the "largely shutting down" of a Vision Pro gaming team and a reduction in the size of the team that produces immersive content for the headset, Bloomberg reported.

In a statement to Bloomberg, Apple said the changes are intended "to evolve our business to deliver the best experiences for our users" and that it will create new roles as part of the restructuring, though the move will also "impact a limited number of existing roles." Apple did not immediately respond to a request for comment from The Verge.

The Vision Pro, which launched in early 2024 as Apple's entry into spatial computing, carries a $3,499 price tag and has not appeared to be a major commercial success. Apple is reportedly working on a slimmer, lighter successor to the current model, which was updated last year with an M5 chip, but the company has suspended work with Samsung Display on a display for an entry-level XR device. Following the success of Meta's smart glasses, Apple is planning to release its own smart glasses, with a target announcement at WWDC in 2027 and a launch by the end of that year, according to Bloomberg.

On the software side, Apple debuted a revamped, AI-powered version of Siri at this year's WWDC, which is set to become available to the public in beta later in the year. The layoffs come as Apple navigates a bumpy transition period, including efforts to strengthen its position in AI. A memory shortage spurred by the AI buildout has driven up production costs for its flagship products, contributing to recent price increases on Macs and iPads, as well as the introduction of a leasing plan for major hardware to attract a broader customer base.

Separately, Apple is currently suing OpenAI, accusing the company of trade theft. The layoffs and restructuring are part of Apple's broader adjustments to focus on newer AI initiatives and upcoming devices, according to sources cited by Bloomberg.
