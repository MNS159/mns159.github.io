TikTok to pay $400 million to settle US child privacy lawsuit

Tech / AI desk

TikTok and its parent company, ByteDance, have agreed to pay $400 million to settle a U.S. Department of Justice (DOJ) lawsuit alleging that the social media platform violated federal children's privacy laws. The DOJ announced the settlement on Friday, resolving a case first filed in 2024 under the Biden administration. The settlement is one of the largest recoveries ever obtained in a case involving the Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA).

Under the terms of the agreement, TikTok will pay $300 million immediately and an additional $100 million upon the entry of an order vacating a prior consent decree entered against TikTok's predecessor, Musical.ly. The DOJ alleged that TikTok allowed millions of children under the age of 13 to use the platform and collected their personal information without notifying parents or obtaining the required parental consent. The lawsuit also claimed that TikTok did not delete accounts when parents requested removal and that the company maintained and used children's data, including for targeted advertising, even after employees raised concerns about underage users. Additionally, the DOJ alleged that TikTok changed aspects of its registration policies in ways that made it more difficult to determine whether users were old enough to join the service.

The settlement includes measures intended to strengthen protections for young users, including stronger age-related controls, additional safeguards for children, and enhanced parental oversight of children's activity and personal information. The DOJ noted that TikTok has undergone significant changes to its ownership, management, compliance functions, and privacy practices since the lawsuit was filed, and that it has implemented extensive measures to strengthen safeguards for younger users. The agreement does not require TikTok or ByteDance to admit wrongdoing.

The 2024 case alleged that TikTok allowed large numbers of children to remain on the platform for years, despite already facing federal action over children's privacy in 2019. In that earlier case, the company agreed to pay $5.7 million to settle allegations that its predecessor, Musical.ly, had violated COPPA, and committed to taking steps to prevent children under 13 from creating accounts. The new settlement comes as TikTok faces additional scrutiny over user safety. Days before the settlement, Bloomberg reported that TikTok had intentionally disabled an algorithmic safeguard for roughly 10% of U.S. users as part of an experiment; the safeguard was designed to reduce the possibility that users would be overwhelmed by harmful content. The report drew criticism from Republican senator Marsha Blackburn of Tennessee and Democratic senator Richard Blumenthal of Connecticut, who sent a letter to TikTok CEO Shou Chew and Adam Presser, the chief executive of the company's U.S. business, questioning the decision.

TikTok did not immediately reply to a request for comment. In January, a deal to put TikTok under a new joint ownership structure in the U.S. was finalized.
