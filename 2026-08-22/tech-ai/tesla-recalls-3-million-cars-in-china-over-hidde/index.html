Tesla Recalls Nearly 3 Million Vehicles in China Over Hidden Emergency Door Releases, Driver Monitoring

Tech / AI desk

Tesla is recalling nearly 3 million vehicles in China as part of a nationwide push by regulators to address the dangers of hidden emergency door releases. The recall covers approximately 2.98 million Model 3 and Model Y electric vehicles, which will receive warning labels and a software update that automatically lowers all windows in the event of a collision. A second, overlapping recall affecting about 2.74 million vehicles will update the driver monitoring system to use the interior camera to track driver gaze rather than relying solely on a steering wheel torque sensor.

Chinese regulators have grown stricter about electronically controlled car doors after a series of fatal crashes where occupants were trapped in burning vehicles. Starting next year, new rules will mandate specific dimensions for exterior door handles on all newly approved vehicles, but existing models like the Model 3 and Y are grandfathered in until the end of 2028. The current recall requires Tesla to add warning labels to car interiors—a task that requires physical access—and push a software update for post-collision window lowering.

The driver monitoring update is aimed at ensuring drivers remain engaged. Tesla’s current system uses only a torque sensor on the steering wheel, but the new software will leverage the vehicle’s interior camera to track the driver’s gaze. Electrek has previously reported that some Chinese Tesla owners defeated the cabin monitoring system by mounting a doll’s head in front of the camera.

Tesla is one of 11 automakers issuing related recalls in China. Others include Xiaomi, Xpeng, and Geely brands Zeekr and Lynk & Co. Nine manufacturers, including Tesla, will install warning labels on their vehicles. All 11 companies will also push software updates as part of the recall. The problem stems from the industry’s shift to electronic door latches, which can fail after a crash. Manual releases are often tucked away or hidden behind panels. Rivian faced similar criticism for its R1 vehicles’ manual release, which required removing a panel to pull a cord; the company moved the release to a more accessible spot on the newer R2. In 2025, Ford briefly halted sales of the Mustang Mach-E and issued a recall over electronic latch malfunctions after power loss.

The U.S. National Highway Traffic Safety Administration opened an investigation into Tesla’s door-latching system last year following Bloomberg reporting, and is considering new safety requirements. One lawmaker has proposed legislation requiring manual releases to be “intuitive to use and readily accessible for the occupant.” Tesla has said it is working on redesigning its door handles moving forward.
