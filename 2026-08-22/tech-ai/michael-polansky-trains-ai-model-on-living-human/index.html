Outer Biosciences keeps human skin alive for weeks, trains AI on living tissue

Tech / AI desk

Michael Polansky, co-founder of Outer Biosciences and partner of Lady Gaga, is training an artificial intelligence model on human skin that remains alive outside the body for up to a month. The startup, founded in 2022, has raised roughly $23 million from backers including Wing Ventures and Initialized, and employs 19 people. The living tissue, sourced from discarded plastic surgery through nonprofit and commercial biobanks, is fed nutrients and cleared of waste by a proprietary support system, maintaining its original structure and molecular programs for weeks — far beyond the industry norm of a few days.

Outer Biosciences uses the prolonged tissue viability to run controlled experiments, such as inducing UVB damage and tracking the biological response over weeks. The data from these experiments is fed into a closed-loop AI model that predicts which untested chemicals are likely to benefit specific skin functions. Those predictions are then tested on the living tissue, and the results — whether correct or not — improve the model. The company now generates a new candidate ingredient roughly every six weeks, with six active leads and several dozen additional hits. By contrast, the universe of formally recognized skin-active ingredients is small: only about 120 to 130 FDA-approved active ingredients for over-the-counter skin drugs, and roughly 200 when including cosmetic ingredients backed by research.

Polansky’s path to this work began in life sciences “accidentally,” he says, through more than a decade alongside Sean Parker in cancer immunotherapy. After graduating from Harvard in applied mathematics and computer science, Polansky worked at Bridgewater Associates, then joined Founders Fund as a principal. When Parker left the fund, Polansky ran Parker’s family office, helping stand up the Parker Institute for Cancer Immunotherapy, where he remains executive director. During the pandemic, his life shifted after meeting Stefani Germanotta (Lady Gaga) in late 2019 at a Parker birthday party, orchestrated by her mother. The two now collaborate: Gaga sits on Outer Biosciences’ board, and her cosmetics brand Haus Labs shares scientific advisory board members with the startup.

Polansky says he chose to speak publicly now because the data his team has gathered gives him confidence. “Trying to do this in private is hard,” he said. “We kind of want to start working in public now.” Outer Biosciences plans to license or sell its finished ingredients to beauty or pharmaceutical companies rather than build its own consumer brand. Four of its six current leads appear likely to reach commercialization. The company also generates revenue from collaborative research partnerships, including a pharmaceutical partner studying why certain cancer drugs cause severe skin rashes, and consumer beauty brands testing the company’s data. Competitors are emerging: Vivodyne, a Philadelphia-based company building lab-grown human organ tissue, recently announced nearly $80 million raised to date.
