HHS document reveals Kennedy's vaccine plans amid broader public health reforms

Health & Science desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

WASHINGTON — President Trump and Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. are pressing ahead with efforts to change how children in the U.S. receive vaccinations, according to a request for information posted Friday by the Department of Health and Human Services. The document offers new clues to the administration's vision for vaccine policy and signals that pursuing changes is a priority, though it does not implement any changes itself. Instead, it asks the public for feedback on a wide variety of ideas and gives 30 days for comment.

Among the questions the HHS document asks the public to consider is whether there should be new categories added to the current vaccines framework. That framework now includes universal recommendations, recommendations for certain risk-based groups, and recommendations based on shared clinical decision-making. The request for information does not specify which new categories might be proposed, but it solicits input on potential modifications to the system.

The vaccine policy move is part of a broader set of public health reforms. In a separate HHS announcement titled "18 Months of Action: President Trump, Secretary Kennedy Deliver Historic Reforms to Make America Healthy Again," the department touted a range of initiatives. The Cleveland.com outlet characterized Kennedy's efforts as "dismantling public health" and said Trump is "letting it happen," though the HHS release framed the reforms as making America healthier.

The request for information gives the public 30 days to submit comments. No further details on the timeline for any potential policy changes have been released. The document does not alter current vaccination requirements or recommendations, but it opens the door to possible restructuring of how vaccines are categorized and recommended in the U.S.
