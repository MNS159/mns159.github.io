Congress

Lawmakers urge FDA to increase scrutiny of Chinese trial data after gene therapy deaths

Health & Science desk

Two Republican lawmakers are calling on the U.S. Food and Drug Administration to tighten oversight of clinical trial data from China, citing three recent deaths in gene therapy studies conducted there. In a letter to Acting FDA Commissioner Kyle Diamantas, Reps. John Moolenaar (R-MI) and Ben Cline (R-VA) urged the agency to reject Chinese clinical data unless the trial site has undergone a recent FDA audit and to conduct a broad review of products already approved in the U.S. based on Chinese results. The letter, obtained by Endpoints News, warns that “accepting Chinese clinical data poses real risks for patients.”

Public Health Policy

HHS document reveals Kennedy's vaccine plans amid broader public health reforms

Health & Science desk

WASHINGTON — President Trump and Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. are pressing ahead with efforts to change how children in the U.S. receive vaccinations, according to a request for information posted Friday by the Department of Health and Human Services. The document offers new clues to the administration's vision for vaccine policy and signals that pursuing changes is a priority, though it does not implement any changes itself. Instead, it asks the public for feedback on a wide variety of ideas and gives 30 days for comment.

FDA Approvals

Ultragenyx receives accelerated FDA approval for metabolic gene therapy Genglycos

Health & Science desk

Rare disease specialist Ultragenyx has received accelerated approval from the U.S. Food and Drug Administration for Genglycos, a one-time gene therapy for glycogen storage disease type Ia (GSDIa), a rare metabolic condition. The approval, the fifth for the California-based biotech, covers children aged 8 years and older and adults. GSDIa occurs in approximately 1 in 125,000 births in the United States, with an estimated 1,500 to 2,500 people currently living with the condition, though the precise number is unknown.
