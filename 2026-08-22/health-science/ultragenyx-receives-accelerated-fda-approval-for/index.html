Ultragenyx receives accelerated FDA approval for metabolic gene therapy Genglycos

Health & Science desk

Rare disease specialist Ultragenyx has received accelerated approval from the U.S. Food and Drug Administration for Genglycos, a one-time gene therapy for glycogen storage disease type Ia (GSDIa), a rare metabolic condition. The approval, the fifth for the California-based biotech, covers children aged 8 years and older and adults. GSDIa occurs in approximately 1 in 125,000 births in the United States, with an estimated 1,500 to 2,500 people currently living with the condition, though the precise number is unknown.

Genglycos is an adeno-associated viral vector (AAV) gene therapy designed to deliver a functional G6PC gene to liver cells. The therapy aims to restore production of glucose-6-phosphatase, enabling more normal glucose release from glycogen stores and reducing reliance on the current standard of care: a strictly controlled diet with frequent doses of uncooked cornstarch. The approval was based on a 48-week Phase III trial involving 44 participants, which showed a statistically significant mean reduction of 31% in daily cornstarch requirements compared with placebo. The study demonstrated a meaningful reduction in treatment burden but did not establish improved long-term clinical outcomes such as fewer hypoglycemic events.

In the trial, seven serious adverse reactions occurred: two anaphylaxis or infusion reactions, two adrenal-gland events, two cases of elevated lactate, and one hypoglycemia event. As with many AAV therapies, Genglycos carries a risk of elevated liver enzymes, and some adverse events were linked to complications from corticosteroids used to manage post-infusion liver inflammation.

Ultragenyx has agreed to conduct confirmatory studies to verify long-term clinical benefit, providing safety and efficacy data from open-label treatment of 50 patients and 20 control patients over two years. A leading GSDIa expert, Dr. David Weinstein, said in a press statement that day-to-day management of the disease requires a relentless regimen of raw cornstarch and strict dietary management, and that any missed cornstarch puts patients at risk of severe hypoglycemia, seizures, and even death. He called the approval a major step forward reflecting almost 30 years of work and scientific progress.

The company did not disclose the treatment price but said it will help eligible patients access Genglycos through its UltraCare program, providing support to navigate treatment access.
