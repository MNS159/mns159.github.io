UK, Canada, Australia condemn Israel's refusal of criminal probe into Gaza aid worker killings as 'shameful'

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts slightly center-left

The United Kingdom, Canada, and Australia have condemned as 'shameful' Israel's decision not to open a criminal investigation into the April 2024 airstrikes that killed seven humanitarian workers from the U.S.-based World Central Kitchen (WCK) in Gaza. The victims included three British nationals, a Pole, an Australian, a Canadian, and a dual U.S.-Canadian citizen. In a joint statement, the three foreign ministries said: 'The victims of this incident and their families deserve justice and accountability, and we will continue to seek answers on their behalf.' The statement noted that the announcement came on World Humanitarian Day, calling it 'particularly egregious.'

The Israel Defense Forces (IDF) said on Wednesday that while there were 'serious failures' in the process that led to the killings, 'the decisions of the commanders did not raise reasonable suspicion of criminal misconduct.' The IDF claimed that an armed guard traveling with the convoy had not been flagged to it beforehand and was falsely identified as a Hamas fighter, and that the convoy deviated from the agreed route. Two officers were removed from their positions at the time and three others reprimanded. Israel's ambassador to Australia, Hillel Newman, defended the decision, stating that 'there was no criminal intention' and therefore 'no criminal responsibility.'

Australia's foreign minister, Penny Wong, said her country was 'outraged' and noted many 'unanswered questions,' including why three strikes occurred minutes apart if the first was a 'catastrophic error.' Australia summoned the Israeli ambassador. Poland's foreign ministry also expressed disappointment and summoned Israel's ambassador in Warsaw. WCK founder José Andrés called the decision 'wrong' and 'painful for all of us,' demanding an independent commission. Relatives of Australian victim Zomi Frankcom said she deserved more than an 'internal, opaque process.' The three countries' statement said the WCK attack was 'one of countless incidents in Gaza where there has been no accountability,' noting that 186 humanitarian workers have been killed in 2025 and that Gaza remains 'the deadliest place to deliver aid.'

The IDF separately announced it would conduct criminal investigations into the killing of five-year-old Palestinian girl Hind Rajab and six relatives in Gaza in 2024, and into the killing of 15 Palestinians, including medics and a UN worker, in March 2025. The IDF had previously denied troops were near the site of the Rajab killing, but media investigations found evidence strongly implicating Israeli tanks. Experts cited by The Guardian noted that criminal misconduct investigations into Israeli troops over the killing of Palestinians very rarely lead to convictions.

The statement came amid a sharp exchange between UK Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband and Israeli Foreign Minister Gideon Saar over Israel's plans to open tenders for about 1,200 settlement homes in the E1 area of the occupied West Bank. Miliband called the tender 'an unacceptable and destructive act,' urging Israel to halt the plans. Saar responded on social media that he rejected 'outright' Miliband's statement, writing: 'The Jewish people have the right to live throughout the Land of Israel.' Israeli settlements in the occupied West Bank are illegal under international law.
