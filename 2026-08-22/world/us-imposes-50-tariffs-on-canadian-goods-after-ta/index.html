US-Canada Trade

US imposes 50% tariffs on $20bn in Canadian goods after talks fail; Canada vows dollar-for-dollar retaliation

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts left

The United States imposed 50% tariffs on approximately $20bn worth of Canadian goods at 12:01 a.m. Eastern Time on Saturday after three days of trade talks in Washington, D.C., collapsed without a deal. Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney immediately announced that Ottawa would retaliate with matching tariffs “dollar for dollar,” escalating the most serious rupture in recent relations between the two longtime allies.

The tariffs, which apply to about 5% of Canada’s annual exports to the U.S., target electronics, industrial machinery, dairy products, hockey sticks, and tongue depressors, adding to existing U.S. duties on Canadian steel, lumber, and autos. U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer blamed Canada for the breakdown, saying it “declined to finalise the trade deal under the terms agreed earlier this week” and that “new demands and walkbacks of other commitments by Canada have upended the careful balance reached in the past days.” Carney countered that “last-minute changes in the US proposed terms were unfair, uneconomic and called into question the reliability of any deal,” and recalled his negotiators. No further talks are planned.

The tariffs stem from President Donald Trump’s accusation that Canada engaged in “discriminatory treatment of American products,” including bans on U.S. alcohol sales in eight of ten provinces and all three territories—a retaliatory measure Canada imposed in early 2025 after Trump’s initial tariff threats. U.S. producers reported an 81% year-on-year drop in Canadian imports of American alcohol. Trump initially set the tariffs to take effect August 19 but granted a three-day pause to allow negotiations. On Friday, he told reporters he expected a deal, but none materialized.

Carney said his government would introduce new measures in the coming days to support affected workers and businesses, and that Canada’s goal was “never a deal at any price or on any deadline.” Ontario Premier Doug Ford voiced support, writing on X: “Team Canada needs to stand together … tariff for tariff, dollar for dollar.” Candace Laing, head of the Canadian Chamber of Commerce, called the tariffs “a body blow to North American competitiveness,” warning they would raise costs for Americans and threaten Canadian customers and small businesses. Trade experts said the 50% duties could price hundreds of Canadian goods out of the U.S. market and strain already vulnerable sectors, potentially causing job losses and store closures.

The political fallout is likely to exceed the economic impact. The two countries traded $880bn in goods and services last year, and many citizens work across the border. Carney, whose firm stance has boosted his popularity in Canada, said “America has changed” and the old relationship will not return. A petition to expel U.S. Ambassador Pete Hoekstra has gathered about 248,000 signatures, accusing him of normalizing Trump’s suggestion of annexing Canada as the 51st state. Leaked audio from a private fundraiser captured Vice-President JD Vance mocking Carney for trying to “out-tough Donald Trump,” a remark unlikely to ease tensions. Manitoba Premier Wab Kinew said, “I think Donald Trump is weak … I think we should fight.”
