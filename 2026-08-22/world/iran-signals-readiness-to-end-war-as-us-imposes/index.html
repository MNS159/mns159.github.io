Iran signals readiness to end war as US imposes new sanctions and Iraqi oil tankers pass through Hormuz

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts slightly center-left

Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian said Friday that it would be an opportune moment to seek to end the war with the United States, according to state media, declaring that Tehran is in a “position of strength and dignity.” His remarks at a medical conference came as the Trump administration announced what President Donald Trump called the “most crushing economic operation ever taken against another country,” and as Iran granted permission for several Iraqi oil tankers to transit the Strait of Hormuz, according to Iran’s state news agency IRNA.

The new US sanctions, announced by Trump on Wednesday and set to be detailed Monday by Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, threaten “tremendous economic consequences” for any country or institution that does business with Iran. Bessent said the measures would “collapse” the Iranian regime and that other nations are “either with us or against us.” In response, Iran’s Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei decried the move as an assertion of extraterritorial sovereignty that would lead to “the complete erosion of sovereignty” and a “return to full-scale classic colonialism.” Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi wrote on X that the sanctions were “bound to fail,” adding, “We have seen this movie before.”

Pezeshkian argued that the war would have to end eventually and that “it is better to end it today, as we are in a position of strength and dignity.” He said the world acknowledges Iran’s victory and pointed to what he described as US attacks on schools, hospitals and infrastructure. He also insisted that the 15-point Memorandum of Understanding signed with the US in June contained no clause indicating capitulation. Meanwhile, Iran’s armed forces chief, Major General Ali Abdollahi, warned of “crushing, punishing and devastating responses” if the US attempts further economic isolation. Tehran has faced US sanctions for decades, and the new measures come after the partial truce agreed in June expired without renewal on Monday.

The Strait of Hormuz remains largely bottled up. Ship-tracking data showed only four commodity vessels sailed through the strait on Thursday, none of them large crude carriers or liquefied natural gas tankers, and thousands of seafarers are stranded on hundreds of ships. However, Iran has allowed several Iraqi oil tankers to pass after requests from Baghdad, IRNA reported Saturday. Iranian parliament speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, who also serves as chief negotiator, said during a visit to Iraq that obtaining special passage for Iraqi tankers was a key request. Ghalibaf also called for intensifying negotiations, saying Washington appears to have concluded it cannot prevail in a direct military confrontation.

The US Navy has maintained a blockade against Iran’s ports, while the USS Abraham Lincoln is heading home after nine months at sea supporting operations against Iran. Trump told reporters Friday that the Iranian side “would love to make a deal, but they’re not ready to make the right deal in my opinion.” China, Iran’s most important trading partner, has not been publicly asked to join the new sanctions, according to Treasury Secretary Bessent, who declined to comment on that question. The economic pressure, Bessent said, would make “large-scale” military operations unnecessary. The mixed messages from both Washington and Tehran leave the path forward uncertain, with Iran’s leadership simultaneously signaling openness to peace and vowing to resist intimidation.
