Ukraine War

Russian double-tap drone strike on Ukrainian shopping mall kills 16; Zelensky vows response

International & World desk · anchored on Reuters

Rescuers in Ukraine worked through the night to search for survivors after a Russian double-tap drone strike on a busy shopping centre in Kryvyi Rih killed 16 people and wounded 130, local officials said. The attack, which President Volodymyr Zelensky condemned as “cynical and despicable,” occurred in two waves: the first drone hit the mall, and a second struck about half an hour later, targeting emergency responders at the scene. Nine people remain missing, including two children, according to regional military head Oleksandr Hanzha. Twenty-three children were among the injured, with 14 of them still hospitalized as of Saturday morning; three children—two boys aged 10 and 16 and an 11-year-old girl—were in serious or extremely serious condition after undergoing surgery, said Oleksandr Vilkul, head of the city’s defence council.

US imposes 50% tariffs on $20bn in Canadian goods after talks fail; Canada vows dollar-for-dollar retaliation

International & World desk

Global Health

Ebola Vaccine Doses Arrive in DRC as Outbreak Death Toll Exceeds 2,500

International & World desk · anchored on Reuters

More than 16,000 doses of the Ervebo Ebola vaccine have arrived in the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC) as the country’s deadliest-ever outbreak continues to spread exponentially, with the death toll surpassing 2,500. The 16,250 doses landed in the capital, Kinshasa, late Friday evening, the first tranche of 70,000 doses that the World Health Organization (WHO) and partners pledged to provide. Additional shipments are expected next week.

Israel-Palestine

UK, Canada, Australia condemn Israel's refusal of criminal probe into Gaza aid worker killings as 'shameful'

International & World desk

The United Kingdom, Canada, and Australia have condemned as 'shameful' Israel's decision not to open a criminal investigation into the April 2024 airstrikes that killed seven humanitarian workers from the U.S.-based World Central Kitchen (WCK) in Gaza. The victims included three British nationals, a Pole, an Australian, a Canadian, and a dual U.S.-Canadian citizen. In a joint statement, the three foreign ministries said: 'The victims of this incident and their families deserve justice and accountability, and we will continue to seek answers on their behalf.' The statement noted that the announcement came on World Humanitarian Day, calling it 'particularly egregious.'
