International & World Ukraine War

Russian double-tap drone strike on Ukrainian shopping mall kills 16; Zelensky vows response

International & World desk · anchored on Reuters

Rescuers in Ukraine worked through the night to search for survivors after a Russian double-tap drone strike on a busy shopping centre in Kryvyi Rih killed 16 people and wounded 130, local officials said. The attack, which President Volodymyr Zelensky condemned as “cynical and despicable,” occurred in two waves: the first drone hit the mall, and a second struck about half an hour later, targeting emergency responders at the scene. Nine people remain missing, including two children, according to regional military head Oleksandr Hanzha. Twenty-three children were among the injured, with 14 of them still hospitalized as of Saturday morning; three children—two boys aged 10 and 16 and an 11-year-old girl—were in serious or extremely serious condition after undergoing surgery, said Oleksandr Vilkul, head of the city’s defence council.

The EU’s foreign policy chief, Kaja Kallas, condemned the attack as “terror by design” and said she was putting forward “the most far-reaching Russia sanctions listings since the start of the war.” Zelensky, speaking in an evening video post, called the strike “an absolute Russian atrocity” and vowed: “We will definitely respond.” He urged Ukraine’s allies to impose “real pressure on the aggressor,” saying, “For peace to be possible, Russia must face real accountability.” Rescue teams from seven other Ukrainian regions were deployed to help search for survivors. Russia’s military has not commented on the strike.

Financial & Markets Treasury & Fed

Bessent's Treasury intervention stirs inflation fears and undercuts Fed credibility

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent's effort to calm the government bond market by accelerating debt buybacks has instead fueled inflation worries, pushing market-based inflation expectations to their highest levels in more than two months and raising questions about the credibility of both Treasury and Federal Reserve policy. The so-called breakeven rate — a measure comparing Treasury yields to inflation-protected securities — rose to 2.34% at the 10-year horizon on Thursday, its highest since June 10, while the five-year breakeven hit the same level, the highest since June 16. Though the measures remain below levels that would signal runaway inflation, the move suggests investors are demanding greater compensation for inflation risk.
