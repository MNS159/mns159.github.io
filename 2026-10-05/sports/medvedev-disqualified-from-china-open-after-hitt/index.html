Medvedev disqualified from China Open after hitting spectator with ball

Sports desk

Daniil Medvedev was disqualified from the China Open semi-final on Monday after striking a spectator in the face with a ball during his match against Novak Djokovic. The third seed trailed 7-5, 5-3 when Djokovic hit a winner past him for 15-15. As the ball ricocheted off the back of the court, Medvedev swiped at it in annoyance, sending it into the air and into the front row of the stand behind him. The fan doubled over holding his face and was still holding his eye as he was helped out of the arena.

After speaking with umpire Mohamed Lahyani and an ATP official, Medvedev was defaulted for a dangerous act, ending the match and sending Djokovic into the final. "Terribly sorry to the spectator, first of all," Medvedev said. "I'm trying to get in touch with him, if he wants it. Hopefully I can apologise in person and see how he feels. I just hope he's OK. That's the most important. I just really care about him right now." Djokovic, who was himself defaulted from the 2020 US Open for a similar incident, expressed sympathy for both the fan and Medvedev, saying, "It's a bit of a shock to be honest. I hope that person is not badly injured. I also feel sorry for Daniil. I know exactly how he feels to be disqualified in a big match. I am sure that Daniil did not want to do this intentionally."

Defaults in tennis are rare and only occur when players violate the sport's code of conduct, which includes ball abuse, physical or verbal abuse, or racquet abuse. Players can normally receive multiple code violations before disqualification, but Medvedev's offense was serious enough to instantly default him. As a result, he loses all points and prize money earned in Beijing—a run that would have seen him return to inside the world's top five. The 30-year-old has a history of on-court outbursts, including a meltdown at last year's US Open when a photographer walked on court, and a fine of almost £31,500 ($42,500) for that behavior. In April, he obliterated a racquet after losing 6-0 6-0 at the Monte Carlo Masters and was again fined for unsportsmanlike conduct. Medvedev told BBC Sport in 2025 that he was trying to work on his on-court behavior, saying, "When I'm on the court I hate to lose; I love to win. My first goal is to win the match, so it brings a lot of adrenaline out and it brings all the energy out. Sometimes it is a good energy. Sometimes it is a bad one."

The default brought an early end to an excellent contest, with the 39-year-old Djokovic having played superbly to outlast an opponent nine years his junior. Djokovic saved 16 of the 18 break points he faced and will face Alex de Minaur in Tuesday's final. Djokovic has a 33-0 record at the China Open, having won the title six times. He has won at least one title a year since 2006, and Tuesday will be his first final since the Australian Open, where he lost to then world number one Carlos Alcaraz in four sets. His 2025 season also included a semi-final run at Wimbledon, a third-round exit at the French Open, and a shock first-round loss at the US Open.

Elsewhere, defending champion Alcaraz overcame fluctuations in form to beat Jaume Munar and reach the Japan Open final. The seven-time Grand Slam champion struggled on serve but gradually upped his level to win 5-7 6-3 6-1. Alcaraz led 5-3 in the first set and served for it before losing 16 consecutive points to squander the opener. He will face Jiri Lehecka in the final after the Czech beat Monaco's Valentin Vacherot 6-4 6-2.
