Brazilian Stocks and Currency Surge After Bolsonaro's First-Round Election Upset

Financial & Markets desk · anchored on Reuters

Brazilian stocks and the currency surged Monday after first-round presidential election results showed candidate Flávio Bolsonaro outperforming pre-election polls and beating incumbent President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva by nearly two percentage points, making Bolsonaro the heavy favorite ahead of the Oct. 25 runoff.

The iShares MSCI Brazil ETF (EWZ) rose more than 12%, Brazil's Bovespa index gained 8%, and U.S.-listed shares of Itaú Unibanco and Banco Bradesco jumped 15% and 19% respectively. On prediction markets, Bolsonaro's odds of winning the presidency soared: on Kalshi his probability rose to over 80% from about 60% before Sunday's vote, while on Polymarket it climbed to 85% from 63%.

Bolsonaro secured more than 47% of the first-round vote, beating Lula's total by roughly two percentage points. Because no candidate received a majority, the two front-runners advance to a runoff. Lula, who is seeking a fourth term, won back the presidency in 2022 by defeating then-incumbent Jair Bolsonaro, Flávio's father. Lula's campaign has tried to tie Flávio Bolsonaro to his father's efforts to contest that 2022 loss and has branded the son as corrupt.

Investors broadly see Bolsonaro as more favorable to markets because he promises greater fiscal discipline. Brazil's deficit-to-GDP ratio stood at nearly 10% in June. The runoff election is scheduled for Oct. 25.
