Spanish PM Sánchez calls early election for 29 November after housing crisis defeats

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts slightly center-left

Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez announced a snap general election for 29 November on Monday, after his minority government suffered two legislative defeats last week on emergency housing decrees. The decision came amid nationwide protests triggered by the televised eviction of an 87-year-old woman from her Madrid home in September, which had intensified public anger over the country's housing affordability crisis.

In a televised address, Sánchez said he needed 'a broader progressive majority' to push through reforms and 'overcome the resistance of the elites.' He acknowledged that his government had 'made mistakes' and that there were still 'a lot of problems to solve, particularly in the areas of inequality, of public services and access to housing.' The two rejected decree laws included measures to suspend evictions of vulnerable tenants until 2030, extend rental leases, increase taxes on tourist apartments, restrict purchases by investment funds, and automatically renew rental contracts—a provision the political right argued would reduce housing supply and raise prices.

Sánchez has led a left-wing coalition of his Socialist party and junior partner Sumar since 2023, but the government lacks a parliamentary majority and relies on support from Catalan and Basque nationalist and pro-independence groups. Spain was not due to hold a general election until summer 2027. The prime minister, in power since June 2018, has faced mounting pressure from corruption scandals involving his wife, his party's headquarters, and a former minister, as well as a migration crisis in the North African enclave of Ceuta over the summer.

The leader of the opposition conservative People's Party, Alberto Núñez Feijóo, welcomed the announcement, saying, 'At last, Spaniards will be able to decide.' He framed the election as a choice between 'decline or change, lies or change, incompetence or change, division or change, corruption or change.' Most opinion polls indicate the People's Party is likely to emerge as the largest party, but it would probably need the support of the hard-right Vox party to form a government.

Sánchez warned Spaniards about what he called the 'extreme-right wave' seen in other countries and argued that Spain should continue to be a global example of 'peace, feminism and prosperity.'

Tens of thousands of people protested in Madrid, Valencia, and Barcelona on Saturday, demanding stronger tenant protections. The eviction of Maricarmen Abascal, 87, who was removed on a stretcher from the apartment she had lived in for more than 70 years after a legal glitch allowed her rent to rise from €500 to €2,650, became a symbol of the crisis. Her lawyer later said she would be able to return home, but the incident fueled widespread anger and increased pressure on the government to act.
