Flávio Bolsonaro wins first round of Brazil presidential election, runoff set for 25 October

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts slightly center-left

Right-wing candidate Flávio Bolsonaro won the first round of Brazil's presidential election on Sunday, securing 47 percent of the vote against incumbent President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva's 45 percent, according to the country's electoral court. Since neither candidate achieved a majority, a runoff election will be held on 25 October. Bolsonaro's result exceeded pre-election expectations, as polls had shown Lula with a slight lead.

Bolsonaro, 45, is the eldest son of former President Jair Bolsonaro, who is serving a 27-year prison sentence for masterminding a failed coup after losing the 2022 election. Flávio Bolsonaro has vowed to grant amnesty to his father and to supporters involved in the 8 January 2023 riots if elected. Lula, 80, is seeking his fourth non-consecutive term, having previously won in 2002, 2006 and 2022, when he beat Jair Bolsonaro by less than 2 percent of the vote. The campaign has been polarizing, with voters prioritizing corruption, crime, the economy and sovereignty.

Beyond the presidency, Bolsonaro's Liberal Party (PL) won the most governorships, Senate seats and federal deputy positions, giving it the largest bloc in Congress. Former first lady Michelle Bolsonaro and another of Jair Bolsonaro's sons, Carlos Bolsonaro, both won Senate seats. The right-wing camp's legislative gains mean that even if Lula wins the runoff, governing will be difficult, analysts said.

Reacting to the result, Bolsonaro said he was "very happy with the real possibility that, three weeks from now, Brazil will give a definitive answer by choosing a modern government." He also said he wanted to "free the Brazilian people." Lula called the outcome "unexpected" and said he had been convinced he would win in the first round. "The game is about to start. We are in extra time. In extra time, we will win over the Brazilian people," Lula said, adding that voters must choose "between strengthening the democratic regime and barbarism."

The election carries implications for Brazil's relationship with the United States. Lula had accused the US of attempting to interfere in the vote, which Washington denied; the president of Brazil's supreme electoral court said no evidence of international interference was found. Bolsonaro successfully lobbied the Trump administration to designate two major Brazilian gangs as terrorist organizations. A win for Bolsonaro would likely see Brazil join the US-led "Shield of Americas" coalition against narco-trafficking. The runoff will determine whether Brazil, Latin America's largest economy, follows the regional trend of electing right-wing presidents aligned with the Trump administration.

Key campaign issues include a corruption scandal involving jailed banker Daniel Vorcaro; Bolsonaro acknowledged asking Vorcaro to fund a biopic about his father after voice recordings emerged. Lula was indirectly affected after police suggested Vorcaro had reached out to a Supreme Court judge seen as an ally. On crime, Bolsonaro has pledged to build maximum-security prisons like those in El Salvador and designate gangs as terrorists. Lula has focused on tackling organized crime finances and improving investigations. On the economy, Bolsonaro promises spending cuts and a debt ceiling, while Lula highlights social welfare programs such as Bolsa Família. Environmental policy differs: Lula tightened protections and reduced deforestation during his previous terms, while deforestation increased under Jair Bolsonaro; Flávio Bolsonaro shares his father's skepticism of human-caused climate change.
