Researcher at Russian plague institute dies of unknown pneumonia; dozens quarantined

Health & Science desk

A 28-year-old female laboratory technician at the Irkutsk Research Anti-Plague Institute of Siberia and the Far East in Russia has died after being diagnosed with pneumonia of “unknown” origin, according to the Russian infectious disease agency Rospotrebnadzor. The researcher, named in local media as Darya Shipilova, died last week in a hospital in Shelekhov, a town just outside Irkutsk. The hospital has been placed under quarantine, no longer admitting or discharging patients, and its laboratory has halted routine tests, though its outpatient clinic continues operating as usual, said Mayor Maxim Modin.

Rospotrebnadzor told CNN that “no micro-organisms associated with the employee’s professional duties were detected in samples taken from the patient” and that the “epidemiological situation” in the region was stable. The agency also said it did not uncover any incidents at the institute “involving pathogenic microorganisms.” Two people who were in contact with the woman tested positive for a common cold virus and two others for COVID-19, while all other contacts tested negative, the agency added. Independent local news outlet Lyudi Baikala, citing unnamed sources, reported that the woman died from pneumonic plague and that several Irkutsk hospitals were placed under quarantine. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said the public should follow official statements rather than “rumors or speculation.”

Anna Popova, head of Rospotrebnadzor, flew to Irkutsk and met with Governor Igor Kobzev and other officials on Friday. Kobzev said everyone who came into contact with the woman is under medical supervision and called the case a suspected “particularly dangerous infection.” Rospotrebnadzor is implementing “a full range of anti-epidemic measures,” he added. Some 197 contacts of the technician are in isolation, according to Russian media reports cited by University College London professor Tim Colbourn, who noted that all remain negative and that there may not be an outbreak. The Irkutsk Aluminum Plant in Shelekhov required staff to wear masks as a precaution, its director said, calling for calm.

US Secretary of State Marco Rubio said Monday that the administration is watching the case closely but that “I don’t think it’s cause for alarm, but it is cause for focus and a cause to just keep an eye on it.” Former Biden administration public health official Ashish Jha, who also led Brown University’s School of Public Health, called on Russian authorities to release more information. “Russian authorities owe the world more transparency on exactly what happened in Irkutsk,” Jha posted on X. He noted that the institute is officially a civilian research facility but that “Russia is well known to have an active military biological weapons program” and that US intelligence agencies have said the program has been expanding. Jha drew parallels to the 1979 anthrax outbreak linked to a Soviet military facility in Sverdlovsk, which killed at least 66 people and took years to become public.

Dr. Nirav D. Shah, a former head of Maine’s CDC, voiced skepticism of the Russian government’s account, saying there is “unconfirmed suspicion that she died of a pneumonic plague after a lab accident resulted in exposure.” He added that the incident is a reminder that the world is “not ready for the next significant public health threat.” Jha also noted reports of Russian security services involvement in the response, calling it “not the posture of a routine natural outbreak, even a normal lab accident.” He warned that “the number of labs working on dangerous pathogens is growing worldwide,” meaning such incidents will likely become more frequent.
