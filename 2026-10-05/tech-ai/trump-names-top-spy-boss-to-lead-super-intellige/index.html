Trump names top spy boss to lead 'Super Intelligence Force' as task force members announced

Tech / AI desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts slightly center-left

President Donald Trump has announced the formation of a new Super Intelligence Force, naming Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton as its chair. The task force, which Trump said will coordinate the federal government's engagement with consumers, public interest groups, religious organizations, critical infrastructure providers, and super intelligence companies, will report directly to Trump and White House Chief of Staff Susie Wiles. The announcement was made Sunday in a post on Trump's social network Truth Social.

Alongside Clayton, the task force's vice chairs will be Federal Trade Commission Chair Andrew Ferguson, Undersecretary of Defense for Research and Engineering Emil Michael, and Office of Personnel Management Director Scott Kupor. The task force has 120 days to produce a report on the risks and opportunities presented by AI. According to its charter, the group will 'develop plans for responding to SI-enabled threats to our society, while preventing overregulation and regulatory capture that would stifle innovation and competition.'

The formation of the task force follows a White House summit last Tuesday where Trump met with tech leaders including Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei, Tesla CEO Elon Musk, and Meta CEO Mark Zuckerberg. At that meeting, the executives signed a 'Joint Commitment on Frontier Responsibilities' that Trump described as 'morally binding' but which critics have noted is non-binding from a legal perspective. The document contained a notable typo, misspelling 'United States.'

Trump has unilaterally sought to rebrand artificial intelligence as 'super intelligence,' signing an executive order to that effect. The rebranding effort was described by some observers as an attempt to distance the technology from public fears about AI's risks. 'AI is going to kill us, it's scary, it is going to take jobs — but super intelligence is not,' one analyst said of the messaging.

Clayton, who previously served as chair of the Securities and Exchange Commission and as U.S. attorney for the Southern District of New York, has argued that one of the biggest risks is 'not being first' in AI development. He replaces venture capitalist David Sacks, whose term as a special government employee expired. The task force has drawn criticism from Democratic Senator Elizabeth Warren, who has called for regulation of AI. Trump has rejected the idea of laws to control the technology's development, despite ongoing debate about potential risks to humanity.
