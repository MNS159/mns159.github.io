Zinedine Zidane appointed France manager after five-year wait

Sports desk

Zinedine Zidane has been appointed manager of the France national team, replacing Didier Deschamps after Les Bleus' fourth-place finish at the World Cup. The former Real Madrid coach, who had been out of management for five years, was unveiled in Paris after talks with the French football authorities began as early as February 2025, following Deschamps' announcement weeks earlier that he would step down after the 2026 World Cup. Zidane will make his touchline debut on Friday against Turkey.

"I had several offers in the last four or five years, but I turned them all down," an emotional Zidane said at his first news conference. "The national team is the only thing I wanted." He offered few tactical details, saying, "I can't explain to you in just a couple of words how we're going to win. I have to arrive, meet with the players, and explain my plans to them." He confirmed his intention "to do things differently" and has called up five uncapped players for the current international break, while also signaling a degree of "continuation" from Deschamps' work.

Zidane, 54, is one of the most celebrated figures in French football. As a player, he won the 1998 World Cup and Euro 2000 with France, and claimed league titles and European trophies with Juventus and Real Madrid. His only previous senior coaching role was at Real Madrid. The appointment carries uncertainty given his five-year absence from management and lack of international experience. Zidane said: "It's new to me, but I'm not scared. This is what I wanted."

Reaction to Zidane's arrival has been almost unanimously positive. Kylian Mbappe, who described having Zidane as national team coach as "like a film" in an interview with Spanish newspaper AS, wrote "Welcome home" on Instagram. Former France teammate Bixente Lizarazu told France Info: "It's a great day for French football. He is more than fit to manage Les Bleus, given what he did at Real Madrid with great players and big egos." Zidane was met by a crowd of supporters carrying flares and banners as he left the French Football Federation's headquarters after his unveiling.

Zidane inherits a team in far better shape than Deschamps did at the start of his tenure, with the squad having scored 20 goals at the World Cup. The current international break includes four Nations League fixtures over three weeks: matches against Turkey, two against Belgium, and one against Italy in Paris. Zidane bypassed a question on Mbappe's captaincy, saying, "The most important thing for him now is to rest, we'll have the chance to speak in September." His homecoming at the Stade de France on Friday, 2 October, against Italy is the first fixture of the new era.
