Penguins forward Andrei Kuzmenko returns to Russia after father killed by bear

Sports desk

Pittsburgh Penguins forward Andrei Kuzmenko has returned to Russia before the start of the NHL season after his father was killed by a bear. Alexander Kuzmenko, a 62-year-old ice hockey coach in Russia, was on a fishing trip along the Krasnaya River in Southern Siberia with a group of seven others when a bear attacked from the trees, inflicting severe facial injuries.

Emergency services were called, but the dense forest made it difficult for them to reach the area. Kuzmenko was taken by river downstream for four hours before being airlifted to hospital, where he was later pronounced dead.

Penguins president Kyle Dubas said the team was "deeply saddened" by the death. "Our deepest thoughts and sympathies are with the Kuzmenko family and their loved ones as they grieve the loss of Alexander. We ask that the privacy of Andrei and the Kuzmenko family be respected at this time," the team said in a statement. The team added: "Andrei chose to be surrounded by his team-mates and practice with the team this morning before returning to Moscow to be with his family this afternoon. Andrei and the Kuzmenko family have the full support of the Penguins organisation during this time."

Kuzmenko, 30, signed a one-year contract with the Penguins this summer and is about to compete in his fifth NHL season, following previous stints with the Vancouver Canucks, Calgary Flames, Philadelphia Flyers and Los Angeles Kings. The NHL season begins on 29 September.
