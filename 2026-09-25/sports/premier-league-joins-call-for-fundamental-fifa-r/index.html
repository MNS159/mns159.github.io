Premier League joins call for fundamental FIFA reform

Sports desk

The Premier League has joined three of Europe’s top domestic leagues in calling for ‘fundamental’ reform of FIFA’s governance, accusing president Gianni Infantino of ‘actively promot[ing] exploitative ideas’ and concentrating unchecked power. In a joint statement with Spain’s La Liga, Germany’s Bundesliga and Italy’s Serie A, the English top flight said the shake-up was needed to make world football’s governing body ‘transparent, objective, proportionate and inclusive’, adding that ‘our sport cannot grow globally if its foundations are undermined’.

The four leagues issued their demand days after Infantino proposed an external review of FIFA’s operations following the collapse of his plan to sell a stake in the World Cup to private investors – the so-called FIFA Forward Enterprise (FFE) scheme, which sparked a global outcry. Referencing ‘one of the biggest abuses of power in FIFA’s history’, the leagues said that ‘while football’s stakeholders ultimately forced the withdrawal of FFE, the conditions that led to FFE remain’. The statement added that ‘fundamental governance reform is essential to restore clear constitutional boundaries within FIFA, including confidence that individual disciplinary matters remain demonstrably insulated from political interference’ – a known reference to the cancelled suspension of United States forward Folarin Balogun at the World Cup after US President Donald Trump called Infantino to request a review of the ban.

The leagues called for three specific reforms: establishing a permanent body of stakeholders – including leagues, clubs and players from men’s and women’s football – with a ‘meaningful role’ in FIFA’s decision-making; ensuring ‘evidence-based decision-making’ with independent impact assessments for major proposals; and creating a separation between FIFA’s regulatory and commercial functions, with ethics, financial oversight and electoral bodies that are ‘genuinely independent of the FIFA administration and presidency’. ‘The wider question,’ they said, ‘is whether FIFA’s president should simultaneously wield such regulatory, commercial and political power without effective checks and balances… governance that concentrates unchecked regulatory, commercial and political power in a single office… is not fit for purpose.’

The intervention follows a legal complaint filed two years ago by Europe’s top leagues and players’ union FIFpro against FIFA with the European Commission, alleging ‘abuse of dominance’ over the expansion of the international fixture calendar and a lack of consultation. FIFA denied the claims at the time and accused some leagues of ‘hypocrisy’. The leagues now argue that their legal action ‘is a consequence of a flawed governance framework in which FIFA can use its regulatory authority to advance its own commercial objectives without accounting for the impact on the economic and social sustainability of national competitions’. They insist: ‘This must change now for the sake of football.’

Infantino has said he will stand for re-election in March despite calls from three confederations for him to step down; his opponents have until 18 November to find an alternative candidate. The four leagues want candidates to support their proposals, saying: ‘Now is the moment to decide not only who will lead FIFA, but also how FIFA should be led.’ The letter is signed by Premier League chief executive Richard Masters, Bundesliga CEO Marc Lenz, La Liga president Javier Tebas and Serie A CEO Luigi De Siervo. Last month Masters told BBC Sport that Infantino had ‘pressed the self-destruct button’ by delivering ‘a self-inflicted wound’ over FFE. FIFA has been approached for comment.
