Cubs' Pete Crow-Armstrong becomes seventh player in MLB history with 40-40 season

Sports desk

Chicago Cubs center fielder Pete Crow-Armstrong became the seventh Major League Baseball player to hit 40 home runs and steal 40 bases in a single season, accomplishing the feat Thursday in a 2-1 win over the Miami Marlins that clinched a National League wild-card playoff berth for the Cubs. Crow-Armstrong entered the game with 45 home runs, tied for the MLB lead; he drew a leadoff walk on seven pitches, then stole second base on the first pitch to Seiya Suzuki to reach the 40-40 milestone. He finished 1-for-3 with a single and a walk.

Crow-Armstrong, 24, hit his 40th home run on Sept. 6, also against the Marlins and against the same starter, Tyler Phillips. At that time he had 33 stolen bases with 18 games remaining. Thursday’s steal was his third in four games. For the season, he has 45 home runs, tied for the MLB lead, ranks fourth with 107 RBI, fifth in stolen bases and leads the National League with a .951 OPS.

Preceding Crow-Armstrong in the 40/40 club were Shohei Ohtani (who posted a 50/50 season), Ronald Acuña Jr. (41/73 in 2024), Alfonso Soriano (46/41 in 2006), Alex Rodriguez (42/46), Barry Bonds (42/40 in 1996) and José Canseco (42/40 in 1988). Crow-Armstrong is the first Cub in franchise history to reach 40 homers and 40 steals. He is also the second Cub with a 30-30 season (joining Sammy Sosa) and the only Cub with at least 30 doubles, 30 homers and 30 steals in a season.

Crow-Armstrong’s 45 home runs are the most ever by a left-handed hitter in Cubs history, surpassing Billy Williams’ 42 in 1970. He is the seventh Cub with 45 or more homers, joining Ernie Banks (twice), Hack Wilson, Andre Dawson, Dave Kingman, Sammy Sosa (five times) and Derrek Lee. At 24 years old, he is just the third player – after Canseco (1988) and Rodriguez (1998) – to reach 40 homers and 30 steals at age 24 or younger.

Crow-Armstrong is widely considered a virtual certainty to win the National League Most Valuable Player award, following Canseco as a 40-40 player who also earned MVP honors. His season has been central to the Cubs’ return to the postseason. The Cubs clinched one of the NL’s wild-card spots with Thursday’s victory.
