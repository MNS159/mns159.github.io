Manchester City found guilty on 114 of 115 Premier League financial charges, reports say

Sports desk

Manchester City have been found guilty on 114 of the 115 Premier League financial charges brought against them, according to multiple reports, in a landmark verdict against the eight-time English champions. The charges were filed in February 2023 after a four-year investigation, as first reported by The Athletic, and cover alleged breaches of the league's spending regulations between 2009 and 2018. No penalties have been determined and the club is expected to appeal.

The allegations comprise 54 counts of failing to provide accurate and current financial information from 2009 through 2018 and 14 alleged violations for failing to submit precise financial reports on player and manager compensation over the same period. CBS Sports reported that the case encompassed allegations of off-the-books payments to former manager Roberto Mancini and sponsorship deals that were backdated and manipulated — claims that first surfaced in November 2018, when German newspaper Der Spiegel published leaked documents obtained by Football Leaks.

Neither City nor the Premier League has officially confirmed the verdict. A City spokesperson told The Athletic: "The Premier League process remains ongoing, with significant elements to be completed, and subject to strict confidentiality. As such Manchester City FC's position remains consistent with the club's statement of February 2023. The club has diligently respected due process for eight years on the basis the Premier League board and executive would behave as an independent, impartial and fair minded regulator, free from partisan influence." In February 2023, City said they were "surprised" by the charges and welcomed review by an independent commission to consider what they called "the comprehensive body of irrefutable evidence" supporting their position.

The case is the culmination of a two-decade rise fueled by Sheikh Mansour's Abu Dhabi United Group, which bought City for £200m in September 2008. City reached a €20m settlement with Uefa in 2014 over Financial Fair Play breaches, and in February 2020 were banned from the Champions League for two years and fined €30m; the Court of Arbitration for Sport overturned the ban five months later and reduced the fine to €10m. After winning their first Champions League in 2023 to complete a treble and a fourth straight Premier League title in May 2024, City faced a 12-week hearing over the Premier League charges at the International Dispute Resolution Centre in London from September to December 2024. The verdict was revealed in September 2026, months after Pep Guardiola's departure following 10 years in charge and the appointment of Enzo Maresca as his successor.

Potential sanctions range from reprimands and fines through points deductions to outright expulsion. Football finance expert Kieran Maguire said: "These charges are so serious that it is difficult to see how the board of Manchester City could survive if they lose the case and it would be hugely embarrassing for the Premier League if they lose the case because the legal costs for both parties are probably in excess of £100 million ($132.5 million)." City chairman Khaldoon Al Mubarak said in May, ahead of the ruling: "Once we have a ruling, believe me, we're going to have a wonderful sit down together and I'll say everything I've wanted to say for the last three years." An appeal is expected, and Premier League chief executive Richard Masters pleaded for patience in August 2025, noting his organisation had no control over the timings.
