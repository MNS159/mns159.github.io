FDA approves Eli Lilly's once-weekly insulin Onswik for type 2 diabetes

Health & Science desk · anchored on Reuters

The U.S. Food and Drug Administration has approved Eli Lilly and Company’s Onswik (insulin efsitora alfa-gobe), a once-weekly basal insulin injection for adults with type 2 diabetes, the company announced Sept.24, 2026. The drug is indicated as an adjunct to diet and exercise to improve glycemic control and is designed to reduce the number of injections by more than 300 per year compared with once-daily basal insulin, Lilly said.

The approval is based on the QWINT Phase 3 clinical development program, which enrolled more than 3,400 adults with type 2 diabetes across four global registration studies. In each trial, once-weekly Onswik met the primary endpoint of non-inferior A1C reduction from baseline, comparable to insulin glargine U-100 or insulin degludec U-100. The overall safety profile was similar to the daily basal insulins studied, according to Lilly.

“For over a century, Lilly has been developing insulins that transformed diabetes care for millions of people worldwide, but delays in initiating treatment and the burden of daily injections can have a real impact on patients’ day-to-day experience managing type 2 diabetes,” said Kenneth Custer, Ph.D., executive vice president and president of Lilly Cardiometabolic Health, in a statement. “Onswik offers a new once-weekly basal insulin option … that expand choice and provide greater flexibility.”

Onswik should not be used by people with type 1 diabetes due to an increased risk of severe hypoglycemia. Common side effects include low blood sugar, allergic reactions, injection-site reactions, lipodystrophy, itching, rash, swelling of hands and feet, and weight gain. Serious risks include severe allergic reactions, hypokalemia, and heart failure when taken with thiazolidinediones. The company noted that the drug is not approved for use in children.

This is the fourth global approval for Onswik for adults with type 2 diabetes, following regulatory actions by the European Medicines Agency, Mexico’s COFEPRIS, and Japan’s PMDA. The Onswik KwikPen will be available in the U.S. in the coming months in two concentrations: 500 units/mL (1,500 units total) and 1,000 units/mL (3,000 units total). Lilly said the approval expands its type 2 diabetes portfolio to include a once-weekly basal insulin alongside existing options across insulins, incretins, and oral and injectable medications.
