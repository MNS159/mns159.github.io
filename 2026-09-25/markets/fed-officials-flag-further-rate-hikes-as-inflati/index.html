Fed officials flag further rate hikes as inflation persists, with possible pre-midterm move

Financial & Markets desk · anchored on Reuters

Philadelphia Federal Reserve President Anna Paulson said Thursday that she and her colleagues may need to raise interest rates further to tame inflation, speaking a week after the Federal Open Market Committee lifted its benchmark rate by a quarter percentage point. The move took the key funds rate to a target range of 3.75%-4%.

In prepared remarks for a fintech conference in her district, Paulson said that while the summer showed some moderation in price pressures, underlying inflation remains around 2.5%-3% — "well above our 2% target, and the gap has shown little signs of closing." She added, "The best I can say about underlying inflation this year is that it hasn't gotten worse," and noted that price pressures have persisted even outside the oil supply shocks from the Iran war and tariffs. She described economic output as "solid" and the labor market as "holding steady."

Looking ahead, Paulson said, "some modest further tightening may be warranted" if conditions evolve as she expects. The rate increase, she said, "brings policy closer to what I believe is needed to return inflation to 2% at a pace that balances inflation with risks to the labor market."

Traders have sharply raised their expectations for Fed tightening. According to CME Group's FedWatch tool, markets now price a 64% chance of a hike in October and another in January, with fed funds futures implying a rate of 4.8% by the end of 2027 — suggesting as many as four additional quarter-point increases. Longer-duration Treasury yields have risen this week to highs not seen since 2004.

The potential for an October move, which would come just before the November midterm elections, has drawn attention; The New York Times noted the prospect of a rate increase landing shortly before the vote.
