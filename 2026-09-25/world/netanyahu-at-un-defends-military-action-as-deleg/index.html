Netanyahu Defends Military Campaigns at UN as Dozens of Delegates Walk Out

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu fiercely defended his country's military campaigns in Iran, Gaza, and elsewhere in the Middle East during a speech to the UN General Assembly on Thursday, as dozens of delegates walked out in protest. Addressing the empty seats, Netanyahu said: 'If there are any other moral cowards who haven't left the hall, please do so now.' He vowed to continue fighting, declaring, 'We're going to win. We have no other choice,' and rejected accusations that Israel is committing genocide in Gaza.

Hours earlier, Palestinian President Mahmoud Abbas addressed the assembly via a recorded video message, accusing Israeli policies—particularly the expansion of Jewish settlements in the occupied West Bank—of threatening 'the very life and existence of the Palestinian people on their homeland' and jeopardizing the two-state solution. Abbas said Palestinians in Gaza are being subjected to 'killing, destruction, starvation and attempts at displacement, as well as a genocidal war.' He spoke via video link because he had been denied a US entry visa.

Netanyahu launched scathing attacks on nations and leaders who have criticized Israel. He mocked accusations of colonialism, saying: 'They accuse Israel, tiny Israel, of colonialism … Among them are people in Britain and in France. For God's sake, they invented the term.' He accused the media of exaggerating violence by Jewish settlers in the West Bank, claiming 'maybe two or three' people are killed a year, while UN figures show 23 Palestinians have been killed in settler attacks this year and 72 since 2023. Netanyahu labeled Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdogan a 'tyrant,' accusing him of supporting Hamas; Turkey's presidential communications director responded that the 'baseless accusations' were incompatible with UN ideals. Netanyahu also criticized New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani, saying 'many Jews no longer feel safe in New York' since his election; Mamdani's office called the speech 'baseless lies meant to sanitize his genocide of Palestinians.'

Netanyahu thanked US President Donald Trump for 'bold leadership,' referring to joint US-Israeli air strikes on Tehran in February. The speech was heavily pitched at Israeli voters ahead of a critical election next month—the first since the 7 October 2023 Hamas attack—and at American audiences as support for Israel fractures abroad. Netanyahu's once-strong image as 'Mr Security' has been challenged by opponents who argue he ignored warnings of the attack; his religious-right wing coalition is trailing in polls.

Outside the UN, several protesters were detained, including actress Susan Sarandon and comedian Hannah Einbinder, after an unscheduled demonstration blocked a junction. The Israeli military launched a ground offensive in Gaza following the 7 October attack, which killed about 1,200 people and saw 251 taken hostage. Since then, more than 73,920 people have been killed in Gaza, according to the Hamas-run health ministry. A resolution by the International Association of Genocide Scholars stated that Israel's conduct meets the legal definition of genocide under the UN convention; Israel denies this, arguing its actions are self-defense.
