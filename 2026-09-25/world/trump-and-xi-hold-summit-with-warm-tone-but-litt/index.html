US-China Summit

Trump and Xi exchange warm words at state dinner but little progress on key issues

International & World desk

US President Donald Trump and China's President Xi Jinping exchanged warm words at a lavish White House state dinner on Thursday evening but gave little indication of progress on the pressing issues that divide the world's two most powerful countries. Xi stressed that the two nations should ensure AI remains "under human control" and act "as responsible major countries," while Trump toasted a future of "harmony, peace and success" for both nations. Tensions remain despite the diplomatic niceties, including over Taiwan, with Xi urging Trump earlier in the day to take the "correct position" on the self-governing island that Beijing claims, amid a weapons package paused by Trump.

Xi, on his first visit to the US in over a decade, welcomed the "historic juncture" in US-China relations and said both leaders' ambitions could "surely be mutually reinforcing." "China and the United States must act as responsible major countries to meet the expectations of our peoples, keep pace with the trend of our times, and explore a new approach for major countries to get along with each other," he said. Trump lavished praise on Xi, telling attendees that while the two countries had "different systems," they had "never gotten along better." However, beyond Xi's reference to AI remaining under human control, little was said about the key disputes over trade, technology, and Taiwan.

Earlier in the day, Chinese state media reported Xi had advised Washington behind closed doors to handle "with prudence" its approach to Taiwan. Washington has yet to comment publicly. The US is legally bound to provide Taiwan with the means to defend itself, but Trump delayed approving a $14bn arms package for it after Xi in May warned that mishandling the issue could cause the two countries to "collide or even enter into conflict." Trump has called the delay a "very good negotiating chip" with Beijing.

On trade, the truce that paused a tit-for-tat tariff war was extended by only a few months - from November to January - rather than the at least a year Beijing had hoped for. This means key disputes on tariffs, China's dominance of rare earth supplies, and access to advanced US chips are kicked into another round of negotiations, suggesting the US wants China to do more before offering longer-term concessions. Xi faces a domestic economy in trouble: while the country sells a record number of goods worldwide, consumers at home are holding back, youth unemployment remains stubbornly high, and property prices have slumped in a country where 80% of people own their own homes.

The dinner was attended by scores of US officials, conservative broadcasters, and business leaders, including Apple's Tim Cook, Nvidia's Jensen Huang, and Elon Musk, who were seated at the same table as Trump and Xi. OpenAI's Sam Altman, Google's Sundar Pichai, Amazon's Jeff Bezos, Mark Zuckerberg, and Jeff Yass were also present, as were leaders of General Motors, Visa, Paramount Skydance, and ExxonMobil. One notable absence was Anthropic, one of the biggest AI firms. The menu showcased "American ingredients with subtle Chinese influence," featuring yellow squash and wild mushrooms, sesame-crusted sea bass, braised bok choy, and vanilla crémeux with sour cherry confit. The wine included Schramsberg Blanc De Noir, a nod to President Nixon's 1972 "Toast to Peace" with former Chinese Premier Zhou Enlai.

Trump presented Xi with a sculpture of a bald eagle, the US national symbol, saying it embodied "the free and soaring spirit of America," with First Lady Melania having participated in its design. Protesters shouted from just outside the White House gates during the arrival, and some were arrested. Xi also announced that two pandas, Ping Ping and Fu Shuang, will arrive in Atlanta in just a few days as China's panda diplomacy resumes. The summit's biggest victory for Beijing may have been the optics: the lavish welcome, a personal airport greeting, and the state banquet helped cement Xi's image at home as Trump's equal, with memes contrasting the two leaders spreading widely on Chinese social media. Trump and Xi will begin the final day of their visit on Friday with tea at the White House's Red Room with their wives, followed by a tour of the US National Archives, before Xi and his wife depart Washington later in the day.
