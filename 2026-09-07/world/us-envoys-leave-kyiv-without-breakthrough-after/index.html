US Envoys Leave Kyiv Without Breakthrough After Moscow Talks

International & World desk

U.S. envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner held talks with Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy in Kyiv on Sunday during their first official visit to Ukraine, but made no breakthrough toward ending Russia’s war. The visit came one day after the envoys met with Russian President Vladimir Putin in Moscow. Putin had declared a three-day ceasefire to ensure the envoys' safety. The envoys traveled to Kyiv by train from Poland, as Ukraine’s airspace remains closed due to the conflict. Ukraine’s rail operator announced their departure from Kyiv early on Monday.

At a joint press conference outside the presidential palace, Witkoff and Kushner described the talks in Moscow and Kyiv as substantive and said new ideas were on the table, though they did not provide details. Kushner acknowledged the challenge, saying the U.S. was working "behind the scenes" to create the conditions for a possible deal. "It’s a very, very hard problem, but we’re going to try," he said. Asked whether the war could end by winter, Kushner responded: "I think if we can achieve that, it'd be an incredible thing. It doesn't mean that we'll - we will achieve it, but it's an effort that's very important to try." Witkoff said earlier that both sides would need to compromise, adding: "We think we have the ingredients to get to that place … and we look forward to continuing this as long as it takes."

Zelenskyy held three rounds of talks with the envoys, the final one joined by national security advisers from the United Kingdom, France and Germany. He said the talks focused on military aid, including Patriot missile systems and other weapons Ukraine will need in the coming months. Zelenskyy said Kyiv was seeking "serious volumes" of U.S. support, with backing from its European partners. "If the war drags on until winter — and that is how things currently look — we will need a 'winter package'," he said, covering air defense and energy infrastructure. Zelenskyy also said Russia had intensified strikes on Ukrainian cities before Russia’s State Duma election later this month, calling the attacks deliberate "terror" against civilians. He expressed gratitude for the presence of European representatives, saying: "Here we are all on the same side."

The envoys’ trip followed talks with Putin in Moscow on Saturday. Kremlin foreign policy adviser Yuri Ushakov described the Moscow talks as constructive, frank, and useful but did not discuss specific outcomes. Ushakov said the talks also focused on economic issues and potentially large Russia-U.S. projects. A White House official said the envoys had "discussed substantive plans which will be announced in the coming weeks." The Kremlin said the Americans had "put forward a number of ideas on possible routes to a settlement," without elaborating. Witkoff said he was hopeful a new round of U.S.-mediated Ukraine-Russia talks would be announced "in short order."

The war has escalated in recent months, with Putin seeking the complete conquest of the Donbas region and Russian drones targeting the Ukrainian capital. Ukraine has focused its drone attacks on Russia’s energy sector and targeted online retailers deep inside Russia. Zelenskyy’s chief of staff, Kyrylo Budanov, told The New York Times last weekend that he did not expect the fighting to end before spring 2027 and predicted further Russian strategic air strikes on Ukraine this winter. Zelenskyy reiterated his calls for security guarantees from the U.S. to prevent Russia from invading again after a peace deal. He said the issue of territory remains so complex that it can only be worked out through direct talks with leaders — himself, Putin, and possibly Trump and/or European leaders. The sides agreed to hold a follow-up meeting involving Ukraine, the U.S. and Europe, with a location still to be set.

The visit was significant symbolically. Zelenskyy had pushed hard for the trip, and the envoys’ train was called the Peace Express. The fact that they were in Kyiv meant that Russia did not bomb the capital, which the Russians had been doing 24/7 before this weekend. Zelenskyy noted that Kushner and Witkoff had done even more than Patriot air defense systems to protect the city. "Please," he said, "come more often so maybe our people can live a little."
