Israeli strikes kill 12 in southern Lebanon, ceasefire at risk

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

Israeli air strikes on the southern Lebanese village of Kfar Roummane have killed at least 12 people, the Lebanese health ministry said, as Lebanese President Joseph Aoun warned that the “dangerous escalation” was jeopardising the US-brokered ceasefire that took effect in June. The strikes overnight and on Sunday targeted a residential building and a vehicle carrying paramedics, bringing the total number of people killed in Israeli attacks across southern Lebanon over the past three days to at least 27, according to the ministry.

The first strike shortly after midnight destroyed a three-storey residential building near the Shuyuiya roundabout in Kfar Roummane, killing 11 people, including two children and four women, and injuring eight others, among them three children and a paramedic from the Islamic Health Association, an emergency service linked to Hezbollah. The second strike hit a vehicle carrying paramedics from the Islamic Risala Scout Association, an emergency service affiliated with the Amal movement, killing one paramedic and injuring two others. The health ministry said the death toll “serves as further irrefutable evidence of the grave violations of international humanitarian law committed by the Israeli occupation, targeting medics and children”.

The Israel Defense Forces (IDF) said it struck “several Hezbollah terrorist infrastructure sites across southern Lebanon” overnight in retaliation for two explosive drones launched by Hezbollah fighters towards Israeli troops operating on the Ali al-Taher ridge, inside the Israeli-declared “security zone” just south of Kfar Roummane. The IDF added that it struck “several terrorists who were identified transferring weapons in the area of Kfar Roummane, in order to remove the threat they posed to IDF soldiers” and that “the claim that several uninvolved individuals were harmed as a result of the strikes is being reviewed”. Separately on Sunday, the IDF said it struck the Nabatieh area and killed several Hezbollah fighters following a drone attack; the Lebanese health ministry reported 11 people killed in Israeli strikes across the south that day, including nine in Nabatieh al-Fawqa and two women in Arab Salim.

A resident of Kfar Roummane, Sara Moallaem, 35, told the BBC she had been asleep with her five children when she heard the explosions and moved them to a safe room. Some of those killed were distant relatives of her husband, she said, insisting they were civilians with no affiliation to Hezbollah. “During the day, it is almost normal, people going back and forth. But as it comes near the night, things escalate, and now my children are afraid and I am packing now for the sixth or seventh time. It is terrible,” she said. “Now that they are targeting civilians, I don’t want to weep for my children.” Large numbers of residents have again been fleeing areas near the strip of territory occupied by Israeli troops.

Lebanese President Joseph Aoun said the escalation was jeopardising the ceasefire agreement between Lebanon, Israel and the US, which calls for Hezbollah to disarm, Israeli forces to withdraw gradually from southern Lebanon, and the Lebanese army to deploy across the area. He urged the international community to put pressure on Israel to stop the attacks. Hezbollah has rejected the framework, saying the provisions linking Israel’s withdrawal to the group’s disarmament cross “all red lines”. There was no immediate comment from Hezbollah on the latest strikes, but on Sunday the group’s member of parliament Ali Ammar declared that “the language of resistance is the only language that is effective against the Israeli enemy”.

The conflict began on 2 March, when Hezbollah – which is supported by Iran – launched rockets into Israel in retaliation for a strike that killed Iran’s supreme leader. Israel responded with a bombing campaign across Lebanon and an invasion of a significant part of the country’s south. At least 4,362 people have been killed in Lebanon during the conflict, including 395 women and 259 children, according to the health ministry, whose figures do not differentiate between combatants and civilians. Israeli authorities say 40 Israeli soldiers and four civilians have been killed.
