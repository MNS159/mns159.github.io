China to inject $54bn into state banks and insurers to shore up economy

International & World desk

China is injecting 360 billion yuan ($53.6bn; £39.7bn) into eight state-owned banks and insurance companies, the finance ministry announced on Sunday, in the latest effort to bolster the financial system and revive the slowing economy. The cash injection, reported by state news agency Xinhua, will go to three large lenders—including the Industrial and Commercial Bank of China and the Agricultural Bank of China—and five insurers, among them China Life Insurance, China Taiping Insurance Group, and the People's Insurance Company of China. The move is intended to enhance the institutions' operating capabilities, risk resistance, and ability to serve the real economy, according to Xinhua.

The capital will be raised through private placements of A-shares to the finance ministry and other state entities, including the China National Tobacco Corporation. The Agricultural Bank of China plans to raise up to 160 billion yuan, the Industrial and Commercial Bank of China up to 100 billion yuan, and China Life Insurance 35 billion yuan; the People's Insurance Company of China aims to raise up to 15 billion yuan. The lenders said proceeds would be used entirely to replenish cash reserves, helping them sustain credit expansion, while insurers said the injection would strengthen their ability to withstand risk.

China's economy has faced mounting headwinds, including a prolonged property market slump, a shrinking workforce, trade tensions with the West, the impact of the Iran war on oil prices, and an ongoing technology rivalry with the United States. Official GDP figures released in July showed the economy grew 4.3% in the second quarter, below Beijing's annual target of around 5% and down from 5% in the first quarter. In March, the government cut its growth target to a range of 4.5%–5%, its lowest since 1991, a move analysts said gave Beijing room to acknowledge pre-existing weakness.

President Xi Jinping has long regarded financial stability as key to national security. State media Global Times said the injection would give banks and financial institutions more resources to channel credit into the real economy while strengthening their ability to withstand external shocks amid global financial uncertainty. The plan was first announced at the annual parliamentary meeting in March, extending a financing tool used to bolster other big state banks last year. The insurance sector, in particular, has struggled with eroding profitability due to persistently low interest rates, with numerous small and mid-sized insurers reporting deteriorating solvency ratios.

China Life Insurance said the capital injection was 'an important step by the country to enhance the financial sector’s ability to serve the real economy and promote the high-quality development of the financial and insurance industries.' The initiative is also expected to position state insurers to help regulators manage smaller, higher-risk insurance companies. The cash infusion comes as Beijing leans on state banks to support growth despite weak loan demand, and as it aims to reshape the economy in the face of long-term challenges such as an aging population and a shrinking workforce.
