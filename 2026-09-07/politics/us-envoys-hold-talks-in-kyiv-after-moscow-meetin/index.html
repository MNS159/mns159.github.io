Ukraine War

US envoys hold talks in Kyiv after Moscow meeting; progress seen as limited

Politics desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum
✓ Facts
  • Witkoff and Kushner met with Russian President Vladimir Putin in Moscow on Saturday for more than three hours behind closed doors, with no announced breakthrough.
  • Putin's foreign policy adviser Yuri Ushakov called the Moscow talks "constructive, frank and useful" and said the sides also discussed potential joint US-Russia economic projects.
  • On Sunday, Witkoff and Kushner traveled to Kyiv by train and met President Volodymyr Zelenskyy; both sides described the talks as "substantive," and European security advisers, including the UK's Jonathan Powell, joined later sessions.
  • Zelenskyy said any agreement must include security guarantees and a "dignified post-war peace," and he did not indicate willingness to cede Ukrainian-held territory in Donetsk or Luhansk.
  • Putin ordered a 72-hour pause on airstrikes on Kyiv to cover the envoys' visit, and Zelenskyy said he would keep Russian airspace clear for their travel.
  • Despite the pause, Ukraine's State Emergency Service reported overnight strikes killed one person and injured at least ten others in regions including Zaporizhzhia and Khmelnytskyi; Ukraine's air force said it downed 82 of 108 Russian drones and six missiles.
  • Russia reported one death from a Ukrainian drone strike in Belgorod and said Ukrainian drones also hit oil facilities near Rostov-on-Don.
  • CIA Director John Ratcliffe recently met with Russian intelligence officials in Moscow, though few details of that meeting have been disclosed.
○ Analysis

The talks mark the first Witkoff-Kushner trip to Moscow since January and the first time either envoy has set foot in Ukraine since Russia's February 2022 full-scale invasion. Diplomatic momentum had stalled for months as U.S. attention shifted to the separate Iran conflict, even as Russia has grown more assertive militarily and diplomatically. The renewed push comes against a backdrop of a largely frozen front line, intensified air and drone warfare on both sides, Black Sea shipping attacks raising fears of global grain shortages, and NATO concern over suspected Russian sabotage and cyberattacks in Europe. The core obstacle remains unchanged from earlier rounds of diplomacy: Russia's demands—territorial concessions, limits on Ukraine's military, and a bar on NATO membership—clash with Ukraine's insistence on security guarantees and no loss of sovereign territory. Both sides used symbolic gestures, such as the temporary halt in strikes on Kyiv, to signal goodwill, but fighting continued elsewhere throughout the negotiations, underscoring the gap between diplomatic rhetoric and battlefield reality.

Steelman

Despite both Washington and Moscow, and both Washington and Kyiv, publicly characterizing their respective talks as constructive or substantive, the fundamental disagreements over territory and security guarantees remain unresolved, and violence continued on both sides even during the negotiating window—suggesting symbolic progress has yet to translate into concrete concessions from either combatant.

Implications

Both sides have signaled openness to further engagement, including a possible revival of trilateral U.S.-Russia-Ukraine talks involving European partners, with a White House official saying next steps are expected to be announced in coming weeks. But with Russia pressing its territorial and military demands and Ukraine refusing to cede ground, any settlement will likely require significant compromise that neither side has yet shown readiness to make, leaving open the possibility of a prolonged winter of fighting even as diplomacy continues.
