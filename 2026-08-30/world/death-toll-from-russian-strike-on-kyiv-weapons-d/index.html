Death toll from Russian strike on Kyiv weapons depot rises to 38 as investigation launched

International & World desk · anchored on AP

The death toll from a Russian drone strike on a weapons depot near Kyiv has risen to 38, Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Sunday, making it one of the deadliest attacks in Ukraine this year. The strike late Friday in the village of Myla, west of the capital, triggered a secondary explosion of shells, mines and drones that damaged nearby homes, including a care home for the elderly and disabled. Emergency crews continued to extinguish fires and clear unexploded ordnance, while 20 people were reported injured and four remained missing, according to Zelenskyy.

The blast, which occurred in the Bucha district, damaged approximately 130 homes and at least 50 residential buildings, according to regional head Tymur Tkachenko. He said 34 of the 38 killed were residents of the care home. The attack came as Russian aerial assaults on Kyiv entered their third day, with nearly constant drone and missile raids disrupting civilian life. Ukraine's air force reported shooting down 233 Russian drones overnight.

Zelenskyy described the storage of explosives near homes as "terrible negligence" and said criminal proceedings had been opened for official negligence. "In the fifth year of the full-scale invasion, making the same mistakes over and over is criminal negligence," wrote Prime Minister Sergiy Koretsky, referring to a similar strike on an ammunition depot in Vyshneve in July. That incident also involved a secondary detonation and damaged over 280 homes.

Russia's Ministry of Defence said it struck a warehouse holding components and launch boosters for long-range drones. The ministry also announced plans to launch "massive strikes" against Ukraine's energy infrastructure, echoing its winter campaign that has previously left thousands without heat and light. Ukrainian officials have warned the coming winter may be the most challenging since the invasion began in February 2022.

In separate attacks, a 14-year-old was killed in the Boryspil district and a two-year-old died after a Russian drone crashed in Kyiv's Obolonskyi district. Five people were killed across the country over the past day, according to local officials. Meanwhile, Ukraine continued its own drone strikes on Russian economic targets, including an oil refinery in the Leningrad region and a military base in Krasnodar. The Russian online retailer Ozon said one of its logistics centers was hit, and the Moscow-installed governor of occupied Zaporizhzhia said four people were injured in a Ukrainian strike on a shopping centre.
