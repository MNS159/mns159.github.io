Six months into Iran war, US targets Banque Misr UAE in economic crackdown as Iranians face mounting hardship

International & World desk

Six months into the US-Israel war on Iran, the United States has moved to cut off the UAE operations of Egypt’s second-largest bank, Banque Misr, from the US financial system, accusing it of processing approximately $1.8bn for 103 companies that are potentially part of Iranian shadow banking networks. The US Treasury’s Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN) proposed a rule on Friday that would revoke Banque Misr UAE’s correspondent banking access, preventing its UAE branches from conducting dollar transactions. The move is part of a broader economic pressure campaign dubbed “Operation Economic Outcast,” which Washington launched after military operations failed to force Iran’s surrender.

The Treasury alleged that Banque Misr UAE’s customers include front companies used by Iran’s Ministry of Defence and the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) to evade sanctions and launder money for Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei. The proposed punishment is expected to take effect in 30 days after a public comment period and will not affect other branches of the bank. Simultaneously, the Treasury’s Office of Foreign Assets Control sanctioned Reza Mohammad Taeedi, general manager of the Dubai branch of Iran’s Bank Melli, for facilitating billions of dollars in transactions for the IRGC Qods Force, and Hong Kong-based Kameng Trading Limited for allegedly laundering money for Iran.

The war began on February 28 with US and Israeli strikes that killed then-Supreme Leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei and key IRGC figures. His son, Mojtaba Khamenei, was selected as the new supreme leader but has remained out of public view. Within Iran’s establishment, there is a split between those seeking a diplomatic resolution and those wanting to fight until the US backs down, but all agree there will be no “capitulation.” International mediators have failed to revive talks, and Washington has imposed its toughest-ever sanctions on Tehran.

Banque Misr said on Saturday it was reviewing the Treasury notice and would contact US authorities for further information, adding that its UAE branch continues to provide services in accordance with applicable rules. The Central Bank of Egypt stressed that the measure is limited to Banque Misr UAE’s dollar transactions and does not affect other Egyptian banks or the bank’s domestic operations. The UAE central bank announced on Sunday it had launched a “special and urgent examination” of Banque Misr’s UAE branches, including a forensic lookback covering the period cited by US authorities.

Iran has rejected the latest sanctions. Economy Minister Ali Madanizadeh said they will fail, and government spokeswoman Fatemeh Mohajerani posted on X that the government would guide the country through the phase with wisdom and resolve. IRGC spokesperson Sardar Mohebi said the US resorting to economic warfare is proof of its defeat on the battlefield. Ali Akbar Dareini, a researcher at the Centre for Strategic Studies in Tehran, said Iran has “a PhD in circumventing sanctions” and that the US goal of causing economic collapse and riots is based on a miscalculation.

For ordinary Iranians, life has become a daily struggle. “I just want a life that does not involve constantly clashing with others, getting more isolated from the rest of the world, and planning for survival,” said Marjan, a Tehran resident who spoke to Al Jazeera on condition of anonymity. The war has led to a salary slump and increased hardship, with no relief expected in the short term. Analysts say the long-term implications of the war have pushed the Trump administration toward economic sanctions, but these are unlikely to compel Iran to meet US demands.
