Crow-Armstrong hits three homers, bolsters NL MVP case

Sports desk

Chicago Cubs center fielder Pete Crow-Armstrong delivered a three-homer, six-RBI performance in a 15-5 win over the Cincinnati Reds at Wrigley Field on Saturday, strengthening his case for the National League MVP award. Crow-Armstrong went 4-for-5 with three home runs, becoming the first Cubs player to record at least four hits, three home runs, and six RBI in a game at Wrigley since George Mitterwald in 1974. He had a chance for a fourth home run in the eighth inning against Reds position player Jose Trevino, who was pitching, but grounded out on a 58.1 mph knuckleball.

Crow-Armstrong is now batting .282 with a .944 OPS, 36 home runs, 89 RBI, and 32 stolen bases, putting a 40-40 season in play. He leads all of MLB with a 7.9 WAR entering Saturday, a figure that will rise after his three-homer game. The 24-year-old is also regarded as perhaps the best defensive center fielder in baseball, ranking third in defensive runs saved (14) among MLB center fielders and second in outs above average (23) among all fielders, according to The Fielding Bible and Baseball Savant, respectively.

Crow-Armstrong's August has been historically productive. According to CBS Sports research, he is just the fourth player in the last 50 years to record at least 12 home runs, 25 RBI, and six steals in a single calendar month, joining Ryne Sandberg (1990), Eric Davis (1987), and Bobby Bonds (1977). His plate patience has also improved, with 71 walks on the season.

The MVP race has shifted heavily in Crow-Armstrong's favor. Before Saturday's game, he was a -390 favorite at FanDuel, ahead of Dodgers star Shohei Ohtani at +300. After the three-homer outburst, Crow-Armstrong's odds moved to -1100, with Ohtani at +650. Ohtani, a four-time MVP and the reigning back-to-back NL MVP, is hitting .283 with a .921 OPS, 30 home runs, and 80 RBI this season. He has also gone 8-2 with a 1.79 ERA and 95 strikeouts over 85.2 innings as a pitcher, but he has not pitched since early July due to a nagging knee problem and has no clear timetable for return. Ohtani is also dealing with right biceps issues.

The Cubs, at 77-59, hold the top NL wild-card position, 1.5 games ahead of the Philadelphia Phillies entering Saturday night's action. They are 7.5 games behind the Milwaukee Brewers in the NL Central. Crow-Armstrong's production has been essential to the Cubs' playoff push, and his Saturday performance further solidified his standing as the MVP frontrunner.
