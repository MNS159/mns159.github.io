Hrgovic stops Itauma in ninth round to win vacant IBF heavyweight title

Sports desk

Filip Hrgovic, a heavy underdog, scored a ninth-round TKO of previously unbeaten Moses Itauma on Saturday at the O2 Arena in London to claim the vacant IBF heavyweight title. The referee waved off the fight as Itauma's corner threw in the towel. Itauma, 21, was taken to hospital afterward as a precaution, promoter Frank Warren said, adding that the Briton had also injured his leg.

Itauma dominated the early rounds, displaying superior hand speed and landing repeatedly with his left hand. He scored a second-round knockdown and appeared to be winning on all scorecards. However, Hrgovic, 34, absorbed the punishment and waited for Itauma to tire. By the eighth round, Itauma was visibly exhausted, his mouth open and legs gone. Hrgovic seized control in the ninth, pummeling Itauma against the ropes until the stoppage.

"He was throwing too much. He was fighting like an amateur," Hrgovic said afterward. "I knew I needed to endure it. I need to stay there. He will get tired and I'll get him in the later rounds." Hrgovic, an Olympic bronze medalist who had previously gone 12 hard rounds with Zhilei Zhang and was leading on two scorecards before a cut stoppage against Daniel Dubois, credited his experience and chin. "Let me ask my young friend something: is chin important?" he said. "Chin is important."

Itauma, who had never fought beyond six rounds professionally, admitted before the fight that he had never even sparred 12 rounds. Warren said the fighter's mindset was to seek an early knockout, a strategy that had served him well in 14 previous professional wins. "Someone said to me he doesn't feel like he'd win a world title unless he knocks him out. I didn't like that," Warren said. Hrgovic advised Itauma to "get a few easy fights, easy wins. Build again and learn off these mistakes."

The fight was Hrgovic's second chance at a full world title. He had been the mandatory challenger for Oleksandr Usyk's IBF belt but took an interim title fight against Dubois in June 2024 instead of continuing to wait, losing by late TKO. Now Hrgovic finally holds a championship. Warren compared Itauma's situation to that of Dubois, who rebuilt after painful setbacks to become a two-time world champion, but noted that British boxing has also seen fighters such as David Price never fully recover from a first defeat.
