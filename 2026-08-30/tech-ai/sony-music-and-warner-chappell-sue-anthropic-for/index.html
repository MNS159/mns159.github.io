Sony Music Publishing and Warner Chappell sue Anthropic over ‘blatant’ copyright theft in AI training

Tech / AI desk

Sony Music Publishing, Warner Chappell, and numerous other music publishers have filed a lawsuit against Anthropic and its co-founders, Dario Amodei and Benjamin Mann, in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California, alleging that the AI lab engaged in a “brazen campaign of illegally torrenting, scraping, and downloading copyrighted works” to train its Claude AI models. Filed late Friday and first reported by Music Business Worldwide, the lawsuit seeks damages for what it describes as “tens of thousands” of copyrighted works, requesting up to $150,000 per work and an additional $25,000 for each instance where identifiable copyright management information was stripped—totaling potentially several billion dollars if a court awards the maximum.

In the filing, the publishers call this “one of the largest and most blatant ongoing thefts of intellectual property in history.” They allege that Anthropic conducted a “massive scale” operation to obtain pirated material, including a claim that co-founder Benjamin Mann used BitTorrent to download over five million pirated books, and that employees downloaded at least two million more from Pirate Library Mirror. The suit also claims Anthropic scraped lyrics from sites like MusixMatch and LyricFind, which had paid to license content from the labels. Specific songs named as being found in Anthropic’s training data include Marvin Gaye and Tammi Terrell’s “Ain’t No Mountain High Enough,” Bon Jovi’s “Livin’ On a Prayer,” Earth, Wind & Fire’s “September,” Leonard Cohen’s “Hallelujah,” and Taylor Swift’s “Paper Rings.”

Anthropic responded with a statement from a spokesperson: “We disagree with the publishers’ claims and we intend to defend ourselves robustly in court.” The company did not immediately respond to a separate request for comment from The Verge. The lawsuit is the latest in a series of intellectual property disputes involving Anthropic. Some of the same lawyers behind this case also represent Concord Music Group and Universal Music Group in a case filed in January, and they led the Bartz v. Anthropic case, in which a group of authors accused Anthropic of using copyrighted works to train products like Claude.

In that earlier Bartz case, Anthropic was ordered to pay $1.5 billion after a judge ruled that while it was legal for the AI lab to use copyrighted works, it was not legal to acquire that content through piracy. Anthropic also recently settled a separate suit brought by the publishing industry for $1.5 billion, and has faced multiple lawsuits from Universal Music Group, Concord, and ABKCO, plus separate suits from BMG and Round Hill Music. The new complaint builds on those cases, notably adding the accusation of “flagrant piracy” involving the illegal torrenting of millions of books that include lyrics and sheet music, and naming the co-founders as individual defendants.

This latest suit is described as particularly broad, intertwining claims about training data acquisition with the operation of Anthropic’s Claude models. It also seeks additional penalties for stripping copyright management information, a key distinction from earlier cases. According to the filing, the publishers intend to hold Anthropic and its founders accountable for what they call the theft of intellectual property on a massive scale, with damages that could reach billions of dollars if the court sides with the publishers and awards maximum penalties. As of publication, the case remains pending, and no trial date has been set.

Update, August 30th: This story has been clarified to note that Sony Music Publishing specifically filed the lawsuit, not to be confused with Sony Music Entertainment.
