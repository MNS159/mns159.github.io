Zelenskyy says 40 dead, 30 missing after Russian double-tap strike on Ukrainian aid centre

International & World desk

At least 40 people are confirmed dead after a Russian double-tap strike hit an aid distribution point in the southern Ukrainian village of Komyshuvakha on Monday, President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Wednesday, warning that more than 30 people may still be under the rubble.

Ukraine said the attack involved nine glide bombs, some weighing 3,000kg, which hit the village in the Zaporizhzhia region as residents had gathered to receive humanitarian aid. The shelter was near a local school being used to distribute aid. In what Ukrainian officials described as a double-tap attack, the first strike hit people who had come for aid, and the second struck the underground shelter where residents and first responders had taken cover. 'First, they hit people who came to receive humanitarian aid, and then rescuers and police officers who arrived at the scene,' Zelenskyy said.

Body-camera footage released Wednesday by Ukraine's national police shows the moments between the two strikes. The video, recorded on a camera worn by a police officer killed in the attack, shows stunned residents staring at the rubble of severely damaged buildings after the first blast. A voice on a two-way radio warns that more glide bombs are being launched toward the village. 'Quickly [go] to the shelter!' an officer tells residents, who are seen moving into a shelter at least two floors underground; seconds later the shelter is hit and the recording stops. BBC Verify confirmed the footage was filmed at the same location as videos released by Ukraine's State Emergency Service showing the aftermath, including a huge crater.

Zelenskyy called the strike an 'insane attack' and lamented that it had drawn 'very little reaction from the world.' 'But it, like thousands upon thousands of other war crimes Russia has committed against Ukraine and Ukrainians, demands a strong response,' he wrote on social media. 'Evil always goes further if it is left unanswered.' Search and rescue operations were continuing for a third day.

The Russian military has not publicly commented on the strike. Russia launched a full-scale invasion of Ukraine in February 2022.
