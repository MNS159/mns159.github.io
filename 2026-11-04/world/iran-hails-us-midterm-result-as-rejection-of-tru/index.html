Iran hails US midterm result as rejection of Trump's war policy

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

Iranian leaders have welcomed the sweeping defeat of US President Donald Trump's Republican Party in Tuesday's midterm elections, casting the result as a rejection by American voters of the war on Iran. Parliament Speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, who led the Iranian delegation in talks with Washington, wrote on X that Americans said "NO TO WAR" and that Tehran endorses that message. He warned that if the US government "betrays their public will to serve international paymasters and blackmailers, it will learn our NO comes with a Nightmare."

Trump acknowledged in a Fox News interview that the poor results were partly due to the war and the related surge in petrol prices, but insisted he did not regret launching a joint attack with Israel on Iran in February. "People agree with what I'm doing on Iran but they really probably weren't willing to do the short term consequences of paying more for gasoline - I understand that," he said. Speaking to the Wall Street Journal, Trump added: "I did the right thing. I would have done it again." He also blamed his absence from the ballot and the losing candidates themselves, saying some had "a lot of flaws" and became "Republican in name only."

Vice President JD Vance, a likely 2028 presidential candidate and key negotiator with Iran, appeared to diverge from Trump, writing on X: "The reason we lost is the war. The president himself said so last night." Citing a trusted pollster, Vance said ending the Iran war and changing the US relationship with Israel were among the top reasons given by Americans who voted for Trump in 2024 but planned to vote for Democrats in the 2026 midterms. Republican strategist James Davis told Al Jazeera the war contributed to the setbacks, but added that the bigger issue was the lack of a clear strategy that gives people confidence.

Iran's Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei, who has not been seen in public since being reportedly seriously wounded in the same Israeli air strike that killed his father and predecessor Ali, said he did not expect US policy to change. In a statement, he said anyone who thought there would be any change from American "hostility towards the Iranian nation" was "gravely mistaken" and warned the war would have "much wider dimensions" if it resumed. President Masoud Pezeshkian maintained that his government had been trying to resolve the crisis through dialogue and warned that no one would benefit if the war resumed. He also said the supreme leader had granted the government powers to negotiate issues previously considered off-limits.

In a separate development on Wednesday, Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) said four oil tankers caught fire after hitting naval sea mines in the Strait of Hormuz, and that its forces had shot down another US MQ-9 drone over the waterway. BBC Verify has not found any evidence that four tankers were destroyed. The UK Maritime Trade Operations centre reported that a tanker was struck by two unknown projectiles about 14 nautical miles north-east of Limah, in Oman's Musandam peninsula, on Tuesday evening, with no casualties. The Strait of Hormuz, through which a fifth of global oil and gas shipments usually pass, was effectively closed after the start of the war due to Iranian attacks and the US naval blockade. A preliminary agreement to end the war and reopen the Strait collapsed in June after Iranian attacks on shipping resumed.
