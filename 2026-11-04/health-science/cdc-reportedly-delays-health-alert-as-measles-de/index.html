CDC reportedly delays measles alert as New York reports first death in 30 years, Iowa confirms 50 cases

Health & Science desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

A child in rural northern New York has died of measles, marking the state's first measles-associated death in more than 30 years, Gov. Kathy Hochul announced Monday. The child died in Lewis County, about 93 miles (150 kilometers) north of Syracuse. The death comes as Ars Technica reported that the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention has held up a health alert, and as Iowa's Department of Health and Human Services has confirmed 50 measles cases in 2026.

According to Ars Technica, the New York death is the seventh measles-linked death reported in the U.S. this year, with all six others reported in Pennsylvania. The CDC has confirmed three measles-associated deaths in the U.S. this year, but state health departments have reported higher numbers; Pennsylvania health officials have reported five deaths and are investigating a possible sixth. Pennsylvania is experiencing a measles outbreak that has stricken more than 1,430 people this year, and, prior to the recent deaths, the state had not seen a measles-related death in 35 years.

The U.S. stopped the domestic spread of measles in 2000 by maintaining high vaccination rates, but those rates have fallen in recent years to below the 95% coverage needed to prevent outbreaks of the viral disease. New York overall has reported 170 measles cases in over a dozen counties. In early October, Hochul declared a disaster emergency because of measles cases in neighboring states and rural areas of New York; the executive order extended the pool of people who can administer measles vaccines to include paramedics, midwives and others, and it allowed nurses to join doctors in ordering testing.

Hochul, a Democrat, called the child's death "a devastating reminder of what's at stake when Washington deliberately erodes decades of progress in protecting Americans from preventable illnesses." She said she was "heartbroken" and sent condolences to the family. "As a mother and grandmother, I cannot imagine what this family is going through," she said.

The boom in vaccine-preventable outbreaks, cases and deaths is being fueled by slipping vaccination rates nationwide as health misinformation and anti-vaccine sentiments have taken hold. Robert F. Kennedy Jr., the U.S. health secretary, spent decades before taking office attacking vaccines and spreading misinformation, and his organization, Children's Health Defense, has been among the most prolific sources of anti-vaccine content. Since taking office, he has waged a multi-front war on vaccines that has included dismantling and obstructing federal vaccine recommendations; launching dubious studies to look for alleged, unspecified harms of vaccines; promoting alleged vaccine injuries; and continuing to spread misinformation. Hochul directly tied the New York death to Kennedy's policies and anti-vaccine activism.
