Ex-Madrigal executive alleges safety cover-up for liver drug Rezdiffra

Health & Science desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

A former top executive at Madrigal Pharmaceuticals has filed a federal whistleblower lawsuit alleging the company suppressed safety concerns about its liver-disease drug Rezdiffra and retaliated against him for raising the issues. Dr. Michael Charlton, who served as senior vice president and head of clinical development from October 2024 until January 2026, filed the suit on Oct. 9 in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Illinois. The lawsuit, which seeks reinstatement or monetary damages, accuses Madrigal of violating federal whistleblower-protection statutes by forcing Charlton to resign after he flagged what he says were serious problems with the drug's safety data and clinical-trial integrity.

According to the complaint, Charlton uncovered upon his arrival at Madrigal that the company had failed to recognize cases of hepatic decompensation in clinical studies and that the FDA Adverse Event Reporting System (FAERS) showed high relative rates of liver toxicity for Rezdiffra compared with other drugs. However, analysts at Citi who examined FAERS data said they found no outsized liver impact for Rezdiffra relative to marketed therapies for other liver conditions. The complaint also alleges that Madrigal co-founder and board director Dr. Rebecca Taub, who was unblinded to treatment assignments, was allowed to prescreen and edit narratives of Rezdiffra-associated liver adverse events before they were reviewed by independent expert panels, compromising trial integrity. In one instance, Taub allegedly requested the removal of a patient who had developed a life-threatening liver complication from an ongoing phase 3 trial after effective unblinding.

Charlton's concerns escalated after Madrigal presented two-year data from the open-label compensated MASH cirrhosis arm of the phase 3 Maestro-NAFLD-1 trial at the European Association for the Study of the Liver (EASL) Congress last year. A quality-control review, Charlton alleges, revealed pervasive problems: almost no data point or result in the presentation could be replicated from the underlying source data, including the primary findings on improvements in liver stiffness. When Charlton brought the discrepancies to CEO Bill Sibold, Sibold allegedly told him to 'stop looking.' Five weeks later, in June 2025, Charlton was moved to a new position that, despite a senior vice president title, had 'no significant leadership' and he lost his seat on the clinical trial protocol review committee. He resigned on Jan. 5, 2026, after concluding his role had been hollowed out.

Madrigal, in a statement, said it intends to defend itself vigorously, adding: 'Rezdiffra has improved the lives of thousands of patients living with MASH, and Madrigal stands firmly behind the safety and effectiveness of our product.' The lawsuit was disclosed in Madrigal's third-quarter securities filing and coincides with Rezdiffra crossing $1 billion in year-to-date sales for the first time since its FDA approval in March 2024. The company's stock dropped about 8% on the day the lawsuit emerged and another roughly 8% the following day, which Citi analysts attributed to the litigation. The case highlights ongoing scrutiny of Rezdiffra, the world's first approved treatment for MASH, and raises questions about the integrity of the clinical data that supported its approval.
