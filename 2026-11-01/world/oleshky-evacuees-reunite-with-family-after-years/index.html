Ukraine war

Oleshky evacuees reunite with family after years under Russian occupation

International & World desk

Hundreds of residents evacuated from the Russian-occupied Ukrainian city of Oleshky crossed into northern Ukraine via Belarus on Saturday, reuniting with loved ones they had not seen for years. The evacuation was made possible by a humanitarian ceasefire brokered by US First Lady Melania Trump that took effect across the frontline Kherson region on Tuesday, providing the first days of peace in the area in four years. Residents had a four-hour window on Friday to leave, with a convoy of 25 buses brought in for up to 750 evacuees, along with ambulances for people with limited mobility. The oldest evacuee was 94 years old, while the youngest was one.

Evacuees described desperate conditions in Oleshky, which was seized by Russia in the first days of its full-scale invasion in February 2022. Trapped between the Dnipro River and Russian forces, residents faced intense shelling, mined escape routes, and drone attacks. Many resorted to cooking garden weeds to survive. Olha, who arrived with her husband Viacheslav and their family, described life as "horror." Viacheslav said: "We came back from hell." Another evacuee, Mykhailo Palii, whose daughters Dasha and Anya sprinted across the tarmac crying as they embraced him, said: "A hole was blown into the bathroom. There were drone attacks and missile strikes." Tetiana and Volodymyr, an older couple carrying their cat, said their home burned down three years ago and they had been living in an abandoned neighbor's house. Up to 6,000 people, including 200 children, were believed to still be living in Oleshky and surrounding areas, with 1,700 in the city itself. Some elderly residents were so weak they had to be carried on stretchers.

Before the war, Oleshky was home to 24,000 people. Bridges across the Dnipro have been destroyed and the riverbank mined. Evacuees traveled through Russian-occupied territory to southern Russia, then flew to Belarus, before being driven across Ukraine's northern border. Ukrainian military positions lie just 5km (3.1 miles) from Oleshky. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky thanked Melania Trump for her efforts and said all evacuees "will receive the assistance they need." Dmytro Lubinets, Ukraine's human rights ombudsman, became the first Ukrainian official since the start of the war to visit Russian-occupied parts of Ukraine, traveling to Oleshky with his Russian counterpart Yana Lantratova. Ceasefire measures will remain in place until the end of Sunday 1 November.

Many evacuees now face the challenge of rebuilding their lives. Olha said she and her family have no relatives elsewhere in Ukraine and will stay with acquaintances. "If we managed to survive in hell, we'll survive here too," she said. Palii said he would look for any work to provide for his family. Several hundred people are understood to have decided to stay in the war-torn city, despite widespread devastation and the lack of gas, electricity and running water. Russia said it had prepared "dozens of tonnes" of aid for those who do not wish to leave. Despite the local ceasefire, the war has continued elsewhere, with Zelensky reporting Russian attacks on Ukrainian energy infrastructure and Ukraine's military striking a Russian missile fuel producer.
