Russian strikes cut power to hundreds of thousands in Ukraine despite energy ceasefire push

International & World desk

Russian overnight strikes on energy infrastructure in four Ukrainian regions left hundreds of thousands of households without power on Sunday, President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said, even as a local ceasefire around the Russian-controlled town of Oleshky held on its final day. The attacks targeted substations, transformers, and power lines in the Dnipro, Vinnytsia, Zhytomyr, and Khmelnytskyi regions, according to Zelenskyy, who wrote on Telegram that "the intensity of the strikes is very high, and the Russians are seeking to maximally exploit the deficit of ballistic missile interceptors."

Ukraine’s air force reported that it had shot down 371 of 433 long-range drones launched by Russia since Saturday evening, with dozens still in the air as of Sunday morning. Six separate drone alerts were issued in Kyiv since midnight. Meanwhile, Russia’s Defense Ministry said its forces had downed 235 Ukrainian drones over Russian regions overnight. In the Nizhny Novgorod region, Governor Gleb Nikitin said a large-scale Ukrainian drone attack caused "significant damage" to an industrial facility, which he did not identify. The region is home to the NORSI oil refinery, one of Russia’s largest. The regional government urged motorists to "buy fuel only as needed and avoid panic buying," suggesting the damaged site was linked to oil-refining or fuel-supply infrastructure. Kyiv did not immediately claim responsibility for that attack.

Zelenskyy also said Ukraine had struck the Kamensky plant in Russia’s Rostov region, which he said is involved in manufacturing Russian missiles. Moscow did not comment on that reported attack. On Friday, Russia accused Ukraine of resuming strikes on its oil refineries and of ignoring a request from U.S. President Donald Trump to stop such attacks, which Trump has blamed for raising diesel costs ahead of U.S. midterm elections on Tuesday. Ukraine says it supports a full ceasefire on energy targets, including its own battered power grid, but that Moscow is unwilling to agree to such a deal.

In an interview with state TV channel Vesti on Sunday, Russian Deputy Prime Minister Alexander Novak, who oversees the energy sector, did not mention the Nizhny Novgorod strike but said Ukrainian strikes on Russian refineries were adding to global fuel market imbalances and affecting prices. The continued long-range air attacks came as a six-day local ceasefire around the Russian-controlled Ukrainian town of Oleshky held on its final day, but there was no letup in fighting elsewhere along the front line.
