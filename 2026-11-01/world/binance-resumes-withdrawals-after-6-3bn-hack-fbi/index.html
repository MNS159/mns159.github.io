Binance resumes withdrawals after record $6.3bn hack; FBI blames North Korea

International & World desk

Binance, the world's largest cryptocurrency exchange, has begun allowing customers to withdraw funds after suspending withdrawals following a record-breaking hack on Thursday. The company said it has covered 90% of the $6.3 billion (£4.7 billion) in stolen assets using company funds, including a $1 billion secure asset fund, a bitcoin reserve, its futures insurance fund, and its corporate treasury. The remaining 10%, consisting of less liquid tokens, may take longer to repurchase, but Binance has pledged to fully cover all losses so that users do not bear the financial impact.

The hack, which occurred on or about October 29, is believed to be the largest cryptocurrency heist in history, eclipsing the previous record of a $1.5 billion theft from the Bybit exchange in 2025. Stolen assets included bitcoin, ethereum, Tether, USD Coin, and seven other widely traded cryptocurrencies. On Sunday, Binance resumed withdrawals in phases, initially covering ethereum, USDT, and USDC on the ethereum blockchain, as well as some others on the BNB Smart Chain blockchain, with Dogecoin and Avalanche resuming later. The company had frozen approximately $3 billion of stolen assets after working with law enforcement and intelligence firms, and the investigation is ongoing.

The Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI) said on Saturday that North Korea was responsible for the theft, attributing the attack to a state-backed hacking entity known as TraderTraitor, which is believed to be part of the Lazarus Group. The FBI stated it had observed the hackers converting stolen assets primarily into ethereum and expected further laundering through decentralized finance and instant swap services. The agency urged crypto exchanges and blockchain analytics firms to block transactions linked to addresses used by the actors. US President Donald Trump said Saturday that the US was still unsure of the culprits, adding, "We're learning about it. We don't know if it's North Korea — but if it is, we'll talk to North Korea about it." North Korea has consistently denied involvement in such attacks.

Binance co-chief executive Richard Teng described the breach as a "complex attack" involving more than one person, a compromised laptop, and sophisticated methods including a "self-destruct mechanism" designed to erase evidence. The company later said it had uncovered additional points of breach beyond the initial compromise. The US Secret Service said Friday it was working "around the clock" with private sector partners to identify, freeze, trace, and recover stolen funds. The hack has heightened concerns about the ability of artificial intelligence models to compromise cryptography and IT security protocols, and it has drawn renewed attention to North Korea's use of illicit cyber activities to generate revenue amid extensive international sanctions.
