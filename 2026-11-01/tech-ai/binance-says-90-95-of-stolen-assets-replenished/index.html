Binance says 90-95% of stolen assets replenished as FBI confirms North Korea link

Tech / AI desk

The FBI has confirmed that North Korea was behind the record-breaking theft of $6.3 billion in cryptocurrency from Binance on October 29, 2026. The agency labeled the malicious activity “TraderTraitor” and said it had observed actors converting the stolen assets primarily into Ethereum, with further laundering expected through decentralized finance and instant swap services. The FBI, working with the U.S. Secret Service and IRS Criminal Investigation, offered few additional details or evidence.

In the wake of the hack, Binance froze all deposits and withdrawals. The company’s CEO has now stated that 90% of the stolen assets have been replenished, and a subsequent update from the company raised that figure to 95%. Binance said it is covering the stolen funds directly and that the remaining affected assets are “less liquid in the market and take longer time to source at the required scale.” The company emphasized that the losses will not be passed on to users.

Binance resumed customer withdrawals on Sunday, November 1, in a phased process depending on network and asset type. As of Sunday morning, the company’s network status page showed a mix of normal, partially suspended, and fully suspended statuses — for example, Bitcoin withdrawals and deposits were still listed as partially suspended, while Ethereum deposits were normal and withdrawals partially suspended. Binance warned users to expect temporary delays or longer processing times due to traffic volume.

The company said it did not make the decision to freeze withdrawals lightly, calling it a necessary precaution while teams worked to contain the loss, assess the impact, restore services, and complete security checks. Binance added that resuming withdrawals is a major milestone but that work continues to strengthen safeguards and complete the recovery process. The company plans to publish a more in-depth incident report when its investigation is complete.
