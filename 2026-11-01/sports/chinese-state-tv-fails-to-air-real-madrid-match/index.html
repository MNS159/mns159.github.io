Chinese state TV fails to air Real Madrid match after Mbappe's Mao remarks

Sports desk

Chinese state broadcaster CCTV did not broadcast Real Madrid's La Liga match against Racing Santander on Sunday, days after star forward Kylian Mbappe sparked a furore in China by referring to former leader Mao Zedong as a "dictator" in a viral video. The match began at 1515 GMT but was not shown on either of CCTV's two sports channels, AFP reporters observed. No other matches from the 11th round of La Liga have been broadcast live on public television so far.

The controversy stems from a viral video in which Mbappe said the funniest internet meme about him he had seen was of his face photoshopped onto that of "some guy -- I think he's a Chinese dictator," an apparent reference to Mao. Mbappe, Real Madrid and La Liga later all apologised for his remarks. China's foreign ministry said it took the player's apology "seriously." China Media Group (CMG), which oversees CCTV, had said it moved on Monday to suspend La Liga broadcasts, adding that apologies from La Liga, Mbappe and Real Madrid were "an absolute prerequisite" for moving past the furore.

CCTV's sports website later reported that CMG had acknowledged the apologies, but it remains unclear whether talks to defuse the situation have begun. Asked by AFP before the weekend, La Liga said that Chinese television had previously shown all games live, without specifying which platforms hosted the broadcasts. AFP journalists did find live broadcasts of matches on Migu, a digital streaming service owned by the state-owned China Mobile telecom network, including Barcelona's 6-1 win over Alaves on Saturday. However, the Racing Santander–Real Madrid match did not appear to be broadcast on Sunday on Migu.

China is a huge market for European football, with CCTV also carrying matches from the Premier League as well as the German, French and Italian top flights. Real Madrid coach Jose Mourinho said on Saturday that the club had "done our part" to put the controversy behind them. Major Chinese brands cancelled sponsorship deals with Mbappe after his comments. It is taboo in China to crack jokes about Mao, who died in 1976 and whose drastic policies are blamed by historians for mass starvation and millions of deaths.

Various overseas sports leagues and players have found themselves in hot water for comments on topics that are politically sensitive to China. China effectively cut ties with the NBA for several years from 2019 after then-Houston Rockets general manager Daryl Morey tweeted his support for Hong Kong protesters.
