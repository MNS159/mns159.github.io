Former European champion Ciara Mageean dies aged 34

Sports desk

Ciara Mageean, the former European 1500m champion and two-time Olympian from Northern Ireland, has died at the age of 34. She was diagnosed with stage four bowel cancer in May 2025. A statement posted on her social media on Sunday said: 'At 6.30am this morning, Ciara lost her battle with cancer surrounded by her loved ones. A beloved daughter, sister, wife, friend and inspiration to so many, she will be deeply missed.'

Mageean, from Portaferry, represented Ireland at the 2016 and 2020 Olympics but was forced to withdraw from the 2024 Paris Games because of an Achilles injury. Earlier in 2024, she clinched the first major title of her career when she took 1500m gold at the European Championships in Rome, beating Great Britain's Georgia Bell and Jemma Reekie in a gripping final. She also won 1500m silver for Northern Ireland at the 2022 Commonwealth Games in Birmingham and claimed European silver in the same year behind Laura Muir.

Her junior career included 1500m silvers at the 2010 World Junior Championships and 2011 European Junior Championships, and she reached the 2010 Commonwealth Games final as an 18-year-old. A serious ankle injury ended her hopes of making her Olympic debut at London 2012. After surgery in 2013, she returned to win the Irish 1500m title in July 2014. In 2016, she won European bronze in Amsterdam but exited the Rio Olympics at the semi-final stage.

Mageean moved to Manchester in late 2017 to join Steve Vernon's Team New Balance. She claimed European Indoor bronze in March 2019 and broke Sonia O'Sullivan's 27-year-old Irish 1,000m record in 2020. At the delayed Tokyo Olympics in 2021, a calf injury disrupted her preparations and she exited in the heats. In 2022, under coach Helen Clitheroe, she beat Muir to win the Diamond League Brussels 1500m in 3:56.63, breaking O'Sullivan's Irish record and recording her first sub-four-minute run. In 2023, she finished fourth in the World Championship final in Budapest and was named BBC Northern Ireland Sports Personality of the Year after breaking O'Sullivan's Irish mile record.

In October 2024, Mageean married her long-term partner Thomas Moran in a small civil ceremony in Belfast. Earlier this year, after her cancer diagnosis, she said she was 'continuing to find the joy in everyday' and told the BBC she was 'grateful' for having a career 'that many others didn't get to have'. Athletics Ireland said in a statement: 'We are heartbroken. An inspiration to so many, Ciara will be remembered for her courage, kindness and incredible spirit. Our deepest sympathies are with her family, friends and loved ones at this difficult time. Rest in peace, Ciara.'
