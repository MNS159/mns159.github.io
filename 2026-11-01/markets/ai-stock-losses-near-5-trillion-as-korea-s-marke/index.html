AI stock losses near $5 trillion as Korea's market rout deepens global AI selloff

Financial & Markets desk

The biggest AI and chip stocks have shed about $5 trillion in market value since Oct. 6, according to Yahoo Finance calculations, with Nvidia and Alphabet each losing roughly $835 billion in market cap and Intel losing nearly half its value in October. The broader U.S. stock market, however, barely noticed: the other 625 large U.S. stocks tracked added about $1.3 trillion, and nine of the S&P 500's 11 sectors rose roughly 1% or more in a single week, a feat not seen since at least 1999. Jeff Hirsch, editor of the Stock Trader's Almanac, calls the pattern 'Octoberphobia' and said he bought into the sell-off on Friday, calling it 'an opportunity.' Hirsch noted that October selloffs like this one often lead into what the Almanac calls the sweet spot of the presidential election cycle, which runs from the fourth quarter of a midterm year through the following June. Since 1950, the S&P 500 has risen in every such period (19 of 19), with a median gain of about 15%; chip stocks have been 8-for-8 since 1994, with a median gain of about 22%.

The sell-off has been especially severe in South Korea, where the KOSPI index crashed from a June 2026 peak, losing more than $1 trillion in market capitalization by mid-July. The index had risen 241% from March 2025 to June 2026, adding nearly $3.3 trillion, but then entered a bear market, shrinking more than a quarter in less than a month. SK hynix and Samsung, which together control 83% of the global high-bandwidth memory (HBM) market and 63% of the DRAM market, fell 38% and 29% respectively in that window. Micron, the closest U.S. competitor, fell about a third. The iShares Semiconductor ETF (SOXX) tumbled about 17% in October, though it remains up more than 50% for the year; on Friday it dipped as low as $460, below the $464 July low that marked the summer's bottom, before closing at $472, back above that line. Hirsch described that pattern as a 'bear trap' — a breakdown that reverses, catching traders betting on more losses. His firm placed a new buy in the sector on Friday.

Korea's volatility is rooted in its shallow capital markets and extreme concentration in memory chips. As of July 2026, Samsung Electronics and SK hynix alone accounted for more than half of the KOSPI's total market capitalization, compared with the Magnificent Seven U.S. stocks making up 37.2% of the S&P 500. The Korea discount — a structural undervaluation of listed enterprises due to lagging financial development and weak corporate governance — combines with a memory discount that depresses valuations for HBM makers. In May and June 2026, foreign investment flowed out of South Korean equities at a record pace, with capital flight exceeding $35 billion. The Financial Services Commission responded by suspending sales of leveraged single-stock ETFs, which had come to dominate trading, peaking at 84% of all volume in June, and tripled the minimum deposit required to trade them to $20,300, effective Aug. 5. The Iran war's re-escalation added pressure through currency weakness and rising energy costs.

Memory has become the highest revenue-generating segment in the global semiconductor supply chain, with Gartner projecting memory revenue to surpass $1 trillion and account for more than 55% of all semiconductor revenue by 2027. South Korea's DRAM exports have increased more than fivefold year-on-year, and semiconductor export volumes have more than doubled. A global memory shortage persists: Samsung has earmarked 70% of its memory production for long-term agreements through 2030, and SK hynix CEO Kwak Noh-Jung has said the shortage could persist through 2030. DRAM prices rose more than 400% from 2024 to 2026, according to JP Morgan. The emergence of CXMT, a Chinese state-backed firm, introduces risks of increased competition and downward pricing pressure that could challenge the incumbent memory triopoly of SK hynix, Samsung, and Micron.

Not all analysts are buying the dip. BofA's Michael Hartnett said it is 'risk-off until global rate hike ends,' pointing to 42 central bank rate hikes worldwide over the past six months. The Fed held rates on Wednesday, but two officials voted to hike. Hartnett's line in the sand for chips is $520 on the VanEck Semiconductor ETF (SMH), which closed Friday near $522; on SOXX that works out to about $469, just above the July low it barely held. Hirsch, by contrast, sees history on the buyers' side: 'Historically, seasonally, it sets up great for this period of time, the best months of the year for semiconductors.' Bulls first need to reclaim about $524, the top of last week's bounce, then the early-October high near $596. Losing the July low would put the February-April range near $370 in play, about 21% below Friday's close. The June-July 2026 crash in Korea, analyst Richard Gray of a Washington foreign policy think tank argues, forewarns what may come in future market corrections, with the threat of a cascading correction across global markets and the U.S.-China tech competition looming large.
