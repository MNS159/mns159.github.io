Sexual assaults happening almost every day in Ceuta, prosecutors say

International & World desk

Spanish prosecutors have recorded 23 sexual assaults, including nine against children, in Ceuta since the mass influx of migrants into the Spanish exclave at the end of July, meaning there has been almost one case every day, chief prosecutor Silvia Rojas said. Rojas told El País that migrant girls were 'particularly vulnerable because of their irregular status in the territory.' She said the influx had led to an exponential increase in sexual assault and that many victims did not file a report, either out of fear or because of their 'current situation in Ceuta.'

At least 72,000 migrants crossed from Morocco into Ceuta on 30 and 31 July. Although most have returned, as many as 5,000 remain, leading to unrest in the autonomous city and a crisis for Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez. Between 1,400 and 1,600 of the estimated 5,000 migrants still in Ceuta are children, according to Rojas. Among the suspected attackers are four Moroccans, three Spaniards and a Belgian, the Efe news agency reports.

The Spanish Society of Epidemiology said the border crisis had highlighted the difficulty in detecting and tackling violence against migrant women and girls. Economy Minister Carlos Cuerpo said a multi-million euro aid package had been approved by the cabinet to help provide essential services and cover care for unaccompanied children. The territory's conservative mayor, Juan Jesús Vivas, has called on Spaniards to take part in demonstrations across mainland Spain in support of Ceuta on Wednesday, which coincides with the city's annual holiday.

The Spanish government is facing bitter criticism from its opponents for its handling of the crisis. The hard-right Vox party said Sánchez had not 'lost control of Ceuta - he has handed it over.' Arantxa Campos, president of the Ceuta Employers' Confederation, told the BBC: 'We can't do anything unless these people leave. We don't have a plan from the government. We don't have anything. We don't even know when they're going to finish screening everyone.'

Sánchez has been careful to avoid blaming Morocco for stoking the surge of migrants, saying on Monday that no intelligence reports pointed to 'any involvement in one way or another' from the Moroccan authorities. Instead he focused on disinformation on social media 'associated with both Russia and Israel and an international far-right movement that always uses migration, not only to attack Spain but also Europe.' Government spokeswoman Elma Saiz later confirmed his allegation, saying there was evidence that 'various digital forums amplified what happened with the intention of fracturing and polarising Europe.' Russia and Israel have both denied any involvement; Israeli Foreign Minister Gideon Sa'ar accused the Spanish prime minister of 'a blatant lie' and Russia's foreign ministry spokeswoman demanded proof.

The prime minister said on Monday that King Felipe VI would visit Ceuta 'as soon as conditions allow,' seen as an attempt to assert Spanish authority over the exclave. Morocco has a long-standing claim over Ceuta and its fellow autonomous city of Melilla. The last time a Spanish head of state visited the territories in 2007, Rabat pulled its ambassador from Madrid, and another royal visit may cause further diplomatic tensions. Sánchez also asserted he was the only prime minister to have made an official visit to Ceuta while in office, having travelled there on 31 July as the crisis took hold. The opposition People's Party has criticised Sánchez for 'exonerating' Morocco; spokesperson Borja Sémper said tens of thousands of people could not have moved towards the Ceuta border without Morocco's knowledge. Morocco has denied orchestrating or being involved in the mass crossing.
