Russian Missile and Drone Strikes Kill 12 in Kyiv, Including Railway Workers

International & World desk

At least 12 people were killed and 20 injured, including two children, in overnight Russian missile and drone strikes on Kyiv and the surrounding region, Ukrainian officials said Tuesday. Six of the casualties were railway workers of the state-owned Ukrzaliznytsia company after a depot was hit in the capital.

In Kyiv, eight people were killed and fires were reported in three city districts. Another four people were killed in the nearby town of Boryspil, where a residential building was struck. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky said nearly 350 rescue workers had worked through the night, and that Russia had fired a 'significant number of jet drones and ballistic missiles.' The Interior Ministry later confirmed that a 26-year-old Indian national was among those killed in Boryspil.

Russia said its 'massive strike using high-precision weapons' targeted Ukraine's military and energy facilities. The attack marks the sixth consecutive day of sustained Russian drone and missile strikes on Kyiv, a strategy analysts say is aimed at exhausting Ukrainian air defenses and undermining civilian morale. Russia's military had warned over the weekend that it was preparing to carry out 'massive' strikes on Ukrainian energy facilities. Zelensky added that eight other Ukrainian regions were also attacked.

The overnight strikes come a day after Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi, a close ally of Russian President Vladimir Putin, urged an end to the war. 'Every day spent in war pushes humanity back, and every step taken towards peace fills humanity with new hope and enthusiasm,' Modi said at a security summit in Kyrgyzstan. India's economy depends heavily on discounted oil from Moscow, and Modi has made several similar pleas since Russia launched its full-scale invasion in February 2022.

Separately, a drone attack hit a major Russian Baltic Sea port in Ust-Luga, triggering a fire, Leningrad regional Governor Aleksandr Drozdenko said. Ukraine says such targets, which handle millions of tons of petroleum products annually, are legitimate because they help finance Moscow's war effort. The latest attacks follow a strike Friday that killed 38 people at a weapons depot in the Kyiv region, causing shells, mines and drones to detonate.
