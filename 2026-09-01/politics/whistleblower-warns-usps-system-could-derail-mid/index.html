Whistleblower warns USPS system could derail midterm mail voting

Politics desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts slightly center-left
✓ Facts
  • A whistleblower disclosure, prepared by the nonprofit Whistleblower Aid on behalf of an anonymous federal official, was submitted Monday to USPS by Sen. Richard Blumenthal (D-Conn.).
  • The disclosure concerns a new USPS online portal, built to let state election officials submit absentee voters' names and ballot-envelope barcodes required under President Trump's executive order on mail voting.
  • The whistleblower says the system was rushed toward a September 1 implementation deadline and was insufficiently tested, and cites a 'zero-percent failure policy' in ballot verification that could delay mailing of ballots 'by the thousands' through repeated verification cycles.
  • Mail voting for the midterms was set to officially begin the week this story was reported.
  • A federal judge in Boston issued a temporary restraining order last week blocking key parts of USPS's implementation plan, ruling it likely illegal because USPS lacks authority to regulate mail-in voting; the Trump administration has appealed to the 1st U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals.
  • Blumenthal sent a letter to Postmaster General David Steiner urging USPS to abandon the plan, calling it 'ill-conceived' and warning it could interfere with voting rights.
  • President Trump has defended the underlying executive order as an effort to curb voting by non-U.S. citizens, a practice that studies and audits cited in the reporting describe as extremely rare; Trump himself has voted by mail.
  • USPS did not respond to NPR's request for comment on the whistleblower disclosure, and NPR notes USPS is a financial supporter of NPR.
○ Analysis

This story sits at the intersection of an ongoing legal fight over a Trump executive order aimed at tightening mail-in voting rules and the operational question of whether USPS can build the required verification technology safely and on time. The order would require states to submit voter and ballot-envelope data through a new USPS portal, but courts have already found the mailing agency likely lacks legal authority to impose such conditions on state-run elections, leading to an injunction now under appeal. Independent of the legal outcome, the whistleblower disclosure raises a separate, more technical concern: that the system itself, built on an unusually compressed timeline with a rigid error-tolerance standard, could fail in ways that delay or block ballots regardless of the courts' ultimate ruling on the order's legality.

◐ Debate

Democratic

  • Sen. Blumenthal argues the USPS plan is 'unconscionable' and risks systemic failure that would infringe on Americans' constitutional right to vote by mail, framing the rushed technology as evidence the agency prioritized speed over voter access.
  • Blumenthal characterizes the zero-percent failure design as effectively engineered to fail, suggesting the disruption to mail voting may not be accidental but a foreseeable consequence of the rules imposed by the executive order.

Republican

  • President Trump has defended his mail-voting directives as necessary to prevent illegal voting by non-U.S. citizens, positioning the USPS verification requirements as an election-integrity safeguard rather than a voter-suppression tool.
  • Proponents of the order would likely argue that new verification technology, even if imperfect at launch, is a legitimate and correctable step toward tighter election security, and that courts—not the whistleblower's technical critique—should determine the policy's ultimate fate.
Steelman

Even setting aside the unresolved legal question of whether USPS has authority to impose these rules at all, the dispute exposes a genuine operational risk: implementing untested verification technology on a compressed timeline could cause ballot delays that affect voters regardless of one's view on the underlying election-integrity policy.

Implications

The courts—first the 1st Circuit and potentially the Supreme Court—will likely determine in the coming weeks whether USPS's plan can proceed at all for the midterms, while separately, if the portal is ultimately deployed, its real-world performance under the described 'zero-percent failure' standard will be closely watched by election officials, voters, and lawmakers on both sides for signs of the ballot delays the whistleblower warns about.
