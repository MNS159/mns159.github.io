Fed Governor Barr Says He Would Support Rate Hike if Inflation Doesn't Ease

Financial & Markets desk · anchored on Reuters

Federal Reserve Governor Michael Barr said Tuesday he would be prepared to support an interest rate increase if inflation does not moderate, adding to a growing chorus of central bank officials signaling a possible tightening at the next policy meeting. Speaking at a banking forum in Washington, Barr said he is concerned about "broader price pressures taking hold" as inflation has remained above the Fed's 2% target for nearly 5½ years.

"If trends in the data give me some confidence that inflation is moderating on a path to 2%, then I think we can take a bit more time to assess our policy stance," Barr said in prepared remarks. "However, if inflation appears not to be moderating sufficiently, then I think we should act decisively to raise rates."

Barr, a permanent voting member on the rate-setting Federal Open Market Committee, supported the July decision to hold the benchmark federal funds rate in a range of 3.5% to 3.75%. Markets Tuesday morning were pricing in about a 66% chance of a rate increase at the next meeting in two weeks, according to the CME Group's FedWatch tool. The comments come as Treasury yields have risen again, with the benchmark 10-year note reaching a level not seen since mid-January 2025, amid fresh worries over the precarious Middle East situation.

Fed Chairman Kevin Warsh last week delivered remarks that markets widely interpreted as tilted toward a rate hike, possibly as soon as the next policy meeting. The most recent inflation readings showed headline prices up 3.7% over the past year, or 3.3% excluding food and energy. The Fed will get one more look at inflation data when the consumer and producer price indexes are released next week.

Barr gave the economy good marks even with elevated inflation. "Consumer spending to date has been largely resilient," he said. "But inflation remains too high — and has been for over five years."
