Stocks Fall as Rising Bond Yields, Oil Prices, and Rate-Hike Fears Weigh

Financial & Markets desk

U.S. stocks opened lower Tuesday, weighed by a jump in bond yields, rising oil prices tied to renewed U.S.-Iran hostilities, and growing speculation that the Federal Reserve may resume interest-rate hikes. The Dow Jones Industrial Average fell about 0.5% in early trading before recovering to a loss of roughly 0.1%, while the S&P 500 lost 0.3%. The tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite dropped 0.7% after initially sliding 1.3%. The declines come as stocks enter September, historically the weakest month for equities, with the S&P 500 averaging a 0.6% loss and posting a positive return only 45% of the time, according to Carson Group data.

Treasury yields continued their march higher, with the 10-year note reaching 4.75%, its highest intraday level since January 2025, before settling near 4.76%. The 30-year bond climbed to 5.27%, hovering near multi-decade highs, before pulling back to 5.24%. Meanwhile, crude oil prices remained elevated as the conflict between the U.S. and Iran escalated. Brent futures traded above $92 per barrel after two oil tankers were struck while attempting to exit the Strait of Hormuz. President Donald Trump warned on Fox News that the U.S. would respond to Iran’s retaliatory strikes on American bases in Jordan, saying, "We're going to hit them hard."

Economic data released Tuesday provided a mixed picture. The Job Openings and Labor Turnover Survey (JOLTS) showed job openings ticked up slightly in July, while the Institute for Supply Management reported that U.S. manufacturing activity expanded for the eighth consecutive month in August, though the pace of expansion slowed modestly. The ISM manufacturing index came in at 55.2, slightly below the 55.6 reading in July, but above the 50 threshold indicating growth. The data arrive ahead of the monthly jobs report scheduled for Friday.

Sector action in early trading showed technology and consumer discretionary stocks under the most pressure. The iShares Expanded Tech-Software Sector ETF had surged over 16% in August, while the iShares Semiconductor ETF finished August up only 1%, after giving back most of its mid-month gains. Some strategists cautioned that the current environment could bring more volatility. LPL Financial chief technical strategist Adam Turnquist said, "There's a lot of macro variables right now that I think can fuel upside in volatility," citing geopolitical tensions and rising bets of a Fed rate hike. He added that a selloff could present a buying opportunity.

Among individual movers, Microsoft shares rose after Bank of America raised its price target to $600 from $500, citing accelerating cloud growth and improving AI efficiency. The analysts noted strength in Microsoft's AI model portfolio and infrastructure integration. Elsewhere, fast-fashion retailer Shein made its Hong Kong Stock Exchange debut, with shares plunging over 9% from the IPO price before recovering slightly to close below the offer price. Its market capitalization stood at $26.5 billion, a fraction of its $100 billion private valuation in 2022. Meanwhile, strategists at Citadel Securities and JPMorgan said they have turned temporarily cautious, though neither believes the bull market is over.
