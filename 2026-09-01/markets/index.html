Bonds

Global Bond Yields Surge as Inflation Fears and Middle East Tensions Rise

Financial & Markets desk

Global government bond yields jumped to multi-decade highs Tuesday as an escalation in Middle East conflict pushed oil prices higher and deepened investor worries that inflation will keep central banks from easing—or even prompt them to raise rates. Japan’s 10-year yield hit 3% for the first time since 1996, Britain’s 30-year yield reached its highest since 1998, Germany’s 10-year yield was at its highest since 2011 and France’s 10-year yield hit its highest since 2008. In the United States, the 10-year Treasury yield rose to 4.79%, the highest since January 2025, while the 30-year yield reached 5.27%; U.S. 30-year yields had already hit their highest since 2007 earlier in August. U.S. stocks opened lower, with the S&P 500 down roughly 0.6%-0.7% and the Nasdaq down about 1%-1.3%.

Fed Governor Barr Says He Would Support Rate Hike if Inflation Doesn't Ease

Financial & Markets desk · anchored on Reuters

Federal Reserve Governor Michael Barr said Tuesday he would be prepared to support an interest rate increase if inflation does not moderate, adding to a growing chorus of central bank officials signaling a possible tightening at the next policy meeting. Speaking at a banking forum in Washington, Barr said he is concerned about "broader price pressures taking hold" as inflation has remained above the Fed's 2% target for nearly 5½ years.

Equities

Stocks Fall as Rising Bond Yields, Oil Prices, and Rate-Hike Fears Weigh

Financial & Markets desk

U.S. stocks opened lower Tuesday, weighed by a jump in bond yields, rising oil prices tied to renewed U.S.-Iran hostilities, and growing speculation that the Federal Reserve may resume interest-rate hikes. The Dow Jones Industrial Average fell about 0.5% in early trading before recovering to a loss of roughly 0.1%, while the S&P 500 lost 0.3%. The tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite dropped 0.7% after initially sliding 1.3%. The declines come as stocks enter September, historically the weakest month for equities, with the S&P 500 averaging a 0.6% loss and posting a positive return only 45% of the time, according to Carson Group data.
