Measles deaths in Pennsylvania spark feud among politicians and health officials

Health & Science desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts slightly center-left

Pennsylvania officials this week reported that two unvaccinated residents died after being infected with measles, with Gov. Josh Shapiro stressing that vaccinations could have averted the deaths. The announcement quickly came under fire from U.S. Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr., a Trump administration appointee, who said in a social post the deaths "may even have been altogether fabricated." The dispute has ignited a public feud among politicians and health officials over the role of the contagious disease in the fatalities.

Medical experts say the brouhaha has diverted focus from an exploding public health threat. "Now it's turned political," said Dr. Amesh Adalja, an infectious disease doctor and senior scholar at the Johns Hopkins Center for Health Security. "It's no longer about getting answers and figuring this out." Dr. Paul Offit, a vaccine researcher at Children's Hospital of Philadelphia, expressed concern that two measles-related deaths in a county with about 400 cases is unusually high; the expected death rate for measles is 1 in 1,000 cases, suggesting a large number of unreported infections in the Lancaster County area.

What is not disputed is that both people who died had been infected with measles and were unvaccinated. The unresolved question is whether measles was the main or a contributing cause. The Lancaster County Coroner, Dr. Stephen Diamantoni, said he is aware of only one death with evidence of measles infection: a newborn baby boy who died August 14 — too young to be vaccinated. Investigators found measles infection in lung tissue but attributed the death to a tear in the spleen. "The baby did not die as a result of measles," Diamantoni said. The Pennsylvania Department of Health stands by its announcement, stating it relies on two questions: Did the person have measles and did they die? No information has been released about the second death.

The United States eliminated the domestic spread of measles in2000 by maintaining high vaccination rates, but rates have fallen in recent years to below the95% coverage needed to prevent outbreaks. In November, international health officials will meet to determine if the U.S. and Mexico have lost their measles-free status. The World Health Organization counts a death as measles-related when a person tests positive and dies within a month of rash onset, unless the death is obviously due to another cause such as trauma.

As of Friday, the CDC has not updated the latest national tally of measles cases and deaths, citing a delay without explanation. Offit warned that vaccination efforts need to be stepped up "to prevent what I think is a coming disaster," especially with children returning to school. Adalja faulted Pennsylvania officials for how they announced the deaths, saying it should have been only health and medical officials stating what is known and not yet known. "They should learn to keep the politicians out of it," he said.
