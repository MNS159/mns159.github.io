Novartis and Bristol Myers Squibb pause cell therapy trials after deaths and inflammatory side effects

Health & Science desk · anchored on Reuters

Novartis and Bristol Myers Squibb have paused multiple trials testing their cell therapy programs against autoimmune conditions after inflammatory side effects emerged in testing, including three patient deaths in Novartis trials.

The deaths occurred in Novartis trials of the CAR-T treatment rap-cel, where patients died from a dangerous immune system reaction. Bristol Myers Squibb also voluntarily halted enrollment in autoimmune trials of its CAR-T treatment zola-cel.

Both cell therapies are being developed with technology intended to speed up production compared with earlier CAR-T products, a factor that may help explain the observed inflammatory side effects.

The pauses affect multiple trials across both companies' cell therapy programs for autoimmune conditions, underscoring safety concerns as the manufacturers continue testing these experimental treatments.
