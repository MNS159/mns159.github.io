Hackers claim millions of patient records stolen in data breach at healthcare giant McKesson

Tech / AI desk

One of the most prolific data-extortion hacking groups has taken credit for a cyberattack against U.S. pharmaceutical distribution giant McKesson, claiming to have stolen millions of rows of patient data including Social Security numbers, diagnoses, and medications. The breach, which McKesson confirmed Friday, is the latest in a string of cyberattacks targeting major healthcare companies.

The ShinyHunters hacking group told TechCrunch that it compromised McKesson’s cloud environment by tricking several employees into granting access through phishing and social engineering tactics. The hackers said they stole a range of personal information, including names, addresses, and Social Security numbers, as well as protected health information such as diagnoses, medications, allergies, and patient notes. They also took McKesson employees’ home addresses. The hackers said they are unsure how many individuals are ultimately affected but claimed to have taken millions of rows of patient data from the company’s cloud-hosted Snowflake and Salesforce environments. ShinyHunters shared screenshots and a sample of the stolen data with TechCrunch, which verified a small subset against public records.

McKesson, based in Texas, is one of the largest U.S. distributors of pharmaceuticals, medicines, medical supplies, and technology to hospitals and healthcare providers nationwide, handling large volumes of patient data. In a statement on its website, the company confirmed that hackers broke into several of its cloud-hosted accounts earlier in the week and exfiltrated data, and that it expected “intermittent service degradation” related to the incident. In a separate notice to customers, Chief Technology Officer Francisco Fraga said the stolen data relates to McKesson’s oncology & multispecialty and medical-surgical units.

Bleeping Computer, which first reported the link to ShinyHunters, said the hackers demanded a $55 million ransom from the company in exchange for not publicly releasing the stolen files. In a statement, McKesson spokesperson Kristina Chang said the company “continues to operate in all lines of business” and reiterated its public statement, adding that McKesson believes it has no ongoing unauthorized activity in its systems. The company declined to answer TechCrunch’s questions about the incident, including what the hackers demanded or how many individuals had data affected.

McKesson is the latest healthcare company or medical device maker to be targeted in a wave of cyberattacks in recent months, as hackers aim to steal large amounts of sensitive medical data to extort companies into paying ransoms. Last week, medical device maker Boston Scientific was hit by a cyberattack that knocked much of its network offline. Earlier this year, another device maker, Stryker, experienced an attack in which hackers abused internal tools to remotely wipe thousands of employee devices. Abbott Laboratories and Medtronic have also experienced cyberattacks, while electronic patient records provider CareCloud and health tech company TriZetto had breaches affecting over 3 million patients each. ShinyHunters has also taken credit for sizable data breaches at Amazon-owned One Medical and dental insurance company DentaQuest.
