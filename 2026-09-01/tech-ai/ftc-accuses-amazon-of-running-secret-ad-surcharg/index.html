FTC, 22 states sue Amazon over alleged secret ad surcharge scheme

Tech / AI desk

The Federal Trade Commission (FTC) and 22 states filed a lawsuit Monday accusing Amazon of secretly charging businesses more for advertising on its platform through a practice the agency described as a 'secret ad surcharge scheme.' The complaint alleges that over more than seven years, Amazon quietly increased the prices advertisers paid through its online ad auctions, affecting more than 1 million brands and sellers and potentially generating tens of billions of dollars in additional revenue for the company.

The lawsuit centers on Amazon's Sponsored Products, Sponsored Brands, and Display ads that appear alongside search results. According to the FTC, Amazon told more than 500,000 small and medium-sized businesses that it operated a 'second-price' auction, where the winning advertiser would pay just one cent more than the next-highest bid, not the full amount of its own bid. The FTC alleges that starting in 2019, Amazon made a 'surreptitious' change without informing advertisers: it added a hidden surcharge it internally called a 'soft reserve price' and used what one internal document termed an 'invented auction participant'—a fake bidder—to push prices higher than true competition would have produced. The complaint characterizes this as a shill bid, arguing that rather than the price coming from a real competing advertiser, Amazon manufactured a higher number for advertisers to beat. As a result, the FTC claims Amazon charged Sponsored Products advertisers their own full winning bid close to 80% of the time, effectively turning what was marketed as a second-price auction into a first-price one.

The FTC contends Amazon made the change because it wanted more advertising revenue and kept it hidden because disclosing it could have led advertisers to lower their bids, cutting into that revenue. Amazon generated more than $68 billion in advertising revenue last year. The 22 states joining the FTC are Alaska, Arizona, California, Colorado, Florida, Idaho, Illinois, Indiana, Iowa, Kentucky, Louisiana, Maryland, Nebraska, New Jersey, New York, North Carolina, Oklahoma, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island, South Carolina, Vermont, and Washington.

In a blog post, Amazon described the FTC's lawsuit as 'misguided,' arguing that the complaint 'fundamentally misunderstands how advertisers operate.' The company stated that its auctions evaluate billions of bids across different placements and formats, so prices naturally vary, and that advertisers are 'properly' informed about the pricing system.
