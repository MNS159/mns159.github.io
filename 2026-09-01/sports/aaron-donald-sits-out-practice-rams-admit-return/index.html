Aaron Donald sits out practice; Rams admit returning star might not play Week 1 in Australia

Sports desk

Aaron Donald, who ended his two-year retirement and rejoined the Los Angeles Rams on Sunday, was absent from practice Monday. Coach Sean McVay said the defensive tackle’s absence was by design — part of a plan to ease the 35-year-old back into football shape — but acknowledged that Donald might not be ready for the season opener against the San Francisco 49ers in Melbourne, Australia.

“Aaron not being out there was part of the plan all along, so that was par for the course,” McVay said after Monday’s session. Donald, a three-time Defensive Player of the Year and 10-time Pro Bowler, had practiced on Sunday after officially announcing his return, but he has not played in an NFL game since January 2024. McVay said the team is trying to determine how much Donald can handle as he ramps up. “I know it is annoying as hell just saying, ‘We’re taking it a day at a time.’ But it is a unique thing. This has never been done before.”

When asked directly about the possibility of Donald missing the Week 1 game in Australia, McVay did not rule it out. “Oh, yeah, absolutely. That could be a possibility. I wouldn’t rule anything out yet. I think sometimes when you put unnecessary parameters or guidelines on something it can skew things.” The Rams are scheduled to fly to Melbourne next week, and McVay indicated the team will likely decide on Donald’s status before the trip to avoid an unnecessary 15-hour flight. “If he’s not ready and that is not what is best for him and for the team, absolutely wouldn’t have him play in Australia unnecessarily.”

Donald himself was non-committal about playing in Week 1. “I need to be at the level that I want to play at and that’s all I can do. Ain’t rushing [anything]. Like I said, just continue to stack them days and see where we [are] at,” he said Sunday. The Rams’ revised contract with Donald appears to account for a possible absence. His one-year, $30 million deal includes $10 million in bonus money tied to being on the active roster each week, paying out at $666,667 per game for a maximum of 15 games, meaning missing Week 1 or a potential Week 18 rest would not cost him any of that bonus.

McVay said the team wants to see Donald on the practice field before making a decision. “The first step was, ‘all right, if you’re coming back let us do that, let us get you out on the practice field. Let us get you acclimated to your teammates to your coaches,’ and then we’ll see what that ramp up period looks like.” The 49ers are scheduled to leave for Australia on Sept. 2, but the Rams will depart later next week, giving McVay and Donald a few more days to assess his readiness for the season opener.

Sources: CBS Sports
