North Korea suspected in record $6.3bn Binance hack

International & World desk

Binance, the world's largest cryptocurrency exchange, has confirmed that $6.3bn (£4.7bn) in digital assets were stolen in a hack that two major blockchain analytics firms say was carried out with 'high confidence' by a North Korean state-backed hacking unit. The theft, which eclipses the previous record of $1.5bn stolen from the Bybit exchange in February 2025, is the largest known cryptocurrency heist. TRM Labs and Chainalysis both identified the Lazarus Group, also known as TraderTraitor, as the likely culprit. Binance said it has frozen around half of the stolen funds—$3bn—but the rest remains unaccounted for, and the company has set aside more than $6.34bn in company funds to cover customer losses.

Binance initially believed the breach began with a compromised DevOps engineer's laptop, but co-CEO Richard Teng later said the investigation uncovered 'additional points of breach.' He described the attackers as using 'a self-destruct mechanism that destroyed tooling and traces as they went, making it more difficult for us to trace and investigate.' TRM Labs said the hackers likely gained access to Binance's hot wallet signing system, using a special authorization to turn a hot wallet—which is connected to the internet—into one that moved funds automatically to the attacker. Chainalysis noted the attacker used laundering patterns 'we have not seen before.'

North Korea, one of the world's poorest countries with severe internet restrictions, has long been accused by experts and governments of using cybercrime to finance itself, including its nuclear weapons programme. A UN panel of experts reported in spring 2024 that it was reviewing 58 suspected cyber-attacks by North Korea on cryptocurrency companies between 2017 and 2023 worth $3bn, estimating that the stolen cash generates half of the country's foreign currency income. One unnamed country told the panel the stolen billions fund '40% of the weapons of mass destruction programmes.' Pyongyang has denied these allegations. The FBI has broadly accused North Korea of 'illicit cybercrime and virtual asset theft activities' and previously attributed the $1.5bn Bybit hack to TraderTraitor.

The US Secret Service said it was 'working around the clock' to identify, freeze, trace, and recover stolen funds, and Binance said it is cooperating with the FBI. While Binance's US-facing exchange was unaffected, the US has jurisdiction because the hack involved US-linked assets such as stablecoins Tether and Circle. Binance told users 'your money is safe, and we're covering every loss.' However, several experts have warned customers that the promise to cover losses may not be legally binding. Daniel Tunkel of the London-based Edwin Coe law firm said recovery would 'come down to what [customers] have been promised in their contracts' and where Binance can be sued for non-performance. The hack spooked cryptocurrency markets on Friday, with Bitcoin falling 5% to $76,800 and Coinbase dropping 12%.

Ciaran Martin, former head of the UK's National Cyber Security Centre, noted that traditional banks have developed sophisticated defences that the fast-growing and lightly regulated crypto sector has yet to match, making it a target. North Korea was behind one of the last large cyber thefts on a traditional bank—the 2016 Bangladesh Bank attack, where hackers tried to illicitly move $951m from an account at the Federal Reserve Bank of New York, ultimately stealing $81m. Experts say the rise of artificial intelligence has given threat actors new ways to carry out crime, and in this case it is plausible that AI was used to infiltrate Binance's systems. The heist underscores how powerful suspected North Korean hackers have become, with tools that have grown sharper over time.
