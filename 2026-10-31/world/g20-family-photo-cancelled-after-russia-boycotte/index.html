G20 family photo cancelled after European allies refuse to appear with Russia's Lavrov

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts slightly center-left

The traditional “family photo” to open the G20 foreign ministers’ meeting was abruptly cancelled on Friday in Atlanta after a host of nations—including Australia, the UK, and several European countries—refused to appear beside Russian Foreign Minister Sergei Lavrov. US Secretary of State Marco Rubio pulled the plug on the snapshot after about one-third of the delegations made it known they would boycott or walk out of the photo. Rubio still opened the talks with an appeal for unity, saying, “We are in a critical moment. We are facing a series of interconnected economic and geopolitical crises.”

European officials had made clear they would not stand with Lavrov. “In no case will there be a family photo with European partners and Mr Lavrov together because … there is no normality with Russia,” Germany’s foreign minister, Johann Wadephul, warned ahead of the session. UK Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband said, “We do not engage in that way with the Russians and have not for a number of years.” Australia’s foreign minister, Penny Wong, condemned Russia’s invasion of Ukraine as “brutal and unjustified” in front of Lavrov, and a spokesperson said Australia supported the US decision to cancel the photo, adding, “Australia has been clear and consistent: we will not normalise Russia’s brutal and illegal invasion of Ukraine.”

The schism in Atlanta is further evidence of the rupture caused by the Trump administration’s attempt to reintegrate Russia into the international community despite its ongoing war against Ukraine. Diplomats described the opening session as cordial but said Russia’s position on Ukraine is unyielding. Those disagreements, along with the Trump administration’s decision not to invite G20 member South Africa, will probably prevent the foreign ministers from producing a joint communiqué when the meetings wrap up on Saturday. Poland’s deputy prime minister, Radosław Sikorski, said, “We have wars going on and we have participants in those wars around the table, so it’s very difficult to agree a common line.”

EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas accused Lavrov of presenting “lies” about the conflict and suggested Moscow is not ready for peace negotiations. “Even if they would have said this in words, their deeds tell something else,” she said, noting that July, August, and September have been the deadliest months for civilians in Ukraine. Lavrov conceded no ground on Ukraine and at a press conference criticised Europeans for their “arrogant boycott” while praising the Trump administration’s willingness to engage with Moscow. Rubio spoke briefly with Lavrov on the sidelines, with the Russian minister saying both sides had a “decent talk.”

The Trump administration on Friday approved the sale of 500 Patriot missiles to Ukraine for an estimated $2.1 billion, but Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy warned that deliveries could take years, saying on X that “deliveries may begin ONLY IN A FEW YEARS.” Two people briefed on the decision said the missiles, which are yet to be manufactured, are not expected to be delivered until a couple of years into the 2030s. Zelenskyy suggested the US and European allies could give Ukraine missiles from their stockpiles now, to be replenished later by the new purchase. Meanwhile, a humanitarian convoy reached the besieged Russian-held city of Oleshky in southern Ukraine on Friday—the first such operation in four years and eight months of war—bringing food and medicine and evacuating residents. US first lady Melania Trump posted that the convoy could safely evacuate more than 750 people.
