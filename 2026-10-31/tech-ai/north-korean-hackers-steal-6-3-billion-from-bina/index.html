North Korean hackers steal $6.3 billion from Binance in record crypto heist

Tech / AI desk

Hackers linked to North Korea have stolen at least $6.3 billion in cryptocurrency from Binance, the world's largest crypto exchange, in what security researchers are calling the largest crypto theft on record. Binance has suspended all deposits and withdrawals while it investigates the breach, which it said stemmed from a compromised engineer's laptop that allowed an attacker to gain access to a service container and submit unauthorized withdrawal transactions. The company said it has seen no evidence of a leak of its private keys.

Binance put the 'estimated total impact' of the hack at about $6.3 billion. Around $3 billion of affected assets, mainly stablecoins, have been frozen by industry partners, according to Binance spokesperson Andie Cooper. However, analysts at Elliptic said the attacker had converted about $3.3 billion into digital assets that could not be frozen and was working to cash those out as traditional currency. Blockchain intelligence firm TRM Labs attributed the breach to a North Korean hacking unit known as TraderTraitor, noting that the theft took about 20 minutes. The same group was blamed for the previous record heist, a $1.4 billion theft from crypto exchange Bybit in 2025.

Binance said it has 'already reserved over $6.34 billion of company funds to cover this incident,' with $5 billion already held in the exact token assets that were impacted. The company said it is actively buying more tokens to cover the remaining amount, though it cautioned that 'some tokens may have low liquidity' and settlement 'also takes time.' Binance stated that 'user funds are safe' but did not immediately provide proof of this claim, saying it will publish an updated proof of reserves once remediation is complete.

The breach is a major blow to Binance, which was co-founded in 2017 by Changpeng Zhao and holds about $140 billion of digital assets. In 2023, the company pleaded guilty to U.S. criminal charges related to breach of anti-money laundering laws and paid a $4.3 billion fine. Peter Ward of the Sejong Institute in Seoul said North Korea has considerable hacking expertise and offers 'near-total protection to its hackers,' adding that 'things may get a lot worse going forward.' Researchers say North Korea's sole motivation is to steal crypto to fund its sanctioned weapons of mass destruction program, as the country remains cut off from the U.S. and global financial system.

Blockchain security firm Elliptic said some of the stolen funds ended up in the same wallet used in a previous cyberattack on crypto exchange Bitget earlier this year, in which hackers stole $351 million. The company faces questions about why its security allowed a single employee's laptop to access systems capable of controlling billions of dollars in funds. Binance did not respond to questions about whether it had sought a third-party security audit prior to the hack.
