Appeals Court Again Rejects Trump Effort to Impound California Ballots

Politics desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts slightly center-left
✓ Facts
  • The Ninth Circuit ruled 2-1 on Friday to reject, for a second time, the Trump administration's request to immediately block California from counting ballots cast by roughly 20,000 voters the Department of Justice alleges are "likely noncitizens."
  • This marks the third consecutive court loss for the Trump administration in its effort to intervene in California's ballot-counting process.
  • The dispute began Oct. 15, when the DOJ sued California after the state was given a 48-hour deadline to review and set aside ballots from the DOJ's list of 20,000 flagged voters pending citizenship verification.
  • The majority opinion, written by Judges Mark J. Bennett and Gabriel P. Sanchez, found the DOJ's request likely violates the National Voter Registration Act, which bars systemic voter-roll purges within 90 days of an election.
  • Judge Patrick J. Bumatay dissented, writing that the DOJ had proposed a "modest remedy" to prevent potentially illegal votes from being mixed with legitimate ballots.
  • California requires voters to attest to citizenship under penalty of perjury at registration, and the state has more than 23 million registered voters, making the flagged list a small fraction of the electorate.
  • The DOJ is pursuing similar actions against Washington state (seeking to segregate ballots from hundreds of alleged noncitizens) and against Alaska, where it is suing to block certification of a U.S. Senate race if the Democratic candidate wins.
  • A federal judge earlier in the week separately rejected the DOJ's bid to block ballot counting and warned that threats against election workers must "cease immediately."
  • The DOJ is expected to appeal the Ninth Circuit's ruling to the U.S. Supreme Court.
○ Analysis

The case centers on the Trump administration's attempt to use DOJ litigation to halt or alter ballot-counting procedures in California based on claims that thousands of registered voters may be noncitizens. Courts at multiple levels—including a federal district judge and now a divided Ninth Circuit panel—have repeatedly found that the DOJ's proposed remedy conflicts with federal voter-registration law and the Constitution's Elections Clause, which assigns election administration primarily to the states. The ruling is part of a broader pattern of DOJ legal action in multiple states (Washington and Alaska) during the run-up to the midterms, raising questions about the proper boundary between federal oversight of voter-roll integrity and state authority over election administration. The matter appears likely to reach the Supreme Court, which could set a precedent on how much latitude federal authorities have to intervene in state election processes close to an election.

Steelman

Both the court majority and the dissent are responding to the same underlying tension—between preventing any noncitizen votes from affecting an election and avoiding disruptive, legally questionable purges this close to voting—without either side in the ruling disputing the basic facts of how the ballots were flagged or processed.

Implications

With the DOJ signaling an imminent appeal, the Supreme Court may soon weigh in on how far federal authorities can go in challenging state-run ballot-counting procedures during an active election, a decision that could shape similar disputes already unfolding in Washington and Alaska.
