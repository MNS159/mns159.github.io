Iran-US conflict

US evacuates Mideast outposts as Iran plot advances; Trump prepares for escalation

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

The State Department has ordered staffing reductions at multiple U.S. embassies in the Middle East, including in Lebanon, the United Arab Emirates, Qatar and Jordan, and placed the U.S. Embassy in Saudi Arabia under “ordered departure,” following a Director of National Intelligence memo warning that Iranian lethal plots against U.S. personnel have reached “advanced stages,” according to U.S. officials and the memo obtained by Axios. The memo, sent Friday by DNI Jay Clayton to Secretary of State Marco Rubio, said Iran had “substantially increased efforts to target US personnel” and that the risk is “relevant to more individuals than are currently cleared to receive this information.” Separately, the U.S. military has been quietly scaling back its troop presence at bases across the region, mirroring drawdowns at the start of the war with Iran in February.

The Pentagon has drawn up options for renewed strikes on Iran, the Washington-based outlet The Hill reported, raising the prospect of a resumption of major combat operations. Military planners have prepared several options for Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, including targeting Iran’s energy facilities, infrastructure, nuclear sites, as well as strategic islands such as Kharg Island—which handles the vast majority of Iran’s oil production—and Qeshm in the Strait of Hormuz, according to U.S. officials. President Donald Trump suggested at a rally Friday that Washington “may do another little Epic Fury,” referring to the codename of the U.S.-Israeli operation that began on February 28, and on Saturday said the U.S. could strike as soon as “tomorrow,” while also holding out the possibility of a “big, beautiful” agreement with Tehran. The U.S. is deploying a third aircraft carrier strike group, the USS Theodore Roosevelt, along with roughly 10,000 additional soldiers and the 13th Marine Expeditionary Unit to the Middle East by the end of November.

Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian accused Washington of derailing negotiations but said efforts to reach an agreement should continue. “We were having negotiations, the cowards [Americans] messed everything up,” he said in remarks broadcast Saturday. The Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps spokesman Hossein Mohebi announced a shift toward an “offensive” military doctrine, warning that countries hosting bases used in U.S. attacks could become targets. On Sunday, Iran’s army said it shot down a U.S.-developed LUCAS drone over Bandar Abbas, according to the semi-official Tasnim news agency. Reuters reported, citing Iranian security officials, that Tehran has drawn up plans to target undersea communications cables in the Gulf in the event of a resumption of U.S. and Israeli attacks. Earlier in the week, Iran claimed seven oil tankers had caught fire or sustained damage in and around the Strait of Hormuz.

October has become the worst month for attacks on ships since the war began in February, with more than 120 attacks reported so far. The initial U.S.-Israeli operation, dubbed “Epic Fury,” began on February 28 after months of strikes on shipping in the Strait of Hormuz and limited progress toward a long-term ceasefire. Trump faces political pressure ahead of the November 3 U.S. midterm elections, while Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu—who has said overthrowing Iran’s government remains Israel’s “primary objective”—has been pushing for an attack as his support dwindles ahead of Tuesday’s election. Analysts caution that renewed escalation carries high risks. “There is a very high risk of miscalculation by either side, a very high risk of rapid escalation,” said Julie Norman, associate fellow at Chatham House. Mohamad Elmasry, a professor at the Doha Institute, said “all of that would suggest that the US and Israel are planning [another round of attacks] on Iran, either soon or very soon,” and warned that “this next round of fighting could actually be more dangerous, more catastrophic than the previous round.”
