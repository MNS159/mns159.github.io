French protests escalate with clashes and mass arrests

International & World desk

More than 1,300 people were arrested across France on Saturday as hundreds of thousands of protesters demonstrated against the cost of living and poor school conditions, with clashes erupting after largely peaceful marches in multiple cities, officials said. The interior ministry estimated 410,000 protesters nationwide, while unions put the number at about 800,000. Police deployed 70,000 officers and used tear gas and water cannon to disperse crowds in several cities, including Paris, while clashes also occurred in Marseille, Bordeaux, and Lyon. The interior ministry reported 53 officers injured and two protesters seriously hurt—one in Toulouse who had several fingers torn off by an explosive device, and another in Clermont-Ferrand.

In Paris, 142 people were arrested after an elite police unit intervened when masked youths smashed car dealership windows and pelted officers with projectiles. Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez said “several thousand radical activists gathered at the rear of the march, including around 500 individuals identified as rioters,” according to AFP. In Marseille, rubbish bins were set ablaze and metal sheets blocked streets; a law enforcement vehicle drove through a burning barricade. In Bordeaux, protesters used boards removed from a building to build a street barricade, while in Lyon police fired tear gas grenades at a crowd that included secondary school and university students. One protester, Zelie, 26, told the BBC that people were “really frustrated” and had been “suffering a lot” since President Emmanuel Macron came to office. Two masked teenage protesters said their generation felt abandoned, adding that looting stemmed from “poverty and the violence of the police.”

Saturday’s demonstrations follow a weekend of even larger unrest that saw 550,000 protesters, more than 2,500 arrests, and damage to an estimated 2,000 shops and businesses across France, with losses running into “hundreds of millions euros,” according to the French Chambers of Commerce and Industry. Macron decreed earlier this week that only protests organized by recognized trade unions would be permitted, and hundreds of people have been jailed in fast-track hearings for vandalism, arson, and looting. The unrest comes six months before a presidential election in which Macron will not stand, leaving voters a choice between far-right National Rally candidate Marine Le Pen and radical-left La France Insoumise (France Unbowed) candidate Jean-Luc Mélenchon. Mélenchon blamed the government, saying police had created “a setup deliberately designed to provoke chaos” that “mutilated two people.” Right-wing figures criticized rioters while left-wing leaders backed the union-led protests.

Similar protests erupted in several other European cities on Saturday. In Barcelona, more than 120 people were arrested after a student protest turned into what local authorities called “gratuitous violence.” In Albania’s capital Tirana, riot police fired tear gas to disperse thousands of anti-government demonstrators, and clashes were reported in Brussels. In Italy, authorities deployed 500 additional soldiers to Milan alongside about 5,000 police and other security personnel, with army vehicles and boarded-up luxury stores in the city center. While Milan’s demonstration remained peaceful, brief clashes occurred in Rome and Turin. Police in Milan detained several minors as a preventive measure after last week’s violence and looting, which involved about 2,000 very young protesters, many of them “naturalized, second-generation Italians,” alongside foreign nationals. In Rome, a small number of protesters tried to force their way into the Colosseum but were ushered out by guards.

The protests across Europe reflect a broad range of grievances including education, corruption, anti-war movements, pensions, and housing. French authorities said the number of arrests nationwide would likely rise as unrest continued into the early hours of Sunday. In the Bouches-du-Rhône region around Marseille, seven businesses were looted or damaged. Meanwhile, a peaceful rally of about 13,000 people against the far-right National Rally party proceeded in Orléans. The sustained unrest underscores deep political polarization in France, with all leading contenders blaming Macron and his ministers for the crisis.
