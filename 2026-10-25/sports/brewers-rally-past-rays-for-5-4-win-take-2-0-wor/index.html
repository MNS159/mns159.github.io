Brewers rally past Rays for 5-4 win, take 2-0 World Series lead

Sports desk

MILWAUKEE — The Milwaukee Brewers overcame a four-run first-inning deficit for the second time this postseason, rallying past the Tampa Bay Rays 5-4 on Saturday night at American Family Field to take a 2-0 lead in the World Series. The Brewers, who also erased a 4-0 hole to sweep the defending champion Los Angeles Dodgers in the NLCS, have now won seven of their nine playoff games after falling behind. Down 4-0 just five batters into the game, Milwaukee methodically chipped away, tying the score in the sixth inning and taking the lead in the seventh to stun the Rays and seize control of the series.

The Rays jumped ahead early when Liam Hicks crushed a two-run home run — the first homer of the World Series — to cap a four-run first inning against rookie Brewers starter Logan Henderson, who gave up four runs in a game for the first time in his short MLB career. But Tampa Bay would not score again. The Brewers began their comeback with two runs in the bottom of the first against Rays ace Drew Rasmussen. David Hamilton cut the deficit to 4-3 in the fourth inning with a run-scoring grounder that went through the legs of Rays first baseman Jonathan Aranda. In the sixth, Sal Frelick — who had gone 103 plate appearances without a home run since July 11 — tied the game by driving a fastball over the center-field fence off Rays pitcher Ian Seymour. Frelick circled the bases in 18.72 seconds. "I was just pumped to try to get back to the dugout," Frelick said. "I don't hit a lot of home runs, especially to center, so I was happy it went out."

The go-ahead run came in the seventh inning. Brice Turang doubled against losing pitcher Tyler Wells on the second pitch of the bottom half, advanced on Garrett Mitchell's flyout to right, and scored when 22-year-old rookie Cooper Pratt looped a sinker from Kevin Kelly into short right field. "As long as there's game left, we have a chance," Pratt said. "I think we've all bought into that. We're never out of it." The Brewers' bullpen — JoJo Romero, Dustin May and DL Hall — kept the game within reach after the first-inning disaster, and late-inning beasts Abner Uribe and Aaron Ashby closed it out. Even when the Rays got a leadoff double from Jonny DeLuca in the ninth and moved a runner to third with Yandy Díaz coming to the plate, manager Pat Murphy stuck with Ashby, who got a strikeout to end the game.

The final play sparked controversy. Díaz checked his swing, but the umpire ruled he had swung, ending the game. After seeing the replay, Dodgers announcer Joe Davis called on MLB to implement a replay system for check swings similar to the automated balls/strikes system (ABS) introduced this year. "We got ABS for the first time this year, getting the balls and strikes right; we need to get a system that can get [check-swings] right," Davis said. Díaz was frustrated, maintaining he had not swung. The Rays also hurt themselves with a series of defensive miscues: Jonathan Aranda botched a foul-ball catch in the first inning and let a weak grounder go through his legs in the fourth; Liam Hicks was thrown out at home plate by Jackson Chourio on a send that was a poor decision despite Chourio's excellent throw; Chandler Simpson dropped a Christian Yelich pop fly in the sixth; and backup catcher Nick Fuentes bobbled a pitch that contributed to a Brewers double steal in the eighth. "Look, we didn't play our cleanest baseball game by any stretch," Rays manager Kevin Cash said. "We pride ourselves on doing everything we can to support the pitchers and making plays behind them. Today was probably not our best day."

The Brewers, who won 103 regular-season games and posted the best run differential in baseball, are now one of only five teams in MLB playoff history to win multiple games after falling behind by at least four runs, and just the third to trail by four runs at home in the World Series and come back to win, joining the 2017 Astros and 2002 Angels. "We're down to the end of the season here," Brewers manager Pat Murphy said. "They're going to tap us on the shoulder here at some point and tell us no more. Guys just, they don't give up. They believe, and they don't give up. They just keep going." The Brewers need two more wins to secure the first World Series championship in franchise history. Game 3 is scheduled for Monday night at Tropicana Field in St. Petersburg, Florida.
