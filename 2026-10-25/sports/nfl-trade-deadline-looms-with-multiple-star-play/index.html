Colts RB Jonathan Taylor emerges as top trade target as NFL deadline nears

Sports desk

With the NFL trade deadline set for 4 p.m. ET on Tuesday, November 10, the Indianapolis Colts are facing decisions on multiple veteran players after firing general manager Chris Ballard. Interim GM Ed Dodds must weigh whether to follow the Miami Dolphins' fire-sale model from last season when they dealt key assets after a front-office shakeup. According to ESPN's Stephen Holder, 'multiple teams' are interested in acquiring three-time Pro Bowl running back Jonathan Taylor if the Colts make him available.

Taylor, 27, remains productive despite playing in a Colts offense that ranks 22nd in both passing and total yards. He is seventh in the NFL with 655 scrimmage yards and tied for sixth with seven combined touchdowns. However, his contract escalates sharply: his base salary jumps from $7 million this season to $15 million in 2027, and trading him would leave Indianapolis with $10 million in dead money next year. The Colts would likely demand a first-round pick to open talks, though a conditional second-rounder or a package similar to the 2022 Christian McCaffrey trade (a second-, third-, fourth- and fifth-round pick) might suffice.

Taylor has not sought a move. "I think it would be a discredit and a disservice to my teammates and this whole organization to think about what could, what should, what may be," he told Holder. But the Colts' front-office transition — and the possibility of future coaching changes — opens the door for dealing veterans. Yahoo Sports reported that the Colts could trade Taylor and/or others, with the deadline still 16 days away at the time of reporting.

Several contending teams have been identified as logical landing spots. The Denver Broncos (30th in yards per carry, 31st in total offense) could pair Taylor with Sean Payton's offense and treat him as a long-term piece. The Seattle Seahawks, defending Super Bowl champions, rank 18th in yards per carry and have struggled with quarterback Sam Darnold's seven interceptions; adding Taylor could stabilize the offense even as Zach Charbonnet and rookie Jadarian Price return from injury. The Minnesota Vikings, one of three undefeated teams, have a championship defense and could give Taylor a chance to chase a title while upgrading a backfield led by Aaron Jones, who will turn 32 in December. The Cincinnati Bengals, despite a potent passing attack, rank 28th in rushing and have shown an aggressive new approach after trading for defensive tackle Dexter Lawrence and signing multiple defensive free agents; head coach Zac Taylor said after a Week 4 loss, "We got to be better… We will be better moving forward."

The New York Giants present a different scenario: first place in the NFC East at 3-2-1 despite losing second-year quarterback Jaxson Dart to a season-ending knee injury. Coach John Harbaugh has publicly expressed desire for a high-volume every-down back, saying, "I would love for us to be in that kind of a circumstance. A Derrick Henry circumstance or a Saquon Barkley circumstance… that would be great, and we haven't." Taylor, who played high school football roughly two hours from MetLife Stadium in New Jersey, could give the Giants a centerpiece for their rebuild while also keeping them in playoff contention this season. With the deadline just over two weeks away, any trade would reshape the contending landscape.
