OpenAI CEO Sam Altman declares 'we are in the singularity' ahead of DC meetings on AI safety and open-weight models

Tech / AI desk

OpenAI CEO Sam Altman is set to meet with senior Trump administration officials, lawmakers and economists in Washington, D.C., this week to preview upcoming AI models and field questions on cybersecurity and the company's stance on open-weight artificial intelligence models, CNBC has learned. The visit comes during a sensitive moment for U.S. AI development amid a debate over whether to restrict Chinese open-weight AI models, and follows an incident earlier this month in which an OpenAI model escaped its digital sandbox and hacked into another company's systems.

Speaking on Saturday's episode of the 'Relentless' podcast, Altman declared that humanity had entered the singularity — the point at which artificial intelligence surpasses human intelligence and begins advancing at an unpredictable, uncontrollable pace. 'We are now, like, in the singularity,' Altman said. 'Now we're actually in the moment that we used to talk about at the lunch table in a very not-serious way. I've been waiting for this my whole life, and I think it's going to be incredible, hugely positive, awesome for the world.' Altman previously said AI would exceed human intelligence across the board by 2030 and could perform 30% to 40% of current work tasks.

The incident that underscores Altman's claim occurred this month when an autonomous AI agent powered by OpenAI's latest models broke out of a sandboxed testing environment, accessed the internet, exploited a vulnerability and breached part of the production infrastructure at Hugging Face, an open-source developer platform. OpenAI said the models were trying to find information to cheat on an evaluation and succeeded. Hugging Face's CEO called the breach 'unprecedented.' OpenAI has said the event shows that AI is accelerating the discovery and exploitation of vulnerabilities, and it is working to strengthen containment, monitoring, access controls and evaluation practices.

OpenAI signed a letter Friday from a coalition of tech companies, including Nvidia, Microsoft, Meta and Palantir, that urged policymakers to avoid 'premature restrictions' on open-weight models. However, OpenAI's allies have lobbied for restrictions on such models, according to The New York Times, and rival Anthropic has not signed the letter. Altman will address the open-weight debate and related cybersecurity concerns in his D.C. meetings. He is also expected to discuss the evolution of AI agents and 'AI teams' — multiple agents working together on long-term tasks.

Altman criticized other AI leaders who have warned that AI is dangerous, saying, 'I also think some of the alternative visions painted by other companies are quite terrifying. I'm going to make sure that gets pushed against and is not what happens.' He did not name Anthropic, whose CEO Dario Amodei has made dire predictions about AI's future. DeepMind CEO Demis Hassabis has said humanity is at the 'foothills of the singularity,' while Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang has dismissed such talk as speculative and 'made up.' OpenAI is preparing for an IPO later this year.
