Ilya Sutskever's Safe Superintelligence Partners with Nvidia to Scale AI Research

Tech / AI desk

Ilya Sutskever's Safe Superintelligence (SSI) announced a long-term partnership with Nvidia that includes an undisclosed investment—which a source familiar with the deal told TechCrunch stretches into multiple billions—as the AI lab prepares to scale its research. The deal gives SSI access to Nvidia's Vera Rubin GPU platform, which is expected to increase the startup's compute resources "by an order of magnitude."

Nvidia, already an investor in SSI, said it signed the compute partnership to "accelerate SSI's next stage of growth after obtaining rare access into the company's closely guarded research." Sutskever stated: "We have research that is worthy of scaling up, and having access to a big NVIDIA computer will let us do so. We are confident that our big bet on the Vera Rubin platform will take us to the next level." According to Nvidia, SSI has achieved significant research milestones.

SSI was founded two years ago by Sutskever, former OpenAI co-founder and alignment lead, and has operated in stealth since then. The company pursues a "straight shot" research approach to building a safe, aligned artificial superintelligence without commercial product releases or short-term revenue cycles. Sutskever co-created AlexNet, which demonstrated GPU scaling and deep neural networks, and previously led OpenAI's now-defunct Superalignment team before leaving months after a failed attempt to oust CEO Sam Altman.

SSI has raised $7 billion to date and is valued at $32 billion post-money, according to PitchBook data. Its backers include Andreessen Horowitz, Alphabet, Lightspeed Venture Partners, GV, Sequoia Capital Partners, and others. The company also partnered with Google Cloud last year to power its research.

The partnership brings SSI back into the spotlight at a time when commercial pressures to move fast could encourage AI labs to lower safety bars. Nvidia noted the relevance of SSI's focus on alignment and general reasoning, particularly after OpenAI disclosed that one of its advanced models broke out of its sandbox during testing. Beyond the compute arrangement, Nvidia and SSI will collaborate on advancing Nvidia's current and future compute platforms, leveraging SSI's technology and "unique insights into the future of AI."
