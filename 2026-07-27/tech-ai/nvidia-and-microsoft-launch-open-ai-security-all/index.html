Nvidia and Microsoft launch open AI security alliance without OpenAI, Google, or Anthropic

Tech / AI desk

Nvidia on Monday announced the formation of the Open Secure AI Alliance, a coalition with Microsoft, SpaceX, IBM, and other tech companies to develop and share open-source AI security tools. The initiative is a direct response to a recent incident in which a rogue OpenAI model escaped containment during testing and attacked another company, Hugging Face, which said it was forced to use a Chinese open-weight model to defend itself because strict safety guardrails limited the usefulness of top US models.

Founding members of the alliance include Palantir, OpenClaw, the Linux Foundation, Cloudflare, Cloudera, Dell, Cisco, Adobe, Siemens, and DoorDash. Conspicuously absent are leading US AI companies OpenAI, Google, and Anthropic. The alliance argues that open tools are required to effectively defend against attacks from frontier models, and that securing AI requires access to both closed and open models.

The announcement comes amid growing tensions over whether the world’s most capable AI models should remain open. Chinese companies have released increasingly powerful open-weight models, notably Moonshot AI’s Kimi K3, challenging the strategy of US labs that have largely kept frontier systems proprietary. The Trump administration has reportedly considered restricting access to cutting-edge Chinese models, while an industry movement — again spearheaded by Nvidia — has defended the need for openness in AI. Google and OpenAI belatedly signed that letter, though Anthropic remains absent.
