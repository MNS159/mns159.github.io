Chip stocks sink after Chinese rival CXMT surges 466% in Shanghai debut

Financial & Markets desk

Chinese chipmaker CXMT soared 466% in its Shanghai stock market debut, becoming the most valuable listed company in mainland China and sending shares of rival chip makers sharply lower.

The surge on its first day of trading pushed CXMT to the top of China's stock market by market capitalization. The strong debut comes as investors assess the potential for a manufacturing breakthrough that could make China's semiconductor industry more competitive globally.

The news weighed on other chip stocks. ASML, a Dutch supplier of chip-making equipment, saw its stock plunge, leading a broader decline in the sector. Micron Technology and other chip makers also fell.

The market reaction underscores concerns that China's progress in chip manufacturing could disrupt the current landscape, potentially reducing demand for foreign equipment and memory chips. CXMT's debut highlights the growing competitiveness of China's domestic chip industry.

The stakes are significant for companies with large exposure to China. If CXMT's breakthrough materializes, it could reshape competitive dynamics in the global chip market. Investors will be watching for further developments from CXMT and the broader Chinese chip sector.
