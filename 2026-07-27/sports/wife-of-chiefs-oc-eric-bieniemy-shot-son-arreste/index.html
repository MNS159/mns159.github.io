Wife of Chiefs OC Eric Bieniemy shot; son arrested

Sports desk

Mia Bieniemy, the wife of Kansas City Chiefs offensive coordinator Eric Bieniemy, is in stable condition after being shot multiple times at the couple's home in Ashburn, Virginia, on Sunday evening, Chiefs coach Andy Reid confirmed Monday. The Loudoun County Sheriff's Office said deputies responded to a report of a shooting at approximately 7:32 p.m. ET and found an adult woman suffering from multiple gunshot wounds. She was transported to a local hospital with serious injuries.

Eric and Mia Bieniemy's son, Elijah Zion Bieniemy, 27, was arrested and charged with malicious wounding, use of a firearm in commission of a felony, and discharge of a firearm inside a dwelling. He is being held without bond at a local detention center, according to the sheriff's office.

Reid told reporters Monday, "Our hearts go out to Eric Bieniemy and his family. Thoughts and prayers are with them. Mia is stable, which is a plus. We all love EB, and you hate seeing those things happen." He added, "Real life, you take care of that. She's a saint, one of God's good blessings. But things happen." Reid confirmed that Eric Bieniemy left the team's training camp in St. Joseph, Missouri, and there is no timetable for his return. "We'll just see how things go," Reid said.

Eric Bieniemy rejoined the Chiefs in January for his second stint on the coaching staff. He previously served as offensive coordinator from 2018-22, helping lead the team to two Super Bowl titles. During that span, the Chiefs led the NFL with 30.1 points per game. He also worked as Reid's running backs coach from 2013-17. After leaving Kansas City, Bieniemy spent the 2023 season as assistant head coach and offensive coordinator with the Washington Commanders, the 2024 season in the same roles at UCLA, and the 2025 season as running backs coach for the Chicago Bears, where he oversaw the league's No. 3 rushing attack.

The Chiefs released a statement saying they are "aware of the incident involving Eric Bieniemy's family." Reid said the organization will support Bieniemy as he works through the situation, adding, "He's got a great support group here as does his family, as needed. And we'll just let time take care of it from there."
