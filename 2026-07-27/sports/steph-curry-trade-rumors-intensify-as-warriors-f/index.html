Steph Curry trade rumors intensify after Warriors' failed LeBron pursuit

Sports desk

Trade rumors surrounding Golden State Warriors guard Steph Curry have intensified following the team's failure to acquire LeBron James in free agency, leaving the franchise at a crossroads with a roster that appears unable to contend for an NBA championship. The Warriors have been linked to high-profile free agents and potential blockbuster trades, but the latest miss has sparked speculation that the team may be planning for a post-Curry future, according to reports from Yahoo Sports and SB Nation.

The Warriors enter the 2026-27 season with Curry expected to carry the offensive load, at least until Jimmy Butler (torn ACL) and Moses Moody return to full health. The team's stagnant offseason, combined with the inability to land James, has led to calls from fans and analysts for the Warriors to trade the two-time MVP. On his podcast, Bill Simmons questioned why the franchise is 'so desperate just to waste the end of the Steph era' and floated a hypothetical trade sending Curry to the San Antonio Spurs. Yahoo Sports' Kevin O'Connor called it a 'shame' and said the team should trade Curry if the focus shifts to the future. CBS Sports' Sam Quinn added that 'in pure basketball terms, it's better for everyone if he asks for a trade.'

Among the potential destinations, the Charlotte Hornets have emerged as a speculative suitor, given Curry's roots in Charlotte where his father, Dell Curry, played for the Hornets. The Hornets possess a large trade exception and significant draft capital, which could be used to acquire Curry without taking on bad salary. However, the franchise is unlikely to part with its young stars Brandon Miller or Kon Knueppel, and any deal would likely involve draft picks. SB Nation noted that adding Curry would improve the Hornets' three-point shooting and public perception after trading LaMelo Ball, but it would also disrupt the team's leadership structure and timeline.

For the Warriors, trading Curry would be a public relations risk and would require a substantial return in draft picks, though the team lacks the assets to acquire a star without giving up key players. The Western Conference features strong contenders in the Thunder, Spurs, Nuggets, and Timberwolves, while the East has built juggernauts in Philadelphia, Cleveland, and Miami. Golden State ranked No. 23 in SB Nation's way-too-early power rankings after James signed with the 76ers.

The Warriors are expected to wait and assess the team's performance early in the season before making any moves. The front office could pursue a trade at the deadline to bolster the roster around Curry, Butler, and Draymond Green. Until Curry signs a contract extension or the team acquires significant help, trade rumors are likely to persist, making Curry one of the biggest names to watch as the season progresses.
