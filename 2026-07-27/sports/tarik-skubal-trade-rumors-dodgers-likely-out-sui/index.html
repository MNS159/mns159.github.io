Dodgers likely out on Tarik Skubal as trade deadline nears; Mets, Astros set selling and buying plans

Sports desk

The Los Angeles Dodgers, widely viewed as a potential suitor for Detroit Tigers ace Tarik Skubal, are not expected to pursue the two-time Cy Young winner if he becomes available before the Aug. 3 trade deadline, according to people with knowledge of the situation who were not authorized to speak publicly, as reported by the Los Angeles Times. The Tigers have not definitively decided to sell — they entered Monday 3 ½ games out of a playoff spot — but Skubal would be the biggest prize on the market if Detroit does pivot to seller. The Dodgers, the two-time defending champions, have deep pockets and a fertile farm system, and they have shown interest in preventing other contenders from acquiring elite talent, but multiple league sources indicate they are not currently moving toward a deal for Skubal.

Skubal, who returned from elbow surgery earlier this season, has pitched like the best starter in fantasy baseball since overcoming early rust. After allowing six homers and nine runs in his first 16 1/3 innings back, he posted a 2.70 ERA and a 35.5% strikeout rate across subsequent outings. On Friday, he struck out a season-high 12 batters against the Kansas City Royals. His resurgence has only heightened interest around the league. Even if the Dodgers remain on the sideline, other contenders may push to acquire him, with the Astros, Phillies, and Rays among the teams active in the starting-pitching market.

Houston Astros general manager Dana Brown reaffirmed Sunday that the club plans to be buyers at the deadline. “The division is wide open. The wild card is wide open. There's some parity there. There's a lot of teams bunched up,” Brown said on the Astros' pregame radio show. “I think the team that gets hot is going to be the team that takes the run. We would anticipate that we're going to be buyers.” The Astros trail the AL West leader by two games and are also two games back in the race for the third AL wild-card spot. Brown identified a left-handed outfield bat as a priority, though he said any offensive upgrade that lengthens the lineup would be the focus.

The Philadelphia Phillies and Tampa Bay Rays each sent a scout to watch Washington Nationals left-hander Foster Griffin in his start Saturday, according to The Athletic. Griffin, an All-Star this season, is 12-2 with a 2.76 ERA and is on a one-year contract, making him a prime trade chip for a Nationals club that remains only one game out of the final National League wild-card spot but is in the midst of a retool. Every contender seeking starting pitching is expected to be involved in the bidding for Griffin.

The New York Mets are poised to be major sellers, with several players hitting free agency after this season, including Luis Robert Jr. (who has a team option for 2027), Clay Holmes (player option), Tyrone Taylor, A.J. Minter, Freddy Peralta, and Brooks Raley. President of baseball operations David Stearns has set a high bar for any deal involving players under control beyond 2026, such as Luke Weaver, Jorge Polanco, and Huascar Brazobán, but is open to moving them if the return is sufficient. Meanwhile, the Chicago White Sox have acquired right-hander José Urquidy, who had an 8.53 ERA in 6 1/3 innings with the Pirates this season and a 7.71 ERA in 2 1/3 innings last season with the Tigers, as a low-cost depth addition.
