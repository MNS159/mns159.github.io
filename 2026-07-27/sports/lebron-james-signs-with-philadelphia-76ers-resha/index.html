LeBron James signs with Philadelphia 76ers, reshaping NBA

Sports desk

LeBron James has signed with the Philadelphia 76ers, a move that immediately reshapes the NBA landscape and gives the franchise a new star pairing at forward. The 22-time All-Star is joining Jaylen Brown, forming what Bleacher Report projects as a starting lineup with two new All-Star starters at the forward positions.

According to ESPN's Ramona Shelburne, the Golden State Warriors were 'also appealing for a time' for James, but the 41-year-old 'only' seriously entertained that option if the Warriors added a proven star forward like Washington Wizards' Anthony Davis. That scenario died when Washington showed little interest in moving Davis, leaving the Warriors to rely on an aging core that includes Stephen Curry, Draymond Green, and Jimmy Butler, who is coming off a torn ACL.

The 76ers' new-look starting five—with James and Brown alongside returning guards Tyrese Maxey and Joel Embiid—immediately vaults them into championship contention. Bleacher Report's ranking of every NBA starting lineup after the signing notes that the 76ers have 'two new All-Star starters' but questions whether they are 'truly the best five in all of basketball.' ESPN reported that the signing puts pressure on Philadelphia to deliver, with NBA coaches and executives reacting to the decision.

The impact extends beyond the 76ers. Sports Illustrated reported that James' decision shifted the NBA Finals odds for the 2026-27 season, while the Warriors' title window appears to be closing. With Curry, Green, and Butler all in their mid-30s and Butler recovering from a torn ACL, the Warriors face an uncertain future after failing to add the star needed to lure James.

Sources: ESPN·Bleacher Report·The Ringer·CBS Sports
