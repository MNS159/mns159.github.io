Iran War

US and Iran pause strikes for third night as talks advance

Politics desk
✓ Facts
  • The US and Iran have paused military strikes for a third consecutive night, US UN Ambassador Mike Waltz said Sunday, describing it as giving negotiations "a little bit of room."
  • The pause follows 13 consecutive nights of US airstrikes on Iran, which Washington said aimed to degrade Iran's ability to threaten shipping in the Strait of Hormuz.
  • An Iranian army spokesperson said Sunday that Tehran had halted its "retaliatory" attacks in the region.
  • Oil prices fell below $90 per barrel Monday morning after hitting $100 a barrel last week for the first time since May.
  • Trump told reporters on Air Force One Monday that Iran had requested a meeting; he had not otherwise commented publicly on the pause as of Monday.
  • Iran and Oman held technical talks in Tehran on Friday and Saturday regarding management of the Strait of Hormuz, according to Iranian foreign ministry spokesman Esmail Baghaei.
  • A June memorandum of understanding between the US and Iran to halt military operations broke down after Iranian forces attacked tankers in the Strait of Hormuz on July 13, prompting the US to resume strikes; Iran's subsequent retaliation killed four American soldiers, and US strikes have since killed dozens of people, per Iran's health ministry.
  • A CBS/YouGov poll found 57% of Americans are frustrated about the conflict, with only 19% expressing optimism; Trump is set to meet Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu at the White House Tuesday.
○ Analysis

The pause marks a tentative de-escalation after nearly two weeks of daily US strikes and Iranian counterattacks that unraveled a June ceasefire agreement. The renewed fighting centered on the Strait of Hormuz, a critical global shipping route for oil and gas, after Iran attacked tankers there in mid-July. The stakes are both military and economic: oil prices spiked to $100 a barrel amid the fighting before easing as the pause took hold, illustrating how the conflict's trajectory has direct global economic effects. Diplomatic tracks are running in parallel — Oman-mediated talks with Iran, and coordination between the US and UK, whose defense secretary plans to meet his American counterpart this week — while the durability of the pause remains uncertain given Waltz's insistence that "all options" remain on the table and Trump's own social-media posts depicting continued military threats against Iran.

Steelman

Both the pause's proponents and skeptics are working from the same unresolved question: whether the lull reflects a genuine diplomatic opening (Iran requesting a meeting, Oman-mediated talks, falling oil prices) or a tactical reset before renewed conflict (Waltz's insistence that "all options" remain on the table and Trump's continued war-themed social media posts) — and the public record as of Monday does not yet settle which is true.

Implications

The coming days carry several concrete tests of the pause's durability: Trump's Tuesday meeting with Netanyahu, the UK-US defense talks on the Strait of Hormuz, and the ongoing Oman-mediated discussions with Iran. Oil markets, which have already reacted sharply to both escalation and de-escalation, are likely to remain sensitive to any signal about whether the ceasefire framework from June can be restored or whether strikes will resume.
