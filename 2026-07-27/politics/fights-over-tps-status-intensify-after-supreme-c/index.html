Fights over TPS status intensify after Supreme Court ruling

Politics desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum
✓ Facts
  • In late June, the Supreme Court's conservative majority, in an opinion authored by Justice Samuel Alito, ruled that lower courts cannot review procedural challenges to the DHS secretary's decisions to end Temporary Protected Status (TPS) for several countries, but left open the possibility of constitutional and 'ultra vires' (authority) challenges.
  • The ruling affects TPS holders from Haiti, Syria, South Sudan, Ethiopia, Burma, Somalia and Yemen; population figures cited include about 4,000 Syrians, roughly 10,000 combined from South Sudan, Burma, Ethiopia and Yemen, about 4,000 Ethiopians, about 1,400 Yemenis, and roughly 330,000 Haitians.
  • Since the ruling, three federal judges have issued orders temporarily preserving TPS protections while new claims are litigated: Judge Brian Murphy (Boston) for Ethiopia, Judge Patti Saris (Boston) for South Sudan, and Judge Matthew Kennelly (Chicago) for Burma through at least August 7.
  • The Department of Justice has moved in multiple courts to lift earlier injunctions quickly so it can begin deportations of TPS holders from the affected countries.
  • The White House, through spokeswoman Abigail Jackson, called the Supreme Court's decision a 'tremendous win' and said it gave DHS 'full authority to terminate' TPS designations.
  • Attorneys for TPS holders are now raising Fifth Amendment due-process claims and arguing that only the U.S. attorney general, not the DHS secretary, has statutory authority to terminate TPS designations; DOJ disputes this, citing language in the Supreme Court opinion stating that TPS decision-making authority rests with the DHS secretary.
  • A federal judge earlier this year found that public statements by President Trump about Haiti suggested the TPS revocation for that country was motivated in part by racial animus; the Supreme Court found the public-statement evidence alone insufficient but did not bar further discovery on the issue.
  • Federal sources told Fox News that ICE is planning to expand operations, potentially beginning this week, to locate and arrest Haitian nationals losing TPS, with Springfield, Ohio identified as a target area due to its large Haitian population; the Supreme Court's certified judgment was expected to be transmitted to lower courts the following Monday.
○ Analysis

Temporary Protected Status is a humanitarian immigration program that lets nationals of countries experiencing war, natural disaster or other crises live and work legally in the U.S. for renewable periods. The Trump administration, through then-DHS Secretary Kristi Noem, moved to terminate TPS designations for several countries, prompting lawsuits arguing the terminations were procedurally flawed or discriminatory. The Supreme Court's June ruling did not resolve the underlying merits of those terminations; it narrowed judges' power to block them on procedural grounds while explicitly leaving room for constitutional claims (due process, equal protection) and questions about which federal official actually holds legal authority to end TPS. That has shifted the litigation into a new, faster-moving phase: advocates are refiling claims under different legal theories to keep injunctions alive, while the administration is simultaneously pushing courts to lift existing protections and preparing enforcement actions on the ground.

◐ Debate

Democratic

  • Immigration attorneys and advocates (e.g., Shane Kadidal, Lupe Aguirre) argue TPS terminations violate due process because work permits and protection from removal constitute liberty and property interests requiring proper procedural safeguards before being stripped away.
  • Lawyers for TPS holders from Ethiopia and other countries contend Congress never transferred TPS termination authority from the attorney general to the DHS secretary, meaning Noem's actions were legally unauthorized ('ultra vires').
  • Advocates for Haitian TPS holders argue the termination was motivated by racial animus, pointing to a judge's earlier finding tied to President Trump's public statements about Haiti, and are seeking additional discovery to substantiate that claim despite the Supreme Court's skepticism of the existing record.

Republican

  • The White House, via spokeswoman Abigail Jackson, frames the Supreme Court ruling as a full vindication of the administration's authority to end TPS designations, calling it a 'tremendous win.'
  • DOJ attorneys argue the Supreme Court's opinion explicitly confirms that TPS authority rests with the DHS secretary, undercutting claims that only the attorney general can terminate the program, and warn that plaintiffs' authority theory would imply TPS designations were never validly made in the first place.
  • The administration's broader position, reflected in its push to end Haiti's TPS status, is that the program was intended to be temporary and has been extended for years beyond its original humanitarian justification, warranting termination now that conditions have changed.
Steelman

Beneath the partisan framing, the core dispute has shifted from whether TPS terminations are good policy to a narrower legal question of who has authority to make and unmake these designations and what process is constitutionally due — a fight over institutional power and procedure that will determine the fate of hundreds of thousands of people regardless of how it's politically characterized.

Implications

Expect continued, fast-moving litigation across multiple federal courts as advocates race to secure new injunctions under constitutional and authority-based theories while DOJ seeks to dissolve existing protections; parallel enforcement actions, including reported ICE operations targeting Haitian communities such as in Springfield, Ohio, could begin before those legal questions are resolved, raising the likelihood of emergency court battles and potential conflicting rulings across circuits that may eventually require further Supreme Court intervention or congressional clarification of TPS authority.
