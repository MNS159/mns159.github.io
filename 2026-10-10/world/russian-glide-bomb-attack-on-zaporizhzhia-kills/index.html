Russian glide bomb attack on Zaporizhzhia kills at least 15

International & World desk

Russia attacked the Ukrainian city of Zaporizhzhia with five glide bombs on Saturday morning, killing at least 15 people, including three children, and wounding at least 18 others, Ukrainian officials said. The bombs struck residential buildings in the southeastern city, which lies less than 10 miles (16km) from the front line. Rescue workers continued searching for survivors under the rubble, with more than 80 personnel involved in the operation. Acting Mayor Regina Kharchenko said the attack hit 'our very heart' and that the deaths of children were 'awful grief for their families. For the city.'

The attack came a day after U.S. President Donald Trump announced a deal to obtain Russian diesel, lifting sanctions on diesel exports. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky said the bombing was Russia's way of expressing 'its thanks' for that deal. 'Russia's choice is war,' Zelensky wrote on social media, adding that the signals Moscow received from the diesel agreement suggested 'it could simply keep attacking.' He said Russia would be ready to end the war only when it felt its ability to drag it out was diminishing, and urged the U.S., Europe, and other partners to limit Russia's capacity to fight.

Zelensky also reported that 13 other Ukrainian regions came under attack overnight, including a daytime missile strike on Kyiv that wounded four people. He said he had spoken with French President Emmanuel Macron about shoring up air defenses and warned of an increased risk of further missile strikes in the coming days. The attacks follow a series of deadly Russian strikes this week: at least 33 people were killed Thursday by a glide bomb on two civilian buses in Kramatorsk, and 22 people, including five children, died Wednesday when a missile hit an apartment block in Pryluky.

Trump announced Friday that Russia would immediately supply more than 300,000 tons of diesel, followed by additional deliveries totaling millions of tons. Analysts view the deal as an attempt to address high fuel prices ahead of U.S. midterm elections. A senior Ukrainian official, speaking on condition of anonymity, said the Ukrainian side was not aware of the deal before the announcement and that it was not tied to discussions between U.S. and Ukrainian negotiating teams meeting in Florida. The Ukrainian delegation has since left Florida and is returning to Ukraine.

European allies expressed dismay at the deal. Swedish Prime Minister Ulf Kristersson said Stockholm regretted the agreement 'as energy revenues are central to the Russian war chest.' The German government said it remained committed to sanctions and was working on further measures. Polish Foreign Minister Radoslaw Sikorski questioned whether Russia could deliver, citing reports of fuel shortages in Moscow and Ukrainian damage to about half of Russia's refining capacity. The number of civilian casualties in Ukraine jumped by 55 percent in the first eight months of this year, according to the UN.
