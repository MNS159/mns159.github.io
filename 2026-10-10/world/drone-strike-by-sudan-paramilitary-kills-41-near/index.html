Drone strike by Sudan paramilitary kills 41 near Ethiopian border

International & World desk

A drone attack by Sudan's paramilitary Rapid Support Forces (RSF) killed 41 people in the country's Blue Nile region near the Ethiopian border on Friday, according to a Sudanese rights group. Emergency Lawyers said the strike targeted a camp for displaced people in the Abu Shandi area of Blue Nile state.

The United Nations Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (OCHA) said it was 'appalled by horrifying reports' of the attack. OCHA noted that initial reports indicated most of the casualties were women and children. The area has been 'under sustained attack in recent weeks, displacing thousands of civilians and exposing many more to grave harm,' OCHA said in a statement.

Sudan has been engulfed in a power struggle between the Sudanese army and the RSF since 2023. The conflict has killed hundreds of thousands of people and displaced more than 14 million from their homes, creating one of the world's worst humanitarian crises, with millions in need of medical assistance.

The army controls much of central and eastern Sudan, while the RSF is headquartered in the western Darfur region and also holds areas in the south. Both sides have been accused of using drone attacks.

United Nations Resident and Humanitarian Coordinator in Sudan, Denise Brown, issued a statement on the attack. The strike underscores the ongoing violence against civilians as the war between the army and the RSF continues.
