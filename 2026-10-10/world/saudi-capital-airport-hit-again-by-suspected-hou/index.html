Saudi capital airport hit again by suspected Houthi attack, sparking evacuations

International & World desk · anchored on Reuters

An international airport in the Saudi capital, Riyadh, was attacked again on Saturday, just days after Yemen's Houthi rebels claimed two successful attacks on the hub, according to international news agencies. King Khalid International Airport was evacuated after a loud blast was heard, with more than a dozen ambulances seen rushing to the site, Reuters reported, citing an eyewitness. Saudi authorities have not commented, while the airport told passengers to check with their airline before traveling there.

The attack comes a day after Saudi authorities said three Saudi nationals, including a pilot, were killed in two Houthi attacks the day prior. Another three people were killed in Houthi attacks on two Saudi airports on 6 and 7 October. The Houthis, who are backed by Iran, say they are targeting Saudi Arabia over its support for government forces in Yemen's civil war.

The escalation in fighting follows the unraveling of a temporary ceasefire that was brokered in 2022 after years of air strikes and ground fighting led to the deaths of hundreds of thousands of people, triggering one of the world's worst humanitarian crises. The informal truce began to unravel in July, when the Houthis announced a 'maritime embargo' against Saudi Arabia and began missile and drone attacks on Saudi airports, oil facilities and tankers in the Red Sea. They said they were retaliating against what they described as a years-long Saudi blockade and for an air strike on Sanaa airport.

The conflict worsened after the Houthis seized strategically important stretches of the Red Sea coast in a rapid offensive, including the port of Mokha on the Bab al-Mandab Strait, which is the narrowest point of the Red Sea and a chokepoint for global shipping. Yemeni pro-government forces have since said they had 'secured' the strait at the southern end of the Red Sea. A Saudi-led coalition was formed in 2015 after Arab countries joined forces to stop the Houthis taking over Yemen from the country's internationally recognized government.
