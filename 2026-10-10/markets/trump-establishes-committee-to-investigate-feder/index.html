Trump establishes committee to investigate Federal Reserve Governor Lisa Cook in latest effort to fire her

Financial & Markets desk · anchored on AP
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

President Donald Trump opened a new front in his effort to remove Federal Reserve Governor Lisa Cook on Friday, establishing a “committee of inquiry” to investigate allegations that she made false statements on mortgage applications. The inquiry will include a hearing at the White House on Nov. 5, according to a presidential memorandum, in which Trump stated that Cook “shall” attend to answer questions from the committee. The hearing will not be public, but a transcript will be released, the memo said.

The move is the latest step in Trump's attempt to fire Cook, who was appointed by former President Joe Biden and is the first Black woman to serve on the Fed's seven-member board. Trump initially sought to remove Cook in August 2025, accusing her of committing mortgage fraud by listing two different properties as her primary residence on loan applications in 2021—a practice that can yield lower interest rates. Cook has denied any wrongdoing and has not been charged with a crime. Her attorneys, Abbe Lowell and Norman Eisen, have described the discrepancy as an “inadvertent error,” noting that other documents referred to one property as a vacation home. The Federal Reserve declined to comment on Trump's announcement.

The committee of inquiry will be composed of Kevin Hassett, the president's top economic adviser; Keith Sonderling, acting director of the Office of Government Ethics; and Andrea Lucas, chair of the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission. In his memorandum, Trump wrote: “As President, it is my job to ensure the laws are faithfully executed, including by firing subordinates who cannot be trusted to tell the truth and follow the law.” The inquiry will determine whether there is “cause” to fire Cook, as required under the law governing the Federal Reserve. The Supreme Court ruled in June that Cook could keep her job after Trump’s earlier attempt, but added a footnote that nothing prevented Trump from trying again as long as she was given proper notice and an opportunity to respond to the charges.

Cook’s attorneys said they are weighing whether she should appear at the hearing. “We have engaged with the White House to evaluate whether the process proposed has a possibility of being a genuine one and not simply a box checking exercise,” Lowell and Eisen said in a statement. “Governor Cook welcomes the opportunity to present the facts so she can clear her name and demonstrate there is no legal basis to fire her.” Scott Alvarez, former chief counsel for the Fed, noted that President William Howard Taft used a similar commission in 1912 to remove two officials from an independent agency, but said the allegations against Cook likely do not meet the “for cause” standard set by the Supreme Court.

The effort to remove Cook is unprecedented in the Fed’s 113-year history; no president has previously sought to fire a Fed governor. Trump has repeatedly called on the central bank to cut interest rates, but the Fed, led by Chairman Kevin Warsh—whom Trump appointed earlier this year—raised rates at its last meeting in September. The vote was unanimous. Analysts expect Trump to use the committee process to justify firing Cook. “We do not see why the White House would start this process if it were not trying to create a way to justify removing Cook from her job,” Jaret Seiberg, managing director of TD Cowen’s Washington Research Group, wrote in a note to clients. The demand that Cook testify before the committee has also renewed concerns about threats to the Federal Reserve’s independence.
