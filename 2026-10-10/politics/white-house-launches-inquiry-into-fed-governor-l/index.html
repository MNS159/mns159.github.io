White House launches inquiry into Fed Governor Lisa Cook

Politics desk · anchored on Reuters
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum
✓ Facts
  • President Trump has set up a White House committee to investigate Federal Reserve Governor Lisa Cook over allegations that she made false statements on mortgage documents.
  • Trump first attempted to remove Cook in August 2025; the Supreme Court blocked that firing in a 5-4 ruling in June 2026, finding she had not been given adequate time to respond to the allegations.
  • In August 2026 the White House sent Cook a letter saying Trump was again considering her removal and gave her 21 days to respond; her attorneys replied on Aug. 26 denying the mortgage fraud allegations.
  • A White House notice dated Oct. 7, 2026, and released Oct. 9 (Friday), scheduled Cook's first committee hearing for Nov. 5, 2026, at the White House.
  • Cook must submit a written statement at least three days before the hearing; the hearing itself will be closed to the public, transcribed, and last no more than four hours, during which she may present argument, written evidence, and witness statements while DOJ lawyers question her.
  • The committee includes three White House staffers, among them National Economic Council director Kevin Hassett, who had been a candidate to replace Jerome Powell as Fed Chair before Trump selected Kevin Warsh.
  • After the hearing, Cook is expected to submit another written statement, following which the committee will recommend to Trump whether there is adequate cause to remove her.
  • The underlying allegation is that Cook listed homes in Atlanta and Michigan as her primary residence on separate mortgage agreements and later rented out the Atlanta property; her attorney says the Atlanta designation was an inadvertent error, that the home was financed as a vacation property, and that Michigan was consistently identified as her primary residence.
○ Analysis

This committee is the mechanism the White House has created to satisfy the Supreme Court's requirement that Cook receive adequate due process before any removal attempt proceeds. The underlying dispute—whether Cook misrepresented residency status on mortgage paperwork—has been the stated basis for Trump's effort to remove her from the Fed board since mid-2025, an effort that stalled after the Court's June 2026 ruling. The new process, with a defined hearing date, document deadlines, and a committee staffed by White House officials including a former Fed-chair contender, represents the administration's attempt to create a formal record that could support a renewed removal decision, with the ultimate call resting with Trump based on the committee's recommendation.

Steelman

The dispute sits at the intersection of two legitimate concerns: whether a Fed governor misrepresented information on financial documents, and whether the president can use an internal, White House-run process to remove an official from an independent agency without compromising that independence—the same due-process question the Supreme Court flagged in blocking the first attempt.

Implications

The Nov. 5, 2026 hearing will produce a transcribed record and a committee recommendation that could set up another removal decision by Trump, which, given the Supreme Court's prior intervention, may again face legal challenge over whether the new process satisfies due-process requirements.
