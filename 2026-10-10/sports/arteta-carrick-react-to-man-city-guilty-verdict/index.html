Arteta and Carrick react to Man City guilty verdict; whistleblower remains in protection

Sports desk

Mikel Arteta says his conscience is clear over Manchester City's Premier League rule breaches during a period when he was assistant manager at the club. The Arsenal boss worked under Pep Guardiola between 2016 and 2019, which overlapped with the nine seasons during which City have been found to have broken rules and disguised secret funding of more than £830m. In his first news conference since the ruling — which the club has appealed — Arteta attempted to bat away questions, saying he wanted to 'respect' the ongoing process. However, when asked directly, 'You were at City for three and a half years, is your conscience clear?', he answered: 'Yes'.

Manchester United boss Michael Carrick said he still has his 'own questions' over the case, which he says 'affected me personally'. Carrick was on the pitch at Sunderland when he learned City had won their first Premier League title in 2012 thanks to Sergio Aguero's injury-time winner. He was still part of Jose Mourinho's squad when United finished runners-up to Guardiola's City in 2018 and was on Ole Gunnar Solskjaer's coaching staff when it happened again in 2020-21. Carrick said getting a title winners' medal years after the event would not make up for missing out at the time. 'In football, the strive to win, the emotion and the feeling about winning, a lot of it is in the moment,' he said. 'You can't take that back.'

The Portuguese computer hacker who released documents which helped trigger the Premier League investigation into Manchester City will remain in witness protection after authorities in the country suspended the decision to end it. Football Leaks founder Rui Pinto had been scheduled to leave the protection programme on Saturday, a ruling he previously said had put him in a 'life-threatening situation'. Pinto, 37, disclosed a tranche of confidential football documents, including emails and financial records relating to City. In 2018, he shared documents with German magazine Der Spiegel, which used them for investigations into City's finances and alleged breaches of financial fair play rules. Pinto became part of Portugal's witness protection programme in 2020 after agreeing to co-operate with authorities. On Friday, Portugal's Ministry of Justice said a decision to remove Pinto from witness protection had been reversed for the time being, with the body responsible for it having not adequately 'notified' Pinto of grounds for its termination or given him the opportunity to challenge the decision. Pinto will now be given 10 days to assess a risk assessment conducted by the special security programmes commission that indicates a low threat to his safety.

An independent commission found City guilty of all charges relating to breaches of the Premier League's financial regulations between the 2009-10 and 2017-18 seasons. The club has appealed against the decision. City boss Enzo Maresca has said the club's titles are 'absolutely not' tainted. On Sunday, City — who have won five out of five in the Premier League this term — face a trip to Anfield to play Liverpool. Liverpool boss Andoni Iraola said: 'It's an exciting game. We are playing at home at Anfield against City that is top of the league, they have won all of their games.' Merseyside Police say an appropriate policing plan will be in place for Sunday but chose not to delve into specifics when asked by BBC Sport. There is a growing expectation the team buses will be welcomed when they arrive and posters on social media have advertised a 'Bus Greeting' on Anfield Road.

Arteta also added he '100%' believed the Premier League remains the best in the world. Asked if he had spoken to Guardiola, he said: 'I prefer not to answer those questions.' When asked if he thought the appeal and sanction needed to be resolved this season to preserve the integrity of the Premier League, he replied: 'The people are responsible to manage this process and to come to the right conclusions and I'm sure it will be done and it will be resolved in the right way. We have to trust that.' Arsenal, who sit three points behind league leaders City, face fifth-placed Leeds on Saturday at Emirates Stadium.
