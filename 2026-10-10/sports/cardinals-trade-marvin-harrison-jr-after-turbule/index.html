Cardinals finalizing trade of Marvin Harrison Jr.

Sports desk

The Arizona Cardinals are finalizing a deal to trade wide receiver Marvin Harrison Jr., according to ESPN's Adam Schefter and NFL Network's Ian Rapoport. The Cardinals informed Harrison on Saturday that he will be inactive for Sunday's game against the Detroit Lions while the trade is completed, but neither the acquiring team nor the compensation has been reported yet.

Harrison, the No. 4 overall pick in the 2024 NFL Draft, has had a difficult third season in Arizona. Through four games in 2026, he has caught five of 13 targets for 85 yards and a touchdown, averaging 21.3 receiving yards per game with a career-low 38.5% catch rate. The 24-year-old son of Pro Football Hall of Famer Marvin Harrison was a three-year starter at Ohio State, where he was the 2023 Big Ten Offensive Player of the Year, a consensus All-American and the 2023 Fred Biletnikoff Award winner. He signed a four-year, $35,374,742 rookie contract that was fully guaranteed, including a $22,547,084 signing bonus.

According to The Ringer's Tom Pelissero, the Cardinals have been asking for a second-round pick "and more" for Harrison, and league sources say the young receiver "won't come cheap." Earlier reports indicated Arizona was not shopping Harrison, but that teams were doing their due diligence and reaching out. Trade speculation has mounted in recent weeks after his slow start, and Harrison recently hired an agent after entering the NFL without representation.

The Indianapolis Colts have been mentioned as one team that "would entertain" trading for Harrison, who would join the franchise his father starred for. The Colts also have a practical need at receiver with Alec Pierce on injured reserve, though the reported asking price could be prohibitive: Indianapolis is already without its 2027 first-round pick from the Sauce Gardner trade and is short on 2027 draft capital. Colts Wire also questioned whether a general manager on the hot seat, trying to save his job, would risk trading away future draft picks he may not be around to use.

Harrison's reduced role in Arizona has coincided with the rise of 2023 third-round pick Michael Wilson, who broke out last season with 78 catches for 1,006 receiving yards and seven touchdowns—second on the team only to All-Pro tight end Trey McBride—and signed a three-year, $75 million contract extension this offseason.

The Cardinals holding Harrison out of Sunday's game signals a deal is imminent, with details expected to be available very soon. This year's NFL trade deadline is Nov. 10.
