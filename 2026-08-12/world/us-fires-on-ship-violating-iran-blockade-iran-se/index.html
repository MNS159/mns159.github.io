US forces fire on ship breaching Iran blockade; Iran sets conditions for reopening Strait of Hormuz

International & World desk · anchored on Reuters
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

The United States military fired two Hellfire missiles from a Navy MH-60 helicopter at a Panama-flagged cargo vessel in the Gulf of Oman on Tuesday, after the ship allegedly attempted to violate the US blockade of Iranian ports. Central Command said the strike disabled the vessel's steering gear and that no casualties were reported among the crew. The attack came as Iran reiterated that the Strait of Hormuz — through which one-fifth of the world's oil and natural gas is shipped during peacetime — will remain closed until the United States meets its demands, including the lifting of sanctions, the release of frozen Iranian assets, and compensation for attacks.

Separately, six people were killed in a Houthi attack on a Saudi cargo vessel in the Bab al-Mandeb Strait off Yemen, according to Yemeni authorities. Four crew members aboard the Egyptian-owned Tihamah died, along with two Yemeni rescuers, while ten others were injured. Houthi-affiliated media later claimed the ship was carrying Saudi military equipment. The Bab al-Mandeb, through which about 11 percent of global oil and 8 percent of liquefied natural gas trade passes, has become a new flashpoint amid the broader US-Israel war on Iran.

Commercial traffic through the Strait of Hormuz has plunged since Iran closed the waterway shortly after US-Israeli bombing began in late February. Shipping data from Kpler, cited by Reuters, showed only eight vessels transiting the strait on Tuesday, below the 10-day average of about 12 and the lowest daily tally since August 5. Before the war, roughly 130 ships passed through the strait each day. The disruptions have sent global oil prices higher.

Iranian officials made clear they are unwilling to reopen the strait without major concessions from Washington. Mohsen Rezaie, head of Iran's Supreme National Security Council, told China's ambassador to Tehran: “As long as America does not change its behaviour and does not accept Iran’s conditions, the Strait of Hormuz will not be opened.” In a post on X, Rezaie also demanded an end to the US naval blockade on Iranian ports and a region-wide ceasefire, including in Gaza and Lebanon. Iran says it is negotiating only with Oman — whose territorial waters the strait also crosses — about future management, with Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi stating that talks are in their “final stages.” Qatar’s foreign ministry spokesperson said he had heard positive messages from both Tehran and Muscat regarding the strait’s reopening.

President Donald Trump told reporters on Tuesday that the United States “totally control[s] the Strait of Hormuz,” a claim Iran has refuted. The White House is now pivoting to a sanctions-based strategy to pressure Iran, even as US weapons stockpiles — particularly air defense interceptors used in Patriot and THAAD systems — have reportedly dwindled. Trump denied reports of shortages and said on Monday he would demand compensation from Iran for deaths and injuries linked to conflicts and attacks over several decades. An adviser to Iran’s Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps said the country is developing the ability to conduct operations on “enemy soil,” according to Iranian state TV.

Diplomatic efforts continue, with Pakistani Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi traveling to Tehran on Tuesday for his sixth visit aimed at persuading Iran to resume negotiations under an interim Memorandum of Understanding brokered by Islamabad and Qatar. That agreement, which kept the strait open for 60 days, collapsed amid disagreements over control of the waterway. The MoU is due to expire next week. Negar Mortazavi, a senior fellow at the Center for International Policy, said the key question is whether the escalating rhetoric from both sides reflects “opening negotiating positions or actual red lines,” warning that if neither side is prepared to compromise, “Hormuz could remain restricted and the confrontation risks becoming another prolonged, open-ended conflict in the Middle East.”
