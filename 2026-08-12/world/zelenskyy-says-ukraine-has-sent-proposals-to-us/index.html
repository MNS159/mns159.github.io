Ukraine War

Zelenskyy says Ukraine has sent proposals to US to end war; Russia uses North Korean missiles

International & World desk · anchored on Reuters, AP
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts slightly center-left

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Tuesday that Kyiv has handed the United States new proposals aimed at ending the four-and-a-half-year war with Russia, while accusing Moscow of using North Korean-supplied ballistic missiles in a deadly strike on the southeastern city of Zaporizhzhia. In his nightly video address, Zelenskyy said, “We have conveyed our proposals to the American side,” but offered no details. He said the US could help strengthen Ukraine’s defenses, particularly air defenses, while increasing pressure on Moscow so that Russia prepares “for an end to the war rather than prolonging it.” US negotiators Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner were last in Moscow in January and have not yet visited Kyiv.

The attack on Zaporizhzhia, which involved ballistic missiles from North Korea, killed seven civilian workers at the Metinvest steel company’s plant and injured 21, according to the company. Three more civilians, including a 15-year-old boy, were killed by Russian drones and artillery in the central Dnipropetrovsk region. Russia used missiles, drones and glide bombs to attack Ukrainian cities on Tuesday, Zelenskyy said. One strike hit the grounds of a children’s hospital, leaving two craters but not hurting anyone, the State Emergency Service said. In his address, Zelenskyy said, “for the first time” Russia “cannot wage war without reinforcements from North Korea,” adding that “the more North Korean strikes there are here in Ukraine, in Europe, the more their missiles and soldiers are used, the more they correct their shortcomings and blind spots,” posing a danger to the rest of the world. He did not present evidence for the claim. Ukraine’s military has previously said dozens of North Korean missiles have been fired by Russia since the full-scale invasion began in February 2022.

Zelenskyy also accused Russia of preparing to use its parliamentary elections next month as a pretext for another large-scale military mobilization. “They are preparing for mobilisation in the autumn, immediately after the imitation of parliamentary elections,” he said, as part of a plan to “create the appearance that Russians supposedly support the war.” Following the “pseudo-election,” Russian President Vladimir Putin “plans an additional rapid mobilisation of several hundred thousand Russians by the end of the year, plus another similar number next year,” Zelenskyy added. Russia’s supreme court on Monday barred the Yabloko party, the only officially registered party that opposes Moscow’s war in Ukraine, from competing in the election. Officials in Moscow have previously denied that another mobilization is necessary.

Separately, Ukraine’s military general staff said its forces struck an oil refinery in the city of Orsk in Russia’s Orenburg region near the border with Kazakhstan, about 1,500 km (930 miles) from Ukraine, causing a fire. The refinery can process about 44 million barrels of crude oil a year. The Russian online retailer Wildberries said its logistical facilities in the Voronezh region near the Ukrainian border came under attack and caught fire. Romania’s defence ministry said its navy destroyed two suspected Russian Gerbera-type drones drifting in the Black Sea about 90 nautical miles (166 km) from the port of Constanta. The UN estimates that more than 16,000 civilians detained by Russia during the war are still being “deprived of their liberty,” with many held incommunicado, and that “widespread and systematic torture and ill-treatment” of both Ukrainian civilian detainees and prisoners of war by Russia are continuing, including sexual violence, said Claudia Fuentes Julio, the UN assistant secretary general for human rights.

On Tuesday, US President Donald Trump announced that Russia released former US Marine Robert Gilman after four years in detention. Trump said Putin agreed to free the 32-year-old “on a humanitarian basis” and that Washington released no prisoners in return. Gilman was expected to arrive at Andrews Air Force Base near Washington, DC, on Tuesday night. He was detained in Russia in January 2022 after an alleged altercation with a police officer and was later designated by Washington as wrongfully detained.
