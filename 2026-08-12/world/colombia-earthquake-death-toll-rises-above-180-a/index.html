Colombia earthquake death toll reaches 181 as rescuers race to find survivors

International & World desk

Rescuers in Colombia are working against the clock to free survivors trapped under rubble after a magnitude-7.4 earthquake struck western Colombia on Monday, killing at least 181 people and injuring nearly 2,600. President Abelardo de la Espriella, who was sworn in just days before the disaster, declared a state of emergency and said the government is deploying all its capabilities to protect lives and deliver aid. The quake hit at 7:34 a.m. local time on Monday, with its epicenter in the remote Chocó department, and was felt across a vast area including neighboring Ecuador, Venezuela and Panama. More than 70 aftershocks have been recorded since.

The hardest-hit cities are Cali, with 95 confirmed deaths, and Pereira, where 79 people have died, according to local officials. At least five people were killed in Manizales. Across the country, more than 1,100 homes were destroyed and over 8,000 damaged, while dozens of larger buildings collapsed. In Cali, a city of 2.2 million, Mayor Alejandro Eder said 239 people remained trapped, 949 were injured, and 45 buildings had either completely or partially collapsed. The main public hospital in Cali collapsed, and 14 hospitals in the city were damaged overall, leaving health facilities overwhelmed. In Pereira, 65 buildings collapsed, and five hospitals were inundated with the injured. Manizales reported 60 buildings partially or totally collapsed. The Colombian geological service said the earthquake was the strongest in the country this century.

Rescue efforts continued through Tuesday and into Wednesday, with the first 48 to 72 hours considered critical for finding survivors. At the collapsed Torres del Limonar building in Cali, rescuers and volunteers worked under scorching heat, digging tunnels toward at least two people detected alive. "Every time there is an alert about someone being alive, hope is reborn that we will find him," said Efraín Gallego, whose 80-year-old brother is trapped in the building with his wife. Rescuer James Velasco said, "In the past I have participated in operations where we have pulled out survivors after seven days trapped." Local media reported the rescue of a woman after 36 hours under rubble. Authorities in Cali, Armenia and Manizales imposed nighttime curfews to prevent looting, and Cali's mayor deployed police and military personnel to the streets.

The earthquake compounds a severe humanitarian situation in a country already grappling with deep poverty and armed conflict, according to Giovanni Rizzo, Colombia director for the Norwegian Refugee Council. The United Nations said it is working to support the government's response, and Secretary-General António Guterres expressed solidarity with the people and government of Colombia. International aid has begun to arrive: the United States announced a $15.5 million donation for emergency shelter, food and other aid, while Mexico, Brazil, El Salvador, Ecuador and Venezuela offered assistance. Spain's foreign minister reported that 164 Spanish citizens were still missing, but no injuries or deaths of Spanish nationals had been confirmed. The Trump administration, which backed President Espriella in his election, provided the U.S. aid.

President Espriella, who won office by less than one percentage point, faces a high-profile test of his ability to govern as the disaster response unfolds. By Tuesday, he said the official death toll stood at 181, with 2,595 injured and at least 195 missing, though civilian-run databases put the number of missing at closer to 4,000. He promised rent subsidies for those who lost homes and an additional 1,000 security personnel for Cali. The president visited Quibdó, the capital of Chocó, and later traveled to Cali to assess the damage. "Colombia has a president whose heart aches for his people and who will do everything in his power to help them in the most difficult moments," he said in a video post. Rescue teams continue to search for survivors, with small successes including the rescue of a dust-covered baby and a woman pulled from the rubble, as the nation braces for more aftershocks.
