Ukraine halts strikes on tankers using Russian port at US request; Russia turns to India for gasoline

International & World desk · anchored on Reuters, AP
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

Ukraine has halted its intensive campaign of drone strikes against oil tankers using the major Black Sea port of Novorossiysk after a request from US Vice President JD Vance, according to Ukrainian officials and other people familiar with the matter cited by the Financial Times. The move came as Russia and Ukraine traded deadly overnight drone attacks that killed a child in Novorossiysk and two people in Ukraine’s southern city of Kherson, regional officials said.

Vance asked President Volodymyr Zelenskyy to halt the strikes during a phone call on 31 July, the Financial Times reported. Since then, Ukraine has not struck tankers near the Caspian Pipeline Consortium (CPC) terminal at Novorossiysk, which handles crude exports from Kazakhstan in which major US energy companies Chevron and ExxonMobil have stakes. A senior Ukrainian official said Kyiv agreed not to strike CPC infrastructure facilities or non-Russian vessels, provided those vessels are not subject to Ukrainian sanctions and are not carrying Russian oil or other Russian cargo. A US administration official confirmed that Washington had warned Ukraine to stop attacking non-Russian vessels in the Black Sea and CPC infrastructure, calling the CPC a vital conduit of Kazakhstan-origin energy for European markets. The official added that the US had reaffirmed Kyiv’s commitment to refrain from such strikes. A person familiar with Ukraine’s position said Kyiv agreed to the request because it hopes to obtain a US license to produce Patriot interceptor missiles and to purchase several hundred of these missiles before winter.

The overnight attacks on Novorossiysk involved hundreds of Ukrainian drones, missiles and unmanned naval systems, according to Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy, who said the operation targeted air defence positions, piers and seaport infrastructure at the naval base. Russia’s Defence Ministry said it downed and destroyed 502 Ukrainian drones. The regional governor of the Krasnodar region, which includes Novorossiysk, said an eight-year-old child was killed and 12 other people were wounded in the region. The mayor of Novorossiysk said the city endured a horrific night, with dozens of buildings damaged. The owner of the Novorossiysk grain terminal, Demetra Holding, said drone strikes damaged the facility. In Russia-occupied Crimea, Ukrainian drones damaged several homes, said the local governor.

In Ukraine, a Russian attack using Shahed drones killed two people and injured at least two others in Kherson, according to the head of the region’s military administration. A Russian attack on the southeastern city of Zaporizhzhia triggered a blaze at a shopping mall and cut power for some 1,200 consumers, said the regional governor. A separate Russian attack on the port of Odesa damaged infrastructure, said the head of the city’s military administration. Russia’s Defence Ministry claimed it hit a fuel depot in Odesa used to supply the Ukrainian army and said its forces had taken control of the village of Vodiane in the Kharkiv region.

Zelenskyy said on Tuesday that Kyiv had handed proposals to US negotiators for a plan to end the war, which began with Russia’s full-scale invasion in February 2022. Separately, Bloomberg reported that Russia has turned to India for gasoline as Ukrainian strikes pummel Russian refineries, though no further details were provided. The US State Department in February expressed displeasure about Ukraine’s attacks on Novorossiysk, saying they have impacted US oil interests in Kazakhstan.
