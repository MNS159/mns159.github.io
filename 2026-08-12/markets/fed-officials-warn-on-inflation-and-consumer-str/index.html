Fed officials warn on inflation and consumer strain; Trump renews push to oust Cook

Financial & Markets desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

Federal Reserve Bank of Cleveland President Beth Hammack warned that a single interest-rate hike is unlikely to be sufficient to curb inflation, which is eroding workers’ wage gains and straining household budgets, while the Trump administration continues its yearlong effort to remove Federal Reserve Board Governor Lisa Cook despite a Supreme Court ruling protecting her position.

In a Yahoo Finance interview published Monday, Hammack said that “one 25 basis point move probably doesn't do a whole lot for the economy. So it's probably some number… But I don't want to prejudge what that number is going to be.” She added that the Fed “shouldn't fade into the background” on its price-stability mandate, stating: “Markets are a complement for the Fed. They're not a substitute. We have to stand behind our words with our actions when appropriate.” Hammack was among three regional bank presidents who dissented at the Fed’s late-July meeting, voting to raise rates by a quarter point to address energy supply shocks from the Iran War that have pushed up gasoline and transportation-dependent grocery prices. The majority voted 9-3 to hold the federal funds rate at 3.5% to 3.75% for the fifth consecutive meeting.

Hammack described rising anxiety among consumers and businesses. “What I have heard from across the Fourth Federal Reserve District reinforces this view: Businesses describe pricing pressures as broadening rather than fading, and consumers are expressing despair over persistently higher prices,” she wrote in a LinkedIn post. The Fed’s next moves will be influenced by two inflation readings: the July consumer price index (CPI) due Wednesday, which analysts expect to show a 2.5% year-over-year core increase, and the personal consumption expenditures (PCE) gauge on Aug. 26. If the reports show higher-than-expected price increases, pressure will mount on Fed Chair Kevin Warsh to push through rate hikes.

Separately, President Donald Trump is continuing efforts to remove Lisa Cook, the only Black woman on the Federal Reserve Board, despite a 5-4 Supreme Court ruling in June that blocked her immediate firing but allowed the president to pursue removal through a process requiring cause. According to the Associated Press, White House Deputy Chief of Staff Dan Scavino sent Cook a letter stating that Trump is “still considering removing you from your position” based on allegations of mortgage fraud made by Federal Housing Finance Agency Director Bill Pulte in 2025. The administration has not filed criminal charges. Cook’s attorney, Abbe Lowell, said in a statement: “These allegations are as baseless now as they were a year ago when President Trump tried to remove Governor Cook and interfere with the independence of the Federal Reserve.” He added that the defense will “challenge this latest pretext and preserve her position and the historic role of the Fed.”

The accusations against Cook are part of a broader pattern of Trump and his allies bringing what critics call politically motivated allegations against perceived opponents, including New York Attorney General Letitia James and former FBI Director James Comey. Cook has consistently denied the claims. The White House has given her weeks to mount a defense, even as courts have rebuked similar efforts. The ongoing standoff underscores tensions between the administration and the Federal Reserve’s independence, as the central bank navigates persistent inflation and consumer strain.
