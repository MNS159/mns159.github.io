CME to launch AI compute futures as T. Rowe Price sees $1.6 trillion capex ahead

Financial & Markets desk

CME Group plans to launch the first futures contracts tied to the cost of running AI chips on Oct. 5, pending regulatory approval, the exchange announced in partnership with Silicon Data. The contracts will allow buyers and sellers to trade against the rental cost of Nvidia's H100 and newer Blackwell B200 graphics processing units, based on Silicon Data indexes that track hourly GPU rental prices. Each contract will represent a month's rent for the Nvidia H100.

"For years, two companies buying the exact same GPU capacity could pay wildly different prices with no way to know who got the better deal. They will now have a benchmark to check that against," said Carmen Li, CEO of Silicon Data, in a statement. "Compute futures give the market something it's never had: a public, tradable reference price for the resource every AI system runs on."

The launch comes as Wall Street finds new ways to finance and gain exposure to the enormous AI infrastructure buildout. Nvidia is working with some of the world's largest asset managers on an effort that could channel as much as $500 billion into AI infrastructure. Compute futures add another layer to that emerging financial ecosystem, allowing investors to gain exposure to the price of the underlying computing capacity itself, while AI developers and data-center operators can use the contracts to hedge their costs or revenues.

Separately, a T. Rowe Price fund manager said AI capital expenditure could hit $1.6 trillion next year, according to a MarketWatch report. The manager argued that hyperscale cloud providers can comfortably fund their investment spending and derive high returns on that invested capital with a short payback schedule, drawing a comparison to 1998 rather than the dot-com bust.
