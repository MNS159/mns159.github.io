Markets await July CPI data amid Fed rate hike uncertainty

Financial & Markets desk

U.S. stock futures edged higher Wednesday as investors braced for the July consumer price index report, a key data point that could determine the Federal Reserve's next move on interest rates. The CPI, due at 8:30 a.m. ET from the Bureau of Labor Statistics, is expected to show a monthly increase of 0.1% for the headline number and 0.2% for core inflation, which excludes volatile food and energy prices, according to the Dow Jones consensus. On an annual basis, economists forecast 3.4% headline inflation and 2.5% core, both down 0.1 percentage point from June but still above the Fed's 2% target.

S&P 500 futures rose 0.3% and Nasdaq-100 futures climbed 0.7% in premarket trading, while Dow Jones Industrial Average futures added 72 points, or 0.1%. The moves came as AI-related stocks rallied on strong earnings: CoreWeave surged 18% after its second-quarter adjusted operating income margin of 5% exceeded expectations, and Super Micro Computer jumped 9% following a quarterly sales forecast that beat estimates. U.S.-listed shares of Nebius Group also rose more than 8%.

The inflation report follows a softer-than-expected July jobs report that showed nonfarm payrolls fell by 23,000 even as the unemployment rate dropped to 4.1%. Traders currently see roughly a 50-50 chance of a rate hike at the Fed's September meeting, according to the CME's FedWatch gauge. The central bank skipped an August meeting and will have the advantage of reviewing both July and August inflation data before its next decision. At its July meeting, the Federal Open Market Committee voted 9-3 to hold its key borrowing rate at 3.5%-3.75%, with the three dissenting members favoring a quarter-point increase.

A hotter-than-expected CPI print could strengthen the case for a September rate hike, while signs of further easing could give the Fed room to hold steady. Bank of America economists said in a client note that if the Fed's primary inflation gauge averages 0.25% increases over the next two months, 'it is all but guaranteed that the Fed will begin hiking rates in September.' Conversely, an average below 0.2% would delay an increase, while anything in between would make September 'a coin flip,' with the decision resting on Fed Chairman Kevin Warsh and his reaction function. Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack, one of the three dissenters at the July meeting, told Yahoo Finance she expects multiple increases likely will be needed, saying 'one 25-basis-point move probably doesn't do a whole lot for the economy.'

Ongoing tensions in the Middle East continue to prop up inflation and yields, as the U.S. and Iran remain deadlocked in negotiations to reopen the Strait of Hormuz. The U.S. enforced its blockade of the strait by firing on a Panama-flagged ship attempting to cross the Gulf of Oman on Tuesday, lifting Brent crude futures close to $90 per barrel. U.S. oil prices pushed above $83 a barrel in morning trading. The 30-year Treasury yield remained at 5.24%, while South Korea's 30-year government bond yield climbed to a record. The July producer price index is due Thursday, followed later in the month by the Fed's preferred PCE inflation gauge.
