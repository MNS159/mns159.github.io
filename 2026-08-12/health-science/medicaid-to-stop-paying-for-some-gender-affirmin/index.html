Medicaid to stop paying for some gender-affirming care for transgender minors

Health & Science desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

The Trump administration announced that Medicaid will stop paying for gender-affirming surgeries and hormone treatments for transgender minors, and the state Children's Health Insurance Program (CHIP) will no longer cover those treatments for individuals under 19. The policy, announced Tuesday, is set to take effect Oct. 13.

Under the rule, Medicaid and CHIP will continue to pay for mental health treatment for transgender minors but will phase out coverage of hormone treatments — including those used to block puberty and to produce sex-related physical changes — for those already receiving them as of April 2027. The Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services did not respond to questions about the policy on Tuesday, and it is not clear how much taxpayer funding currently goes toward gender-affirming treatments for transgender youth.

At least 27 states have already banned gender-affirming care for those under 18, and many major hospitals in states without bans have stopped providing some treatments. Gender-affirming surgeries for minors are rare; puberty blockers and hormone treatments are more common. The American Medical Association and most other major medical organizations support access to gender-affirming care, though the American Society of Plastic Surgeons said earlier this year that it found insufficient evidence that the benefits of chest, genital and facial surgeries on minors experiencing gender dysphoria outweigh the risks.

President Trump said in a social media post Tuesday that he directed Dr. Mehmet Oz, the CMS administrator, to make the change. "We are not going to pay for our innocent children to undergo these barbaric surgeries and practices, which result in unthinkable and irreversible harm to their young bodies," Trump wrote.

Advocates for LGBTQ+ young people condemned the policy. GLBTQ Legal Advocates and Defenders legal director Josh Rovenger said the rule "is putting up barriers between parents and their ability to make the best medical decisions for their children" and would face a legal challenge. Human Rights Campaign President Kelley Robinson said in a statement, "The Trump administration is terrorizing trans youth and their families with these kinds of actions, and it has to stop." HRC is already suing the administration over efforts to bar gender-affirming care coverage under federal employee benefit plans.

The announcement is the latest in a series of administration policies targeting transgender people since Trump returned to office last year. Other moves include barring transgender athletes from girls' and women's sports, blocking people from choosing male, female or X on passports, and releasing a report urging therapy only for transgender youth. Most of these changes have faced legal challenges: the U.S. Supreme Court allowed the passport policy and sports bans to stand, while a lower court ruled against the administration's declaration that the treatments are unsafe and ineffective.
