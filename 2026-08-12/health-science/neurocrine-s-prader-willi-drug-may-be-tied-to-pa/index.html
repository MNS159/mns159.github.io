Neurocrine's Prader-Willi drug linked to patient deaths and severe side effects, experts warn

Health & Science desk

A group of physicians and experts in Prader-Willi syndrome (PWS) has alerted clinicians to a series of patient deaths and severe side effects potentially associated with Vykat XR, a newly approved drug from Neurocrine Biosciences. In a notice issued Tuesday, the experts reported that seven patients prescribed the drug have died, and more than 100 serious adverse events—primarily hospitalizations for swelling, respiratory, and heart complications—have been logged in the FDA’s Adverse Event Monitoring System since the drug’s clearance.

Vykat XR was approved by the Food and Drug Administration in March 2025 to curb the intense hunger sensation in children and adults with PWS, a rare disease characterized by an insatiable desire to eat. The experts emphasized that neither the deaths nor the severe side effects have been definitively linked to the drug, but they stated, “The intention of this statement is to increase awareness of the risks for people with PWS when starting Vykat XR.”

The warning comes from a group of physicians and experts specializing in PWS, who are urging clinicians to be vigilant about potential complications. The FDA’s adverse event database shows the reports span a period since the drug’s approval, though the experts did not specify a timeframe for the deaths or hospitalizations. Neurocrine Biosciences has not yet issued a public response to the alert.

The situation raises significant concerns for the PWS community, as Vykat XR is the first FDA-approved treatment specifically targeting the hyperphagia (intense hunger) associated with the syndrome. The experts’ notice serves as a cautionary step while regulators and the company investigate whether the drug is causally linked to the reported fatalities and serious adverse events.
