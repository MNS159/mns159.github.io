Vaccine Policy

Trump executive order on childhood vaccines draws criticism from health experts, industry

Health & Science desk

President Donald Trump on Monday signed an executive order that seeks to unilaterally change the federal government's approach to childhood vaccination, recommending fewer shots, longer intervals between doses, and a split of the combined measles, mumps, and rubella (MMR) vaccine into three separate shots — a move for which the president has acknowledged he has no evidence. The order, which also directs states to reconsider school vaccine mandates, has drawn immediate pushback from health industry leaders and public health experts, who argue it undermines trust in established recommendations and could lead to more missed doses and disease outbreaks.

Drug Safety

Neurocrine's Prader-Willi drug linked to patient deaths and severe side effects, experts warn

Health & Science desk

A group of physicians and experts in Prader-Willi syndrome (PWS) has alerted clinicians to a series of patient deaths and severe side effects potentially associated with Vykat XR, a newly approved drug from Neurocrine Biosciences. In a notice issued Tuesday, the experts reported that seven patients prescribed the drug have died, and more than 100 serious adverse events—primarily hospitalizations for swelling, respiratory, and heart complications—have been logged in the FDA’s Adverse Event Monitoring System since the drug’s clearance.

Biosecurity

Researchers use AI to create novel viruses for first time, raising promise and concerns

Health & Science desk

For the first time, researchers have used artificial intelligence to generate novel viruses in a laboratory setting, according to a paper published in the journal Science. The work, led by computational biologist Brian Hie of Stanford University, involved training AI models on genomic sequences to design entirely new viral DNA. The researchers then introduced that DNA into bacteria, which acted as factories to produce viruses that went on to infect other bacteria. Hie recalled that when the team first presented their successful results, the lab spontaneously broke into applause—the first time that had happened in his lab meetings.
