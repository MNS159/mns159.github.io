International & World Disaster

Colombia earthquake death toll reaches 181 as rescuers race to find survivors

International & World desk

Rescuers in Colombia are working against the clock to free survivors trapped under rubble after a magnitude-7.4 earthquake struck western Colombia on Monday, killing at least 181 people and injuring nearly 2,600. President Abelardo de la Espriella, who was sworn in just days before the disaster, declared a state of emergency and said the government is deploying all its capabilities to protect lives and deliver aid. The quake hit at 7:34 a.m. local time on Monday, with its epicenter in the remote Chocó department, and was felt across a vast area including neighboring Ecuador, Venezuela and Panama. More than 70 aftershocks have been recorded since.

The hardest-hit cities are Cali, with 95 confirmed deaths, and Pereira, where 79 people have died, according to local officials. At least five people were killed in Manizales. Across the country, more than 1,100 homes were destroyed and over 8,000 damaged, while dozens of larger buildings collapsed. In Cali, a city of 2.2 million, Mayor Alejandro Eder said 239 people remained trapped, 949 were injured, and 45 buildings had either completely or partially collapsed. The main public hospital in Cali collapsed, and 14 hospitals in the city were damaged overall, leaving health facilities overwhelmed. In Pereira, 65 buildings collapsed, and five hospitals were inundated with the injured. Manizales reported 60 buildings partially or totally collapsed. The Colombian geological service said the earthquake was the strongest in the country this century.

US forces fire on ship breaching Iran blockade; Iran sets conditions for reopening Strait of Hormuz

International & World desk · anchored on Reuters

The United States military fired two Hellfire missiles from a Navy MH-60 helicopter at a Panama-flagged cargo vessel in the Gulf of Oman on Tuesday, after the ship allegedly attempted to violate the US blockade of Iranian ports. Central Command said the strike disabled the vessel's steering gear and that no casualties were reported among the crew. The attack came as Iran reiterated that the Strait of Hormuz — through which one-fifth of the world's oil and natural gas is shipped during peacetime — will remain closed until the United States meets its demands, including the lifting of sanctions, the release of frozen Iranian assets, and compensation for attacks.

Markets await July CPI data amid Fed rate hike uncertainty

Financial & Markets desk

U.S. stock futures edged higher Wednesday as investors braced for the July consumer price index report, a key data point that could determine the Federal Reserve's next move on interest rates. The CPI, due at 8:30 a.m. ET from the Bureau of Labor Statistics, is expected to show a monthly increase of 0.1% for the headline number and 0.2% for core inflation, which excludes volatile food and energy prices, according to the Dow Jones consensus. On an annual basis, economists forecast 3.4% headline inflation and 2.5% core, both down 0.1 percentage point from June but still above the Fed's 2% target.

Trump executive order on childhood vaccines draws criticism from health experts, industry

President Donald Trump on Monday signed an executive order that seeks to unilaterally change the federal government's approach to childhood vaccination, recommending fewer shots, longer intervals between doses, and a split of the combined measles, mumps, and rubella (MMR) vaccine into three separate shots — a move for which the president has acknowledged he has no evidence. The order, which also directs states to reconsider school vaccine mandates, has drawn immediate pushback from health industry leaders and public health experts, who argue it undermines trust in established recommendations and could lead to more missed doses and disease outbreaks.
