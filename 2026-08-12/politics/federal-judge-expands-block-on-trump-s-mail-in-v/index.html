Federal Judge Expands Block on Trump's Mail-In Voting Executive Order for Midterms

Politics desk
✓ Facts
  • U.S. District Judge Indira Talwani in Boston issued a ruling Tuesday expanding a prior block on part of President Trump's executive order (EO 14399) affecting mail-in voting.
  • The new preliminary injunction bars the U.S. Postal Service from implementing or enforcing Section 3 of the EO nationwide, covering the Nov. 3 midterms or any earlier federal election; her June ruling had only blocked USPS compliance in the 23 states (led by California, Massachusetts, Nevada and Washington) plus Washington, D.C., that sued.
  • The EO, signed by Trump in March, directs DHS to compile lists of confirmed U.S. citizens eligible to vote and share them with states, instructs USPS to set new standards for mail-in ballots, and directs DOJ to prioritize investigations of election officials who issue ballots to people deemed ineligible.
  • Talwani found the plaintiffs are likely to prove the EO's directives exceed presidential authority under the Constitution, that the administration provided no evidence of fraudulent absentee voting, and that the order is causing voter confusion.
  • On July 25, the 1st U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals, in a 2-1 decision, denied a DOJ request to pause Talwani's June injunction while the administration's appeal proceeds.
  • The injunction does not prevent USPS from issuing nonbinding guidance on ballot-mail envelopes, and Talwani ordered the plaintiff organizations to post a $100 bond by Aug. 18.
  • The Trump administration is expected to appeal Tuesday's ruling to the 1st Circuit, and the Department of Justice has previously said it could seek emergency relief from the Supreme Court.
○ Analysis

This ruling is the latest step in an ongoing legal fight over Trump's March executive order aimed at tightening rules around mail-in and absentee voting, which the administration frames as an election-integrity measure targeting noncitizen voting. Voting rights groups and Democratic-led states sued, arguing the order intrudes on states' constitutional authority over election administration. Talwani's June ruling limited the block to the states that sued; her Tuesday ruling extends that block nationwide ahead of the 2026 midterms, meaning USPS cannot implement the challenged mail-ballot provisions anywhere while litigation continues. The case is moving through the federal appellate system, with the 1st Circuit already declining to pause the earlier injunction and the Supreme Court still expected to weigh in, making the final legal status of the order unresolved.

Steelman

Both sides are effectively litigating a foundational separation-of-powers question—how far a president can direct federal agencies to set rules for state-run elections—while courts so far have focused less on fraud evidence (which the administration hasn't presented) than on questions of constitutional authority.

Implications

With the 1st Circuit appeal expected and the Supreme Court's involvement still pending, the legal status of Trump's mail-in voting order remains unsettled going into the Nov. 3 midterms, meaning USPS's mail-ballot practices could still change again depending on how appellate courts rule in the coming weeks.
