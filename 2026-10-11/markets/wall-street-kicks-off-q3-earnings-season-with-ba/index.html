Wall Street Kicks Off Q3 Earnings Season With Bank Results, High Expectations

Financial & Markets desk

The third-quarter earnings season begins this week, led by the largest U.S. banks, as investors gauge whether corporate profits can sustain the stock market's momentum amid rising Treasury yields and elevated oil prices. Analysts forecast that earnings among S&P 500 companies will rise approximately 31% year over year, with technology and AI-related businesses accounting for roughly two-thirds of the increase, according to LSEG estimates. The S&P 500 climbed to an all-time high last week, but the benchmark has been volatile recently due to concerns that the Federal Reserve may raise rates further to curb inflation.

JPMorgan Chase, Goldman Sachs, Citigroup and Wells Fargo are scheduled to report results on Tuesday, followed by Morgan Stanley and Bank of America on Wednesday. Overall, 25 S&P 500 companies are set to release quarterly figures this week. JPMorgan is expected to report double-digit earnings growth from a year earlier, after posting a 41% year-on-year earnings jump last quarter. Goldman Sachs' top and bottom lines are forecast to have grown 10% and around 6%, respectively. Citigroup is expected to report sharp year-on-year earnings growth, while Wells Fargo is forecast to post earnings growth of more than 10%, per LSEG data. Morgan Stanley's top line is expected to have grown nearly 10%, and Bank of America is expected to report single-digit year-on-year earnings and revenue growth.

The results come at a delicate time for the financial sector. Over the past month, the Financial Services sector was the second-worst performing sector in the S&P 500, losing roughly 4%. As of Friday's market close, the five major banks have lost roughly $270 billion in market capitalization. Only 35% of institutional investors expect bank stocks to outperform the broader market, down from 68% in July and 82% in December, per a Truist Securities study. Wells Fargo shares are down 13% over the past month, the most among six major U.S. banks.

Analysts are watching for how banks discuss the impact of higher interest rates. Seaport Research Partners said of JPMorgan: "While we raised our [net interest income] forecast for next year on the higher forward curve ... we are above consensus on expenses, which could be guided higher as well." TD Cowen initiated Goldman Sachs as a buy, stating: "GS is leveraging its enormous scale to benefit from the favorable capital markets and asset-price backdrops." Jefferies analyst David Chiaverini has a buy rating on Citigroup, citing its "improving return profile." Barclays has an overweight rating on Bank of America, though analyst Jason Goldberg expects "somewhat mixed results."

Beyond banks, investors will also get results from UnitedHealth and Johnson & Johnson, providing a view on the healthcare sector, and from ASML and Taiwan Semiconductor, offering checks on the AI trade. On the economic data front, Wednesday's consumer inflation report is expected to be a major factor for the Federal Reserve's rate decision at its October meeting. The wholesale inflation check via the Producer Price Index is due Thursday, along with readings on industrial production and manufacturing activity on Friday.

The broader earnings picture shows growth moderating from the second quarter, when S&P 500 earnings increased nearly 54% year over year, the highest rate since 2021. Energy sector earnings are projected to rise approximately 115% year over year, following a roughly 30% increase in U.S. crude oil prices during the quarter amid the ongoing U.S.-Israeli conflict with Iran. Consumer staples and real estate are among the sectors with the lowest expected year-over-year earnings growth. Sameer Samana, head of global equities and real assets at Wells Fargo Investment Institute, said: "It's all AI and, to a lesser extent, energy and materials, but that's because of geopolitics. It wouldn't surprise me if 70-80% of the growth can be attributed to tech and AI."
