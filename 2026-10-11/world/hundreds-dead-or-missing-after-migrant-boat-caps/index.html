Migrant Crisis

At least 139 dead, 84 missing after migrant boat capsizes off Djibouti

International & World desk

At least 139 people, including 15 children, have died and 84 remain missing after a boat carrying more than 300 migrants capsized off the coast of Djibouti, the United Nations migration agency said Sunday. The vessel, which had departed from Djibouti bound for Yemen with an estimated 305 people aboard, went down near the port town of Obock and the Guhere area on Thursday night into Friday, according to survivors cited by the International Organization for Migration (IOM). Around 82 people were rescued, all Ethiopian nationals, as Djiboutian authorities continued search and rescue operations.

The IOM revised its death toll upward from 109 after 30 more bodies were recovered. Of the bodies recovered so far, 52 were men, 42 were women and 15 were children. IOM Djibouti mission chief Alexandre Coissac called the loss of life 'devastating' and said the tragedy was 'a stark reminder of the dangers faced by migrants along this migration corridor as they seek economic opportunities.' Local authorities are arranging burials for the dead, while survivors have medical care available and the IOM is providing assistance to 64 of them at its response center in Obock, including healthcare and support to restore contact with their families.

The crossing from Djibouti to Yemen via the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, which measures just 20 kilometers (12.4 miles) at its narrowest point, is a popular but perilous route for thousands of migrants from the Horn of Africa. Many undertake the journey driven by poverty, insecurity and the growing impacts of climate change, hoping to reach Gulf countries for safety and work. Since September 10, the IOM said its Displacement Tracking Matrix had observed more than 20,000 movements from Ethiopia to Djibouti. More than 900 migrants died or went missing taking the same route in 2025, during an 18% increase in the number of people attempting the journey, according to IOM statistics.

Yemen remains engulfed in a devastating civil war that has lasted more than a decade, with fighting between Houthi fighters and the Saudi-backed Yemeni government resuming last month. Despite the conflict and growing instability across the wider region, migrants continue to attempt the crossing. The conflict has also displaced more than 130,000 people across Yemen, and thousands of Yemenis have fled to Djibouti to escape the fighting. The IOM urged donors and partners to strengthen support for Djibouti's response, including search and rescue operations and life-saving assistance, and said it was working in close collaboration with local authorities to help migrants and prevent deaths at sea and on land.
