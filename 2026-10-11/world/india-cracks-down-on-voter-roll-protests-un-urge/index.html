India frees 'cockroach' party leaders as protests over voter rolls enter second day

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts slightly center-left

New Delhi saw a second consecutive day of sweeping security measures on Sunday as India's Gen Z-led Cockroach Janta Party (CJP) pushed ahead with protests demanding the resignation of the country's chief election commissioner, a day after police boarded their flight and detained the movement's founder and two other senior leaders. Abhijeet Dipke, Saurav Das and Ashutosh Ranka were freed by a magistrate's court after 24 hours in detention on condition that they commit "no breach of peace" for one year. Media reports said about 7,000 protesters were detained and released on Saturday, with hundreds more held on Sunday, in a crackdown that drew a special Supreme Court hearing in which judges called the detentions illegal and ordered the government to restore internet services, allow normal metro and rail operations, permit free movement and end illegal detentions.

The protests centre on a nationwide drive to update electoral rolls that the Election Commission describes as a routine exercise to remove dead, duplicate or ineligible voters but that the CJP and opposition parties say is being manipulated to benefit Prime Minister Narendra Modi's Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP). At least 130 million voter names have been purged so far, and tens of thousands of people in many states allege they were wrongly removed; Al Jazeera reported that millions of voters, including Muslims, were deleted in a process marked by technical errors and a perceived lack of transparency. The CJP — which is not registered as a political party — is seeking electoral reforms and has demanded the resignation of Chief Election Commissioner (CEC) Gyanesh Kumar, whom it accuses of disenfranchising voters to help the BJP. The Election Commission and the BJP deny the allegations. An investigation by the Indian Express newspaper found that two of the commission's three members objected to many of Kumar's decisions; the commission is a constitutional body whose decisions are taken by majority.

Saturday's operation turned the capital into a fortress. More than 30,000 police and paramilitary personnel were deployed, according to local media; at least 45 metro stations and Delhi's main railway stations were shut; major train services were cancelled; mobile internet was suspended across several areas; and some protesters travelling from outside the city were de-boarded en route. Authorities refused permission for the planned protest at Jantar Mantar, the designated protest site near parliament, which was sealed off with barricades, concrete blocks and anti-riot vehicles equipped with tear-gas launchers. Police entered an Air India plane after it landed from Mumbai to detain Dipke, Das and Ranka; in a video Dipke shared on Instagram, he told them, "I have come to get arrested. I am not afraid of you guys." Another video showed him saying, "This is dictatorship." Those detained also included YouTuber Dhruv Rathee, All India Students' Association national president Neha Bora, and opposition figures including former chief ministers Mamata Banerjee of West Bengal, Arvind Kejriwal of Delhi and Pinarayi Vijayan of Kerala. About 600 people were arrested in Mumbai, according to the ANI news agency. Small protests elsewhere, including at the Dilli Haat crafts market, were quashed, and journalists from outlets including Scroll.in and Newslaundry were detained, with some reporting that their equipment was seized.

Police said all detainees had been released by Sunday, after the Supreme Court, at a special hearing, called the detentions illegal. In his first comments after his release, Dipke posted on X that "Modi, as PM, is an embarrassment to India. Never has this country seen a bigger coward and bully," and said the CJP "will neither back down nor be intimidated." The leaders accused the Modi government of "waging war" on its own citizens to protect the election commissioner. Das condemned police for "arriving like mafia dons" and "abducting" the group on the plane, and Rathee said he had been "kidnapped." At a news conference, Ranka insisted the bail conditions did not bar further demonstrations. "Peaceful protest is our constitutional right and Jantar Mantar is a designated protest site, so this doesn't mean we can't do peaceful protest," he said. He added: "Breach of peace is done by Prime Minister Narendra Modi, Home Minister Amit Shah and CEC Gyanesh Kumar."

The CJP grew out of a post Dipke wrote on X in May — shortly after completing a master's degree at Boston University and having previously worked with the opposition Aam Aadmi Party — asking, "What if all cockroaches come together?" The phrase responded to a remark by Chief Justice Surya Kant likening jobless young people to "cockroaches," which he later said was misinterpreted. The post went viral, and the movement tapped into anger over unemployment, exam paper leaks and government accountability. During five weeks of protests this summer, the group occupied Jantar Mantar to demand education reforms, and its campaign ended in July with the resignation of Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan.

On Sunday, much of central Delhi remained barricaded, with offices, shops and restaurants shut and rows of yellow police barriers lining Connaught Place, one of the city's busiest commercial hubs. The BBC witnessed protesters being chased, detained and bundled into buses as small groups slipped past barricades chanting "Gyanesh must go!" Some demonstrators acknowledged the impact of the leadership's absence. "Without them, we're feeling a little lost," one protester told the BBC, "but that doesn't mean we'll stop our fight." With crucial state elections just months away, the CJP and student groups said protests would continue despite the detentions, and leaders vowed they would neither back down nor be intimidated.
