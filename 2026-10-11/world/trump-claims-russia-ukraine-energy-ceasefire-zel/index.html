Trump claims Russia-Ukraine energy ceasefire; Zelenskyy disputes agreement as strikes continue

International & World desk · anchored on Reuters, AP
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts slightly center-left

US President Donald Trump announced Sunday that Ukraine and Russia have agreed to an "energy ceasefire," writing on social media: "Effective immediately, there is an ENERGY CEASEFIRE in the War between Russia and Ukraine. Both parties have agreed. Do not break it!" Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy, however, disputed the claim, stating earlier Sunday that Ukraine was "ready to stop striking Russian oil refineries if Russia stops striking our energy infrastructure," and adding that Russia's President Vladimir Putin "does not want to do so."

Trump also intensified his criticism of Zelenskyy, telling reporters at the White House on Saturday: "He better damn well stop" attacking Russian oil refineries, and adding, "I think it's time for Ukraine to get a new president." Trump blamed Zelenskyy for rising diesel prices, saying Russia supplies a large portion of global diesel. The comments followed Trump's decision to temporarily lift sanctions on Russian fuel to allow a diesel supply deal with Moscow, a move Zelenskyy warned would embolden Russia. "Today, Russia said thank you for the lifting of sanctions on diesel exports by dropping five glide bombs on residential buildings in Zaporizhzhia," Zelenskyy said Saturday.

Russian attacks on Ukraine continued through the weekend. The State Emergency Service of Ukraine said six people were injured in overnight attacks on four districts in the Dnipropetrovsk region. On Saturday, at least 20 people including four children were killed in Russian attacks on Zaporizhzhia, local officials confirmed. Among the dead was a six-year-old girl whose grandfather, Anatoliy Chumniy, was seen sobbing over her body after rescuers pulled her from rubble. Russia has also struck Ukrainian data centers, causing internet outages and prompting some firms to move servers abroad.

Zelenskyy, who has been pushing for a full ceasefire, told ABC on Sunday that he would organize elections if Putin agrees to a 60-day total ceasefire. "Please tell Putin, give us two months, 60 days of total ceasefire, 60 days, and we will organise elections," he said. In response to Trump's suggestion that Ukraine should replace him, Zelenskyy said "it's up to Ukrainians" to choose their leader and noted that Putin would also like to change who is leading Ukraine. The tense relationship between Trump and Zelenskyy extends back to Trump's first term, when he pressured Zelenskyy to investigate Joe Biden and his son Hunter Biden, and has included repeated public rebukes, including Trump calling Zelenskyy a "dictator without elections" in February 2025.
