Nvidia confirms $13 billion acquisition of AI platform Hugging Face

Financial & Markets desk

Nvidia (NVDA) on Thursday confirmed its plans to acquire the open-weight AI platform Hugging Face for roughly $13 billion, sending shares up more than 1% in early trading as stocks edged higher. The deal, valued at $12.93 billion per a press release, will give Nvidia access to a platform used by more than 18 million developers and 200,000 companies to share over 3 million models. MarketWatch described the acquisition as giving Nvidia access to 'one of the most important parcels of real estate in the AI market' and promoting open-source technology.

Nvidia emphasized that Hugging Face would remain 'an open platform for the entire AI ecosystem' and that it would continue to support open-weight and open-source AI models. 'We believe in having a healthy ecosystem of closed models and open models leads to a world where you're going to continue to train these models and run these models at scale,' said Justin Boitano, Nvidia's enterprise computing general manager, on a call. CEO Jensen Huang posted on X that 'open models strengthen safety and cybersecurity, accelerate innovation and diffusion, and enable sovereignty.' The deal is expected to close in the first half of 2027, pending regulatory approval.

The acquisition comes as tech stocks rose Thursday, with the S&P 500 up 11.5% year-to-date through Sept. 1, the Nasdaq Composite up 12.3%, the Dow up 9.8%, and the Russell 2000 up 17.7%. Shares of Snowflake (SNOW) soared following a blowout quarter, while Broadcom (AVGO) dropped despite strong results as its revenue forecast missed estimates. Long-dated bond yields slipped, with the 10-year Treasury falling to 4.73% ahead of Friday's jobs report.

BD8 CEO and Nvidia investor Barbara Doran called the deal a 'great strategic move,' arguing Nvidia is 'positioning themselves to be much more of an AI platform rather than just a supplier of chips.' 'Right now, chip … demand is huge, but they are positioning themselves for the day when that will slow down,' she said, adding that given Nvidia's expected free cash flow of near $200 billion for fiscal-year 2027, '$13 billion … is not a big bite.'

Separately, Morgan Stanley's head of U.S. public-policy research, Ariana Salvatore, said public opposition to data centers—driven by higher utility bills, environmental concerns, and quality-of-life disruptions—has become 'powerful and bipartisan.' She believes the capital-expenditure story is still intact: 'We have over a trillion dollars in CapEx from the hyperscalers next year.' However, she expects 'timing delays' and 'geographical dispersion' as hyperscalers redirect capacity toward areas with greater support.
