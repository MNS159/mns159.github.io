Snowflake stock surges 23% on strong earnings and AI product momentum

Financial & Markets desk

Snowflake shares jumped 23% in after-hours trading Wednesday after the data analytics software maker reported fiscal second-quarter earnings and revenue that handily beat Wall Street expectations and raised its full-year product revenue forecast, citing growing demand for its artificial intelligence coding products.

For the quarter ended July 31, Snowflake reported adjusted earnings per share of 62 cents, well above the 45 cents expected by analysts, and revenue of $1.55 billion, above the $1.48 billion consensus. Total revenue rose 35% year over year. On a GAAP basis, the company recorded a net loss of $191.7 million, or 55 cents per share, an improvement from a loss of $297.9 million, or 89 cents per share, in the same period last year.

Product revenue, the company's key metric, reached $1.49 billion in the quarter, up 37% year over year, marking the third consecutive quarter of product revenue growth acceleration. The company pointed to gains from CoCo, its AI coding agent that helps developers write and manage software automatically. CoCo ended the quarter with more than 9,100 accounts, an increase of over 2,000 during the quarter. The company also said its CoWork product grew to 5,800 accounts. Snowflake added 692 net new customers during the quarter, up 32% year over year, bringing its total to more than 14,500 customers globally. Remaining performance obligations — a measure of contracted future revenue — reached $9 billion, up 30% year over year.

For the fiscal third quarter, Snowflake said it expects product revenue of between $1.588 billion and $1.593 billion, representing 37% to 38% year-over-year growth, above the $1.50 billion consensus. Management raised its full-year product revenue forecast to $6.07 billion, representing 36% year-over-year growth, up from the $5.84 billion, or 31% growth, it had projected in May. The company also increased its full-year adjusted operating margin outlook to 14.5%, from a prior target of 13.5%.

CEO Sridhar Ramaswamy pointed to growing adoption of the company's AI products as a driver of the results. "The results demonstrate how the AI transformation that's sweeping enterprises is compounding Snowflake's advantage," Ramaswamy told MarketWatch. "More and more customers are migrating their data and data work onto Snowflake so that they have an AI-ready data foundation." CFO Brian Robins attributed the results to strength in the company's core data platform and a step-up in AI revenue.

As of Wednesday's close, Snowflake shares were up 39% year to date, while the S&P 500 index had gained about 12% in the same period. If the stock rises by a similar margin when regular trading resumes Thursday, it would mark one of the largest single-day gains since Snowflake went public in 2020. The strong guidance suggests demand for Snowflake's data and AI tools is accelerating even as the broader software sector faces scrutiny over the pace of AI-driven spending. Executives were scheduled to discuss the results with analysts on a conference call at 5 p.m. ET.
