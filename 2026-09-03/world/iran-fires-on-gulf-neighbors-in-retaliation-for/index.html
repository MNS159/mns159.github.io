Iran War

Iran strikes Gulf allies after U.S. bombing kills 4 at wedding party

International & World desk

Iran fired on U.S. allies in the Gulf early Wednesday, retaliating for American airstrikes that included a strike on a wedding party in the coastal town of Kuhestak, Iran. Iranian state media reported that four people—two women and two children, ages 4 and 16—were killed and at least 68 wounded in that attack. The deputy governor of the province initially said five died, but later revised the figure without explanation.

The U.S. Central Command said Tuesday’s strikes targeted Iranian military sites including air defense systems, radar, and maritime assets. Two weapons experts told the Associated Press that fragments from a missile used in the wedding-party strike matched a U.S.-made SLAM-ER cruise missile, described as a very precise, air-launched weapon that can be retargeted in flight. CENTCOM spokesman Capt. Tim Hawkins said the military was aware of the reports and was looking into them but did not confirm an investigation.

The latest violence ended a monthlong lull in fighting that began Feb. 28, when Israel and the United States attacked Iran. The war has driven oil prices up more than 30% to about $95 a barrel and sent Iran’s currency to a record low of 2.2 million rials per U.S. dollar. The standoff centers on the Strait of Hormuz, through which about a fifth of the world’s traded oil flowed before the conflict. Despite U.S. assertions of success in clearing mines and escorting vessels, only a handful of ships transit daily, and Iran has regularly attacked them.

Iran’s newly appointed Supreme National Security Council chief, Mohsen Rezaei, warned on social media: 'You will soon see that Iran’s new strategy on the battlefield, in diplomacy, and in confronting the economic blockade will shatter your foundations.' Separately, Iran reported that four members of its paramilitary Revolutionary Guard and 10 Basij volunteers were killed in U.S. attacks. Hezbollah, backed by Iran, fired two explosive drones at Israeli soldiers in southern Lebanon without causing casualties, and Kurdish authorities in Iraq said they intercepted 10 armed drones near Irbil.

In addition to the Jordan strikes earlier, Kuwait and Bahrain reported coming under Iranian attack with no casualties. Saudi shipping firm Bahri said two Filipino sailors were killed late Monday as their vessel transited the Strait. A ceasefire brokered in June collapsed within weeks, and mediator Pakistan has been trying to restart talks. U.S. President Donald Trump said late Tuesday he would not push Iran to negotiate, writing on social media: 'I couldn’t care less if they sign a worthless, to them, agreement. … When are the Iranian people going to rise up and fight?'

Analysts warn that neither side can sustain the current cycle of attack and retaliation without risking a full-scale war. Hamidreza Azizi of the Crisis Group said: 'At some point, one of the sides may see a need to escalate in order to break this cycle … which is a full-scale war.' Sascha Bruchmann of the International Institute for Strategic Studies noted that the U.S. aimed to restore confidence in global oil markets by projecting control of the Strait, and that Iran's latest attacks appeared intended to re-inject insecurity.
