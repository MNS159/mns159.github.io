Iran War

Iran strikes Gulf allies after U.S. bombing kills 4 at wedding party

International & World desk

Iran fired on U.S. allies in the Gulf early Wednesday, retaliating for American airstrikes that included a strike on a wedding party in the coastal town of Kuhestak, Iran. Iranian state media reported that four people—two women and two children, ages 4 and 16—were killed and at least 68 wounded in that attack. Climate

WMO warns of potentially record El Niño as toxic haze spreads across Southeast Asia

International & World desk

The World Meteorological Organization (WMO) has warned that the current El Niño could become the strongest since at least 1950, lasting until at least February 2027, as a blanket of toxic haze from weeks-long wildfires in Indonesia spreads across Malaysia, Singapore and the Philippines. UN Secretary-General António Guterres said the world has entered a 'danger zone of extreme weather' and that 'El Niño is being supersized before our eyes.' The WMO reported that sea surface temperatures in the Pacific's key monitoring region have been well over 2°C above average in recent weeks, with monthly averages potentially approaching 4°C above normal, and subsurface temperatures in some places more than 8°C above average.

Feminist icon Gloria Steinem dies at 92

International & World desk

Gloria Steinem, the author and activist who became one of the most visible symbols of the women’s movement in the United States and a fierce advocate for feminist causes throughout her life, has died at the age of 92. She died on Wednesday at her home in New York City, according to her foundation’s social media pages. The foundation said she 'passed away peacefully' and that her 'near-century on earth were years well-lived, and she continued working for equality until the very end.'

Venezuela/US Policy

US defends Venezuela oil deal amid criticism of 'gunpoint diplomacy'

International & World desk

The Trump administration is defending its sweeping oil agreement with Venezuela’s interim government, announced last week, as critics on both sides of the political spectrum denounce the deal as coercive and potentially damaging to democratic prospects. US energy secretary Chris Wright, who arrived in Caracas on Tuesday night, denied that Washington was seeking to commandeer Venezuela’s oil resources, telling CNBC: “Absolutely not.” Wright characterized the deal as “an absolutely historic transformation of both Venezuela and the relations between Venezuela and the US,” and said it represented “President Trump’s grand plan to replace conflict with commerce.” The White House has described the agreement as “the biggest oil deal in world history” that will give the US government “powerful governance rights” over about 65 billion barrels of Venezuela’s oil reserves, securing US “energy dominance for the next century” and “purging foreign malign influence from our back yard” by excluding China and Russia.
