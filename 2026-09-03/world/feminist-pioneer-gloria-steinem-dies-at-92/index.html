Feminist icon Gloria Steinem dies at 92

International & World desk

Gloria Steinem, the author and activist who became one of the most visible symbols of the women’s movement in the United States and a fierce advocate for feminist causes throughout her life, has died at the age of 92. She died on Wednesday at her home in New York City, according to her foundation’s social media pages. The foundation said she 'passed away peacefully' and that her 'near-century on earth were years well-lived, and she continued working for equality until the very end.'

Steinem, cofounder of Ms. Magazine, was known for her trademark streaked hair parted down the middle and large aviator glasses. Much of her life as an advocate was spent on the road, speaking at college campuses, community centers, rallies, and marches. In recent years, she enjoyed writing and spending time in the community, and hosted talking circles for hundreds of people in her home. 'You can’t do it on the phone or on the web,' she told The Associated Press in a 2015 interview. 'You have to be there with all five senses.'

Born on March 25, 1935, in Toledo, Ohio, Steinem did not attend school regularly until around seventh grade due to her father’s work as a traveling antique dealer. Her parents separated when she was 11, and she took care of her mother, who suffered from depression. 'I’d already been the very small parent of a very big child, my mother. I didn’t want to end up taking care of someone else,' she was quoted as saying. She began freelancing as a journalist after graduating from Smith College. In 1963, she went undercover as a Playboy bunny for Show magazine, producing the exposé 'A Bunny’s Tale' that shed light on poor pay and sexist treatment. She later helped found New York Magazine in 1968.

Steinem credited a 1969 abortion speak-out in a New York church with changing her career path. She had undergone a secret illegal abortion herself at age 22. 'That speak-out was the beginning of activism for me,' she told the AP in 2022. 'Either our control over our own bodies is equal, regardless of sex or race, or we’re not living in a democracy.' In 1971, she co-founded Ms. Magazine, the first mass-market feminist monthly, which tackled issues such as abortion, sexism, equal pay, and domestic violence. She also founded the National Women’s Political Caucus that year. Other groups she created or helped create include the Coalition of Labor Union Women, the Women’s Media Center, Voters for Choice, and the Ms. Foundation for Women.

Steinem believed the movement should represent women of every race. 'If you’ve suffered discrimination, you’re sensitive to it on every level. I learned feminism largely from Black women. Women of color basically invented feminism,' she told the New Yorker. In 2013, then-President Barack Obama awarded her the Presidential Medal of Freedom, the highest US civilian honor. Steinem never had children and married at age 66 to South African businessman and environmentalist David Bale, father of actor Christian Bale. He died of brain cancer in 2003. 'I hope this proves what feminists have always said — that feminism is about the ability to choose what’s right at each time of our lives,' she said in a statement about the marriage.

Her foundation’s social media posts said: 'Gloria’s greatest gift was her ability to listen to others, to make others feel seen and heard. Her words, actions, and example gave people permission to be their truest selves. Gloria lived true to her independent spirit, always with curiosity and a great sense of humor.'
