NBA hammers Clippers with five first-round pick penalty over Kawhi Leonard scandal

Sports desk

The NBA imposed the most severe penalty in league history on the Los Angeles Clippers on Wednesday, stripping the team of five first-round draft picks for violating the collective bargaining agreement's rules against cap circumvention. In addition, owner Steve Ballmer was suspended for one year and head of basketball operations Lawrence Frank was suspended for six months. The National Basketball Players Association agreed to the punishment, leaving the Clippers without a straightforward path to arbitration or an appeals process, though the team is reportedly considering a legal challenge.

The league took four of the Clippers' own first-round picks (2030 through 2033) and the 2029 first-round pick the team acquired from the Indiana Pacers in the Ivica Zubac trade. That leaves Los Angeles with four first-round picks, assuming the previously agreed-upon trade of Kawhi Leonard to the Toronto Raptors is completed: a 2027 swap right (likely to become Toronto's pick), a top-three-protected 2029 swap with the Philadelphia 76ers, and unprotected Toronto picks in 2031 and 2033. Because of the Stepien rule, which requires teams to control a first-round pick every other year, the Clippers cannot trade any of those remaining picks. The earliest they could trade a future first-rounder is the 2035 pick, which would become available in two years.

The penalty stems from an investigation into the Clippers' handling of the Kawhi Leonard acquisition and subsequent contract. The NBA determined the team circumvented the salary cap, though specific details of the violation were not released. The NBPA's agreement to the penalty effectively blocks the Clippers from challenging it through the league's grievance process. The most comparable punishment came in 2000, when the Minnesota Timberwolves lost five first-round picks for cap circumvention related to the Joe Smith signing. However, the Timberwolves eventually had two of those picks returned after admitting fault, a step the Clippers have not taken.

The Clippers had already begun a roster teardown before the penalty was announced, trading James Harden for Darius Garland and extracting two first-round picks for Zubac, as well as two picks and a swap for Leonard. As a result, the team still has a foundation of Garland, a two-time All-Star, and 2025 No. 5 overall pick Keaton Wagler. The franchise also retains significant cap flexibility—roughly $50 million in projected space for 2027—and no guaranteed contracts beyond the 2028-29 season. The Los Angeles market remains a draw for free agents, but the league's modern free-agency landscape, in which stars rarely change teams via open market, complicates any quick rebuild.

CBS Sports' analysis notes that the most successful template for overcoming such an asset deficit is the Brooklyn Nets' turnaround after trading away multiple first-round picks in 2013. The Nets rebuilt through smart low-cost additions, late-first-round finds, and preserving cap space to attract stars in 2019. The Clippers have stronger starting pieces than the Nets did, but the Western Conference is deeper, and the era of superstar free-agent moves has largely ended. The path to contention likely requires outlier moves—finding a Jalen Brunson-type undervalued star, using cap space to absorb bad contracts for picks, or taking high-risk fliers on players other teams are reluctant to keep. The Indiana Pacers' 2025 Finals run, built without a top-five pick, offers a more recent example of what is possible, but it demands precise management and significant luck.
