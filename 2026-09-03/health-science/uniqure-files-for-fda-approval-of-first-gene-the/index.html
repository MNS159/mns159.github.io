UniQure files for FDA approval of first gene therapy for Huntington's disease

Health & Science desk · anchored on Reuters

UniQure has submitted a biologics license application (BLA) to the U.S. Food and Drug Administration for AMT-130, its gene therapy for Huntington’s disease, and has also applied for approval in the United Kingdom. The company is requesting FDA priority review, which, if granted, would set a six-month review cycle and could lead to an accelerated approval decision in late April or early May 2027. If approved, AMT-130 would be the first genetic treatment for Huntington’s disease.

The application is supported by three-year data from a Phase 1/2 trial that compared AMT-130 to a propensity score-matched external control from the Enroll-HD natural history database. In September 2025, uniQure announced the data showed a 75% slowing of the neurodegenerative disease. “We are grateful to the FDA for its leadership in advancing regulatory science to meet the urgency of this disease, and to the MHRA for its commitment to advancing rare disease treatments in the U.K.,” uniQure CEO Matt Kapusta said in a statement on Wednesday.

The filing follows months of regulatory reversals. In November 2025, the FDA told uniQure it “no longer agrees” that the Phase 1/2 data were adequate to support a BLA, reversing a prior agreement on the trial’s protocols and statistical analyses. In February 2026, former FDA Commissioner Marty Makary appeared to disparage the therapy in a CNBC interview, describing a product in which researchers drilled a burr hole in patients’ skulls and found no benefit at the end of the randomization period—widely believed to refer to AMT-130. Four months later, both Makary and former Center for Biologics Evaluation and Research director Vinay Prasad had left the agency, and the FDA again reversed course, agreeing that the three-year data were sufficient to support an accelerated approval application.

Analysts see continued regulatory risk but also positive developments. William Blair, in a Wednesday note resuming coverage, said the Phase 1/2 trial design “aligns with the FDA’s plausible mechanism pathway publication and draft guidance on gene therapies for small populations, which specifically stipulates that external controls may be an appropriate option in certain contexts.” The firm also noted that uniQure has reached alignment with the FDA on key elements of a confirmatory trial. Stifel analysts added that the company is “confident” the confirmatory study—expected to include about 200 patients with 2:1 randomization between AMT-130 and standard of care—“will be well underway before the FDA gives their decision on accelerated approval, which is in-line with the agency’s expectations.”

William Blair predicted a “measured” launch for AMT-130 “given the requirement for surgically guided administration” and estimated peak global sales of $3.2 billion in 2035. “Given the impressive results demonstrated with the therapy, we believe AMT-130 has the potential to be a first- and best-in-class treatment for HD,” the analyst said. The company has not yet disclosed further details on the confirmatory trial.
