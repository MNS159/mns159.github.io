Trump's CDC director ordered delay in counting Pennsylvania measles deaths

Health & Science desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

In a highly unusual move, the U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention has declined to count two measles-associated deaths reported by Pennsylvania in its national tally, acting on an order from new CDC Director Dr. Erica Schwartz, according to two people with knowledge of the decision. The CDC delayed its weekly Friday measles update and, when it posted over the weekend, listed the deaths as an asterisk with a footnote stating that it does not have enough information to determine whether measles caused or contributed to the fatalities. Pennsylvania health officials have said they thoroughly investigated both deaths before announcing them and provided all required epidemiological data.

The decision escalates a feud between U.S. Department of Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. and Pennsylvania Governor Josh Shapiro, as the state confronts a growing measles outbreak. Kennedy last week suggested the deaths might be 'altogether fabricated,' pointing to the Lancaster County coroner's initial statements. The coroner, Dr. Stephen Diamantoni, has since said his office is investigating the death of an infant who died from a ruptured spleen but had evidence of measles virus in lung tissue, suggesting infection before birth. The Atlantic reported an interview with an Amish couple whose infant son died shortly after birth; the mother, who had severe measles while pregnant, assumed the baby died because of the infection, as her midwife told her.

Public health experts with deep knowledge of CDC protocols called Schwartz's move unprecedented and a dangerous politicization of data. Dr. Nirav Shah, former CDC principal deputy director, said, 'The idea that you would second-guess the determination of the state epidemiologist is pretty remarkable.' He noted that for decades the CDC has counted deaths as 'associated' with an infection regardless of whether the person died 'from' or 'with' it. Dr. Deb Houry, former CDC chief medical officer, pointed out that a measles-associated death in New Mexico last year was added to the national tally while still under investigation. 'This is a big deviation in protocol,' she said.

Dr. Jeremy Faust of Harvard Medical School said Schwartz had failed her first major test as CDC director, asking whether she will 'simply be a communications attache to Secretary Kennedy' or 'speak for science, even when it goes against what my bosses think.' In her first agency-wide meeting, Schwartz told staff she would not hesitate to disagree with Kennedy if necessary but would not do so publicly. The HHS press secretary defended the delay as reflecting 'the best available information and our commitment to transparency,' adding that 'telling the American people the truth' is a foundational principle of the Trump administration.

The controversy echoes disputes during the COVID-19 pandemic over 'died from versus died with' distinctions, which critics say were used to confuse rather than clarify. Dr. Brian Castrucci of the de Beaumont Foundation said casting doubt before a complete review 'risks turning routine scientific verification into a manufactured controversy.' Dr. David Margolius, Cleveland's public health director, described the shift from sharing information to becoming a 'data editorializer' as frustrating, saying the focus should be on protecting neighbors rather than political debate. The CDC has not said when it might update the national count.
