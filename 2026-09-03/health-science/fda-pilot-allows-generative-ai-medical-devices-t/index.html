FDA pilot allows generative AI medical devices to reach patients before authorization

Health & Science desk

The Food and Drug Administration is provisionally allowing some developers of generative AI medical devices to launch their products on the market without marketing authorization, as part of a pilot program designed to test new regulatory approaches. The agency recently accepted four devices into the TEMPO pilot, including AI products from Cadence and Limbic.

The TEMPO pilot is intended to increase the number of technologies available under the Medicare ACCESS model, an experiment that pays for technology to help Medicare beneficiaries manage chronic conditions. By allowing devices to reach patients before formal authorization, the program gives both the FDA and participating companies a chance to experiment with generative AI regulation in real-world settings.

The move comes as the FDA continues to wrestle with how to regulate medical devices that rely on generative artificial intelligence to make decisions about patient care. The pilot offers a provisional pathway to market while the agency develops more permanent oversight frameworks.

The four devices accepted into the program represent an early test of this approach. The pilot's outcomes could shape how the FDA handles the growing number of AI-driven medical devices seeking to enter the U.S. market.
