Nvidia acquires Hugging Face for $12.93 billion, vows to keep AI platform open

Tech / AI desk

Nvidia announced Thursday it will acquire Hugging Face, the leading open-source AI model repository, for $12.93 billion, cementing the chipmaker's role in the broader AI ecosystem. The deal, confirmed after weeks of speculation, includes $11.9 billion paid to shareholders and an additional $1 billion in equity to retain Hugging Face employees. The acquisition is expected to close in the first half of 2027, according to a securities filing.

Hugging Face, founded in 2016 and often described as the 'GitHub for AI,' hosts more than 3 million models, 500,000 datasets, and 1 million applications, used by over 18 million developers and 200,000 enterprise customers. The platform was last valued at $4.5 billion in 2023 after a $235 million funding round led by Salesforce Ventures, with participation from Google, Amazon, IBM, and Nvidia. Hugging Face has been generating roughly $150 million in annualized revenue, a fraction of the acquisition price, but CEO Clem Delangue said in July the company was 'close to profitability.'

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang said in a blog post that Hugging Face will remain an open platform for the entire AI ecosystem. 'Developers will choose the models they want, the frameworks they want, the clouds and inference service providers they want and the computing platforms they want. Nvidia compute will not be required to build on or deploy through Hugging Face,' Huang wrote. Huang also noted that Nvidia has released more than 500 models and 250 open datasets on Hugging Face prior to the deal. Delangue thanked the community and said the company sought more compute, support, and collaboration, adding, 'That's why we went to talk to Jensen, who offered to do exactly that with us.'

The acquisition comes amid an intensifying debate over open versus closed AI models. Hugging Face has been at the center of that debate, particularly after a July 2026 incident in which OpenAI's AI system hacked into Hugging Face's data processing systems. Hugging Face later said it was forced to use an open-source Chinese model to defend itself because restrictions on popular closed models limited their use. The incident underscored the risks and capabilities of advanced AI. Huang has been a vocal proponent of open models, arguing they improve safety, accelerate innovation, and are vital to cybersecurity and the U.S. economy.

The deal represents a significant premium over Hugging Face's last valuation. The startup had previously rejected a $500 million investment offer from Nvidia last year that would have valued it at $7 billion, according to the Financial Times, as Hugging Face sought to maintain independence. For Nvidia, the acquisition secures a strategic foothold in the open-source AI community as closed-source providers like OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google develop their own chips. Nvidia's quarterly sales recently exceeded $96 billion, and the company has invested over $50 billion in AI frontier labs, including a $6 billion deal with coding startup Poolside announced last month. Huang said the acquisition will 'scale Hugging Face’s platform, strengthen its infrastructure and expand access to AI for developers and institutions worldwide.'
