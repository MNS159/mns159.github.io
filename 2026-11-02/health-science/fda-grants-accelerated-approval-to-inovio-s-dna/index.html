FDA grants accelerated approval to Inovio's DNA-based immunotherapy Voceryq for rare HPV airway disease

Health & Science desk

The U.S. Food and Drug Administration on Oct. 30 granted accelerated approval to Voceryq (formerly INO-3107), Inovio Pharmaceuticals' first commercial product and the first FDA-approved plasmid DNA-based immunotherapy. The treatment is indicated for adults with recurrent respiratory papillomatosis (RRP), a rare disease in which infection with human papillomavirus types 6 and 11 causes benign papillomas to grow in the airways, throat and larynx, often requiring repeated surgeries to maintain breathing, speaking and swallowing. Inovio expects initial shipments in about 60 days and full commercial deployment in the first quarter of 2027.

Voceryq consists of two DNA plasmids: one encoding the E6 and E7 proteins of HPV-6 and HPV-11, and another encoding human interleukin-12 to enhance the immune response. The therapy is administered via Inovio's proprietary CELLECTRA device, which injects the treatment into muscle tissue and applies short electrical pulses to facilitate cellular entry. The drug can be stored under standard refrigeration and has a three-year shelf life. Inovio Chief Medical Officer Michael Sumner said the straightforward distribution requirements should allow administration in a doctor's office.

The accelerated approval was based on a multicenter clinical trial involving 32 adults who had undergone at least two surgeries in the year before treatment. Among 26 patients who had at least three surgeries in that period, 27% required no additional surgery in the 12 months after treatment. Inovio President and CEO Jacqueline Shea said the FDA recognized the therapeutic benefit of Voceryq in reducing surgery frequency. The approval followed an initial regulatory hurdle: when Inovio submitted its application in March, the FDA indicated the company had not provided adequate information for the accelerated pathway, reflecting a broader tightening of rare-disease approvals under then-Commissioner Marty Makary.

In a statement, Karim Mikhail, director of the FDA's Center for Biologics Evaluation and Research, called Voceryq "a meaningful scientific breakthrough" that "could mean fewer surgeries, fewer disruptions, and more time living their lives on their own terms." He noted that adults with RRP often face an exhausting cycle of repeated surgeries, and that the approval represents a plasmid DNA-based immunotherapy that harnesses the immune system to fight the underlying cause of the disease. Before Voceryq, patients had limited options beyond surgery, which carries risks of permanent vocal cord or airway damage, Sumner explained during an investor call.

Under the accelerated approval pathway, Inovio is required to conduct a confirmatory study. Sumner said the company has agreed with the FDA that the study will enroll at least 50 adult and pediatric patients with RRP who required three or more surgical interventions in the 12 months prior to treatment. The study will evaluate the number of surgical interventions needed over at least three years following treatment, comparing results to an external control group or a scientifically justified threshold. A draft protocol is expected for FDA review before the end of 2026, with final alignment by mid-2027. Inovio may also explore continued treatment for patients without a sustained complete response.

Inovio priced Voceryq at approximately $115,000 per dose, or $460,000 for the four-dose regimen. The company said current funding supports operations through early 2027 but that additional capital will be needed to fully fund the launch. Based on claims data, Inovio estimates at least 25,000 people in the U.S. are living with RRP, and Shea said the vast majority of the market remains available to Voceryq as patients seek alternatives to surgery. The Recurrent Respiratory Papillomatosis Foundation recently published a position statement supporting early initiation of immunotherapy, and Shea said the key opinion leader community is extremely supportive of starting treatment as soon as possible after diagnosis.
