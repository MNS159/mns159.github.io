Judge dismisses case against ex-FBI officials in Hunter Biden probe, citing DOJ 'weaponization'

Politics desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum
✓ Facts
  • U.S. Magistrate Judge Zia Faruqui dismissed the criminal complaint against former FBI officials Nikki Floris and Bradley Benavides on Monday, ruling the government failed to establish probable cause.
  • Floris and Benavides had been charged about two weeks earlier with lying to Congress in 2023 about their knowledge of the FBI's investigations into Hunter Biden's laptop.
  • The investigation into Floris and Benavides was opened on FBI Director Kash Patel's orders the day after President Trump's mid-July primetime address repeating claims of 2020 election fraud.
  • The case was brought as a criminal complaint rather than through a grand jury indictment; DOJ grand juries in Washington, D.C. have recently declined to indict in other politically charged cases, including the attempted indictment of Cassidy Hutchinson.
  • Faruqui noted the charges were filed roughly two weeks before the midterm elections despite a DOJ policy against timing actions to influence elections.
  • At a preliminary hearing, an FBI agent testified he feared the probe would be seen as 'political,' and that prosecutor Carlton Davis, a top aide to U.S. Attorney Jeanine Pirro, had acknowledged by phone that the complaint was flawed.
  • Faruqui previously struck an amended complaint from the docket after prosecutors tried to add an obstruction charge without judicial approval.
  • A DOJ spokesperson said the department disagrees with the ruling, is 'evaluating all options,' and said its actions are guided by 'facts, the evidence, and the law — not politics.'
○ Analysis

The case centers on two former FBI officials accused of misleading Congress about their roles in the bureau's past investigations into Hunter Biden, including the handling of his laptop — a matter that has been a long-running flashpoint between the FBI and congressional Republicans since the 2020 election. The prosecution was launched under the Trump administration's DOJ and FBI leadership years after the alleged false statements were made, and a federal magistrate judge concluded the process was procedurally irregular, rushed, and timed near a major election, leading him to dismiss it outright rather than let it proceed to a grand jury. The ruling fits into a broader pattern cited in the reporting: several politically sensitive prosecutions pursued via criminal complaint, rather than grand jury indictment, have recently stalled or been rejected in Washington's federal courts. Because the judge's decision does not permanently bar prosecution — DOJ could seek a grand jury indictment or appeal to the district's chief judge — the case's ultimate resolution remains open, even as Monday's ruling delivered a sharp, on-the-record rebuke of how the charges were brought.

Steelman

Both the judge's findings and the underlying dispute point to the same unresolved tension: the DOJ says officials who lie to Congress must be held accountable, while the dismissal highlights procedural shortcuts, timing, and an unusual charging method that raise doubts about whether due process — not political calendars — drove the case.

Implications

The Justice Department can still attempt to revive the case by seeking a grand jury indictment or appealing Faruqui's ruling to the district's chief judge, though his finding that prosecutors fell short of even the low probable-cause standard makes that path harder. The episode is likely to intensify scrutiny of how the DOJ times and structures politically sensitive prosecutions, and may factor into broader debates over FBI and DOJ independence as additional Trump-era cases move through Washington's courts.
