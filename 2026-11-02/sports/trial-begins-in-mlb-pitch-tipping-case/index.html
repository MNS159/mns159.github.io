Trial Opens for Guardians Pitchers Accused of Tipping Pitches to Gamblers

Sports desk

Former Cleveland Guardians pitchers Emmanuel Clase and Luis Ortiz went on trial Monday in Brooklyn federal court on charges of accepting bribes to help gamblers win hundreds of thousands of dollars by betting on individual pitches in Major League Baseball. The jury trial, expected to last about a month before Judge Kiyo A. Matsumoto, began with jury selection; the judge said she hoped to complete selection quickly enough for opening statements later Monday.

According to an indictment, Clase, 28, began providing gamblers with information about his pitches in 2023 and recruited Ortiz, 27, into the scheme in 2025. The gamblers, both from the Dominican Republic, paid the pitchers thousands of dollars and used the inside information to win at least $460,000 on in-game prop bets on the speed and outcome of specific pitches. Clase and Ortiz have pleaded not guilty to charges including wire fraud conspiracy, honest services wire fraud conspiracy, money laundering conspiracy, and conspiracy to influence sporting contests by bribery.

Clase, a three-time All-Star and two-time American League Reliever of the Year, was in the fourth season of a five-year, $20 million contract when the charges were revealed in November 2025. Ortiz had earned $782,600 as a starting pitcher for the Guardians in the 2025 season. Both are on non-disciplinary leave from the club without pay while their case proceeds. Ortiz’s lawyers had unsuccessfully sought to have him tried separately from Clase.

Defense lawyers are expected to argue that the pitchers did not collude with gamblers and were instead varying pitch placement strategically to fool batters. On Friday, Judge Matsumoto ruled that prosecutors cannot show the jury evidence that Clase owed $500,000 in U.S. taxes, because the government provided the evidence to defense lawyers only days earlier, well after a deadline for turning over evidence. Prosecutors had wanted to use the tax debt to counter any defense claim that Clase had no financial incentive to collude, given millions of dollars in accounts in the Dominican Republic.

Several professional baseball players, including catchers and baseball executives, are among expected witnesses. MLB and the Guardians have said they fully cooperated with authorities. MLB said it contacted federal law enforcement after beginning to investigate unusual betting activity.
