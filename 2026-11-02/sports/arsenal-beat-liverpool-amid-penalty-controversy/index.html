Arsenal beat Liverpool amid penalty controversy

Sports desk

Arsenal moved three points clear at the top of the Premier League table with a rare 1-0 victory at Liverpool on Sunday, their first win at Anfield since 2012. The match was overshadowed by a controversial moment in the second half when Liverpool striker Alexander Isak appeared to be denied a penalty after Arsenal defender Jurrien Timber pulled his shirt as a cross came into the area. Replays showed Timber with a handful of Isak's shirt, seemingly preventing the Sweden international from heading the ball, but referee Andy Madley did not see an offence and video assistant referee (VAR) Stuart Attwell decided there was not enough for a review.

Liverpool manager Andoni Iraola was left frustrated by the decision. "There is a clear grab of the shirt," he said after the defeat. "The answer is you have to dive, if you don't dive they won't call it." Iraola's comments reflect a growing debate about whether players must exaggerate contact to win penalties. In a similar incident in February, Fulham's Calvin Bassey went to ground after a shirt pull by Sunderland's Brian Brobbey, and VAR Tony Harrington sent referee Craig Pawson to the monitor to award a penalty. "If he goes to the floor, they call the penalty 100%," Iraola added. "It shouldn't be like this but it is what it is."

The incident has reignited discussion about the threshold for VAR intervention in the Premier League. Shirt pulling is not an automatic foul; the determination is always about how it affects the opponent. Former Premier League assistant referee Darren Cann noted that Isak did not even appeal, saying, "It has to be sustained holding - some will say it was sustained, some will say it wasn't. But it's interesting that Isak didn't even appeal." Danny Murphy, speaking on Match of the Day, said, "Arsenal got lucky. If Isak maybe makes a bit more of it. I hate to see players exaggerate, but he's going to score. It's a big moment in the game."

The Premier League has seen a high bar for VAR intervention this season, with only one missed intervention according to the Key Match Incidents Panel. The number of subjective reviews (14) after nine rounds is exactly the same as last season. Uefa referees' chief Roberto Rosetti said in the summer that there were "too many reviews," leading to attempts to play for or earn video decisions. There is now a greater focus on players who dive or embellish contact to 'win' penalties. Iraola clearly does not share the sentiment that the balance is right, but the video referees argue they are getting it right with only one missed intervention so far this season.
