Russian Bombing Kills 24 at Ukrainian Aid Centre; Kyiv Says Attack Shows No Desire for De-escalation

International & World desk

At least 24 people were killed and five injured after Russian aerial bombs struck an aid distribution centre in the village of Komyshuvakha in Ukraine's southern Zaporizhzhia region, Ukrainian officials said on Monday. The attack involved at least nine 3,000kg bombs that hit a local school being used for humanitarian needs, according to regional head Mykhailo Semikin. President Volodymyr Zelensky said that after the first strike, a second attack targeted the place where people were sheltering. Among the dead were two rescuers and four police officers; 27 people were initially reported missing.

Zelensky described the attack as "utterly horrific" and said the Russians could not have been unaware that the site was used for humanitarian aid.

Semikin described the attack as a "terrible tragedy." A day of mourning was declared for Tuesday across the entire Zaporizhzhia region. The Russian military has not publicly commented on the strike.

In the Black Sea port city of Odesa, a warehouse was targeted and homes were damaged.
