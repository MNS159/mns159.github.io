Trump administration asks Supreme Court to block 20,000 California midterm ballots over noncitizen claims

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

The administration of President Donald Trump has asked the Supreme Court to stop California from counting mail-in ballots from more than 20,000 voters it claims are not US citizens. The emergency request, filed on Monday, one day before the midterm elections, is the latest development in a legal battle between the administration and the Democratic-leaning state. The Justice Department, run by Trump's former personal attorney Todd Blanche, asked for a "limited injunction" directing California to "stop commingling suspected illegal ballots with legal ones" pending verification of voters' citizenship status.

The voter list was compiled by the Department of Homeland Security. California says the list is unreliable and could include thousands of US citizens. An analysis by an immigrant rights group and a voting rights watchdog based at the University of California Los Angeles found that thousands of US citizens appear to be inexplicably flagged as ineligible. US District Judge David Carter, who rejected the administration's initial lawsuit in October, noted that people flagged as noncitizens on voter rolls in other states have repeatedly turned out to be naturalised US citizens. "If the executive can discount votes and threaten an ongoing election due to unproven allegations, then our democracy is in danger," Carter wrote.

Voting has been under way in California since early October, and state officials say it would be nearly impossible to separate the ballots in question. A federal judge last week rejected the administration's request, raising concerns about the data's reliability. A federal appeals court also rejected the request on Friday. It is unclear if the Supreme Court will respond in time for a ruling to have an effect on Tuesday's election.

Trump has repeatedly made false claims about widespread voter fraud, but studies and state audits have shown that ineligible voting, including by noncitizens, is rare. For the current midterm election cycle, Trump has focused on claiming non-US citizens are trying to vote, despite these claims being repeatedly debunked by research and investigation. California Governor Gavin Newsom, an outspoken Trump critic, has warned the administration against interfering in the state's voting process and announced the deployment of election law experts, poll observers and a help line for voters to report intimidation or interference.
