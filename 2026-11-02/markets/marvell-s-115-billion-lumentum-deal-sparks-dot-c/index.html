Marvell’s $115 Billion All-Stock Lumentum Deal Triggers 10% Stock Plunge and Dot-Com-Era Concerns

Financial & Markets desk

Shares of communications semiconductor giant Marvell Technology (NASDAQ:MRVL) plunged 10% as of 12:54 p.m. EDT after the company announced it had agreed to purchase optical laser leader Lumentum (NASDAQ:LITE) in an all-stock deal valued at roughly $115 billion, or a 24% premium.

The deal is financed entirely through stock. While the 24% premium is not described as egregious, Lumentum’s stock had already rocketed higher over the past two years, and Marvell’s purchase price represents more than five times Lumentum’s share price just one year ago. That history has fueled investor fears that Marvell may be paying a steep price for the asset.

Marvell likely coveted Lumentum for the same reason the target’s stock has rallied over the past year: its proprietary optical laser technology is increasingly sought and required by next-generation artificial intelligence systems. AI not only requires many more optical interconnects between data centers, but as speeds increase, server racks will also begin replacing traditional copper interconnects with near-packaged and co-packaged optics inside racks, and eventually even inside servers. Marvell already produces a co-packaged optics solution, but management appears to see more opportunity in combining Marvell’s suite of digital signal processors and XPU-attach connectivity with Lumentum’s indium phosphide laser technology to yield highly differentiated products for next-gen AI systems.

The market is not in the mood for an announcement like this, as investors have recently been dumping AI-related stocks amid a host of potential fears ahead of the mid-term elections. However, AI-related stocks are volatile and have seen severe sell-offs from time to time, yet as long as the long-term AI story remains intact, these stocks have recovered. As of now, while there is a lot of fear and concern, recent reports from major tech companies still show booming AI-related revenues, with guidance for strong growth in 2027 and 2028. As such, both Marvell and Lumentum may be worth a look during this severe sell-off.
