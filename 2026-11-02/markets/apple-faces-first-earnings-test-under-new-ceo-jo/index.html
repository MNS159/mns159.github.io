Apple faces first earnings test under new CEO John Ternus as memory costs surge

Financial & Markets desk · anchored on Reuters

Apple reports fiscal fourth-quarter earnings after the market close on Monday, the first earnings call with John Ternus as chief executive since he took over from Tim Cook on Sept. 1. Analysts polled by LSEG expect earnings per share of $1.99 and revenue of $113.39 billion, while Bloomberg consensus estimates put EPS at $2 and revenue at $113.46 billion. Revenue is expected to rise about 11% from $102.46 billion a year earlier, when Apple reported EPS of $1.85. Apple shares fell 0.4% in premarket trading Monday.

Wall Street is focused on iPhone revenue, which is projected at $56.48 billion to $56.59 billion, with Yahoo Finance citing a 15% year-over-year increase. The quarter includes eight days of sales of the new iPhone 18 Pro, but the iPhone Duo did not reach store shelves until after the quarter ended: it went up for preorder Oct. 16 and on sale Oct. 23, though Apple could still offer color on demand. Apple also did not announce a new entry-level iPhone 18, and it raised iPhone prices by $100, which may have hurt demand in the quarter. Services, Apple's second-largest segment, is expected to top $31.58 billion; Mac sales are projected at $9.69 billion, wearables at $8.99 billion and iPad at $6.83 billion.

The 5 p.m. ET call will be the first with Ternus at the helm, and investors want to hear about Apple's plans in China after its AI software got approved, the slew of recent product launches, and how the company is grappling with global shortages of memory and flash storage. Reuters framed the report as Ternus's first earnings test 'as memory costs surge,' while Bloomberg said Apple's '$1 Trillion Rally Leaves No Room for Error on Earnings.'

Apple has been a relative safe haven among tech stocks this year. Its shares are up roughly 30% over the past 12 months, while Amazon, Google and Nvidia are up single digits, Microsoft is flat and Meta is down about 15%. Over the past month, Google, Meta and Nvidia declined by double digits, with Google and Meta hit after their earnings and Nvidia partly hurt by fears about the AI trade; Amazon is off slightly, leaving just Apple and Microsoft in the green. Apple has been largely insulated from the AI market's highs and lows because it does not spend billions on data centers and does not rely on chip sales for revenue. Earlier this month, its market capitalization eclipsed $5 trillion for the second time; Nvidia is the only other company to reach that milestone.

Apple is also riding a wave of new product launches. Beyond the iPhone 18 Pros and iPhone Duo, it has debuted new AirPods, Apple Watches, Macs and iPads. Earlier this week it released an upgraded iPad mini, an improved iMac and more powerful MacBook Pros, including one with a touchscreen display, and earlier this month it debuted its first smart home hub, the Apple HomeView. Its next major releases are expected early next year and will include a base iPhone 18 and a follow-up to the iPhone Air.
