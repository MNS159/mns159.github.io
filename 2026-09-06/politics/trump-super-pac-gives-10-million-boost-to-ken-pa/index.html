Trump super-PAC gives $10 million boost to Ken Paxton in Texas Senate race

Politics desk
✓ Facts
  • Federal Election Commission filings published Saturday show Trump's super-PAC MAGA Inc. gave $10 million to political media company Del Ray Media to fund the ad buy.
  • The money funds two attack ads targeting Democratic Senate candidate James Talarico, criticizing his tax positions and touting Paxton's tax-and-housing policy proposals; ads will run in digital and TV formats statewide.
  • The race pits Republican Texas Attorney General Ken Paxton against Democratic state Rep. James Talarico for a Texas U.S. Senate seat in the 2026 midterms.
  • Talarico's campaign has raised $72 million since it began, mostly from small donors, compared with $16.8 million raised by Paxton's campaign, according to the Texas Tribune.
  • Trump endorsed Paxton shortly before the late-May Republican primary runoff, which Paxton won over incumbent Sen. John Cornyn.
  • This $10 million ad buy is described as MAGA Inc.'s first major expenditure of the 2026 general election cycle; the group has also held a Michigan rally for Senate candidate Mike Rogers, spent over $800,000 supporting Sen. Lindsey Graham in a primary runoff, and spent nearly $600,000 opposing Rep. Thomas Massie's renomination.
  • Trump told reporters MAGA Inc. has raised close to $1 billion and has $400-500 million in current funds, which he referred to as "my money."
  • Senate Majority Leader John Thune said in a Fox News interview that Trump's team needed to commit resources to the Texas race to help preserve the Republican Senate majority.
○ Analysis

This story documents a specific financial transaction — a $10 million super-PAC ad expenditure — within the broader context of one of roughly a dozen Senate races expected to determine which party controls the chamber after the 2026 midterms. MAGA Inc., though legally independent of the Trump campcampaign and White House, is run by former Trump aides and advisers over whom Trump is reported to have influence; its spending in Texas follows public lobbying from Senate GOP leadership and comes despite Talarico's large financial advantage in direct campaign fundraising. The filing and the ad buy occurred against the backdrop of a Republican midterm convention beginning in Dallas, adding a secondary point of contrast between the two campaigns' messaging strategies.

◐ Debate

Democratic

  • Talarico's campaign has characterized MAGA Inc. as a "billionaire-backed dark money" group and accused it of lying about Talarico's record, framing its own campaign as a small-donor, "people-powered movement."
  • Talarico has publicly criticized the concurrent Republican midterm convention as a gathering of "billionaire mega-donors and their puppet politicians," tying the $10 million ad buy to broader complaints about wealthy influence over politics, including tariffs and tax policy he says are raising costs for ordinary people.

Republican

  • MAGA Inc. spokesperson Alex Pfeiffer said Talarico, dubbed "High Tax Talarico" by the PAC, holds "radical policies" that would "take money out of Texans' pockets," and said the ad spending is meant to inform voters of this before the election.
  • Senate Majority Leader John Thune argued that Republican leadership needs "all hands on deck" and that Trump's resources must be deployed in Texas specifically because losing the seat could cost the GOP its Senate majority.
  • Paxton publicly thanked Trump for the ad support, crediting his own "Protecting the Texas Promise Agenda" — including a doubled child tax credit, medical expense tax breaks, and a homebuyer tax deduction — as a contrast to what he calls Talarico's tax-raising record.
Steelman

Both campaigns agree that money is playing an outsized role in this race — Talarico leads dramatically in direct campaign fundraising while Paxton is now being bolstered by outside super-PAC spending, meaning the ultimate financial playing field may end up far more even than either campaign's own fundraising totals suggest.

Implications

With MAGA Inc. reportedly holding several hundred million dollars in reserve, this $10 million buy may be an opening move rather than the extent of its involvement in Texas; further spending from Republican-aligned or Democratic-aligned outside groups could follow as polls continue to show a close race, and the outcome could meaningfully affect which party holds the Senate majority after 2026.
