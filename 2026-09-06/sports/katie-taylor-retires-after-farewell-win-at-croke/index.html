Katie Taylor retires after farewell win at Croke Park

Sports desk

Katie Taylor, the undisputed light-welterweight champion, retired from boxing after a unanimous decision victory over Flora Pili at a sold-out Croke Park in Dublin on Saturday. The 40-year-old Irishwoman finished her professional career with 26 wins from 27 fights, three undisputed championship reigns across two divisions, and an Olympic gold medal. Taylor called the night 'the highlight of my career' and said her retirement was 'a dream ending to a dream career.'

Fighting before a crowd of 83,000 at Croke Park, less than three miles from where she had her first official bout 25 years ago, Taylor was sharper throughout, repeatedly beating Pili to the punch and closing the fight with rapid combinations as the crowd rose to its feet. 'It was everything I expected and more,' Taylor said at her final post-fight news conference. 'Thankfully I was able to hold my emotions together tonight as I was getting emotional all week.'

Taylor's journey in boxing began in 2001, when at age 15 she competed in the first sanctioned women's bout in Ireland since the sport was outlawed for women in 1991. She wrote to role model Deirdre Gogarty at age 10 expressing frustration over the lack of opportunities and dreaming of Olympic inclusion. In 2007, her talent helped convince International Olympic Committee members to add women's boxing to the programme for the London 2012 Games, where she won lightweight gold in a victory over Sofya Ochigava that became a moment of national sporting joy.

Key figures in boxing paid tribute to Taylor's career. Lightweight champion Caroline Dubois called her 'one of the best to ever do it,' while Claressa Shields said Taylor deserved to 'ride off into the sunset.' Super-featherweight champion Alycia Baumgardner described Taylor as 'a trailblazer who helped set the stage for all of us.' Former world champion Carl Frampton said Taylor's relationship with the Irish public was unlike anything else in boxing: 'There will never be another one like her.' Former cruiserweight champion Tony Bellew called the exit 'one of the most remarkable exits the sport had seen.'

Taylor's amateur career included every major medal, and her professional highlight came in 2022 when she defeated Amanda Serrano at a sold-out Madison Square Garden in the first women's bout to headline the famous arena. She avenged her sole professional defeat — a loss to Chantelle Cameron — before headlining the historic Croke Park event. Taylor said her story resonates with people because of 'our ups and downs' and added: 'My story is the story of everyone in this room: we all have our ups and downs. I think people resonate with your vulnerabilities and your successes.' Taylor indicated she will remain in the sport, with Bellew noting she would likely 'sail off into the sunset' or mentor younger fighters.
