Leclerc crashes out of Italian GP; Vettel drives Schumacher's Ferrari

Sports desk

Ferrari driver Charles Leclerc crashed out of the Italian Grand Prix at Monza on Sunday, bringing out a red flag after just two racing laps. Leclerc was defending fourth position from McLaren's Oscar Piastri at the end of the second lap when his Ferrari suffered a snap of oversteer and lost the rear through the fast Parabolica right-hander. He spun into the outside barriers at high speed but walked away unhurt. Race officials displayed the red flag to repair the barriers.

Before the race, four-time Formula One champion Sebastian Vettel drove Michael Schumacher's 2002 Ferrari F2002 for a ceremonial lap around Monza in front of the Tifosi. The demonstration marked the 30th anniversary of Schumacher's first victory with Ferrari at the same circuit in 1996. Vettel, who retired in 2022, called the experience emotional and said it brought back childhood memories. "I got to sit in that car and drive it. It felt so close, I could almost feel and hear him," Vettel told Sky. He wore a race suit reading: "For my friend Michael, there's still a race to win."

The race had begun with Alpine's Pierre Gasly on pole position, his first of the 2026 season, but Mercedes' George Russell passed Gasly for the lead at the start. Leclerc had pushed his Ferrari teammate Lewis Hamilton wide at the opening corner, dropping Hamilton to eighth. Championship leader Kimi Antonelli (Mercedes) started 19th due to a grid penalty. The full starting lineup included Oscar Piastri and Leclerc on the second row, followed by Hamilton, Max Verstappen, Antonelli, and others.

Vettel, who drove for Ferrari from 2015 to 2020 without winning a title, admitted that driving the F2002 in front of the packed grandstands made him realize he misses Formula One. "Maybe I'll return to Formula 1 in a different role. You're always a racer. But it depends on what happens next," he said. Since retiring, Vettel has become co-owner of Team Germany in SailGP and has been active in environmental causes.

Leclerc's crash was a devastating blow for Ferrari at its home race, especially after a promising qualifying that put him fourth on the grid. The red flag halted proceedings as marshals repaired the barrier at Parabolica. The race resumed with Russell leading Gasly, and Ferrari left to recover from Hamilton's demotion and Leclerc's retirement.
