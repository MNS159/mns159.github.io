LSU crushes Clemson 51-10, deepening questions about Dabo Swinney's program

Sports desk

LSU opened its season with a dominant 51-10 victory over Clemson on Saturday night in Baton Rouge, handing the Tigers the worst loss of Dabo Swinney's tenure at Clemson. The blowout, which saw LSU outgain Clemson in first downs 38 to 8 and limit the visitors to 40 rushing yards, immediately renewed scrutiny over Swinney's resistance to modern college football trends and the decision to rehire Chad Morris as offensive coordinator. Clemson managed only 83 passing yards from quarterback Christopher Vizzina before being pulled before halftime, and the Tigers' offense finished with negative rushing yards at the break. Freshman Tait Reynolds also struggled, completing 4 of 6 passes for 22 yards and losing nine yards on the ground.

Swinney had defended the Morris hire earlier in the offseason, saying, "There are always tough decisions that have to be made in this profession, and though I had to make a couple tough decisions, it was an easy decision to hire Chad Morris." The move was widely criticized, as Morris had not been a successful offensive coordinator since his first stint at Clemson from 2011–2014 and had failed as a head coach at Arkansas and as Auburn's offensive coordinator. The source material notes that no other Power Four school would have hired Morris, and likely no Group of Six school either.

The loss continued a pattern of decline for Clemson since its peak under Swinney, which included two national championships. The program's downturn accelerated after the 2021 season and included a 7–6 record last year despite having multiple early-round NFL draft picks. The source material draws parallels to the trajectory of former Oklahoma State coach Mike Gundy, who was fired after a similarly stubborn and loyal approach. Swinney's current contract runs through 2031 and carries a $57 million buyout, but the article suggests Clemson leaders could pressure him to retire at the end of the 2026 season to reduce that figure.

LSU, meanwhile, looked every bit like a College Football Playoff contender under coach Lane Kiffin. The Tigers' performance is expected to lift them into the top 10 of the AP poll, possibly as high as No. 8, after they entered the season ranked No. 11. The game was framed as a clash between the new era of college football — epitomized by Kiffin's aggressive use of the transfer portal — and Swinney's old-fashioned approach. The source material concludes that Clemson's run as a nationally elite program ended in Louisiana, and the question now is how much longer Swinney will remain at the helm.

Sources: CBS Sports
