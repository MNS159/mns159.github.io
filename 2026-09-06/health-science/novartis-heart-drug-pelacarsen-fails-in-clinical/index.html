Novartis Heart Drug Pelacarsen Fails in Clinical Trial, Shocking Cardiologists

Health & Science desk

Novartis and Ionis Pharmaceuticals announced that their experimental heart drug, pelacarsen, failed in a major clinical trial, a result that surprised company executives and cardiologists and raised questions about an emerging class of medicines targeting a genetic risk factor for heart disease.

The drug was designed to lower levels of lipoprotein(a), or Lp(a), a type of cholesterol linked to heart attacks and strokes that is not reduced by standard statin therapy. The trial enrolled patients with high Lp(a) levels and a history of cardiovascular events, testing whether pelacarsen could reduce the risk of major adverse cardiac outcomes.

According to the New York Times, the failure was described as shocking to Novartis and cardiologists. Biopharmadive.com reported that the outcome spurs questions about the viability of the entire class of drugs aimed at lowering Lp(a), as pelacarsen was one of the most advanced candidates in development.

Novartis had acquired the drug through its $9.7 billion purchase of Ionis’s cardiovascular unit in 2019, and the company had invested heavily in the program. The trial’s failure leaves the field without a clear path forward for Lp(a)-targeting therapies, though other companies continue to develop similar treatments.

The companies have not yet released detailed data from the trial, and analysts expect further analysis to determine whether any subgroups of patients might have benefited. The result is a setback for the approach of targeting Lp(a) as a way to prevent heart disease beyond traditional cholesterol management.
