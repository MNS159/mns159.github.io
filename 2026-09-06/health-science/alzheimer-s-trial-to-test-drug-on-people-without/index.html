Alzheimer's trial to test drug on people without symptoms to see if it can be prevented

Health & Science desk

An experimental Alzheimer's drug will be offered to healthy middle-aged people deemed to have a higher risk of developing the condition, in a trial aiming to prevent dementia before symptoms emerge. The first European participants in the international PrevenTRON study will be screened on Monday by the Surrey and Borders Partnership NHS foundation trust, which is leading recruitment in the UK. The phase III trial is recruiting about 1,600 people aged 55 to 80 from around the world.

Participants from across the UK will be tested to confirm they have no memory problems, then offered a blood test to assess levels of p-tau217, a biomarker that can predict cognitive decline and the development of Alzheimer's. Previous research found that people with the highest levels of p-tau217 had an estimated 78% chance of developing cognitive impairment within 10 years. Blood tests measuring p-tau217 have also been shown to identify Alzheimer's disease with up to 95% accuracy.

The trial will investigate whether prescribing trontinemab, a drug developed by the Swiss pharmaceutical company Roche, can stop dementia from developing in people with elevated p-tau217 who meet the trial criteria. Trontinemab attacks amyloid plaques, a toxic protein that builds up in the brain and slows down – and eventually kills – brain cells. Early research found that it cleared amyloid plaques in nine out of 10 people with early-stage Alzheimer's or mild cognitive impairment within 28 weeks.

Prof Ramin Nilforooshan, consultant psychiatrist and chief investigator for the PrevenTRON trial, said the study demonstrates how researchers are shifting their focus from cure to prevention. "For decades, research has focused on treating Alzheimer's disease after symptoms appear. PrevenTRON addresses a different question, whether we can act earlier, before symptoms begin," he said. He stressed the experimental nature of the treatment, telling the Telegraph: "I'm not saying this trial is magic. It's an investigational medicine. We need to wait and see: will it work or not?"

At least 15 hospitals and clinics across the UK are expected to take part in the study by the end of the year. The trial is expected to last four to six years. Participants will be randomly assigned to receive either trontinemab or a placebo and will then be monitored to see how quickly they develop any signs of dementia. Alzheimer's disease is thought to develop inside the brain up to 10 years before people typically seek treatment for memory problems. It affects around one in 14 people over the age of 65 and one in six people over the age of 80. Those interested in being screened can contact the Surrey and Borders Partnership research team at 01372 216584 or research@sabp.nhs.uk.
