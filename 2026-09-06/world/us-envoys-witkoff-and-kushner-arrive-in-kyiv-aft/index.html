Ukraine War

US envoys Witkoff and Kushner arrive in Kyiv after Moscow talks in new Ukraine peace push

International & World desk · anchored on Reuters

US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner arrived in Kyiv on Sunday for talks with Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy, a day after holding more than three hours of discussions with Russian President Vladimir Putin in Moscow. The visit marks the envoys' first official trip to Ukraine as part of a renewed effort by US President Donald Trump to end the four-and-a-half-year conflict. Because Ukraine's airspace remains closed due to the war, the pair traveled by train from Rzeszow, Poland, after flying into that country. Putin declared a three-day ceasefire to ensure the envoys' safety, and both Russia and Ukraine pledged not to strike each other's capitals during the negotiations.

The Moscow talks on Saturday ended without any announced breakthrough. Putin's foreign policy adviser, Yuri Ushakov, described the discussions as "substantive, very frank and constructive" and said "a whole series of ideas for a peace settlement" had been formulated, but he did not discuss specific outcomes. Ushakov said the talks also focused on economic issues and potentially large Russia-US projects. Witkoff and Kushner "promised to take Putin's assessments" to their talks in Kyiv, Ushakov added, and the two sides agreed that Putin and Trump would stay in touch. Senior Russian official Kirill Dmitriev, who also participated, called the visit an "important peacemaking visit."

Ahead of the Kyiv meeting, Zelenskyy wrote on social media that Ukraine is ready for a "constructive" discussion with its US counterparts. "We are interested in bringing the end of the war closer," he wrote. "Peace is needed. Security guarantees are needed. A just and sustainable postwar peace is needed. Our proposals are prepared." Reuters reported that national security advisors from the United Kingdom, France, and Germany were also in Kyiv for talks with the US envoys, citing a source familiar with the matter.

The push to end the war has lost momentum in recent months after talks with Putin at the Kremlin in January failed to produce a result, and as US attention has been focused for the last six months on its war with Iran. Negotiators have struggled to bring both sides together on key issues including the future of the industrial Donbas region of eastern Ukraine. For Ukraine, hard security guarantees from its European allies and Washington are crucial to deterring Russia from further aggression. Kyrylo Budanov, Zelenskyy's chief of staff, told the New York Times last weekend that he did not expect the fighting to end before spring 2027 and predicted further Russian strategic air strikes on Ukraine this winter.

The air war has intensified as troops have struggled to make progress along the 1,250-kilometer (775-mile) front line. In a social media post on Saturday, Zelenskyy said Russia had carried out overnight strikes on Kyiv and Boryspil international airports and linked the attack to the planned US visit, writing: "Clearly, these Russian strikes on the airports are a reaction to the discussions and preparations for the possibility of the US side using an aircraft to travel to Ukraine." Russia launched ballistic missiles from its Rostov and Voronezh regions and deployed 167 attack drones overnight, Ukraine's air force said. Ukrainian air defenses shot down or suppressed 128 drones, according to the air force. Russian attacks killed five people and wounded five more at an industrial facility in Ukraine's southern Dnipropetrovsk region, local military administration head Oleksandr Hanzha said. A further two people were wounded in an overnight Russian attack on Ukraine's Kyiv region, said Tymur Tkachenko, head of the regional military administration. In the Boryspil district, a fire sparked by a strike partially destroyed a nine-story residential building. Russian forces also repeatedly struck a shopping center in the southern Mykolaiv region with drones overnight, injuring a 72-year-old man and a 17-year-old girl. Ukraine has focused its drone attacks on Russia's energy sector and targeted online retailers deep inside Russia to try to bring the war home to the public. Ukrainian attacks wounded three people, including a 14-year-old girl, in Russia's border region of Belgorod, local officials said. Russia's Ministry of Defense said its forces had shot down 53 Ukrainian drones overnight.
