Israeli air attacks on southern Lebanon kill at least four

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage is balanced across the spectrum

Israeli air attacks in southern Lebanon on Sunday killed at least four people and wounded 20 others, according to Lebanese authorities, in the latest violation of a truce. Lebanon’s official National News Agency (NNA) reported strikes on the towns of Nabatieh, Arab Salim, Nabatieh al-Fawqa and Kfar Reman.

In Arab Salim, two women were killed and six people wounded after Israeli forces issued a forced displacement order for a building in the town, the NNA said. In Nabatieh al-Fawqa, Lebanon’s Health Ministry confirmed at least two people were killed in an Israeli air strike. A strike on Nabatieh city destroyed a historical building owned by the brother of a Lebanese member of parliament, damaging several nearby structures. In Kfar Reman, an air attack destroyed the home of the late former mayor; five people were treated at Nabatieh hospitals and discharged. Another strike hit the Sayyida Masouma Garden, destroying what remained of the municipal park after it was hit the previous day.

Israel said it carried out the strikes in response to Hezbollah launching two drones toward its forces in southern Lebanon, describing the attack as a "blatant violation" by the armed group. Since March, when Hezbollah attacked Israel during the US-Israel war on Iran, Israel has conducted air strikes and a ground invasion across Lebanon. Lebanese authorities say more than 4,300 people have been killed. A broader US-Iran Memorandum of Understanding in June and a subsequent framework agreement between Israel and Lebanon reduced the number of attacks, but Lebanon continues to report strikes, artillery shelling and widespread demolitions of civilian buildings in southern villages and towns.

On Friday, Israeli strikes in southern and eastern Lebanon killed four people, including an 18-year-old woman, as Israel said it hit Hezbollah targets in response to a drone launch. Earlier this week, Israel announced it had taken "operational control" of the strategic Ali al-Taher ridge near Nabatieh; Hezbollah has not commented. Israeli forces are still operating inside a self-declared "security zone" about 10 km (six miles) deep inside Lebanon. Israeli public broadcaster Kan reported that the military is preparing to reposition three to four kilometres closer to the Israeli border along a new "Grey Line", pulling back from parts of the "Yellow Line" that extends north of the Litani River, though the plan has not been approved by political leaders.

Hezbollah condemned Israel’s campaign, saying it is taking place amid "complete international silence and blatant American complicity" and reiterated its rejection of "fruitless direct negotiations" with Beirut. Qatar’s Prime Minister and Foreign Minister Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman bin Jassim Al Thani spoke by phone with Lebanese Prime Minister Nawaf Salam on Saturday, as Doha pressed for de-escalation.
