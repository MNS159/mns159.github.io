US strikes three Iranian oil tankers after Iran fires missiles at warships

International & World desk
LiberalConservative
Coverage tilts slightly center-left

The United States struck three Iranian oil tankers on Saturday in retaliation for Iran's ballistic missile attacks on two US warships near the Strait of Hormuz, marking a sharp escalation in the months-long conflict. US Central Command said its forces targeted a tanker off Iran's Kharg Island, the hub through which Iran exported 90 percent of its crude before the war, a second tanker near Jask, and an unladen vessel in the Gulf of Oman whose crew was ordered to abandon ship. The strikes followed an Iranian ballistic missile launch against a US aircraft carrier and a navy destroyer, which CENTCOM said successfully evaded the attacks without injury to US personnel. 'If you shoot at two of our ships, we will impose an even higher economic cost — taking out three of yours,' CENTCOM commander Admiral Brad Cooper said.

The tit-for-tat attacks are the latest in a struggle for control of the Strait of Hormuz, a strategic waterway through which 20 percent of the world's oil supplies flowed before the war began on February 28. Iran effectively shut the strait in retaliation for US-Israeli attacks, and the US has maintained a naval blockade of Iranian ports. Washington insists the waterway is open, but data from marine analytics firm Kpler shows vessel traffic has plummeted: a 10-day average of just 13 ships per day, compared to pre-war flows of about 20 million barrels of oil daily. Brent crude futures closed at $96.28 a barrel on Friday, up from roughly $70 before the war, and US diesel prices hit a record $5.85 per gallon.

Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps said it launched 'several ballistic missiles' at the US warships, accusing them of 'harassing Iranian ships' and enforcing the blockade. 'The Iranians did not only target two ordinary warships – they targeted a missile destroyer and, remarkably, an aircraft carrier … the backbone of the American campaign against Iran,' defence analyst Wolfgang Pusztai told Al Jazeera, calling it 'a very, very significant development.' Iran's state media warned all vessels in the Gulf to avoid 'unauthorised waterways' or face targeting. Separately, Iran claimed it struck an unmanned US vessel trying to enter the strait on Sunday, according to the Associated Press.

The renewed exchanges come after a weekslong lull in direct US-Iran military action. The US says the tankers it struck were part of a 'multibillion-dollar shadow network' financing Iran's Revolutionary Guard. Iran's foreign ministry condemned the strikes as illegal and a threat to international peace, while Iranian lawmaker Hassan Ghashghavi described the conflict as 'an existential war with America,' arguing that the US naval blockade violates international law. Experts say neither side is likely to back down. 'The winner is going to be the one that can accept more costs and punishment,' said Sina Azodi, director of the Middle East studies programme at George Washington University. 'There's going to be more clashes … There can be miscalculations. There can be more civilian casualties.'

The escalation is adding to global energy supply concerns. US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has announced economic measures targeting Tehran's oil revenue, and President Donald Trump claims the US is in 'total control' of the strait. However, polls show a majority of Americans oppose the war, and Trump's approval rating has dropped to 33 percent. Analysts predict oil prices could rise above $100 per barrel, and Iran's military is reportedly preparing to break the blockade. 'Does it make Iran surrender? No, it compels Iran. It incentivises Iran to lash out and retaliate' more harshly, said Ali Akbar Dareini, an analyst at the Center for Strategic Studies in Tehran.
